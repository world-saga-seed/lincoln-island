## a

After: “The Frenchman whom chance threw on board my vessel sixteen years ago?”

The Captain speaks in October of 1868, so his sixteen years put the Frenchman on board in 1852. A few pages on, the same account gives the night as the 6th of November, 1866, and the escape as the 22nd of June, 1867. The colony’s own dates will not move. The colonists left Richmond in March of 1865, in the war, and this is their fourth year on the island. By the same account, too, the Captain had lain six years in his port beneath the island when their balloon fell, which brings him there about 1859. His sixteen years agree with that. The dates of 1866 and 1867 agree with nothing else in the papers: they fall while the vessel lay in her port under the island and went nowhere. I take them for a slip in the setting down, and the Captain’s own count for the true one.
