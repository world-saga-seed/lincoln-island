## Outline

### 1. The dwelling regained; Jup fed (II.7 ¶1–¶3)

- The colonists have won back their dwelling without taking the old overflow, which spared them masonry; the narrator calls it lucky that the apes were seized with a terror both sudden and inexplicable just as the assault was about to begin by another way. *(Drafted.)*

  - quote: désordre et non dégât

### 2. Plans around the table (II.7 ¶4–¶8)

- Before bed, round the table, Cyrus and the others discuss urgent projects. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: discutèrent quelques projets dont l’exécution était urgente
- The two chief ones: a bridge over the Mercy to link the south of the island with Granite-house, then a corral for mouflons or other wool-bearing animals. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: l’établissement d’un pont sur la Mercy
- Both answer the clothing question: the bridge will let them carry the balloon envelope (linen), the corral will give wool for winter clothes. *(Drafted.)*

- Cyrus means to put the corral at the sources of the Red Creek, where there is pasture; the road from the Grande-Vue plateau to the sources is partly made; a better cart is wanted and a draught animal if one can be taken. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: aux sources mêmes du Creek-Rouge
- Nab draws attention to the poultry-yard, which must be near the kitchen; the best place is the lake shore beside the old overflow; the pair of tinamous taken on the last excursion will be the first trial of domestication. *(Drafted.)*

### 3. 3 November: the bridge begun (II.7 ¶9–¶12)

- The next day, 3 November, the new works begin with the bridge, all hands employed; saws, axes, chisels and hammers on their shoulders, the colonists, turned carpenters, go down to the beach. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Le lendemain, — 3 novembre
- Pencroff wonders whether Jup might take it into his head to pull up "the ladder he so gallantly sent back yesterday". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qu’il nous a si galamment renvoyée hier
- Cyrus has the foot of the ladder fixed with two stakes driven in the sand; the colonists then go up the left bank of the Mercy to the bend of the river. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: deux pieux, solidement enfoncés dans le sable

### 4. The bend, and the plan to make an island of the plateau (II.7 ¶13–¶25)

- They stop at the bend to see whether the bridge should go there; the place suits. From it to Port Ballon, "discovered the day before on the south coast", is three and a half miles, and a carriage road can be cut. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: découvert la veille sur la côte méridionale
- Cyrus tells his plan, long meditated: to isolate the Grande-Vue plateau completely, against four-footed animals and apes. Granite-house, the Chimneys, the poultry-yard and the upper plateau for sowing would be protected. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: isoler complètement le plateau de Grande-Vue
- The plateau is already defended, the French says, "on three sides" by water, though it goes on to name four: on the north-west by the shore of Lake Grant, from the angle at the old overflow to the cut made on the lake's east shore. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: depuis l’angle appuyé à l’orifice de l’ancien déversoir
- On the north, from that cut to the sea, by the new watercourse (the creek made on the plateau and the beach, above and below the fall), which needs only deepening. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il suffisait, en effet, de creuser le lit de ce creek
- On the whole east edge by the sea itself, from the creek's mouth to the Mercy's mouth. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Sur toute la lisière de l’est, par la mer elle-même
- On the south from that mouth to the Mercy's bend, where the bridge is to stand. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: jusqu’au coude de la Mercy où devait être établi le pont
- What remains is the west, between the bend and the south angle of the lake, under a mile: a wide, deep ditch to be filled from the lake, its overflow joining the Mercy by a second fall; the lake level will drop a little, but the Red Creek's flow is enough. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: sur une distance inférieure à un mille
- Cyrus says the plateau will then be a true island, linked to the rest of the domain only by the bridge on the Mercy, the two small bridges at the fall, and two more to be built (one over the ditch, one on the left bank of the Mercy); if these can be raised at will the plateau is safe from surprise. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le plateau de Grande-Vue sera une île véritable
- Cyrus has drawn a map of the plateau; the plan is approved unanimously; Pencroff cries "The bridge first!". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Au pont, d’abord !

### 5. Building the bridge, 3 to 20 November (II.7 ¶26–¶31)

- Trees are chosen, felled, trimmed and cut into beams, planks and joists. The bridge is to be fixed on the right bank and movable on the left, raised by counterweights like a lock bridge. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: se relever au moyen de contre-poids
- A plate caption (placed after ¶26) notes the work. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Ce fut un travail considérable.
  - at: II.7 pl2
- The Mercy is about eighty feet wide; piles are driven into the bed with a pile-driver, to make two arches able to bear heavy loads. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: la Mercy était large de quatre-vingts pieds environ
- The narrator says tools, ironwork, the engineer's skill and the colonists' seven months' practice were all there; Spilett holds his own with the sailor. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: depuis sept mois, avaient nécessairement acquis une grande habileté de main
- The bridge takes "three weeks" (seventeen days by the dates; flagged in the notes); they lunch on the site and return to Granite-house only for supper, the weather being magnificent. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dura trois semaines
- Jup acclimatises and watches his new masters with great curiosity; Pencroff does not yet give him full liberty, waiting until the plateau is closed; Top and Jup play together, Jup doing everything gravely. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Top et Jup étaient au mieux
- On 20 November the bridge is finished; its movable part, balanced by counterweights, tilts easily; when raised there is a gap of twenty feet between hinge and last crosspiece, too wide for animals. *(Drafted.)*

### 6. The envelope, and the second wheat field (II.7 ¶32–¶38)

- The colonists want to bring the balloon envelope to safety; this needs a cart at Port Ballon and a road through the thick Far-West. Nab and Pencroff scout to the port and find the "stock of cloth" unharmed in the grotto where it was stored; work on the plateau goes on. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le « stock de toile » ne souffrait aucunement
- Pencroff says finishing the plateau will let them set up the poultry-yard with no fear of foxes or other pests; Nab adds they can clear the plateau and transplant wild plants; Pencroff adds: "our second wheat field". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: notre second champ de blé

### 7. The ditch and the Glycerine Creek, 21 November to mid-December (II.7 ¶39–¶40)

- On 21 November Cyrus begins to mark the ditch that will close the plateau on the west, from the south angle of Lake Grant to the Mercy's bend; two to three feet of vegetable earth, then granite, so nitro-glycerine is made afresh. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Le 21 novembre, Cyrus Smith commença à dessiner le fossé
- In under a fortnight a ditch twelve feet wide and six deep is dug; a second cut in the lake's rocky rim sends the water into the new bed, forming a small stream named "Creek-Glycérine", a tributary of the Mercy; the lake level falls, almost imperceptibly. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Creek-Glycérine
- The bed of the beach brook is widened and the sand held by a double palisade. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: une double palissade
- With the first fortnight of December the works are finished; the plateau, an irregular pentagon of about four miles' perimeter, is ringed by water. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: une sorte de pentagone irrégulier ayant un périmètre de quatre milles environ

### 9. The envelope and the draught animal (II.7 ¶47–¶51)

### 10. 23 December: two onaggas (II.7 ¶52–¶60)

- On 23 December Nab cries out and Top barks; the colonists, working at the Chimneys, run up. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Un jour, le 23 décembre

  - quote: dont les ponceaux n’avaient pas été fermés
- Harbert names them onaggas, between the zebra and the quagga; no long ears, finer shape. Pencroff says whether asses or horses they are "moteurs" and worth capturing. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Ce sont des onaggas
- Pencroff creeps through the grass to the small bridge over the Glycerine Creek and tips it; the onaggas are prisoners. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le fit basculer, et les onaggas furent prisonniers

  - quote: on les laisserait aller et venir librement sur le plateau

### 11. Harness, the road to Port Ballon, and the envelope brought home (II.7 ¶61–¶64)

- Harness and traces of plant fibre are made; a few days after the capture the cart is ready, and a straight road, or cut, runs through the Far-West from the Mercy's bend to Port Ballon; toward the end of December the onaggas are tried for the first time. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ce fut vers la fin de décembre qu’on essaya pour la première fois les onaggas
- Pencroff has tamed them enough to eat from his hand, but once harnessed they rear and are hard to hold; the narrator adds that the onagga is harnessed in southern Africa and has been acclimatised in Europe. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ils se cabrèrent
- That day all but Pencroff, who walks at the head of his beasts, ride in the cart to Port Ballon; it arrives without mishap and the envelope and the balloon's rigging are loaded the same day. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: on put y charger l’enveloppe et les divers agrès de l’aérostat
- At eight in the evening the cart recrosses the Mercy bridge and stops on the beach; the onaggas are unharnessed and stabled; Pencroff sighs so loudly that the echoes of Granite-house ring. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: À huit heures du soir
