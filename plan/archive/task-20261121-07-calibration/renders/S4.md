# S4: I.22 ¶41

¶41

Towards the 25th, after a fresh alternation of snow and rain, the wind shifted to the south-east, and suddenly the cold became extremely severe. According to the engineer's estimate, the mercurial column of a Fahrenheit thermometer would have marked not less than eight degrees below zero (22°.22 centigrade below freezing-point), and this intensity of cold, rendered still more painful by a piercing wind, lasted for several days. The colonists were again obliged to shut themselves up in Granite-house, and, as it was necessary to stop up hermetically all the openings of the façade, leaving only the strict passage needed for renewing the air, the consumption of candles was considerable. In order to economize them, the colonists often had no other light than the flame of the hearths, where fuel was not spared. Several times one or another of them went down to the beach, amid the pieces of ice which the flood heaped up there at every tide, but they soon climbed back to Granite-house, and it was not without difficulty and pain that their hands held on to the rounds of the ladder. In that intense cold the rungs burned their fingers.
