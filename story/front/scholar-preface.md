# A Note on the Papers

The papers printed here came to the Society from the estate of Mr. Herbert Brown, who farmed the colony’s land in this State until his death, about the year 1920, at the age of seventy. He was the last of them. When the estate was settled, his heirs gave the Society what the colonists had kept: their account of the island, the engineer’s reckonings, and a number of loose sheets. I was asked to read them for the Society, and to say where they could be trusted.

They can be trusted almost everywhere. The account was set down by men who had every reason to be exact, and most of it was written soon after the events. I have not corrected it, nor improved it, nor tried to tell it better.

But a record kept over four years, in more than one hand, does not always agree with itself. Here a date is a day off from the date a few pages back. There an island is said to lie to the north-east, when the engineer’s own reckoning, and the course the colonists sailed to reach it, put it to the south-west. An hour is given that the hours around it cannot allow. I have noted these, and only these. Where the papers disagree with something outside them, a book or a map or a man’s memory, I have let it be. Their own word is all I am qualified to weigh.

I have gone about it in this way. Where two passages disagree, I have looked for a third that decides between them, and where I found one I have said which reading it supports, and why. Where I found none, I have said so and left the matter open. I have not supposed a reason the papers do not give. Where the same mistake comes back, as a reckoning carried forward once will come back, I have written one note and pointed the later places to it.

The notes are lettered in each chapter, to keep them apart from the numbered notes that came with the papers.

*Iowa.*
