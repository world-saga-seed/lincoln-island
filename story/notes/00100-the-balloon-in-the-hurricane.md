# The Balloon in the Hurricane (notes)

## Notes for drafting

- **Where it sits.** The first chapter of Part I, "Les naufragés de l’air"; it opens in the middle of the fall and ends on the beach with one man missing. Nothing precedes it. *(Drafted.)*
- **Cast.** *(Drafted.)* unless marked.
  - The five passengers, first at ¶17 (the voices from ¶1): "cinq passagers". Only one is named here, by address: "monsieur Cyrus" (¶3).
  - A "voix puissante" (¶11) and a "a male voice" (¶36) lead the throwing-out; the text does not say they are one man, or that either is Cyrus. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - Top, the dog, first at ¶52, named in ¶53; he is "close to his master" (¶52), the master unnamed. *(Drafted.)*
  - The missing passenger, ¶63, unnamed; the chapter does not say who.
  - Havana and Guadeloupe are named only as earlier disasters (¶14).
- **Places.** *(Drafted.)*
  - The air over the Pacific (¶13); the zone of the storm from the 35th parallel north to the 40th south (¶14); Havana and Guadeloupe (¶14).
  - The land sighted to the south-west, "rather high" (¶56), "island or continent" not known (¶58); the sandy beach where they land (¶61).
- **Things.** *(Drafted.)*
  - The balloon, 50,000 cubic feet (about 1,700 cubic metres), with a tear in its envelope (¶24, ¶32, ¶49, n2).
  - The wicker basket, cut away at about 2 p.m. (¶34, ¶46); the hoop and the net (¶25, ¶47).
  - Ballast, munitions, arms, provisions, pocket things, all thrown (¶5, ¶20, ¶25).
  - A sack of ten thousand francs in gold, thrown (¶38, ¶39).
  - An anchor is mentioned (¶28) but not used.
- **Time.** *(Drafted.)* unless marked.
  - 23 March 1865, about four in the evening: the cry (¶13).
  - The hurricane ran from 18 to 26 March (¶14); at 23 March it had lasted five days (¶18).
  - The night of 23/24 March (¶21). 24 March: dawn (¶21), about eleven (¶22), noon (¶24), one (¶31), two (¶35), four (¶51, ¶59), half past four (¶60, "Half an hour later", from four) *(My reading, to confirm.)*, two minutes later the landing (¶61).
  - The chapter covers about a day and a half of story time (from about 4 p.m. on the 23rd to about 4.30 p.m. on the 24th); the hurricane's history runs back to the 18th.
- **Clues.** *(Drafted.)* unless marked.
  - Where the balloon came from and who the passengers are: "Whence came this balloon?" (¶18). Answered in I.2.
  - Who is missing: only "The missing passenger" (¶64). I.2 ¶58 names him.
  - The land to the south-west, and where on the earth it lies: "they scarcely knew towards what part of the world the hurricane had carried them" (¶58).
  - The balloon, relieved of the passengers' weight, "vanished into space" (¶62), after the tear that "it was impossible to repair" (¶49).
  - The sudden bound after the sea blow, "suddenly relieved of another part of its weight" (¶61), which ¶64 explains by the missing man.
- **Length.** 66 numbered paragraphs (¶1–¶66), 2,375 words in them; the summary line ¶0 is 46 words more. Two footnotes, one plate caption (after ¶66). *(Drafted.)*

## Open and flags

- **Time of the 24th: two "four o'clock" lines.** ¶51: "At four o'clock, the balloon was no more than five hundred feet above the surface of the water." ¶59: "Now, at four o'clock it was plain the balloon could no longer stay up. It was skimming the surface of the sea." The same hour gives 500 feet and skimming the waves. Card neither. *(Drafted.)*
- **Two four o'clocks, two days.** ¶13 puts the opening cry at "about four in the afternoon, during the day of 23 March 1865", with the sea "not five hundred feet" (¶10); ¶51 puts the balloon at five hundred feet at four o'clock on 24 March. *(Drafted.)*
- **When the hurricane ended.** ¶14: "It was a hurricane, without intermission, that lasted from 18 to 26 March." ¶22 (24 March): "He seemed to have killed himself." *(Drafted.)*
- **Start in the storm, or before it.** ¶18: "He obviously could not have left during the hurricane." Against I.2 ¶55 and ¶57: "the balloon, leaving on an oblique course, disappeared" in the hurricane of 20 March (the hurricane began on the 18th by I.1 ¶18 and I.2 ¶20). *(Drafted.)*
- **Wind direction.** ¶14 "a gale from the north-east" and ¶30 "from north-east to south-west"; against I.2 ¶20, "a moderate north-west wind", which turned to a hurricane. *(Drafted.)*
- **Distance.** ¶18 gives "two thousand miles in twenty-four hours" for five days; I.2 ¶1 and ¶57 give seven thousand and "more than six thousand" miles (see the notes of 00200).
- **Wikisource corrections.** None in I.1 (texts/README.md lists none for this chapter; the scan check read page 2, ¶4 to ¶17 and n1, and found a match). The text keeps the print's "très-loin" (¶18). *(Drafted.)*
- **Footnotes.** n1 and n2 are Hetzel's; n1 gives the metric speed, n2 the volume in cubic metres. *(Drafted.)*
