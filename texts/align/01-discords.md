### 2. cut: the barometer reading (I.1 ¶14; White ¶14)
- The French gives the fall of the barometer to 710 millimetres; White gives no reading. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le baromètre tomba à sept cent dix millimètres
  - white: —

### 3. cut: the Bureau-Veritas as source of the shipwreck count (I.1 ¶14; White ¶14)
- The French says the ships thrown on the coast were counted in the Bureau-Veritas returns; White says only "hundreds of shipwrecks" and names no source. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: que les relevés du Bureau-Veritas chiffrèrent par centaines
  - white: hundreds of shipwrecks

### 4. condensed: the dates of the Havana and Guadeloupe hurricanes (I.1 ¶14; White ¶14)
- The French dates the two earlier hurricanes to the day (25 October 1810, 26 July 1825); White gives the years only. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: l’un le 25 octobre 1810, l’autre le 26 juillet 1825
  - white: in 1810 and 1825

### 5. cut: footnote n1, the speed in metric units (I.1 ¶16 n1; White —)
- The French footnote converts ninety miles an hour into metres per second, kilometres an hour and leagues; White has no footnote. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Soit 46 mètres par seconde ou 166 kilomètres à l’heure
  - white: —

### 6. changed: the clouds of dawn (I.1 ¶21; White ¶19)
- The French says the clouds were "plus vésiculaires" (finer, more vesicular) and had risen; White says they were "emptied". *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: les nuages, plus vésiculaires, étaient remontés dans les hauteurs du ciel
  - white: the emptied clouds rose high into the heavens

### 7. cut: the date, 24 March (I.1 ¶21; White ¶19)
- The French dates the first signs of calm to the morning of 24 March; White says only "with the coming of day". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Dès le début de cette journée du 24 mars
  - white: with the coming of day

### 8. cut: the wind drops to "grand frais", half its speed (I.1 ¶21; White ¶19)
- The French says the wind fell to a "grand frais" and that the speed of the air layers was halved, still "une brise à trois ris"; White keeps only the "three reef breeze". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: la vitesse de translation des couches atmosphériques diminua de moitié
  - white: had subsided into what sailors would call a “three reef breeze.”

### 9. condensed: the hurricane "killed itself" (I.1 ¶22; White ¶20)
- The French personifies the hurricane as having killed itself, and says the clear air is a limpidity one sees and even feels; White says only that the storm "was ended". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Il paraissait s’être tué lui-même.
  - white: but rather as if it was ended

### 10. cut: the noon altitude, 2,000 feet (I.1 ¶24; White ¶21)
- The French gives noon and a height of two thousand feet above the sea; White gives neither the hour nor the height. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Vers midi, l’aérostat ne planait plus qu’à une hauteur de deux mille pieds au-dessus de la mer.
  - white: —

### 11. cut: footnote n2, the capacity in cubic metres (I.1 ¶24 n2; White —)
- The French footnote gives about 1,700 cubic metres; White has no footnote. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Environ 1 700 mètres cubes.
  - white: —

### 12. cut: the horizon of forty miles (I.1 ¶29; White ¶23)
- The French says the passengers, from their height, could see over a radius of forty miles; White says only that the ocean was limitless to them. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dont les regards s’étendaient alors sur un rayon de quarante milles
  - white: limitless even to them from their commanding height

### 13. condensed: the wind's direction, north-east to south-west (I.1 ¶30; White ¶24)
- The French gives the line of drift as from north-east to south-west; White gives only "toward the southwest". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: suivant la direction du vent, c’est-à-dire du nord-est au sud-ouest
  - white: moving rapidly along with the wind toward the southwest

### 14. condensed: the lightest object and the vertical displacement (I.1 ¶48; White ¶38)
- The French says throwing out the lightest object causes a vertical displacement (either way); White says it makes the balloon "rise". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Il suffit de jeter l’objet le plus léger pour provoquer un déplacement dans le sens vertical.
  - white: It is only necessary to relieve it of the lightest object in order to have it rise.

### 15. condensed: "no human means" (I.1 ¶50; White ¶38)
- The French says no human means could now save them and that they could count only on God's help; White says only that the men "must look to God for succor". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Aucun moyen humain ne pouvait les sauver désormais.
  - white: the men having done everything they could do, must look to God for succor

### 16. condensed: land that may or may not be hospitable (I.1 ¶58; White ¶43)
- The French says the passengers did not know whether the land was inhabited or not, hospitable or not; White says "inhabited or desert" and drops the question of welcome. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qu’elle fût habitée ou qu’elle ne le fût pas, qu’elle dût être hospitalière ou non
  - white: whether inhabited or desert
