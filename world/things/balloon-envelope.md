# The balloon's envelope: the cotton cloth saved from the pine

*The balloon's cotton envelope, found hanging in a pine on 2 November with a torn lower appendix, stored at port Ballon, fetched by cart and made into the colony's linen.*

Keywords: enveloppe, envelope, aérostat, balloon, cotton, toile, cloth, vernis, varnish, soupape, valve, linen

- **What it is.**
  - Top brings back a scrap of strong canvas, soiled with mud, from the forest (II.5 ¶62, ¶63). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: un lambeau d’étoffe souillée de boue
    - quote: C’était un morceau de forte toile
  - It is the whole remnant of the balloon, "our air-boat" (II.5 ¶84). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: C’est tout ce qui reste de notre bateau aérien, de notre ballon qui s’est échoué là-haut
  - It is a cotton cloth of fine quality, some hundreds of ells of it, once rid of its varnish (II.5 ¶87). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: ces quelques centaines d’aunes d’une toile de coton de belle qualité, quand elle serait débarrassée de son vernis
  - It has a valve, springs and a copper fitting; apart from the break it is in good state, and only its lower appendix is torn (II.5 ¶89). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: l’enveloppe, avec sa soupape, ses ressorts, sa garniture de cuivre
    - quote: L’enveloppe, sauf la fracture, était en bon état, et, seul, son appendice inférieur avait été déchiré
  - Pencroff counts on linen for years from it, handkerchiefs and shirts (II.5 ¶86). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: Voilà de quoi nous fournir de linge pendant des années
    - quote: faire des mouchoirs et des chemises
  - The colonists may keep it whole for a new escape by air, or put it to use as cloth (II.5 ¶87). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: s’ils voulaient tenter une nouvelle évasion par les airs
  - Pencroff would cut a foresail and a jib from it for a good boat of about twenty tons and clothe the colony with the rest; Smith answers "We shall see" (II.5 ¶91, ¶92). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: nous construirons un bon bateau d’une vingtaine de tonneaux
    - quote: découper dans cette toile une misaine et un foc
    - quote: Quant au reste, il servira à nous habiller
  - It is heavy and has to be left in safety until a cart can take it (II.5 ¶94). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: cette charge de toile, de cordes, de cordages, dont le poids était considérable
  - It is stored in a rock cavity by the shore (see [the balloon store](../places/balloon-store.md)) (II.5 ¶94). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: une assez vaste cavité rocheuse
  - Smith, a practical mind, cannot think of keeping it as a balloon and risking a hot-air balloon over a sea that is practically without limits to leave the island; that would be admissible only for people short of everything (II.7 ¶47). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: se risquer dans un ballon à air chaud pour quitter l’île
    - quote: Cyrus Smith, esprit pratique, n’y pouvait songer
  - It is brought to Granite-house in the cart at the end of December (II.7 ¶63). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: on put y charger l’enveloppe et les divers agrès de l’aérostat
  - Its seams are unpicked, and the thread reused (II.8 ¶2). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: celui qui avait déjà servi à la couture des bandes de l’aérostat
    - quote: Ces longues bandes furent décousues
  - It is degreased with soda and potash got by burning plants, so that the cotton, rid of its varnish, regains its natural suppleness and elasticity, then bleached perfectly white by the open air (II.8 ¶3). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: dégraissées au moyen de soude et de potasse obtenues par incinération de plantes
    - quote: reprit sa souplesse et son élasticité naturelles
    - quote: il acquit une blancheur parfaite
  - It makes the colony's linen (see [the linen](the-linen.md)) (II.8 ¶4). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: Quelques douzaines de chemises et de chaussettes
  - Its inexhaustible cloth is to furnish the canvas for the sails of the mill (see [the windmill](the-windmill.md)) (II.16 ¶84). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: l’inépuisable enveloppe du ballon leur fournirait la toile nécessaire
- **Appears in:** II.5, II.7, II.8, II.16
- **Open:**
  - The engineer calls it a hot-air balloon, but chapter I.1 speaks of its gas escaping through a tear (II.7 ¶47; I.1 ¶49). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: se risquer dans un ballon à air chaud pour quitter l’île
    - quote: Le gaz fuyait par la déchirure
