# II.8: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller j. Beads corrected: none.*

### 1. cut: the sailors' gift for sewing (II.8 ¶2; White ¶1)
- The French adds the general remark that sailors are remarkably apt at needlework; White gives only the fact that Pencroff sews unequalled. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Personne n’ignore, en effet, que les marins ont une aptitude remarquable pour le métier de couturière.
  - white: —

### 2. condensed: potash, incineration and the cotton's softness (II.8 ¶3; White ¶2)
- The French has soda and potash from burning plants, and the cotton regaining its natural softness and elasticity; White has only "soda procured as before" and the varnish removed. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: soude et de potasse obtenues par incinération de plantes
  - white: by means of soda procured as before

### 3. condensed: the game (II.8 ¶6; White ¶3)
- The French names agoutis, peccaries, capybaras and kangaroos; White says only birds and beasts. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Agoutis, pécaris, cabiais, kangourous, gibiers de poil et de plume
  - white: which fairly swarmed with birds and beasts

### 4. changed: leaving "their domain" becomes leaving the island (II.8 ¶7; White ¶3)
- Cyrus asks where chance might throw him and his friends if they left their domain; White makes it leaving the island, and drops the chance (hasard) of being thrown somewhere. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: dans le cas où ils quitteraient leur domaine
  - white: in case they left the island

### 5. addition: elder "shoots" (II.8 ¶9; White ¶4)
- The French says the colonists use the berries of the elder for coffee, and names the family (caprifoliacées); White adds "shoots" and drops the family. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: les baies de ces arbrisseaux, qui appartiennent à la famille des caprifoliacées
  - white: the colonists had already used its shoots and berries in place of coffee

### 6. condensed: fuming nitric acid and the natural saltpetre (II.8 ¶10; White ¶5)
- The French says the second ingredient is fuming nitric acid, which Cyrus, having sulphuric acid, had already made by acting on natural saltpetre; White has only "nitric acid, which Smith could easily produce as before". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ce n’était que de l’acide azotique fumant
  - white: the only other substance necessary for the manufacture of pyroxyline was nitric acid, which Smith could easily produce as before

### 7. cut: the "great inequality of effect" (II.8 ¶11; White ¶5)
- Of the faults of pyroxyle the French lists great inequality of effect, excessive inflammability and instantaneous deflagration; White omits the first and shortens the third. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: une grande inégalité d’effet
  - white: —

### 8. condensed: damage to firearms (II.8 ¶11; White ¶5)
- The French says the too instantaneous burning can damage firearms; White says only "too instantaneous a deflagration for firearms". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qui peut dégrader les armes à feu
  - white: a too instantaneous deflagration for firearms

### 9. condensed: ordinary against fuming nitric acid (II.8 ¶12–¶13; White ¶6)
- The French says Cyrus had only ordinary nitric acid, not the fuming (monohydrate) acid first named, and so mixed it with sulphuric acid to get the same result; White gives the mixture and drops that he lacked the fuming acid and why he mixed. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: n’avait à sa disposition que de l’acide azotique ordinaire, et non de l’acide azotique fumant
  - white: Smith made a mixture of three parts of nitric acid with five of concentrated sulphuric acid

### 10. changed: horseradish becomes charlock (II.8 ¶14; White ¶7)
- Raifort (horseradish) is rendered "charlocks", a different plant. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: épinards, cresson, raifort, raves
  - white: spinach, cresses, charlocks, and radishes

### 11. changed: charcoal becomes coal (II.8 ¶14; White ¶7)
- Charbon, which here is the charcoal Cyrus has already named, is rendered "coal". *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: On véhicula également de notables quantités de bois et de charbon.
  - white: They also hauled large quantities of wood and coal

### 12. cut: the oyster-bed renewed (II.8 ¶15; White ¶8)
- The French says the oyster-bed's stock was frequently renewed; White says only that it gave a daily supply. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dont les produits étaient fréquemment renouvelés
  - white: it furnished a daily supply of excellent mollusks

### 13. cut: iron hooks (II.8 ¶15; White ¶8)
- Pencroff's set-lines are armed with iron hooks in the French; White omits the iron. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: des lignes de fond, armées d’hameçons de fer
  - white: Pencroff had made set-lines

### 14. changed: turtle-hunting dated "about this time" becomes "sometimes" (II.8 ¶16; White ¶9)
- The French places the turtle hunt "about this time"; White makes it an occasional activity. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: On fit aussi, vers cette époque, la chasse aux tortues marines
  - white: Sometimes the little party hunted the sea-turtles

### 15. cut: the eggs' hard white shells and uncoagulating albumen (II.8 ¶16; White ¶9)
- The French describes the eggs as white-shelled and hard, with albumen that does not coagulate; White says only "round eggs". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: à coque blanche et dure, et dont l’albumine a la propriété de ne point se coaguler comme celle des œufs d’oiseaux
  - white: enclosing the round eggs, which were left to the sun to hatch

### 16. changed: crucifers become shell-fish (II.8 ¶18; White ¶11)
- The turtle broth is seasoned with aromatic herbs and a few crucifers (cabbage-family plants); White says "a handful of shell-fish thrown in". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: relevé d’herbes aromatiques et agrémenté de quelques crucifères
  - white: seasoned with herbs, and a handful of shell-fish thrown in

### 17. cut: the females lead the salmon (II.8 ¶19; White ¶12)
- The French says the females, seeking spawning places, go ahead of the males and make a great noise in the fresh water; White has only that the shoals ascended to spawn. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: les femelles, allant rechercher des endroits convenables pour frayer, précédaient les mâles et faisaient grand bruit à travers les eaux douces
  - white: Shoals of salmon ascended the Mercy for many miles, in order to spawn.

### 18. changed: the salmon's number and length (II.8 ¶19; White ¶12)
- About a thousand fish up to two and a half feet long become "full of these fish" and "upwards of two feet". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Un millier de ces poissons, qui mesuraient jusqu’à deux pieds et demi de longueur
  - white: The river was full of these fish, which measured upwards of two feet in length

### 19. cut: the green dome over the road (II.8 ¶31; White ¶20)
- The five-mile walk goes "under a dome of greenery" in the French; White omits it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: sous un dôme de verdure
  - white: went with the onagers to the site, five miles distant, over what they called Corral Road

### 20. changed: "behind the southern spur" becomes "opposite the southern slope" (II.8 ¶32; White ¶20)
- The site is on the back (revers) of the mountain's southern spur; White puts it "opposite the southern slope". *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: au revers même de la croupe méridionale de la montagne
  - white: opposite the southern slope of the mountain

### 21. cut: "even the most agile" (II.8 ¶32; White ¶20)
- The palisade must be high enough that even the most agile animals cannot clear it; White says only high enough to keep in the animals. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: assez élevée pour que des animaux, même les plus agiles, ne pussent la franchir
  - white: sufficiently high to keep in the animals

### 22. changed: horned animals become cattle (II.8 ¶32; White ¶20)
- The corral holds a hundred horned animals, mouflons or wild goats; White writes "one hundred cattle", which suggests another animal. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: une centaine d’animaux à cornes, mouflons ou chèvres sauvages
  - white: one hundred cattle, moufflons or wild goats

### 23. changed: stakes become trees (II.8 ¶33; White ¶21)
- The trees felled for the road supply about a hundred stakes (pieux); White says the road furnished "some hundred trees" set in the ground. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ils fournirent une centaine de pieux
  - white: furnished some hundred trees

### 24. changed: the entrance at the front becomes the back (II.8 ¶34; White ¶21)
- The gate is in the front part of the palisade; White puts it at the back. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: À la partie antérieure de la palissade
  - white: At the back part of the palisade

### 25. condensed: more than two thirds escape (II.8 ¶39; White ¶24)
- The French gives of about a hundred mouflons driven, more than two thirds escaping; White says only "many escaped". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: plus des deux tiers échappèrent aux rabatteurs
  - white: many escaped

### 26. changed: about ten goats become a dozen (II.8 ¶39; White ¶24)
- "Une dizaine" of wild goats is rendered "a dozen". *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: une dizaine de chèvres sauvages
  - white: a dozen wild goats

### 27. changed: wool becomes milk (II.8 ¶40; White ¶24)
- The herd will give not only wool but skins; White has "milk and skins", dropping the wool that the corral was built for. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: non-seulement la laine, mais aussi les peaux abonderaient
  - white: milk and skins be plenty in the future

### 28. changed: the west coast becomes the southern coast (II.8 ¶42; White ¶26)
- The third road runs from the enclosure toward the west coast; White says southern. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: se dirigea vers la côte occidentale
  - white: directed towards the southern coast

### 29. condensed: the roads (II.8 ¶42; White ¶26)
- The roads improved are those of the corral and Port Balloon; White says "the existing roads". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: les routes du corral et du port Ballon
  - white: the existing roads

### 30. cut: vitelottes in the kitchen garden (II.8 ¶43; White ¶27)
- The beds hold lettuce, vitelottes, sorrel, radishes, horseradish; White omits vitelottes and renders horseradish as "charlock" (as in No. 10). *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: laitues, vitelottes, oseille, raves, raifort et autres crucifères
  - white: lettuce, sorrel, radish, charlock, and other crucifers

### 31. changed: monarda becomes mountain mint (II.8 ¶44; White ¶28)
- Oswego tea comes from the monarda (monardes didymes); White names "mountain mint", a different plant. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: fourni par les monardes didymes
  - white: made from the mountain mint

### 32. cut: the shovellers, and the pouch moved to the bustards (II.8 ¶45; White ¶29)
- The poultry yard holds a pair of houbara bustards, a dozen shovellers (souchets) with the membranous appendix on the bill, and cocks; White has no shovellers and gives the membranous appendix to the bustards. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: une douzaine de souchets, dont la mandibule supérieure était prolongée de chaque côté par un appendice membraneux
  - white: a membranous pouch extending on either side of the upper mandible

### 33. softened: "guerre de sécession" becomes "the Rebellion" (II.8 ¶48; White ¶32)
- The neutral name for the war is replaced by the Northern term. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: Où en était cette guerre de sécession ?
  - white: In what condition was the Rebellion?

### 34. cut: "for the good cause" (II.8 ¶48; White ¶32)
- The French says the North triumphed for the good cause; White gives the triumph without the judgement. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le nord avait triomphé pour la bonne cause
  - white: the North must have triumphed

### 35. softened: "exiles" becomes "colonists" (II.8 ¶48; White ¶32)
- A newspaper would have been welcome to the exiles of Lincoln Island; White says "colonists". *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: pour les exilés de l’île Lincoln
  - white: by the colonists of Lincoln Island
