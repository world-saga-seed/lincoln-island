# Foreword

This book is a demonstration of long-form generation on a large work. It was made by language models, a model playing its author and other sessions doing the work, by the World Saga Seed method. A human read it as a reader, and did more than read. These things in it are the human’s:

- the reading of XIX bis aloud, which changed the chapter in thirteen places;
- two later turns in the same chapter, the net snag and the false hope;
- in the same chapter, the turn of the float paragraph, that no Pacific fishery used glass;
- the vandalism itself, described below;
- the scholar: how he came by the papers, that he notes only what the papers show against themselves, and the note on the title of XIX bis, which is the human’s sentence word for word.

Everything else here that is not Verne’s or White’s is the models’ work.

It was made in two stages.

**First, the book was restored.** Jules Verne’s *L’Île mystérieuse* (Hetzel, 1875) came into English in 1876 as *The Mysterious Island*, in the translation now given to Stephen W. White. White cut as he went, and changed things. Here each of Verne’s sixty-two chapters was set against the French, paragraph by paragraph. Where White left words out, they are put back in his manner. Where he changed what Verne said, it is set right. Where his chapter stood too far from the French to mend, the chapter was written anew from the French in his English. White’s names, spellings and forms of address are kept, except where he changed Verne’s: the boat is Verne’s *Bonadventure*, not White’s *Good Luck*. Verne’s own slips in print are listed in the errata kept with the files (`texts/errata.md`), with how each was taken: most as printed, or as White took them. White’s glossary of 1876 follows the last chapter.

The map after the forewords shows the restoration. It has one band for each chapter, as long as Verne’s French. The band darkens where words White cut were put back, and a mark above it stands for each place a patch changed what he said. A hatched band is a chapter written anew. It is drawn from the patches themselves, about three thousand two hundred of them.

**Then we vandalized it.** That is our word for it. We added a scholar, a member of an Iowa county historical society, who writes the second foreword and footnotes the places where the story disagrees with itself and explains them away where the papers let him, leaving the rest open. And we added a chapter, XIX bis, “The Float in the Bar”, which Verne never wrote. It is set between his chapters XIX and XX, in the first days of June 1865.

**Taking the vandalism out.** The book is kept as plain files, and anyone may fork it. Every addition lives in its own files, so deleting them is all it takes, and the book builds without them:

- the scholar’s preface, `story/front/scholar-preface.md`;
- his notes, `story/scholar/`;
- the chapter XIX bis, `story/prose/01950-the-float-in-the-bar.md`.

What is left is Verne’s novel, unabridged, in White’s English, with this foreword and the map. Delete those too if you like.
