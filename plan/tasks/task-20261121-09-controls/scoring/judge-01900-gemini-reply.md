### 2. Accuracy: 9/10

The translation tracks the French closely, preserves all dates (May 22, May 28, May 31), measurements (80 feet, 30 feet, 40 feet, 200 feet, 100 rungs, 40 feet, 25–30 gallons, 4.5 liters), and White’s exact name conventions (*Cyrus Smith, Pencroff, Herbert, Neb, Gideon Spilett, Granite House, the Chimneys, Prospect Plateau, Jacamar Woods, Forests of the Far West, Mandible Capes, Claw Cape, Union Bay*). There are three minor shifts in sense or vocabulary:

1. **“ce sont de mauvais parages que ces parages du Pacifique” → “these regions of the Pacific are dangerous waters”**: *Parages* already means “waters/quarters/latitudes,” so splitting it into “regions” and “waters” is slightly redundant, and *mauvais* (“bad/nasty/unfriendly”) is narrowed to “dangerous.”
2. **“monardes didymes” → “*Monarda didyma*”**: Verne uses the French vernacular/horticultural plural (*monardes didymes*), whereas Text A shifts to italicized binomial Latin (*Monarda didyma*), creating a slight grammatical mismatch with the following plural verb (“which are known”).
3. **“sur la gauche de la rivière” → “on the left bank of the river”**: Adds “bank” (strictly accurate geographically, though slightly more specific than the French).

* **Changed facts/senses:** 3 minor shifts (0 factual errors).

### 3. Force: 8/10

Text A captures the brisk, workmanlike tone of Verne’s adventure prose well, retaining exclamation marks and rhetorical emphasis, though it occasionally softens idiomatic punch or sharpens neutral phrasing:

1. **Softening (idiomatic intimacy):** *“n’avaient pas tardé à se tutoyer”* → *“had not been long in addressing each other like old comrades”* (Paragraph 23). Because English lacks a T-V distinction, translating *tutoyer* as “addressing each other like old comrades” softens the specific, culturally loaded milestone of switching from *vous* to *tu* between a freedman and a white sailor.
2. **Softening (colloquial directness):** *“faisaient merveille”* → *“did wonders”* (Paragraph 27) and *“n’y regardait pas de si près”* → *“was not so particular”* (Paragraph 36) slightly flatten the crispness of the French idioms.
3. **Sharpening (register/intensity):** *“se défonça”* (“caved in / broke inward”) → *“gave way”* (Paragraph 14, slightly softer), whereas *“mauvais parages”* (“bad waters/quarters”) → *“dangerous waters”* (Paragraph 11) sharpens the threat.
4. **Softening (final joke):** *“au cinquième au-dessus de l’entresol !”* → *“on the fifth floor above the entresol!”* (Paragraph 40). Leaving *entresol* untranslated in English rather than rendering it as “mezzanine” or “ground floor” slightly blunts the Parisian tenement joke for an American reader.

* **Softenings/sharpenings:** 4 instances.

### 4. Period Voice: 9/10

The text consistently uses American orthography (*harbor* equivalents: *marvelous, behavior/humor, fibers, leveled, practiced, traveled/traveled* roots, *center/liters, mold/color* patterns—specifically *marvelous, fibers, leveled, humor, liters, odor/fragrant, practiced*) and 19th-century syntactic inversions (*“were commenced the works,” “to which had been given the name,” “Space was not wanting”*).

### 5. Prose: 8/10

Text A reads convincingly like a faithful late-19th-century American translation rather than a modern pastiche in costume. It achieves this primarily through authentic period syntax—retaining French-influenced participial clauses (*“This plan decided upon,” “This staircase once drawn up”*), passive inversions (*“Not fewer than a hundred rungs were counted”*), and formal nominalizations (*“the fabrication of a stout rope ladder,” “the transport of materials”*)—without falling into pseudo-Victorian fustian (“prithee,” “ere,” “forsooth”). Its only tell is an occasional hyper-literal calque (*“admitted the five windows”* for *admettait bien*, *“All the more so as to these medicinal plants...”*) that feels slightly more classroom-faithful than Stephen W. White’s brisker journalistic English.
