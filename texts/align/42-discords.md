# II.20: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller m. Beads corrected: none.* White has no footnote at all, so the 106 against 83 question of note n1 does not arise; the gale's speed is itself cut (discord 1).

### 1. cut: the gale's speed and its footnote (II.20 ¶1; White ¶1)
- The French gives the wind as forty to forty-five miles an hour, with a footnote converting it to kilometres; White says only "a strong blow" and has no footnote. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il acquit une vitesse de quarante à quarante-cinq milles
  - white: The breeze changed to a strong blow

### 2. cut: Pencroff could not even have reached the Mercy (II.20 ¶1; White ¶1)
- White drops that Pencroff, even had he wished, could not have made the Mercy's mouth, which is why he must keep the sea. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Pencroff n’eût pas même pu atteindre l’embouchure de la Mercy
  - white: so all that Pencroff could do was to bend the jib down to the mainmast as a stay-sail

### 3. cut: the danger of water on deck (II.20 ¶2; White ¶2)
- White drops that the boat was well ballasted, and that heavy water aboard could still have endangered her had the hatches not held. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: d’énormes paquets d’eau, tombant à bord, auraient pu le compromettre, si les panneaux n’avaient pas résisté
  - white: Pencroff, although he had every confidence in his boat, waited anxiously for daylight.

### 4. cut: the fire had guided them, and all three took it for the engineer's (II.20 ¶3; White ¶3)
- White drops that the fire had served the three to find the island on a dark night and that they could not doubt the engineer had lit it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Ce feu leur avait servi à reconnaître la situation de l’île pendant cette nuit sombre
  - white: He and his companions also had certainly seen this light, and yet Smith declared that he knew nothing about it.

### 5. cut: Pencroff's reply on the gulf's shelter (II.20 ¶9; White —)
- White has nothing for Pencroff's answer that the wind can enter only by the narrow pass between the capes, the north cape covering the south, so that the boat could lie there a year without straining at her anchors. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: n’a que cet étroit goulet creusé entre les deux caps
  - white: —

### 6. cut: the Norwegian fiords (II.20 ¶25; White ¶22)
- White drops the comparison of the lava walls to the fiords of Norway, only barer. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: les fiords de la Norwége
  - white: And, indeed, these steep lava walls afforded no landing place on all their circumference.

### 7. changed: the date of Smith's talk (II.20 ¶37; White ¶32)
- The French dates it 25 April; White dates it the 26th. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le 25 avril, pendant la soirée
  - white: on the 26th of April

### 8. softened: Pencroff and Neb on the supernatural (II.20 ¶70; White ¶64)
- The French says they were "not far from believing" the island subject to a supernatural power, and that they were given to the marvellous by nature; White says they "readily concluded" it was. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: n’étaient pas éloignés de croire que l’île Lincoln fût subordonnée à quelque puissance surnaturelle
  - white: readily concluded that Lincoln Island was subject to some supernatural influence

### 9. cut: May is the north's November; the winter looks hard (II.20 ¶71; White ¶65)
- White drops the equivalence of May to northern November and the forecast of a hard, early winter. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: mai, — novembre des zones boréales
  - white: The bad weather began early, coming in with May

### 10. changed: when Ayrton came to Granite-house (II.20 ¶73; White ¶66)
- The French has Smith offer Ayrton a winter place, Ayrton promise to come when the corral work was done, and come about mid-April; White has him come "when the bad weather began", which is May. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Ce qu’il fit vers la mi-avril
  - white: when the bad weather began, he had returned to Granite House

### 11. changed: the gales damage the mill (II.20 ¶75; White ¶67)
- The French says the mill and the poultry yard suffered most; White says the fields and the poultry yard. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Le moulin et la basse-cour eurent particulièrement à souffrir
  - white: damaged the fields and the poultry-yard

### 12. cut: the frozen stream, and shooting the beasts off (II.20 ¶76; White ¶68)
- White drops that the stream, when frozen, gave the jaguars and apes an easy passage, and that the colonists often had to fire to keep them off. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: lorsqu’il était gelé, leur offrait un passage facile
  - white: there was danger of the bolder of these beasts making a descent on the fields and domestic animals of the colonists

### 13. condensed: the winter hunts (II.20 ¶77; White ¶69)
- The French names the birds (ducks, snipe, teal, pintails, lapwings), the hunters (Spilett and Harbert with Jup and Top), the two routes to the marshes and the two- or three-mile limit; White has "now and then a grand hunt". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: canards, de bécassines, de sarcelles, de pilets et de vanneaux
  - white: now and then a grand hunt in the frozen marshes of Tadorn’s Fen

### 14. cut: the four hard months (II.20 ¶78; White ¶69)
- White drops that the hard winter lasted four months, June to September. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: juin, juillet, août et septembre
  - white: Thus the winter passed

### 15. condensed: the trees that leaf in October (II.20 ¶80; White ¶71)
- The French names the new leaves among the conifers: nettle-trees, banksias, deodars; White has "young leaves ... on the trees". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: des micocouliers, des banksias et des deodars
  - white: the young leaves were appearing on the trees on the edge of the forest

### 16. changed: the date of the photograph (II.20 ¶82; White ¶71)
- The French dates it 17 October; White dates it the 28th. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le 17 de ce mois d’octobre
  - white: It was the 28th of October.

### 17. changed: where the lens came from (II.20 ¶87; White ¶74)
- The French has Harbert unscrew a strong lens from one of the telescopes; White has him make a glass from one of the lenses of the photographic instrument. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: une forte lentille qu’il dévissa de l’une des lunettes
  - white: a magnifying glass made from one of the lenses of the instrument
