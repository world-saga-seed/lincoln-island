# The Nitro-Glycerine and the New Fall (outline)

*Chapter 01700: the French I.17 (`texts/fr-1875/17.md`). Before: 01600. After: 01800. Third person, past tense; Verne's narrator. Every beat is marked (Drafted.): it is what the French holds.*

## Outline

### 1. 7 May: the dugong on the shore; Cyrus and the reporter keep the mystery between them (I.17 ¶1–¶9)

### 2. The current and the outlet found (I.17 ¶10–¶14)

### 3. The plan: lower the lake by three feet (I.17 ¶15–¶26)

- Back at the Chimneys, they find Harbert and Pencroff unloading their wood. Pencroff jokes that the woodcutters are nearly done and that Cyrus will need masons; Cyrus answers that he needs chemists. The reporter says they will blow up the island, in part at least. *(Drafted.)*

### 5. 8 May: the pyrites heap (I.17 ¶39–¶44)

- On 8 May Cyrus begins. The pyrites are chiefly coal, silica, alumina and iron sulphide, the last in excess; the iron sulphide must be isolated and turned into sulphate, from which sulphuric acid will be drawn. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Le lendemain, 8 mai, l’ingénieur commença ses manipulations.
  - quote: il s’agissait d’isoler le sulfure de fer et de le transformer en sulfate
- The narrator says sulphuric acid will be of great use later for candles and tanning, but for now Cyrus reserves it for another use. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: la fabrication des bougies, le tannage des peaux
- Behind the Chimneys, on levelled ground, he lays a heap of branches and chopped wood with pyritous shale propped against each other and a thin layer of pyrites broken to the size of a nut. The wood is lit, the shale takes fire, more crushed pyrites are piled in an enormous heap covered with earth and grass with a few vents, like a charcoal mound. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: réduites à la grosseur d’une noix
  - quote: quelques évents
- The change needs ten to twelve days to turn iron sulphide to iron sulphate and alumina to aluminium sulphate, both soluble. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dix à douze jours
- Meanwhile Cyrus sets other work going, "not zeal but fury". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: C’était de l’acharnement.

### 6. Glycerine and soap from the dugong's fat (I.17 ¶45–¶46)

- Nab and Pencroff have put the dugong's fat in large earthen jars. Cyrus means to isolate glycerine by saponifying it with soda or lime; lime gives insoluble calcium soaps, soda a soluble soap for household cleaning, so he seeks soda. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: recueillie dans de grandes jarres de terre
  - quote: ne devait donner que des savons calcaires, insolubles
- Sea plants abound (glasswort, ficoids, fucus); they are dried and burned in open pits for several days until the ashes fuse into a compact greyish mass, "natural soda". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: salicornes, ficoïdes
  - quote: soude naturelle
- Treating the fat with soda gives a soluble soap and, neutral, the glycerine. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: un savon soluble, et, de l’autre, cette substance neutre, la glycérine

### 7. Saltpetre found; pottery and furnace; 18 May (I.17 ¶47–¶49)

- Cyrus still needs potassium nitrate, saltpetre, for his later preparation. He could make it from potash and nitric acid, but nitric acid is what he wants in the end: "a vicious circle". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: l’azotate de potasse
  - quote: un cercle vicieux
- Nature supplies it: Harbert finds a deposit in the north of the island at the foot of Mount Franklin, and the salt need only be purified. *(Drafted.)*

- These labours last about eight days and are done before the sulphide has changed. In the following days the colonists make refractory pottery of plastic clay and build a brick furnace of special design for distilling the iron sulphate. All is done "about 18 May", at the moment the chemical change ends. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Ces divers travaux durèrent une huitaine de jours.

### 8. The sulphate crystals and sulphuric acid, 20 May (I.17 ¶50–¶54)

### 11. The firing device (I.17 ¶69–¶74)

- Nitro-glycerine is usually fired by a fulminate cap, since it needs a shock; merely lit, it would burn without exploding. Cyrus could make a gun-cotton-like cap, having nitric acid, but he knows nitro-glycerine detonates on shock and decides to use that first. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: allumée simplement, cette substance brûlerait sans éclater
  - quote: la nitro-glycérine a la propriété de détonner au choc
- No one can stand by to strike with a hammer and survive. Cyrus hangs a mass of iron of several pounds on a plant fibre above the hole; a second sulphured fibre tied to the middle of the first trails several feet along the ground. Lit, it burns to the first, which breaks and drops the iron on the nitro-glycerine. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: une masse de fer pesant plusieurs livres
  - quote: Une autre longue fibre, préalablement soufrée
- After sending his companions away, he fills the hole to the brim, throws a few drops on the rock under the iron, lights the fibre and goes back to the Chimneys. A plate shows him leaving. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: remplit le trou de mine de manière que la nitro-glycérine vînt en affleurer l’ouverture

- The fibre was to burn twenty-five minutes, and twenty-five minutes later an explosion goes off; the whole island seems to tremble, a spray of stones flies up as from a volcano, the Chimneys' rocks sway, and the colonists, more than two miles from the mine, are thrown down. *(Drafted.)*

  - quote: Un triple hurrah s’échappa de leurs poitrines
  - quote: se précipitait d’une hauteur de trois cents pieds sur la grève
