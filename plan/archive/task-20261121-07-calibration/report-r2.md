# Report, round 2

## A2 (I.1 ¶14)
- "comme des mascarets": rendered "like the bore of a river". A mascaret is a tidal bore. "Bore" is the English word of the day, but standing alone ("like bores") it reads badly, so I added "of a river" to make the word plain. That is a small addition.
- "des trombes qui broyaient tout sur leur passage": rendered "whirlwinds". A trombe is a waterspout at sea and a tornado over land. These trombes level "territoires entiers", so I chose the land sense. "Waterspouts" would be the literal word.
- "témoignages de sa fureur": rendered "testimonies of its fury". I kept Verne's word rather than "traces" or "proofs".
- "qui se dessinait obliquement à l'équateur": rendered "which was drawn obliquely to the equator". I kept the figure of drawing.
- "Bureau-Veritas": given as "Bureau Veritas", without the hyphen, as the firm was known in English.

## A3 (I.14 ¶74–¶75)
- "chiffrer l'opération": rendered "work out the operation in figures", to keep "chiffrer" (to put into figures).
- "on le voit": rendered "as is seen".
- "concurremment avec les Anglais": rendered "concurrently with the English", word for word. "In common with" would be smoother.
- "par 77° 3′ 11″" and "par soixante-dix-sept degrés plus soixante-quinze degrés": rendered "in 77° 3′ 11″" and "seventy-seven degrees plus seventy-five degrees west". In the second I dropped the "par" construction for English idiom.

## A5 (II.17 ¶28, ¶58, ¶59, ¶79, n1)
- "son hôte": rendered "its guest". The word can also mean host or occupant. The house is being made ready for a newcomer, so I took "guest".
- "armoire": rendered "wardrobe". "Press" or "cupboard" were the other choices.
- "contre-maître du Britannia": rendered "boatswain". On a merchant ship a contre-maître is the boatswain or the mate. The outline also gives "boatswain".
- "révolte": rendered "revolt", not "mutiny", to keep Verne's word.
- The opening guillemets of the quoted narrative (« at the head of ¶58, ¶59, ¶79): rendered as an opening “ on each paragraph, the English convention for a narrative running over several paragraphs.
- "(Note de l'Éditeur.)": rendered "(Note of the Publisher.)". The éditeur is Hetzel, the publisher, not an editor in the English sense.
- "Les Enfants du capitaine Grant": rendered as the English title "The Children of Captain Grant". I left it unitalicised because the French text as given marks no italics.
- The footnote is marked [n1], as the French marks it, and set after ¶79.

## P1 (II.19 ¶2)
- "pour l'honneur de la Confédération américaine": rendered "for the honour of the American Confederation", word for word. In English, "Confederation" sits close to "the Confederacy", which was the South. Verne means the Union. I kept his word, as the rules require, and did not change it to "the Union".
- "patrie", used twice: rendered "their country" and "that country". For the first clause, "dans leur pays", I wrote "in their own land" so as not to say "country" three times.
- "ensanglantait": rendered "was perhaps still drenching with blood".
- "la rébellion du sud": rendered "the rebellion of the South", with its force kept.

## P2 (III.20 ¶10)
- "Le capitaine Nemo a bien fait de mourir !": rendered "Captain Nemo did well to die!". It is literal. The sense is that it was well for him he died before seeing this. I did not explain it.
- "une fois": rendered "once", which here means on one occasion.

## S7 (II.12 ¶90)
- "sur le littoral du sud": rendered "along the southern shore".
- "depuis une heure déjà": rendered "had already been blowing ... for an hour". I moved "already" for English word order.

## S8 (II.13 ¶29)
- "dans le crépuscule solaire": rendered "in the twilight of the sun". I kept the precision that this is twilight from the sun, against "evening twilight".
- "s'éteignit": rendered "was soon extinguished", to keep the image of a light put out.
- "Au soir": rendered "In the evening".

## S9 (III.4 ¶49)
- "avant la naissance de l'étrave": rendered "before the rise of the stem". "Naissance" is where the stem begins to rise from the keel. "Beginning of the stem" is the plainer alternative.
- "qu'il eût été impossible d'aveugler": rendered "to stop". "Aveugler une voie d'eau" is the seaman's term for stopping a leak.
- "la membrure": rendered "the very frames".
- "gournables": rendered "treenails".
- "jusqu'aux façons d'arrière": rendered "as far as the after run". The façons are the hull's narrowing lines fore and aft.
- "les virures, déchiquetées, ne tenaient plus": rendered "the strakes, torn to shreds, no longer held together". I added "together" for English idiom.
- "séparée": rendered "wrenched away". The literal word is "separated". I strengthened it to match "avec une violence inexplicable". That is a slight departure.
- "carlingue": rendered "keelson".

## S10 (III.10 ¶40)
- "quinquina": rendered "Peruvian bark", the usual English of 1876. "Cinchona" or "quinquina" were the other choices. Verne's dialogue around this paragraph uses "quinquina", and other renderers may differ here.
- "la serpentaire": rendered "the serpentary" (Virginia snakeroot), the apothecaries' English name.
- "le marronnier de l'Inde": rendered "the horse-chestnut".
- "le salicine": rendered "the salicine", the period spelling. The French gives it the masculine article, and I kept the article.

## S11 (III.11 ¶30)
- "une longue traite": rendered "a long stage".
- "ustensiles divers": rendered "divers utensils", a period form.
- "rester compacte": rendered "keep close together".
- "sous aucun prétexte": rendered "under any pretext whatever". "Whatever" is added for emphasis, a slight addition.
- "ces épaisses forêts": rendered "those thick forests".

## S12 (III.13 ¶3)
- "il ne savait pas lui-même être dans la maison du corral": rendered "he did not himself know that he was in the corral house".
- "cette terrible exécution": rendered "this terrible execution".
- "sous l'influence d'une violente surexcitation": rendered "and under the influence of a violent excitement". I added "and" before the second phrase, a small change of syntax.
