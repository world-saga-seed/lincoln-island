# II.14: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller k. Beads corrected: beads 17 to 23 (the plate's letters, BR…TAN…A, are White ¶18 and belong to no French paragraph; French ¶24 and ¶26 are both inside White ¶19, with French ¶25, Spilett's "Peu importe", folded into Pencroff's speech; French ¶27 is the start of White ¶20; French ¶29 is the first half of White ¶21 and French ¶30 its last sentence plus White ¶22; French ¶31–¶32 are White ¶23).*

### 1. changed: "a glimmer of instinct" becomes "a glimmer of intelligence" (II.14 ¶0; White ¶0)
- The French chapter summary promises a gleam of instinct; White's promises a gleam of intelligence. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Une lueur d’instinct
  - white: A GLIMMER OF INTELLIGENCE

### 2. changed: the "rough" chimney becomes a "large" one (II.14 ¶4; White ¶1)
- The French chimney is crude (grossière); White's is large. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: une cheminée grossière
  - white: a large chimney

### 3. cut: the dust that long years had gathered (II.14 ¶5; White ¶2)
- White drops that years, perhaps long ones, had accumulated the dust. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: que de longues années, peut-être, avaient accumulée
  - white: —

### 4. cut: the castaway would not have left his things in a boat of his own making (II.14 ¶14; White ¶11)
- White drops Pencroff's last reasons: the arms and tools are the last relics of the wreck, and a man who escaped in a boat he had made would still less have left such necessaries. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: S’il s’était sauvé sur un canot fait par lui, il eût encore moins abandonné ces objets de première nécessité !
  - white: —

### 5. condensed: the stream's mouth on the left of the shore (II.14 ¶21; White ¶17)
- The French says the lawn led to the shore, on whose left the stream's mouth opened; White says the lawn reaches the stream's bank. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: sur la gauche duquel s’ouvrait l’embouchure du ruisseau
  - white: extended to the bank of the little stream

### 6. cut: the plank probably from the ship's bulwarks (II.14 ¶23; White ¶17)
- White drops that the lettered plank was probably one of those that had formed the wrecked ship's bulwarks. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: probablement une de celles qui formaient les pavois du navire naufragé
  - white: —

### 7. changed: Spilett's "Peu importe" given to Pencroff (II.14 ¶25; White ¶19)
- In the French the reporter says it does not matter what country the ship belonged to and Pencroff agrees; in White Pencroff says it himself. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: — Peu importe, Pencroff !
  - white: However, it don’t matter to what country the man belongs

### 8. cut: the eastern coast (II.14 ¶29; White ¶21)
- White drops that the party came back to the east coast of the island. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: revoyaient la côte orientale de l’île
  - white: —

### 9. cut: "to the last bone" (II.14 ¶32; White ¶23)
- White drops that some wild beast must have devoured him to the last bone. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: l’avait dévoré jusqu’au dernier ossement
  - white: some animal must have devoured his body

### 10. cut: pigs that Lincoln Island lacks (II.14 ¶36; White ¶27)
- White drops that Lincoln Island has no pigs, and "one or two pairs" becomes "a couple". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: un ou deux couples de ces porcs, dont l’île Lincoln est dépourvue
  - white: a couple of these pigs

### 11. condensed: vegetables of the old and new continents (II.14 ¶37; White ¶28)
- Harbert's seeds will give all the vegetables of the old and new continent in the French; White says "some of our own vegetables". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qui nous donneront tous les légumes de l’ancien et du nouveau continent
  - white: which will give us some of our own vegetables

### 12. cut: the distance and direction of the cries (II.14 ¶43; White ¶33)
- White drops that the cries came from a few hundred paces to the north of the islet. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: à quelques centaines de pas dans le nord de l’îlot
  - white: —

### 13. changed: "about to do him harm" becomes "throttling him" (II.14 ¶48; White ¶36)
- The French says the creature was about to do Harbert harm; White says he was throttling him. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qui allait lui faire un mauvais parti
  - white: who was throttling him

### 14. changed: beard to the chest becomes beard to the waist (II.14 ¶56; White ¶43)
- The French beard reaches the chest; White's reaches the waist. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: barbe inculte descendant jusqu’à la poitrine
  - white: tangled beard descending to his waist

### 15. cut: the enormous hands (II.14 ¶56; White ¶43)
- White drops the creature's huge hands, and "excessively long" nails become "long". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: mains énormes, ongles démesurément longs
  - white: long nails

### 16. changed: reason "not wholly out" becomes "all intelligence had forsaken him" (II.14 ¶62; White ¶49)
- In the French Spilett, looking him in the eye, thinks he sees that reason is not wholly extinct; White has him see that all intelligence had gone. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le reporter crut voir que toute raison n’était pas éteinte en lui
  - white: the reporter could see that all intelligence had forsaken him

### 17. changed: a continuous whistle becomes a wheezing (II.14 ¶73; White ¶59)
- The French has a continuous whistling from the prisoner's lips; White has "a wheezing sound". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Un sifflement continu s’échappait de ses lèvres
  - white: A wheezing sound escaped from his lips

### 18. changed: the prisoner recoils from the flame (II.14 ¶78; White ¶62)
- The French says he shrank back from the flame and his unconscious look went out; White says only that he soon ceased regarding it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: mais bientôt il recula, et son regard inconscient s’éteignit
  - white: but very soon he ceased regarding it

### 19. changed: "deaf and mute" becomes "insensible, and mute" (II.14 ¶81; White ¶63)
- The French has him deaf as well as dumb; White says "insensible". *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: sourd et muet tout ensemble
  - white: insensible, and mute

### 20. changed: the date of the departure, 15 October becomes "the 17th" (II.14 ¶93; White ¶74)
- The French dates the departure morning 15 October; White says "the 17th", which is also the day White's next paragraphs reach after forty-eight hours at sea. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le lendemain, — 15 octobre, —
  - white: the next day, the 17th

### 21. cut: the east-north-east bearing (II.14 ¶94; White ¶74)
- White drops the course, east-north-east, and says only "towards home". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: mit le cap à l’est-nord-est
  - white: headed directly towards home

### 22. condensed: the sea breaking over the bow (II.14 ¶96; White ¶76)
- The French says the sea broke violently over the bow of the boat; White says only that Pencroff was "anxious at the lookout ahead". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: l’état de la mer, qui déferlait violemment sur l’avant de son embarcation
  - white: anxious at the lookout ahead

### 23. cut: reefing, tacking and the seas breaking over the sails (II.14 ¶98; White ¶77)
- White drops the work in the gale: sails swept by seas, reefs taken, tacks changed in short boards. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: prendre des ris, et souvent changer les amures, en courant de petits bords
  - white: —

### 24. softened: the boat "entirely capped" by a wave becomes "struck" by one (II.14 ¶98; White ¶77)
- The French has a wave wholly cover the Bonadventure; White says only that a huge wave struck the sloop. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le Bonadventure fut entièrement coiffé par une lame
  - white: a huge wave struck the sloop

### 25. cut: the three stunned at the prisoner's act (II.14 ¶100; White ¶78)
- White drops that Pencroff, Spilett and Harbert, stupefied, simply let him act. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: absolument stupéfaits, l’avaient laissé agir
  - white: —

### 26. cut: how well the boat held the sea (II.14 ¶102; White ¶79)
- White drops the remark that the Bonadventure had kept the sea marvellously. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Du reste, il avait merveilleusement tenu la mer.
  - white: —
