# II.1: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller h. Beads corrected: none.*

### 1. changed: the harmless rodent becomes "a pig" (II.1 ¶1; White ¶1)
- The French says the shot was found in the body of a harmless rodent; White says a pig. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: trouvé dans le corps d’un inoffensif rongeur
  - white: found in the body of a pig

### 2. cut: however they had searched (II.1 ¶1; White ¶1)
- The French says no human being had shown himself however thorough their search; White says only that none had been seen. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: quelque recherche qu’ils eussent faite
  - white: In all this time no human being had been seen.

### 3. cut: Cyrus feels the shot (II.1 ¶4; White ¶4)
- Cyrus weighs the shot between forefinger and thumb; White has him turning it between his fingers only. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le palpa entre l’index et le pouce
  - white: Taking the bit of lead between his fingers he turned it round and about for some time

### 4. condensed: Pencroff's "a hundred times no" (II.1 ¶10; White ¶10)
- The sailor's triple denial and his "Que diable !" are reduced to a single "No!". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Non ! cent fois non ! mille fois non !
  - white: “No!” cried the sailor

### 5. changed: a hollow tooth becomes a broken one (II.1 ¶14; White ¶14)
- Pencroff challenges Nab to find a hollow tooth in his jaw; White says a broken one. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: si tu trouves une dent creuse dans ce râtelier-là
  - white: if you can find one broken one in the whole set

### 6. cut: the gale that threw the castaways ashore (II.1 ¶15; White ¶15)
- Cyrus infers that castaways have been thrown on the coast by a storm in recent weeks; White drops the storm. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: des naufragés ont été jetés par une tempête sur un point de la côte
  - white: some people have been shipwrecked somewhere upon the coast

### 7. cut: "the reasoning of courage" (II.1 ¶36; White ¶35)
- The narrator's phrase for Harbert's coolness is left out, and the spear with it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: « le raisonnement de la bravoure »
  - white: considerable hardiness and coolness

### 8. changed: "temporary castaways" without disabling their craft (II.1 ¶43; White ¶42)
- The French says the gale threw them on the island without disabling their vessel ("désemparé" is dismasted or crippled); White says without their having to abandon it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: sans avoir désemparé leur embarcation
  - white: without having to abandon their vessel

### 9. changed: "nearly 200 feet" becomes "more than 200 feet" (II.1 ¶48; White ¶47)
- The kauris rise to nearly two hundred feet; White has them rising more than that. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: à une hauteur de près de deux cents pieds au-dessus du sol
  - white: rising mere than 200 feet in height

### 10. changed: "several square miles" becomes "many miles" (II.1 ¶56; White ¶52)
- The forest dome measures several square miles; White says "many miles in extent". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: mesurant plusieurs milles carrés
  - white: a vast impenetrable dome many miles in extent

### 11. cut: the Mercy's source cannot be made out (II.1 ¶56; White ¶52)
- Harbert cannot follow the Mercy or find the point on the mountain where it rises; White keeps only that the course could not be seen. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: reconnaître le point de la montagne dans lequel elle prenait sa source
  - white: Even the course of the Mercy could not be seen

### 12. cut: Cyrus shakes his head (II.1 ¶59; White ¶54)
- Cyrus listens, shakes his head and says nothing; White has him listen "without comment". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: secoua la tête et ne dit rien
  - white: There Smith listened to the lad’s report without comment.

### 13. changed: head and legs become head and eyes (II.1 ¶67; White ¶61)
- Neither the head nor the feet of the turtle could be seen; White says head and eyes. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: On ne voyait plus ni sa tête, ni ses pattes
  - white: neither its head nor eyes were visible

### 14. cut: the turtle's head (II.1 ¶70; White ¶63)
- The French describes the head that was left showing: small, flat, widened behind by large temporal pits; White's paragraph ends at the taste of the flesh. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ne laissait plus entrevoir que sa tête petite, aplatie
  - white: —

### 15. changed: "fifty soups" become "fifty pounds of soup" (II.1 ¶84; White ¶76)
- Pencroff reckons the loss as fifty soups at least; White turns it into pounds. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Avoir laissé échapper cinquante potages au moins !
  - white: to let at least fifty pounds of soup, escape!

### 16. changed: "jokingly" becomes "calmly" (II.1 ¶86; White ¶78)
- The sailor retorts in jest; White has him assert it calmly. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: riposta plaisamment l’intraitable marin
  - white: calmly asserted the stubborn sailor

### 17. changed: the narrator's doubt becomes Smith's reticence (II.1 ¶100; White ¶91)
- In the French the narrator says one would not dare to affirm that Cyrus was convinced; White says Smith himself did not venture to say so. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: On n’oserait l’affirmer.
  - white: He did not venture to say that.
