### 1. cut: why the brig seemed gone (III.3 ¶1; White ¶1)
- The French gives a reason the brig might have left, that she thought she had too strong a party to deal with; White gives only that she might have gone. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: pensant avoir affaire à trop forte partie
  - white: They might have supposed she had weighed anchor and gone off in the night.

### 2. condensed: what shells could do to Granite-house (III.3 ¶3; White ¶3)
- Smith says the pirates will not think of sending shells that could do irreparable damage; White has only that they will not turn their guns upon it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: les pirates n’auront pas l’idée d’envoyer là des obus qui pourraient causer d’irréparables dommages
  - white: the pirates will never think of turning their guns upon it

### 3. condensed: the crossing and landing on the islet's eastern shore (III.3 ¶12; White ¶7)
- The French has Ayrton and Pencroff cross the canal, land on the islet and hide in the hollows of its eastern shore, five minutes after the others vanish; White says only that each was at his post. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: débarquaient sur l’îlot et se cachaient dans les anfractuosités de sa rive orientale
  - white: A few minutes afterwards each was at his post.

### 4. changed: rolling mist and the mastheads (III.3 ¶15; White ¶7)
- The French has the mast-trucks come out of the vapours and great wreaths of mist roll on the sea before a breeze scatters them; White has the ocean covered with ripples. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: de grosses volutes roulèrent à la surface de la mer
  - white: the ocean was covered with ripples

### 5. condensed: the night's intruder in the pirates' minds (III.3 ¶20; White ¶7)
- The French lists what the pirates cannot make out of the half-naked man: that he forced the powder-room door, fired six shots, killed one and wounded two, whether he escaped, swam ashore, whence he came; White keeps only that they were puzzled by the attempt on the magazine. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qui avait déchargé son revolver six fois sur eux, qui avait tué un des leurs et blessé deux autres
  - white: Harvey and his crew must have been exceedingly puzzled by the adventure of the night, and especially by Ayrton’s attempt upon the powder magazine.

### 6. cut: no trace of dwelling; had the inhabitants fled inland (III.3 ¶20; White ¶7)
- The French adds that there was no trace of habitation on the shore and asks whether the inhabitants had fled inland; White stops at no one being seen. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: En tout cas, il n’y avait aucune trace d’habitation. Les habitants avaient-ils donc fui vers l’intérieur ?
  - white: Yet no one could be seen either on the shore or the high ground.

### 7. cut: Harvey the prudent man reconnoitring (III.3 ¶21; White —)
- The French says this is what the pirate chief must be asking himself, and that as a prudent man he sought to know the ground before committing his band; White has nothing. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: en homme prudent, il cherchait à reconnaître les localités avant d’y engager sa bande
  - white: —

### 8. cut: his glasses showed no colonist (III.3 ¶22; White ¶8)
- The French says Harvey's best glasses had not let him see a single colonist hidden in the rocks; White says only that he hesitated. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Ses meilleures lunettes, sans doute, ne lui avaient pas permis d’apercevoir un seul des colons blottis dans les roches.
  - white: Evidently Harvey was hesitating.

### 9. cut: the screen of branches, the dwelling, the capes (III.3 ¶22; White ¶8)
- The French says the veil of green branches and creepers over the windows was unlikely to catch his eye, that he could not imagine a dwelling hollowed in the granite, and that nothing from Cape Claw to the Mandible Capes round Union Bay told him the island was or could be occupied; White drops all of it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Depuis le cap Griffe jusqu’aux caps Mandibule, sur tout le périmètre de la baie de l’Union, rien n’avait dû lui apprendre que l’île fût et pût être occupée.
  - white: —

### 10. cut: the boat's route round Wreck Point under the brig's guns (III.3 ¶32–¶33; White ¶11)
- The French has the boat keep five cables off the islet's receding shore as far as Wreck Point, curve round it under the brig's guns and head for the mouth of the Mercy, so as to catch the colonists between two fires whatever their number; White has only a pull for the south end of the islet. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Ils s’avancèrent ainsi jusqu’à cinq encâblures de la partie rentrante du littoral que terminait la pointe de l’Épave
  - white: they pulled for the southern extremity of the islet, evidently with the intention of coming up on the other side

### 11. changed: the narrow channel and the flood (III.3 ¶36; White ¶12)
- The French has the flood begin to rise with its usual violence because of the narrowness of the channel (pertuis); White says the tide ran up stream, because of the narrowness of the river. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Comme le flot commençait à monter avec sa violence habituelle, que provoquait l’étroitesse du pertuis
  - white: The tide was running up stream with its customary swiftness, due to the narrowness of the river

### 12. cut: the pirates could be destroyed one by one (III.3 ¶39; White ¶14)
- The French says that if the pirates went on attacking this way they could be destroyed one by one; White leaves it out. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ils pouvaient être détruits un à un
  - white: —

### 13. cut: three or four useless cannon shots (III.3 ¶41; White ¶15)
- The French has three or four shots fired from the brig with no possible result as the boat returns; White has only the howls of rage. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: trois ou quatre coups de canon furent tirés, qui ne pouvaient avoir aucun résultat
  - white: The wounded were lifted on deck, amid howls of rage.

### 14. cut: drunk with rage and the night's drinking (III.3 ¶42; White ¶15)
- The French says the dozen who took the boat were drunk with rage and perhaps still with the previous night's drinking; White says only "furious". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ivres de colère et peut-être encore des libations de la veille
  - white: A dozen furious convicts manned the boat

### 15. changed: ten shots become a dozen (III.3 ¶45; White ¶16)
- Pencroff and Ayrton run the gauntlet of about ten shots; White says a dozen. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: non sans avoir essuyé une dizaine de coups de fusil
  - white: running the gauntlet of a dozen shots

### 16. changed: the islet overrun becomes surrounded (III.3 ¶46; White ¶16)
- The French says the islet was invaded and the pirates ranged over it in all directions; White says it was surrounded and searched. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: l’îlot était envahi et que les pirates de la première embarcation le parcouraient en tous sens
  - white: the islet was surrounded and thoroughly searched by the pirates

### 17. cut: the second boat breaks up on the reef (III.3 ¶47; White ¶17)
- The French says the second boat, carried onto the reefs, broke up at the river mouth; White says only that she was hurried upon the rocks. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: s’y brisa à l’embouchure de la Mercy
  - white: the boat itself was irresistibly hurried upon the rocks at the mouth of the river

### 18. changed: "impossible" becomes "will risk" (III.3 ¶55; White ¶25)
- The reply that the brig cannot come into the channel, since she would risk stranding and being lost, becomes a statement that she will risk foundering. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: C’est impossible, car il risquerait de s’y échouer et de s’y perdre !
  - white: “She will risk foundering and utter destruction.”

### 19. softened: "by the thousand devils of hell" (III.3 ¶57; White ¶27)

  - quote: Par les mille diables d’enfer !

### 22. cut: the tide and Pencroff's disbelief (III.3 ¶62; White ¶28)
