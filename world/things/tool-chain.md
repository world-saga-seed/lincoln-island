# The tool chain: the colonists' made things in order

*Every thing the colonists make or obtain by a process, in book order, with its inputs, its makers and its card.*

Keywords: tool chain, made things, crafts, industry, inputs, order of invention

- **Firewood** (I.4 ¶32): dead dry wood from under the conifers, gathered and bundled by Pencroff and Harbert, with no tool named; the card [firewood](firewood.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The raft of logs** (I.4 ¶39): fairly large pieces of dead wood bound with dry lianas, made by Pencroff and Harbert in an eddy of the river, in an hour, with no tool named; the card [raft of logs](raft-of-logs.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The liana rope** (I.4 ¶63): dry lianas plaited by Pencroff into a rope several fathoms long, by hand; the card [liana rope](liana-rope.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The Chimneys, closed up** (I.5 ¶1): sand, stones, interlaced branches and wet earth, stopped into the galleries by Pencroff, with a narrow gut left for smoke; the card [the Chimneys](../places/the-chimneys.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The hearth** (I.5 ¶11): large flat stones laid at the end of the first left corridor by Pencroff, with logs and small wood on them; the card [hearth](hearth.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The first fire** (I.5 ¶78): dry grass, leaves and moss, a paper cone from Spilett's notebook and a single match struck on a pebble, by Harbert, blown up by Pencroff; the card [first fire](first-fire.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The burnt linen** (I.6 ¶16): part of Pencroff's large-checked handkerchief, reduced to a half-burnt rag at Harbert's suggestion that burnt linen can serve as tinder, by Pencroff, the means of burning not stated, and kept in a cavity of the Chimneys' central chamber; the card [burnt linen](burnt-linen.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The clubs** (I.6 ¶18): two stout branches broken from the first tree at the forest's edge by Pencroff, their points worn on a rock by Harbert, with no tool, there being no knife; the card [clubs](clubs.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The tétras lines** (I.6 ¶41): thin lianas tied end to end, fifteen to twenty feet long, with strong curved thorns of a dwarf-acacia bush tied on as hooks and big red worms as bait, by Pencroff; the card [tétras lines](tetras-lines.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The tétras jelly** (I.8 ¶39): juice of the tétras flesh mixed into water by Pencroff, in two big bivalve shells fetched by Harbert, and later a jelly with scraps of flesh (I.8 ¶54); the card [tétras jelly](tetras-jelly.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The litter** (I.8 ¶49): the main branches stripped by Pencroff, Nab and Harbert, with no tool but their arms, from a puny maritime pine on the high dune, covered with leaves and grass, in about forty minutes (I.8 ¶50), and later with mosses and long grasses (I.8 ¶85); the card [litter](litter.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The seaweed bed** (I.9 ¶16): algae and wrack that had stayed nearly dry, laid for Smith in the central passage of the Chimneys, by the party; the card [seaweed](seaweed.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The sticks of young fir** (I.9 ¶86): three sticks torn from the trunk of a young fir by Harbert, Nab and Pencroff; the card [clubs](clubs.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The second raft of logs** (I.9 ¶118): set up quickly by Pencroff, as he had the first time, though for want of fire it seemed a useless task; the card [raft of logs](raft-of-logs.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The Chimneys' partitions, set up again** (I.10 ¶13): partitions of stones and sand, rebuilt in the corridors by the engineer and the reporter, who had "employed the day well" (I.10 ¶14), no tool named; the card [the Chimneys](../places/the-chimneys.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The spit** (I.10 ¶12): set up before the Chimneys' fire by Pencroff with Nab's help, its material not stated; the card [spit](spit.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The lens** (I.10 ¶8): the glasses of Smith's watch and of the reporter's, filled with water and their edges made to stick together with a little clay, by Smith, no tool named; the glasses went back into the watches afterwards (I.10 ¶19); the card [lens](lens.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The burnt linen, again** (I.10 ¶19): a little more of the checked linen burnt by Pencroff before the climb, to serve as tinder; the card [burnt linen](burnt-linen.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The camp fire on the mountain** (I.10 ¶45): brushwood and dry moss fetched by Nab and Harbert, a hearth of stones laid by Pencroff, the "briquet" struck on flint so that the burnt linen caught the sparks, and blown up by Nab; the card [spark pebbles](spark-pebbles.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The map of the island** (I.11 ¶21): drawn at once by Spilett from the summit, on Smith's advice, with sufficient precision, the means not stated; the card [island map](island-map.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The measuring pole** (I.14 ¶7): a straight pole about a dozen feet long, measured against Smith's own height, ten feet standing above the sand once planted two feet down (I.14 ¶18), by Smith; the card [measuring pole](measuring-pole.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The plumb line** (I.14 ¶7): a stone fixed to the end of a flexible fibre, handed by Smith to Harbert; the card [plumb line](plumb-line.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The slate slab and its figures** (I.14 ¶22): a flat slate stone kept from earlier excursions, on which the sum was scratched with a sharp shell, by Smith; the card [slate slab](slate-slab.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The hibiscus net** (I.14 ¶36): a net of hibiscus fibres, made by Nab, which held the meal and then the oysters; the card [fibre net](fibre-net.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The shadow rod** (I.14 ¶66): a six-foot rod stuck in the smooth sand, tilted to the south, and small wooden pegs stuck in to mark the shadow's shrinking, by Smith; the card [shadow rod](shadow-rod.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The seal-skin bellows** (I.15 ¶30): two seal skins taken untanned from seals killed on the islet (I.15 ¶22, ¶28) by Nab and Pencroff in three hours, stretched on wooden frames and sewn with fibres by Smith with the colonists' help, with only the two steel blades of Top's collar, in three days, a clay pipe at its end and a mechanism of frames, fibre cords and counterweights (I.15 ¶44); the card [seal bellows](seal-bellows.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The road through the Jacamar wood** (I.15 ¶33): a way cut, no tool named, obliquely through the thickest of the wood from south-east to north-west by the party on 20 April; the card [the wood of the Jacamar](../places/jacamar-wood.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The branch hut** (I.15 ¶40): branches mingled with lianas and plastered with clay, set up in under an hour at the forest's edge by the colonists; the card [the branch hut](the-branch-hut.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The iron** (I.15 ¶43, ¶45): ore broken small and cleaned by hand, laid in layers with coal in a cubic mass with the bellows' pipe at its centre, by Smith with the colonists; a spongy bloom (I.15 ¶45); the card [iron](iron.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The granite anvil and the bloom-hammer** (I.15 ¶46): the first bloom hafted on a stick to forge the second on an anvil of granite, by the colonists; the card [the granite anvil](granite-anvil.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The iron tools** (I.15 ¶47): bars forged by 25 April into pincers, tongs, picks and pickaxes, by Pencroff and Nab under Smith's direction; the card [the iron tools](iron-tools.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The steel** (I.15 ¶49): the iron heated with powdered charcoal in a crucible of refractory clay, by Smith; the card [steel](steel.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The steel tools** (I.15 ¶50, ¶51): axe heads, plane blades, hatchets, bands for saws, chisels, pick and shovel irons, hammers and nails, worked with the hammer from the steel by Smith, Nab and Pencroff, heated red and quenched in cold water, finished on 5 May (I.15 ¶52); the card [the iron tools](iron-tools.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The pyrite heap** (I.17 ¶41, ¶42): schistose pyrites hauled on the hurdle by Smith, Harbert and Spilett, several tons in a day (I.17 ¶37, ¶38), then piled on branches and chopped wood fired behind the Chimneys, covered with earth and grass with vents, begun on 8 May; the card [the pyrite heap](the-pyrite-heap.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The dugong fat** (I.17 ¶45): fat taken from the dugong's carcass by Nab and Pencroff and kept in great earthen jars; the card [the dugong fat](dugong-fat.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The natural soda** (I.17 ¶45): sea plants gathered, dried and burnt in open-air pits for several days till the ash fused; the card [the soda](soda.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The glycerine and the soap** (I.17 ¶46): the dugong's fat treated with the soda by Smith, giving a soluble soap and glycerine; the cards [the glycerine](glycerine.md) and [the soap](soap.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The refractory pottery and the sulphate furnace** (I.17 ¶49): pottery of plastic clay and a brick furnace of a special design, made by the colonists under Smith in the days after the first eight, finished about 18 May; the cards [the refractory pottery](refractory-pottery.md) and [the sulphate furnace](sulphate-furnace.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The iron sulphate** (I.17 ¶50): the roasted heap put in a basin of water, stirred, left to settle, decanted and evaporated in part, till crystals settled; the card [the iron sulphate](iron-sulphate.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The sulphuric acid** (I.17 ¶54): the crystals calcined in closed vessels in the furnace and the vapours condensed, by Smith, on 20 May, twelve days after beginning; the card [the sulphuric acid](sulphuric-acid.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The nitric acid** (I.17 ¶55): the saltpetre attacked by the sulphuric acid and distilled, by Smith; the card [the nitric acid](nitric-acid.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The nitro-glycerine** (I.17 ¶58): the nitric acid put with glycerine concentrated on a water bath, by Smith alone, away from the Chimneys, several pints; the card [the nitro-glycerine](nitro-glycerine.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The mine hole** (I.17 ¶67, ¶68): bored obliquely into the granite of the lake's east bank from an outer edge, with a pick, by Pencroff relieved by Nab, begun at dawn and finished about four in the evening; the card [the mine hole](mine-hole.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The firing device** (I.17 ¶72): an iron mass of several pounds hung over the hole from a post by a plant fibre, and a long sulphured fibre tied to its middle, burning twenty-five minutes, laid by Smith; the card [the firing device](firing-device.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The new fall** (I.17 ¶75, ¶77): the lake's granite frame split by the explosion, giving a stream and a fall of three hundred feet; the card [the new fall](../places/the-new-fall.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The torches** (I.18 ¶18): resinous branches cut by Nab and Harbert on the lake's shore, lit with the briquet; the card [the torches](torches.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The old outlet's mouth, raised** (I.18 ¶14): a hole twenty feet wide and two high raised with picks by Nab and Pencroff in under an hour; the card [the old outlet](../places/the-old-outlet.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The opening in the cavern's front wall** (I.18 ¶62, ¶63): a hole through a wall three feet thick with a pick, by Pencroff, then Nab, then Spilett, in two hours, the pick falling through at the last blow; the card [Granite-house](../places/granite-house.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The facade openings** (I.19 ¶14): five windows, a large bay, bull's-eyes and a door, blasted out of the granite with the remaining nitro-glycerine and finished with the pick and mattock, by Smith, in a few days from 22 May; the card [Granite-house](../places/granite-house.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The rope ladder** (I.19 ¶19, ¶28): uprights of curry-jonc fibre plaited with a windlass, rungs of a red-cedar kind, a hundred rungs in two lengths with a landing levelled with the pick, by Pencroff under Smith, fixed on 28 May; the card [the rope ladder](the-rope-ladder.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The pulley and the fibre cords** (I.19 ¶20): other cords of vegetable fibre and a coarse pulley fitted at the door, with which the bricks were hoisted; the cards [the fibre ropes](fibre-rope.md) and [the pulley](pulley.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The partitions of Granite-house** (I.19 ¶20, ¶43): a very rudimentary frame of partitions raised easily, with the bricks hoisted up and the lime to hand, to divide the apartment, finished on 31 May; the card [Granite-house](../places/granite-house.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The kitchen flue** (I.19 ¶43): a pipe of brick earth led obliquely through a hole pierced in the granite above the kitchen window, made by Smith; the card [Granite-house](../places/granite-house.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The blocking of the old outlet** (I.19 ¶44): rock quarters rolled to its mouth and strongly cemented, disguised with plants set in the gaps, by Smith; the card [the old outlet](../places/the-old-outlet.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The water channel** (I.19 ¶45): a small cut below the lake's level, through the old outlet, giving twenty-five to thirty gallons a day, by Smith; the card [the water channel](the-water-channel.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The snares** (I.20 ¶2; I.22 ¶13): loops of woody fibre, later of curry-jonc fibre, made by Pencroff; the card [the snares](snares.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The candles** (I.20 ¶18 to ¶25): about three hundred pounds of seal fat saponified with lime, freed with sulphuric acid, the liquid oleic acid pressed out, and the margaric and stearic acids moulded by hand round vegetable-fibre wicks, by Smith, in twenty-four hours, on 5 and 6 June; the card [the candles](candles.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The snuffers** (I.20 ¶25): a fine pair of snuffers made by Smith; the card [the snuffers](snuffers.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The scissors** (I.20 ¶27): scissors made in June so the colonists could cut hair and trim beards, maker not named; the card [the scissors](scissors.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The hand saw and the furniture** (I.20 ¶28; I.22 ¶27): an égohine saw made at infinite pains that divided wood into planks, then tables, seats, wardrobes and bed frames with zostera mattresses, the colonists the carpenters; the cards [the hand saw](hand-saw.md) and [the furniture](the-furniture.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The two bridges** (I.20 ¶29): two small bridges twenty to twenty-five feet long on the plateau and on the beach, framed with trees squared with the axe, by the carpenters in a few days; the card [the two bridges](the-two-bridges.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The dragon-tree drink** (I.20 ¶31): the woody roots of the dragon trees fermented into a sour drink, a kind of beer; the card [the dragon trees](dragon-trees.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The maple sugar** (I.20 ¶31; I.22 ¶48, ¶49): the sap of the sugar maple evaporated in great earthen vessels, stirred by Nab with a wooden spatula, poured as syrup into clay moulds made in the kitchen furnace, in a day; the card [the maple sugar](maple-sugar.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The pit traps** (I.22 ¶6, ¶7): pits dug in the ground and roofed with branches and grass, baited, by Harbert, Pencroff and Spilett on the plateau and at the forest edge; the card [the pit traps](pit-traps.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The wicker baskets** (I.22 ¶28): baskets and hampers of various sizes plaited from the purple osiers cut by Pencroff and Harbert, by the colonists in the winter; the card [the wicker baskets](wicker-baskets.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The pirogue** (II.1 ¶34 to ¶36; II.2 ¶1): a flat-bottomed canoe of douglas bark, stripped from firs the hurricane had felled, sewn in strips and meant to be joined with riveted nails, on a frame of flexible crejimba rods, with three benches, a gunwale for two oars and a stern oar, twelve feet long, by Pencroff with Smith's help, with the colonists' imperfect tools, in five days to 29 October; the card [the pirogue](the-pirogue.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The chest taken to Granite-house** (II.2 ¶26 to ¶44): an oak chest found stranded on the sand with two barrels, kept afloat by them at the rising tide, towed by the pirogue three miles in an hour and a half to the shore before Granite-house and hauled on the sand; the barrels were detached and the locks forced with a pincer, by Pencroff, Nab and the colonists; the card [the chest](the-chest.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The westward track** (II.4 ¶4, ¶5): a way hacked through the forest from the camp at the Mercy fall, the first axe blows struck among lentisk bushes a little above the cascade, steered by Smith's pocket compass, meant to join the Red Creek road later; the colonists, with the chest's felling axes; the card [the westward track](../places/the-westward-track.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The balloon's envelope and gear recovered** (II.5 ¶80 to ¶96): the envelope, net, retaining ring and anchor found hanging in a gigantic pine, got down by Nab, Harbert and Pencroff climbing the tree in nearly two hours, dragged to the shore and stored in a rock cavity whose opening was to be hidden; the cards [the balloon's envelope](balloon-envelope.md), [the balloon's gear](balloon-gear.md) and [the balloon store](../places/balloon-store.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The Mercy bridge** (II.7 ¶26 to ¶31): a bridge of piles and planks, fixed on one bank and tipping on counterweights on the other, from trees felled, trimmed and sawn into joists, beams and planks, the piles driven into the riverbed with a pile driver, tools and ironwork used, by the colonists, Smith directing, in three weeks to 20 November; the card [the Mercy bridge](the-mercy-bridge.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The second wheat field** (II.7 ¶37, ¶38): a field prepared and fenced with a strong, high, pointed palisade, with noisy clappers and dummies of Pencroff's invention against birds, and seven hundred and fifty grains of the first crop sown in small regular furrows; the cards [the wheat field](../places/the-wheat-field.md) and [the scarecrows](scarecrows.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The ditch and the Creek-Glycérine** (II.7 ¶22, ¶39, ¶40): a ditch twelve feet wide and six deep blasted with nitro-glycerine through two or three feet of earth and the granite below, from the lake's south angle to the Mercy's bend, with a second cut in the lake's rocky rim to fill it, by Smith, from 21 November to the first fortnight of December; the card [Creek-Glycérine](../places/creek-glycerine.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The poultry-yard, dovecote and stable** (II.7 ¶44 to ¶46, ¶59): a palisaded yard of two hundred square yards on the lake's south-east shore with compartmented huts of branches, a dovecote in one corner built by Smith, and a stable with good litter near it, by the colonists in December and after 23 December; the cards [the poultry-yard](../places/basse-cour.md), [the dovecote](../places/the-dovecote.md) and [the stable](../places/the-stable.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The harness and the road to port Ballon** (II.7 ¶61): harness and traces of plant fibres for the onaggas, and a straight cut through the Far-West forest from the Mercy bend to port Ballon, by the colonists, the road hardly roughed out; the cards [the harness](harness.md) and [the road to port Ballon](../places/port-ballon-road.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The linen** (II.8 ¶1 to ¶4): shirts, socks and sheets sewn with the chest's needles from the balloon cloth, whose seams were unpicked by Spilett and Harbert and whose thread was reused, the cloth degreased with soda and potash got by burning plants and bleached white in the open air, Pencroff sewing best, in the first week of January; the cards [the linen](the-linen.md), [the balloon's envelope](balloon-envelope.md), [the potash](potash.md) and [the soda](soda.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The sealskin shoes** (II.8 ¶5): large, long shoes of sealskin leather to replace the shoes and boots brought from America, maker not named; the card [the sealskins](sealskins.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The iron shot** (II.8 ¶8): grains of iron, easy to make, larger than lead shot so that a charge holds fewer, to replace lead that Smith found no trace of on the island; the card [the iron shot](iron-shot.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The pyroxyle** (II.8 ¶9 to ¶13): guncotton from elder pith (cellulose) steeped for a quarter of an hour in ordinary nitric acid mixed with concentrated sulphuric acid, washed and dried, by Smith, to spare the chest's powder; the cards [the pyroxyle](pyroxyle.md), [the elder](elder.md) and [the nitric acid](nitric-acid.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The bottom lines** (II.8 ¶15): bottom lines armed with iron hooks, set in the lake and the Mercy by Pencroff, which take fine trout; the card [the bottom lines](bottom-lines.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The salted salmon** (II.8 ¶19): several hundred salmon of a run up the Mercy kept back by a few weirs, salted and put by for the winter, by the colonists; the card [the salted salmon](salted-salmon.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The corral and the route du Corral** (II.8 ¶30 to ¶35): a round palisade of about a hundred stakes from the road's felled trees, pointed and fire-hardened, with bolted crosspieces and props, a two-leaf plank door barred outside and vast plank sheds, in a meadow at the Red Creek's sources, by Smith and the colonists in three weeks, reached by a new road of five miles; the cards [the corral](../places/corral.md) and [the route du Corral](../places/route-du-corral.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The spruce beer** (II.8 ¶44): a beer of young shoots of the "abies nigra", boiled and fermented, by Smith; the card [the spruce beer](spruce-beer.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The veranda** (II.8 ¶47): a veranda covered with climbing plants on the edge of the Grande-Vue plateau, raised by Nab with his own hands; the card [the veranda](../places/the-veranda.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The lathe** (II.9 ¶6): a turning lathe set up in Granite-house, to turn small toilet and kitchen utensils and buttons (later the boat’s pulleys), by Smith, tools and materials not named; the card [the lathe](the-lathe.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The peccary sty** (II.9 ¶18): a sty built near the poultry-yard for young peccaries, builders and materials not named; the card [the peccary sty](../places/the-peccary-sty.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The hydraulic lift** (II.9 ¶31, ¶32): the lake diversion at the head of the overflow enlarged to make a strong fall at the bottom of the corridor, a paddle cylinder below it joined outside to a wheel wound with a strong cable carrying a banne, a long rope to engage and disengage the motor, by Smith, working for the first time on 17 March; the card [the hydraulic lift](the-hydraulic-lift.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The glassworks** (II.9 ¶33, ¶34): the old pottery kiln adapted to glassmaking after several failed trials, fuelled with the ground’s coal, by Smith, with Spilett and Harbert helping for some days; the card [the glassworks](the-glassworks.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The glassblower’s pipe** (II.9 ¶35): the "canne", an iron tube five or six feet long, made from a long thin strip of iron rolled like a gun barrel, by Pencroff, the hardest tool of the glassworks; the card [the glassblower’s pipe](glass-blowers-pipe.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The window glass** (II.9 ¶36 to ¶41): a batch of a hundred parts of sand, thirty-five of chalk and forty of sulphate of soda with two or three of powdered charcoal, melted in refractory-clay crucibles, gathered on the pipe, blown by Harbert into a bubble a foot across, swung by Smith into a cylinder, capped, split, reheated and flattened with a wooden roller, on 28 March and after; the cards [the window glass](window-glass.md) and [the fulgurites](fulgurites.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The glassware** (II.9 ¶42): glasses and bottles taken as they came from the end of the pipe, Pencroff blowing some himself; the card [the glassware](glassware.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The cycas flour** (II.9 ¶54, ¶62): the floury pith of the cycas stems pressed to drive off the mucilaginous sap, in a press set up by Smith, made into cakes and puddings by Nab; the cards [the cycas](cycas.md) and [the cycas press](the-cycas-press.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The Bonadventure** (II.10 ¶3 to ¶7; II.12 ¶79 to ¶88): a decked fir sloop of thirty-five feet of keel, her seams caulked with dry zostera oakum and boiling pine tar, ballasted with twelve thousand pounds of granite set in lime, her ironwork forged at the Chimneys, by Smith and Pencroff, from April to 10 October; the cards [the Bonadventure](bonadventure.md), [the zostera](zostera.md) and [the pine tar](pine-tar.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The koula skins** (II.10 ¶19): skins of three koulas killed in the last fortnight of April, tanned with sulphuric acid; maker not named; the card [the koula skins](koula-skins.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The tobacco** (II.10 ¶33): leaves dried thin, chopped and roasted on hot stones, by Smith and the colonists, out of Pencroff’s sight, and given him in a filled pipe on 31 May; the card [tobacco](tobacco.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The whale oil** (II.10 ¶67): the whale’s blubber cut in slices, in pieces of about a thousand pounds, melted in great earthen vessels at the spot of the stranding, losing a third of its weight; the card [the whale oil](whale-oil.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The baleen traps** (II.10 ¶69, ¶75; II.11 ¶28): a dozen baleen plates cut in six and sharpened, bent, iced over, hidden in fat and sown on the snow at the forest edge, by Smith, set at the end of June; the cards [the baleen](baleen.md) and [the baleen traps](baleen-traps.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The felt** (II.11 ¶3 to ¶9): the mouflons’ wool scoured in vats at seventy degrees for a day, washed with soda baths, pressed dry, steeped in a soapy solution and stamped in a water-driven fulling mill into a thick felt, by Smith with the colonists, in June; the cards [the felt](felt.md), [the fulling mill](the-fulling-mill.md) and [the mouflon wool](mouflon-wool.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The albatross message** (II.11 ¶35): a succinct notice written by Spilett, sealed in a sack of strong gummed canvas and tied to the neck of a captured albatross on 30 June; the card [the albatross message](the-albatross-message.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The Bonadventure’s sails** (II.11 ¶39; II.12 ¶12): cut from the balloon’s envelope and sewn with strong boltropes, by Pencroff with Harbert at the sailmaker’s needle; the card [the Bonadventure’s sails](the-boats-sails.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The Bonadventure’s rigging and pulleys** (II.12 ¶12): hemp cordage from the balloon’s net and cables, made into halyards, shrouds and sheets, with pulleys turned by Smith on the lathe, by Pencroff; the cards [the rigging](the-boats-rigging.md) and [the lathe](the-lathe.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The Lincoln flag** (II.12 ¶12): a blue, red and white flag dyed with island plants, thirty-seven stars and a thirty-eighth for the state of Lincoln, made by Pencroff; the cards [the Lincoln flag](the-lincoln-flag.md) and [the dye plants](dye-plants.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The Tabor seeds** (II.13 ¶80; II.14 ¶41, ¶80; II.16 ¶79): vegetable seed gathered from the old Tabor plots by Harbert, brought back in the Bonadventure and sown with care on the Grande-Vue plateau; the cards [the Tabor seeds](tabor-seeds.md) and [the kitchen garden](../places/the-kitchen-garden.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The Tabor stores** (II.14 ¶5, ¶80; II.15 ¶44, ¶46): a barrel of powder, a barrel of lead, boxes of caps, shotguns and tools taken from the Tabor hut by Harbert and Spilett and landed at Lincoln; the cards [the Tabor powder](the-tabor-powder.md), [the Tabor lead](the-tabor-lead.md), [the Tabor primers](the-tabor-primers.md), [the Tabor guns](the-tabor-guns.md) and [the Tabor tools](the-tabor-tools.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The third wheat harvest** (II.16 ¶80, ¶81): the crop of the third field, reaped on 15 November, four thousand bushels, more than five hundred million grains, with a dozen bushels kept to sow each year; the card [the grain of wheat](grain-of-wheat.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The windmill** (II.16 ¶82 to ¶87): timber for the cage and mechanism, big sandstones from the north of the lake for millstones and balloon cloth for the sails, to Smith’s plans, a cylindrical pepper-pot cage on a pivot with a pointed roof, four sail frames set in the shaft with iron tenons, a case for two millstones, a hopper, an oscillating trough called the "babillard" and a bolter, by Nab and Pencroff as carpenters with everyone working, finished on 1 December; the cards [the windmill](the-windmill.md), [the sandstone](sandstone.md) and [the balloon’s envelope](balloon-envelope.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The first flour and loaf** (II.16 ¶94): two or three bushels of wheat ground on the mill’s first morning, and a fine loaf, a little compact, leavened with beer yeast, on the table next day; the cards [the first loaf](the-first-loaf.md) and [the beer yeast](beer-yeast.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The corral house** (II.17 ¶27, ¶28): a wooden house made as comfortable as possible, built in under a week at the corral about twenty feet from the stables, with a bed, table, bench, wardrobe and chest, for Ayrton; made by the colonists with the tools they took there, decided on 10 December and finished by 20 December; the cards [the corral house](../places/the-corral-house.md) and [the corral house furniture](the-corral-house-furniture.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The draw-plate and the iron wire** (II.18 ¶33 to ¶39): a steel plate pierced with conical holes of several sizes, tempered "de tout son dur" and fixed on a frame sunk in the ground a few feet from the great fall, the fulling mill’s shaft winding the iron rods through it, by Smith, with his companions drawing; the wire, forty to fifty feet in each length, was got in a few days in January 1867; the cards [the draw-plate](the-draw-plate.md), [the telegraph wire](telegraph-wire.md) and [the fulling mill](the-fulling-mill.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The telegraph battery** (II.18 ¶40 to ¶47): glass flasks of nitric acid, each stoppered with a cork pierced by a glass tube plugged with clay and filled with a solution of potash got by burning plants, with two zinc plates from the chest’s lining, after Becquerel’s pattern, by Smith, two batteries in all; the cards [the telegraph battery](the-telegraph-battery.md), [the zinc](zinc.md), [the nitric acid](nitric-acid.md), [the potash](potash.md) and [the glassware](glassware.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The telegraph** (II.18 ¶46 to ¶49): iron wire carried on posts fitted with glass insulators along the corral road, an electromagnet of soft iron and a plate at each end, a needle on a dial lettered with the alphabet, installed from 6 February and complete on 12 February, by Smith; the cards [the telegraph](the-telegraph.md), [the telegraph posts](telegraph-posts.md), [the glass insulators](glass-insulators.md) and [the telegraph instruments](telegraph-instruments.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The photographs** (II.18 ¶59 to ¶66): views of the island and portraits of its inhabitants, made on glass plates sensitised with the chest’s collodion and silver nitrate, fixed with hyposulphite of soda and printed on the chlorinated papers, by Spilett and Harbert, in the spring of 1867; the cards [the photographic apparatus](photographic-apparatus.md), [the photographic landscapes](the-photographic-landscapes.md) and [the portraits](the-portraits.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **Harbert’s plate** (II.20 ¶82 to ¶85): a negative of the whole Union Bay taken with the camera’s lens at a window of the great hall and fixed in the dark closet, by Harbert, on 17 October; the cards [the photographic plate](the-photographic-plate.md) and [the dark closet](../places/the-dark-closet.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The foliage screen** (III.1 ¶79 to ¶82): boughs and lianas fetched from the edge of the Jacamar wood and hung before the windows of Granite-house on Smith’s order, by the colonists other than Nab and Ayrton, no tool named; the card [the foliage screen](the-foliage-screen.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The salvage of the spars and sails** (III.4 ¶10 to ¶23): the Speedy’s masts, yards and sails made fast with ropes by Ayrton and Pencroff from the pirogue, hauled up the strand by the colonists together, the sails unbent and dried, on 18 October; the cards [the Speedy’s spars](the-speedys-spars.md), [the Speedy’s sails](the-speedys-sails.md) and [the pirogue](the-pirogue.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The salvage of the cargo** (III.4 ¶56 to ¶75): chests and casks hoisted out through the opening in the hull with a tackle by Ayrton and Pencroff, ferried to the beach in the pirogue and stored in Granite-house at high water, 18 to 21 October; the cards [the Speedy’s cargo](the-speedys-cargo.md) and [the pirogue](the-pirogue.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The salvage of the powder** (III.4 ¶61, ¶62): about twenty copper-lined barrels found in the stern amid the projectiles and taken out with care by Smith and his companions, with axes in hand, on 18 October; the card [the Speedy’s powder](the-speedys-powder.md). *(Drafted.)*
- **The Speedy’s cannon, anchors, chains and ballast** (III.4 ¶75): found on the bed of the channel by Ayrton and Pencroff, who dived several times before the sands swallowed them, the four cannon floated ashore with empty casks, 19 to 21 October; the cards [the Speedy’s cannon](the-speedys-cannon.md), [the Speedy’s anchors](the-speedys-anchors.md) and [the Speedy’s ballast](the-speedys-ballast.md). *(Drafted.)*
