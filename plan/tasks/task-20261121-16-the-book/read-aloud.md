I read XIX bis aloud from the ereader, and as I went I read it the way I want it. These are the places where what I read isn't what's on the page, cleaned up from the recording.

1. **Pencroff's promise (¶ "We will go and see").** Read: "If it is worth telling, I'll tell it, and to-morrow night at latest." Herbert promised, **the more readily for the last word**, and no one would wonder…
2. **Leaving Granite House (2 June, new paragraph after the tools).** "Toward eight o'clock the ebb began to near its lowest. Pencroff threw a piece of sailcloth over his shoulders, and said aloud that the fish did not mind the rain, if he did, and went out by the door, Herbert after him. Neb called to them that they were two madmen, and went back to his saucepans."
3. **The ball, described.** "It was a ball of glass, covered in seaweed and almost invisible in the barnacles."
4. **The knife.** For v1's "his knife could not get at it": Pencroff works the blade down beside the glass till he feels the cord with its point and saws at it blindly a long while; the sand closes on the blade at every stroke; he draws it out not knowing whether he has cut the cord in half or cut nothing at all.
5. **"I have never saw the like."** "I have put in at many good ports, my boy, and I have never saw the like."
6. **The glass floats explained (new, two paragraphs after "The sailor may well say so").** Only in late years had the fishermen of northern Europe, in the cod fisheries of Norway, begun to buoy their great nets with globes of blown glass, which do not drink water as cork does and neither rot nor sink; still a novelty of the northern seas, which an American sailor might well never have seen. That such a float lay on Lincoln Island would astonish no one who knows the roads the sea opens to floating bodies: the South Pacific's great round (up the coasts of Chile and Peru, west below the line, south, then east under the westerlies); whatever falls into it, a spar, a cask, a float, it carries for months and years. "The sea is patient, and sooner or later it brings in everything it does not sink."
7. **Herbert on the letters.** After "Latin, I think," said he: "but a word in Latin may be read a dozen ways, and here there are not letters enough to choose among them, and Mr. Smith would know better than I."
8. **Herbert swept off (new, the retreat).** The hollows the ebb had scoured run toward the gut; the bottom goes from under Herbert's feet; the current carries him off, turns him over and draws him seaward. Pencroff throws himself after him, catches him by the collar, loses him, catches him again by the arm, plants himself against the stream and hauls him onto firmer sand; then takes him under the shoulder and carries rather than leads him. At the wall Herbert coughs up the seawater, laughs and says it was nothing; Pencroff does not laugh.
   (Replaces v1's "Pencroff took the lad by the arm. They waded, ran, waded again.")
9. **The cord gives way.** The sand they dug from round the ball no longer holds it; it rocks under the sea; the cord the sailor's knife worked upon, if not severed, had been weakened. At last it gives way. (v1: "what was left of the rotten cord gave way".)
10. **Why Pencroff keeps silent (replaces v1's ¶137, "might give back something else").** He meant to tell it, he had promised it, but the rest could not be told by halves: to speak of the float was to speak of the bar, the hour of low water let slip, the flood he, a seaman, had not watched, and the lad turned over in the current like a piece of weed; he had taken the boy out alone on a making tide, against a rule he had kept all his life, for the pleasure of laying a treasure on the table with his own hands. Pencroff would have confessed any folly in the world to Smith, but not that one. And the float was in pieces on the islet; what good could it do anyone now? The sailor looked into the fire and held his tongue.
11. **Herbert's silence (new).** Herbert looked at him and understood, and he held his tongue too. It was the first time he had kept anything from the engineer, and it sat ill with him; but he would not buy his own ease at the sailor's cost. He told himself the float was lost and there was nothing left to tell, and tried to believe it.
12. **3 June, out again.** "This time Pencroff kept the lad on his landward side, and did not take his eyes off the gut."
13. **The ending (replaces v1's Herbert line and the silence).** "Five of us with picks, and we'd have had it," he said. But he shook himself, as Top does coming out of the water. "Bah," said he. "The sea has taken, and the sea will give us something else. She owes us that much, my boy, and she pays her debts." And he put his great hand on the lad's shoulder, and they went back across the sand to the lines. **But the sea had taken back its secret, and it was to keep it a long while yet.**
