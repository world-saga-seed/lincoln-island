# Logic check, 01950: knowledge and causality; character and story

*Dimension: who knows what and when, cause of each beat, what can be seen; motives (above all the two silences), the narrator's reach, the seams with 01900 and 02000. Every quote below was verified with grep -F. Outline = story/outline/01950-the-float-in-the-bar.md; notes = story/notes/01950-the-float-in-the-bar.md; ledger = plan/settled/ledger/2026-11-21.md.*

Count: 0 breaks, 3 strains, 3 nits.

## 1. Neither silence, as the outline now states it, covers keeping the float itself back (strains)

- Outline, After, *(Settled R1121-79, R1121-134, R1121-135.)*: "Harbert to spare the sailor, Pencroff from what the day cost the boy."
- Outline, beat 6, *(Settled R1121-134, R1121-135.)*: "It was nothing, Pencroff. And no one need know that I took a ducking." and "He spares the sailor; that is his own reason."
- Ledger R1121-134 (the reader's point the author accepted): "a boy coughing up sea water is a reason to tell". Same row, the reason it gave Pencroff: "he will not have the others hoping on a thing that came to nothing". Ledger R1121-135: "its reason now this, not shame named".

Why it fails: Herbert's line is about hiding his ducking. But the ducking is not what has to be kept back, since the joke ("the sea took the pair of us") already tells the house they were soaked, and the float could be told without the near-drowning. So his stated reason covers the ducking, not the float. Pencroff's reason in After is the very reason R1121-134 called a reason to tell. The reason that did fit (his 1 June rule against raising hopes on a thing that came to nothing) is in the ledger but is no longer in the outline's After or beat 6. Nothing now says why Pencroff, who is free to tell the float once it is gone, does not, and why Herbert's hiding of a ducking becomes silence about a float through III.16.

Fixes:
1. Restore in After and in beat 6 that Pencroff's reason is his own 1 June rule: nothing to show now, so nothing to tell the others, who would hope for a thing that came to nothing. Herbert's "It was nothing" then refers to the float and the ducking together. Recommended.
2. Keep After as written and add to Herbert's line a clause covering the float ("there is nothing to tell"), so that his own reason reaches it.
3. Say in the notes that the two silences are different in kind and need not cover the same thing (the ducking, the float), and leave the float's silence to Pencroff's rule alone.

## 2. Pencroff keeps a man-made object from the man whose job is to watch for strangers (strains)

- Outline, beat 2, *(Settled R1121-34.)*: "He takes only Harbert, so as not to raise hopes." and *(Settled R1121-109.)*: "Harbert's cry at "made by men's hands" is the hope of a ship".
- 01900 outline, ¶4 to ¶12 (Drafted): "Cyrus says what is easy for them will be easy for others" and "enemies, if none within, may come from without". Pencroff was in that talk and "makes no more objection".
- world/false-assumptions.md: "Smith guards against others coming, men or beasts" and "No one thinks of a helper."

Why it fails: two days earlier, in Pencroff's hearing, Smith made a whole house's design out of the danger of others arriving. A made thing washed up under that house is exactly the news Smith asked to be told; Pencroff knows it, and he knows the hope of a ship is only one of its two readings. The one cause the outline gives ("not to raise hopes") also fails on its own terms, since the first act of the secrecy is to raise Herbert's hopes.

Fixes:
1. Give the cause one line in the notes: on 1 June the thing is only half seen and may be a stone (beat 2 already has him hedge), and he will not trouble Smith with a stone; the doubt that was real on 1 June is gone by the evening of 2 June. Recommended, since it needs no new lore.
2. Name in beat 2 the reason Herbert and not the others: he is the one hand fit to dig and read (item 18, "Harbert chips and reads"), which answers the "hopes" contradiction.
3. Accept the strain as Pencroff's lapse (the chapter's cost of secrecy, R1121-37) and say in the notes that the page does not defend it.

## 3. The reason for leaving the tools is thin beside the stakes Pencroff has himself named (strains)

- Outline, beat 3, *(Settled R1121-52, R1121-72.)*: "because picks carried out of Granite-house would be asked about".
- Outline, beat 2, *(Resolved by judgment, overturnable.)*: "It is his daily errand, so no one asks, and it is no lie." and beat 1: "Storms reshape this shore" (Pencroff knows the sea will move or bury the float).
- Outline, What the chapter is, *(Settled R1121-37.)*: "That cost is the secrecy: five could have dug the float out in the hour of low water".

Why it fails: the pick is the only cost the outline sets against a chance that, by Pencroff's own sea sense, the next squall will take. A man who asks for the lines as an errand can ask for a spade for bait or shellfish just as easily, and nobody asks a sailor why he digs. The chapter's point (the cost of secrecy) works better if the secrecy is more than that: right now it is cheap for him to carry the tool and dear for him to go without.

Fixes:
1. Leave as is: the thin cause is the point, and a reader sees the cost. Only the notes need a line that this is a lapse of Pencroff's, not a necessity.
2. Add a second cause: the one spade Pencroff would have to ask Smith for is kept in the storeroom where Smith and Nab are at work that morning (the shutters and indoor work, R1121-69). Needs a line in the notes placing the tools.
3. Let Pencroff think of the spade and decide against it for fear of the question, as the draft already has him think of the picks. Recommended; the outline's beat 3 already holds this.

## 4. Herbert's "no one need know that I took a ducking" sits against the joke told at the hearth (nit)

- Outline, beat 6, *(Settled R1121-59.)*: "The joke: "The lines took nothing, and the sea took the pair of us.""
- Outline, beat 6: "The joke is all they tell. It is true, and it is less than the whole."
- Outline, beat 6, *(Settled R1121-134, R1121-135.)*: "no one need know that I took a ducking."

Why it fails: the three at home see both of them soaked and hear that the sea took the pair. What Herbert wants hidden can only be the near-drowning in the hollow, not "a ducking". If the page leaves it as "ducking", a reader may take it as the soaking and find the request pointless.

Fixes:
1. Make the thing hidden the being swept off, in the notes (what Herbert means by it), leaving the words. Recommended.
2. Change the word to match: that he was carried off his feet.
3. Leave it, since the joke is Pencroff's cover and Herbert's phrase is a boy's understatement.

## 5. The cord is rotten, yet it resists a knife and two men (nit)

- Outline, beat 4, *(Settled R1121-72, R1121-129.)*: "saws blind at it a long while" (and "it is glass, stuck fast, and they cannot turn it").
- Outline, beat 4, Proposal from the draft: "the rotten cord comes out of the sand on the side toward the gut".
- Outline, beat 5, *(Settled R1121-65, R1121-66, R1121-68.)*: "what is left of the rotten mooring breaks".

Why it fails: a rotten cord that parts of itself under one surge is hard to square with an hour's blind sawing and with two pairs of hands unable to work the ball free. The weight is in the net and the sand (beat 4's "heavy with sand"), not the cord, and the outline does not say so.

Fixes:
1. Say in the notes that what holds is the buried net and sand, and the lashing is only what the knife finds, cut or not. Recommended; no new lore.
2. Drop "rotten" from the lashing and keep it for the cord on the surface.
3. Leave it; the knife does not know where the sound strand is.

## 6. The hour of low water on 2 June is "toward eight" in one settled line and about nine in another (nit)

- Outline, beat 3, *(Settled R1121-130.)*: "Toward eight, as the ebb nears its lowest".
- Notes, item 7, *(Settled R1121-80.)*: "low water about 8 in the morning on 1 June, about 9 on the 2nd".
- Notes, item 9: "The squall arrives about an hour after low water".

Why it fails: if they leave toward eight, the lowest water is about nine, as the item says, so the line is consistent only if the walk out takes an hour. It does not (the bar is "a cable or two" off, R1121-74), and a drafter reading the pair may fix the time either way and shift the squall.

Fixes:
1. In the notes add a clause that they leave toward eight and reach the bar about nine at the lowest water, with the squall about ten. Recommended.
2. Change "toward eight" to "toward nine" in the outline.
3. Leave it; the page gives no clock hour.
