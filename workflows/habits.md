# Habits: recorded on the sheets, tested in the prose

*Refinement. Add this when one character's tic shows up on another.*

A habit is anything a character does again and again that a reader comes to expect: a gesture, a phrase, a thing they always check, a thing they never do. Drafters invent habits freely and copy each other's, so one character's tic spreads to people it does not belong to. The sheet is where a habit is decided; the prose is checked against it.

## Where habits live

- **A sheet** gets a `## Habits` section: one bullet per habit, its mark and source, what the narrator can see of it, and the chapters where it shows. A **Not:** line records a habit the person does not have, where a drafter is likely to give it to them.
- **A card** gets a **Habits** line in the same terms.
- A habit in the prose that no sheet records is a **drafter's habit:** proposed for a sheet, given to the right person, or cut.

## Grounding

When a habit is needed, derive it from the person's trade or life. Ask: what habits come from doing their work every day? Propose a few; the writer picks. Never borrow another character's.

## The loop

1. **Extract** ([prompt](#extract)): one agent lists every repeated behavior per character in the drafted chapters, with two or more quotes each, against what the sheets record.
2. **Verify** each quote.
3. **Sort, with the writer:** keep (onto the sheet, marked *(Proposal, from the draft.)* until settled), give to the right person, or cut. Habits shared by two characters are the first suspects.
4. **Record** the kept habits; **sweep** the prose for the cut ones. Never sweep the writer's own sample lines.
5. **Test every batch** with the habits critic (`drafting/prompts/habits.md`).

## Extract

> You are reading the drafted chapters of a novel, in the folder {REPO} (read-only; write only {OUTPUT}; run no git; do not launch agents). Read {CHAPTERS}. For every character who appears in more than one scene, list each behavior they repeat (two or more times): gestures, phrases, ways of moving or speaking, what they check, carry, touch or look at, how they react, and the narrator's own habits of attention and telling. For each: the character; the habit in a few words; every quote that shows it (exact, 25 words at most, with file and line); and whether their sheet in `story/notes/characters/` already records it, quoting the line. Then list every habit shared by two or more characters. Do not judge which to keep. Inventing a habit is worse than missing one.
