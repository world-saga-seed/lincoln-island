# III.1: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller m. Beads corrected: none.* White's date for the sighting here is "the 17th of October", the French's; White's II.20 has the photograph on the 28th (see 42-discords.md, discord 16).

### 1. cut: Pencroff finds the speck from the plate (III.1 ¶3; White ¶1)
- White has Pencroff sweep the horizon to "the point indicated" and drops that it is the very point that made the speck on the photographic plate. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: c’est-à-dire sur celui qui avait fait l’imperceptible tache du cliché photographique
  - white: Pencroff seized the spy-glass and swept the horizon till his gaze fell upon the point indicated.

### 2. softened: Pencroff's oath on seeing the ship (III.1 ¶4; White ¶2)
- "Mille diables" is dropped. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: Mille diables ! C’est bien un navire !
  - white: “No doubt of it, she’s a ship!”

### 3. cut: the gravest event since landing (III.1 ¶9; White ¶7)
- White drops the narrator's judgement that this was the gravest incident since their arrival. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le plus grave qui se fût produit depuis leur arrivée sur l’île Lincoln
  - white: moved alternately by fear and hope

### 4. addition: "an American vessel" (III.1 ¶10; White ¶7)
- The French hopes the ship may be "a piece of the fatherland"; White says "perhaps it was an American vessel", which the French does not say. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: c’était peut-être un morceau de la patrie qui venait à leur rencontre
  - white: perhaps it was an American vessel

### 5. condensed: the routes the ship is off (III.1 ¶12; White ¶8)
- The French names the routes of the long-voyage ships to the Polynesian archipelagos, New Zealand and the American coast; White has "out of the usual track". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: les longs courriers des archipels polynésiens, de la Nouvelle-Zélande et de la côte américaine
  - white: out of the usual track

### 6. changed: Smith's fear of any ship but the Duncan (III.1 ¶24; White ¶15)
- The French has Smith say that any other ship would seem suspect and that he always fears a visit from Malay pirates; White's "dangerous seas for Malay pirates" says the seas are dangerous for the pirates, not that they are ill-frequented. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: car tout autre navire me semblerait suspect
  - white: These are dangerous seas for Malay pirates.

### 7. softened: Pencroff's oath on guarding the island (III.1 ¶35; White ¶27)
- "Mille diables" is dropped. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: Mille diables ! s’écria le marin, j’y resterai plutôt tout seul
  - white: “I would sooner stay here alone to guard it,” cried the sailor

### 8. cut: whether the repentant man feels pardoned (III.1 ¶45; White ¶34)
- White keeps the question of the twelve years' expiation and drops the second, whether the repentant man still did not feel pardoned in his own eyes or others'. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Le coupable repentant ne se sentait-il pas encore pardonné, soit à ses propres yeux, soit aux yeux des autres ?
  - white: Did not twelve years’ abandonment on a desert island seem to him a sufficient expiation?

### 9. changed: Smith dreads the ship; White has him believe it an enemy (III.1 ¶55; White ¶43)
- The French says Smith, at bottom, feared the ship's coming more than he wished it; White says he was inclined to believe her an enemy. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: il redoutait plutôt qu’il ne désirait l’arrivée de ce navire
  - white: was inclined to believe the ship rather an enemy than a friend

### 10. cut: the ship is no prao, so Smith's fears may be unfounded (III.1 ¶56; White ¶43)
- White gives that she is a brig and no Malay proa, and drops the inference that the engineer's fears might not be borne out, and that Ayrton confirmed the rig. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Il était donc permis de croire que les appréhensions de l’ingénieur ne se justifieraient pas
  - white: By the help of the glass they could make out that she was a brig, and not one of those Malay proas, used by the pirates of the Pacific.

### 11. cut: she will vanish behind Claw Cape, and it is five o'clock (III.1 ¶57; White —)
- White has nothing for the narrator's note that on her heading the ship would soon be hidden behind Claw Cape, that watching her would mean going to the heights of Washington Bay, and that it was already five and the dusk near. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il était déjà cinq heures du soir
  - white: —

### 12. cut: the plan to light a fire at Port Balloon (III.1 ¶58–¶62; White —)
- White has nothing for Spilett's question whether to light a fire to signal their presence, the decision that it be done, and the plan that Neb and Pencroff go to Balloon Harbor to light it, broken off when the ship changed her course. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Il fut donc décidé que Nab et Pencroff se rendraient à Port-Ballon
  - white: —

### 13. cut: a capital of Pacific piracy (III.1 ¶76; White ¶55)
- White drops the colonists' questions: a pirate ship rivalling the Malay praos, the island as a fence for stolen cargoes, a winter refuge, a capital of Pacific piracy. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: sorte de capitale de la piraterie du Pacifique
  - white: A dozen ideas rushed across the minds of the colonists

### 14. cut: the pirates might explain the island's unexplained events (III.1 ¶94; White ¶65)
- White drops that a past descent by the pirates would explain certain particulars that had been inexplicable until then, and that Smith saw the black flag as a direct threat to their work. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ce qui aurait expliqué certaines particularités restées inexplicables jusqu’alors
  - white: Might they not have effected a descent once before, and might not some accomplice be now concealed in the unexplored part of the island.

### 15. cut: Smith's judgement that the colony is gravely compromised (III.1 ¶95; White ¶65)
- White drops that Smith could answer none of his questions and felt the brig's arrival must gravely compromise the colony. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: la situation de la colonie ne pouvait être que très-gravement compromise par l’arrivée de ce brick
  - white: Smith was profoundly anxious.
