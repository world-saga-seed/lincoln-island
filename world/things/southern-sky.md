# The southern sky seen from the crater: the stars Smith and Harbert saw on 29 March

*The stars that came into the circle of the crater as it widened: Antares at the zenith, beta of the Centaur, Fomalhaut, the Southern Triangle and the Southern Cross.*

Keywords: étoiles, constellations, ciel austral, Antarès, Fomalhaut, Triangle austral, Centaure, southern stars, latitude

- **What it is.**
  - As the crater widened above them, new stars entered the field of vision, the splendid constellations of the austral sky shining (I.10 ¶55). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: Les magnifiques constellations de ce ciel austral resplendissaient
  - At the zenith shone the splendid Antares of the Scorpion (I.10 ¶55). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: Au zénith, brillaient d’un pur éclat la splendide Antarès du Scorpion
  - Near it shone the beta of the Centaur, thought to be the star nearest the earth (I.10 ¶55). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: cette β du Centaure que l’on croit être l’étoile la plus rapprochée du globe terrestre
  - Then came Fomalhaut of the Fish and the Southern Triangle, and, almost at the antarctic pole, the Southern Cross (see [the Southern Cross](southern-cross.md)) (I.10 ¶55). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: Fomalhaut du Poisson, le Triangle austral
- **Appears in:** I.10
- **Open:**
  - Antares "at the zenith" is given without a latitude and is not checked in these chapters against the latitude Smith finds in I.14 ¶25.
    - quote: Au zénith, brillaient d’un pur éclat la splendide Antarès du Scorpion
