# The Tabor landing: the sand beach on Tabor’s east coast where the Bonadventure lies

*The sand beach, behind reefs and capricious passes, where the Bonadventure grounds her stem on Tabor and lies at anchor for two days.*

Keywords: grève, beach, mouillage, anchorage, côte orientale, east coast, récifs, reefs, passes, marée, tide, Tabor

- **What it is.**
  - At about noon the Bonadventure’s stem strikes a beach of sand; the anchor is thrown, the sails lowered and the crew lands (II.13 ¶50). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: vint heurter de son étrave une grève de sable
  - The boat is moored fast so that the ebb of the sea cannot carry her off (II.13 ¶52). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: afin que le reflux de la mer ne pût l’emporter
  - A cone of two hundred and fifty to three hundred feet rises half a mile from it (see [the Tabor cone](tabor-cone.md)) (II.13 ¶52). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: un espèce de cône, haut de deux cent cinquante à trois cents pieds, qui s’élevait à un demi-mille
  - The reefs leave passes between them, capricious ones, through which Pencroff feels his way in (II.13 ¶49). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: des passes assez capricieuses que les récifs laissaient entre eux
  - It lies on the east coast, where the boat is held by her anchor biting deep in the sand (II.14 ¶29). *(My reading, to confirm.)*
    - quote: la côte orientale de l’île et le Bonadventure, maintenu par son ancre, qui mordait profondément le sable
  - The boat can leave only when the next morning’s tide makes itself felt (II.14 ¶80). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: dès que la marée du lendemain matin se ferait sentir
- **Appears in:** II.13, II.14
