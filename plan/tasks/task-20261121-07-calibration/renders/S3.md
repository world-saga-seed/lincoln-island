# S3: I.17 ¶49

¶49

These various labours lasted about eight days. They were therefore finished before the transformation of the sulphide into sulphate of iron had been accomplished. During the days which followed, the colonists had time to make some refractory pottery of plastic clay, and to build a brick furnace of a particular arrangement, which was to serve for the distillation of the sulphate of iron when that should be obtained. All this was finished towards the 18th of May, at about the moment when the chemical transformation was coming to an end. Gédéon Spilett, Harbert, Nab, and Pencroff, skilfully guided by the engineer, had become the most adroit workmen in the world. Necessity, besides, is of all masters the one who is most listened to, and who teaches the best.
