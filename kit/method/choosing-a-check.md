# Choosing a check: when to spend more

The three critics (logistics and physics, the causal chain, the character invariant) check the outlines before drafting ([the actor-log workflow](../../plan/workflows/actor-log.md#the-light-pass-basic)). They run at three settings. Quick is the default, because it is the cheapest per fault found. Claude recommends a higher setting at these moments, and you decide.

## The trade

A critic's run is a sample: two runs of the same setting find different faults. A higher setting costs more tokens but not much more of your time, because every run is merged into one interview before you read it. So the question is never "how much of my time", but "how many tokens is a missed fault worth here?"

| Setting | Tokens per chapter | Found, in the kit's test |
|---|---|---|
| Quick | about 45k | 60 to 73% |
| Thorough | about 90k | 80% |
| Deep | about 300k | different faults; with Thorough, 93% |

## Quick: the default

- Routine outline edits: a few beats moved, a line settled.
- Outlines that will still change. A deep check on an outline that will be rewritten next week is spent twice.
- The first pass on a new horizon, to clear the plain faults before anything costlier runs.

## Thorough: before a commitment

Recommend it when a missed fault would be expensive to unpick:

- **Just before drafting a horizon.** After this, a fault is in the prose, and every draft built on it must be redone.
- **After a restructure** or any bulk change to the outlines: chapters moved, a character's timeline changed, an arc re-cut.
- **When the offstage cast is busy:** several people working out of the narrator's sight in the same stretch.
- **When a draft came back with continuity slips** the outline should have caught.

## Deep: where a slip costs the book

Recommend it, aimed at the chapters that need it, not the whole horizon:

- **Set pieces:** the scenes the book is built around.
- **Craft and trade scenes,** where someone works a skill on the page. The deep chain was the only setting to catch a character doing what he had not learned (know-how).
- **Clue chapters** in a mystery or a puzzle box, where a slip gives away or breaks a reveal.
- **The last pass before release** of a volume.

## What no setting catches

A cause that was never written, a beat with nothing to start it, escaped every setting in the test. Your answer to the interview's question (why did they do it? what moved them?) is the check for it, and it is also where the story's choices are yours.
