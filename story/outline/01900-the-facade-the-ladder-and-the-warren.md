### 2. The plan, and why a door and a rope ladder (I.19 ¶4–¶12)

  - quote: cinq fenêtres et d’une porte percées sur la façade
  - quote: l’ancien déversoir offrait un escalier naturel
- Cyrus says what is easy for them will be easy for others; he means to stop the outlet at its mouth, seal it tight, and if need be hide the entrance by raising the lake with a dam. They will enter by an outside rope ladder which, drawn up, makes access impossible. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: obstruer ce déversoir à son orifice, le boucher hermétiquement

- Pencroff asks why so many precautions: the animals have not seemed very formidable, and the island has no natives. Cyrus asks him whether he is quite sure. Pencroff answers they will be sure only when the island is explored throughout. *(Drafted.)*

### 3. The facade opened with nitro-glycerine (I.19 ¶13–¶14)

- The facade will have five windows and a door for the "apartment", and a large bay and bull's-eye windows for the great hall. It is eighty feet up, faces east and takes the first rays; it lies on the stretch of wall between the salient at the Mercy's mouth and a line above the heap of rocks forming the Chimneys. The bad north-east winds hit it only aslant. Until sashes are made, thick shutters will close the openings. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: une large baie et d’œils-de-bœuf
  - quote: était exposée à l’est, et le soleil levant la saluait de ses premiers rayons
  - quote: ceux du nord-est, ne la frappaient que d’écharpe
  - quote: des volets épais
- Cyrus uses the remaining nitro-glycerine to open the places, localised in effect; pick and pickaxe then finish the pointed outline of the five windows, bay, bull's-eyes and door. A few days after the work began, Granite-house is lit by the morning light. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Il avait encore une certaine quantité de nitro-glycérine à sa disposition

### 4. The interior plan (I.19 ¶15–¶17)

- The apartment is to have five compartments looking on the sea: on the right an entrance with the door where the ladder ends, then a first kitchen-room thirty feet wide, a dining room forty feet, a dormitory of equal width, and a "spare room" Pencroff asked for, next to the great hall. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: une première chambre-cuisine, large de trente pieds
  - quote: une « chambre d’amis », réclamée par Pencroff
- The rooms do not take the cavity's whole depth: a corridor runs between them and a long store for utensils, provisions and reserves, protected from damp. The small grotto above the great cavern is to be the attic. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: un long magasin
  - quote: comme le grenier de la nouvelle demeure
- With the plan fixed, "the miners became brickmakers again" and bricks are brought to the foot of Granite-house. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Les mineurs redevinrent donc briquetiers

### 5. The rope ladder and the pulley; the rooms built (I.19 ¶18–¶20)

- Until now they reached the cavern only by the old outlet: up to the Grande-Vue plateau by a detour along the river bank, down two hundred feet by the passage, and up as much to return. Cyrus resolves to make a solid rope ladder. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: à descendre deux cents pieds par le couloir
  - quote: une solide échelle de corde
- The uprights are of the "curry-rush" fibres twisted by a winch, strong as a thick cable; the rungs are of a red cedar with light, resistant branches; Pencroff works it "like a master". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: formés des fibres du « curry-jonc » tressées au moyen d’un moulinet

- Other plant-fibre ropes are made and a rough pulley is fixed at the door, so bricks can be hoisted to Granite-house's level. Lime is not lacking and some thousands of bricks are ready; the partition frames, very rudimentary, go up easily and in a very short time the apartment is divided into rooms and a store. ### 6. The workers; Pencroff's dreams; Cyrus's worry (I.19 ¶21–¶27)

### 8. The hunt; the aromatic warren and herbs (I.19 ¶30–¶34)

- During these works food is not neglected: every day the reporter and Harbert, now "the colony's purveyors", hunt a few hours. They work only the Jacamar Wood, left of the river, since without bridge or boat the Mercy has not been crossed; the great forests called the Far West are unexplored and kept for the first fine days of next spring. Kangaroos and wild boar abound. *(Drafted.)*

- Harbert finds, toward the south-west angle of the "lagoon", a natural warren, a slightly damp meadow covered with willows and aromatic herbs (thyme, wild thyme, basil, savory), loved by rabbits. *(Drafted.)*

### 10. 31 May: partitions done; the kitchen chimney (I.19 ¶43–¶43)

- On 31 May the partitions are finished; only furnishing remains, work for the long winter days. A chimney is built in the first room, the kitchen. It may smoke in strong east winds, which are rare, and Nab the cook is not particular. *(Drafted.)*

## The seam with 01950 (ours, not Verne's)

*No Verne beat above changes. These are the lines the inserted chapter, [01950](01950-the-float-in-the-bar.md), leans on. Its beats begin on 1 June, the morning after this chapter's 31 May.*

- The house ends with its windows shuttered for want of glass: "du verre à vitre" (¶46). The float the insert finds is glass. *(Proposal.)*
- Cyrus keeps his fear of never being rescued to himself, "en dehors du passage des navires, il pouvait craindre de n’être jamais secouru" (¶22). The first secret kept in the house is his. Pencroff's in 01950 is the second. *(Proposal.)*
- "l’homme qui « sait » réussit" (¶24). In 01950, knowing is what costs them the time they needed. *(Proposal.)*
- The view from the windows takes in "Toute la baie de l’Union" (¶48). Pencroff's sighting in 01950 is made on the beach, not from these windows (R1121-54). *(Proposal.)*
