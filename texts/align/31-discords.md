# II.9: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller j. Beads corrected: none. (The two inset coordinate lines after ¶81 and ¶82, ln4 and ln5 in `texts/fr-1875/31.md`, are not in the side file; I read them there.)*

### 1. cut: shelves, cupboards and the tools answering the thunder (II.9 ¶6; White ¶3)
- The French says the shelves and cupboards left nothing to be desired and that the grinding of tools and the lathe answered the thunder; White has only the rack for arms. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ni les étagères, ni les armoires ne laissaient à désirer
  - white: They also made a rack for the arms, which were kept with the utmost care.

### 2. cut: Jup's room beside the general storeroom (II.9 ¶7; White ¶3)
- Jup's cabin is near the general storeroom in the French; White gives no place. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: près du magasin général
  - white: he occupied a chamber apart

### 3. changed: "soon my equal" becomes "almost my equal" (II.9 ¶9; White ¶5)
- Nab says Jup is his pupil and soon to be his equal; White says Jup is almost his equal now. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Mon élève, répondait Nab, et bientôt mon égal !
  - white: “He is my pupil and almost my equal!”

### 4. condensed: who teaches Harbert what (II.9 ¶13; White ¶8)
- Cyrus teaches Harbert the sciences and Spilett the languages; White says only that the engineer and the reporter are masters pleased to teach him. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dans l’ingénieur pour les sciences, dans le reporter pour les langues
  - white: he found in the engineer and the reporter masters pleased to teach him

### 5. cut: teaching by example (II.9 ¶14; White ¶8)
- Cyrus means to teach by example as much as by word; White has only that he desired to teach the lad all he knew. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: de l’instruire par l’exemple autant que par la parole
  - white: It was the engineer’s desire to teach the lad all he himself knew.

### 6. changed: "three or four" fine days become "two or three" (II.9 ¶16; White ¶10)
- The number of fine days after the storm is lowered by one, and the excursions they allowed are dropped. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: sauf trois ou quatre belles journées qui favorisèrent des excursions de toutes sortes
  - white: with the exception of two or three fine days

### 7. cut: the foal is a female (II.9 ¶17; White ¶11)
- The French says the female onagga's young is of its mother's sex; White says only that a little onager was born. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: un petit qui appartenait au même sexe que sa mère
  - white: a little onager was born

### 8. cut: the peccary pen's success and fattening (II.9 ¶18; White ¶12)
- The French says the trial succeeded fully and that the young were being "civilised", that is fattened, by Nab; White omits both. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: essai qui réussit pleinement
  - white: The domestication of piccaries was also attempted

### 9. changed: Jup pulls the peccaries' tails; White has him cut them off (II.9 ¶18; White ¶12)
- Jup pulls the young peccaries' tails in mischief; White has him cut them off, which is cruelty of another order. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: leur tirer la queue
  - white: He did, indeed, cut off their tails

### 10. changed: the paddle cylinder is "below" the fall; White puts it above (II.9 ¶31; White ¶24)
- The French places the paddle cylinder under the fall, joined outside to a wheel wound with the cable; White puts a paddle-wheel above the fall with the rope round its axle. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Au-dessous de cette chute, l’ingénieur installa un cylindre à palettes
  - white: Above this fall the engineer placed a paddle-wheel

### 11. cut: the overflow runs down the interior well (II.9 ¶31; White ¶24)
- The French says the fall's overflow runs off by the interior well; White has no overflow. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dont le trop-plein se déversa par le puits intérieur
  - white: —

### 12. cut: engaging and disengaging the motor (II.9 ¶31; White ¶24)
- The long cord lets the motor be engaged or disengaged; White says the basket can be raised or lowered by the motor. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: permettait d’embrayer ou de désembrayer le moteur hydraulique
  - white: they could raise or lower the basket by means of the hydraulic motor

### 13. condensed: what the lift hoists, and the old ladder (II.9 ¶32; White ¶25)
- The French lists wood, coal, provisions and the colonists themselves, and says nobody regretted the ladder; White says only that everything was hoisted. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: tous les fardeaux, bois, charbons, provisions et colons eux-mêmes furent hissés
  - white: everything was hoisted into Granite House by its means

### 14. condensed: where each glass ingredient comes from (II.9 ¶34; White ¶26)
- The French says the shore gives sand, lime the chalk, sea plants the soda, pyrites the sulphuric acid and the soil the coal for the kiln; White says the engineer had the substances at hand. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le rivage fournissait le sable, la chaux fournissait la craie, les plantes marines fournissaient la soude, les pyrites fournissaient l’acide sulfurique, et le sol fournissait la houille
  - white: the engineer had at hand

### 15. cut: how Pencroff makes the blowpipe (II.9 ¶35; White ¶26)
- Pencroff makes the iron blowpipe from a long thin iron band rolled like a gun barrel; White says only that he succeeded in making it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: une bande de fer, longue et mince, qui fut roulée comme un canon de fusil
  - white: This Pencroff, however, succeeded in making

### 16. condensed: refractory crucibles (II.9 ¶36; White ¶27)
- The crucibles are of refractory clay; White says only earthen vessels. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dans les creusets en terre réfractaire
  - white: placed in earthen vessels

### 17. cut: the left bank of the Mercy (II.9 ¶44; White ¶35)
- The French places the hunt in the Far-West forest on the left of the Mercy. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: sur la gauche de la Mercy
  - white: went into the forests of the Far West

### 18. condensed: the game that passes (II.9 ¶44; White ¶35)
- The French names the kangaroos, capybaras and agoutis that pass within range and escape Harbert's gun; White says only "the game did not suffer". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: bien des kangourous, des cabiais ou des agoutis passèrent à bonne portée
  - white: the game did not suffer

### 19. changed: "nearly bread" becomes "the next thing to do" (II.9 ¶62; White ¶52)
- The French says the cycas flour is not yet real wheat bread but almost touches it; White says only that real bread was the next thing to do. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: Ce n’était pas encore le vrai pain de froment, mais on y touchait presque.
  - white: They had not yet real wheaten bread, but it was the next thing to do.

### 20. changed: fortune "fixed" and safe from reverses becomes the summit of desires (II.9 ¶66; White ¶54)
- The French asks who can flatter himself that he has fixed fortune and is safe from its reverses, a foreboding; White asks who has reached the summit of his desires. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: Mais qui pourrait se flatter d’avoir jamais fixé la fortune et d’être à l’abri de ses revers !
  - white: But who is able to flatter himself that he has attained his fortune and reached the summit of his desires?

### 21. cut: the sextant "found in the chest" (II.9 ¶70; White ¶57)
- Spilett's question points at the sextant found in the chest; White drops where it was found. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: depuis que vous possédez ce sextant trouvé dans la caisse
  - white: since you have had the sextant

### 22. cut: the new sextant is the better instrument (II.9 ¶72; White ¶59)
- Spilett's reason for checking is that the sextant is more perfect than the instrument Cyrus used; White gives no reason. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: avec cet instrument qui est plus parfait que celui que vous avez employé
  - white: “Well, wouldn’t it be well enough to do so?”.

### 23. changed: the first observation's latitude (II.9 ¶81 ln4; White ¶66)
- The first observation's latitude is 30° to 35° south in the French; White gives "between 36° and 35°". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: En latitude sud : de 30° à 35°.
  - white: latitude 36° and 35° south

### 24. cut: Tabor's offset from Lincoln (II.9 ¶91; White ¶72)
- The French says Tabor lies two and a half degrees farther west and two degrees farther south than Lincoln Island; White gives only Tabor's position. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: c’est-à-dire à deux degrés et demi plus à l’ouest et deux degrés plus au sud que l’île Lincoln
  - white: “This island is situated in 153° of longitude and 37° 11’ of latitude.”
