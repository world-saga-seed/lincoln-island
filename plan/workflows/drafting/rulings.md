# The rulings of the page

*Basic.*

*The house style of* Lincoln Island*: the one file every drafter, critic, reviser and trimmer reads, and the rules the critics enforce. Each line cites its ledger ID. A new ruling goes here once, not into every prompt. The numbers are White's own, measured on his Part I (`plan/archive/task-20261121-10-rulings.md`).*

*The notes are the facts; this file only collects what drafters keep getting wrong. Keep it short enough to read whole. When it passes about two pages, move the detail to the voice sheet or a card and leave a one-line pointer.*

## The page

*The narration: who, what tense, what distance. These are the lines a drafter breaks most often.*

- **Verne's narrator, third person, past tense.** In the neighbours, as the French has it. In 01950 he stays with Pencroff and Herbert, and gives a thought briefly, as Verne does ("pensa Harbert", II.1 ¶79). He never stays long inside a head. *(Settled R1121-90.)*
- **Foresight only where Verne has it.** In the neighbours, exactly where the French looks ahead (as I.4 ¶16, II.9 ¶67). In 01950, none: one glance forward and the reader starts looking for a hand. *(Settled R1121-91.)*
- **The future may shape what is planted, never what is said.** A chapter states only what the cards show on the page at or before it.
- **Never trim a clue.** No trim, cut or revision removes a line listed in `plan/reread-hooks.md` or in a chapter's clue notes. A line that looks like a clue and is listed nowhere is kept and reported.
- **The neighbours render the French whole.** Every sentence of the French, and nothing that is not in it. Do not add, explain, soften, sum up or leave out. Where Verne is technical, be technical as he is; where he gives a figure, give it as he does. *(Settled R1121-103.)*
- **Explanation as Verne explains.** The neighbours keep every explanation of process and science the French gives, at its length. 01950 explains only what a sailor or a naturalist on the spot would know (tides, net floats, barnacles). It never explains the float's origin, and nothing the register holds open (world rule 11). *(Settled R1121-92.)*
- **Spelling is White's American:** color, honor, labor, favor, harbor, neighbor, and "-ize" as he has it. The British "-our" forms are banned. See the [voice sheet](../../../story/voice/white.md). *(Settled R1121-21.)* 
- **Contractions:** none in narration. In speech, sparing, as White's (42 in all Part I), mostly Pencroff's. *(Settled R1121-93.)*
- **Names are White's forms on the page,** as [White's names](../../../story/voice/names.md) lists them (Herbert, Gideon Spilett, Neb, Granite House, Prospect Plateau); the cards keep the French. Never a name from memory of another translation. *(Settled R1121-20, R1121-107.)*
- **Words the page never uses:** run the banned list, `tools/tells.py FILE --banned story/voice/banned.txt`, and fix every hit in narration. *(Settled R1121-21.)*
- **Draft plain, do not polish.** No notes, marks or comments inside a prose file.

## The register

- **Plain, exact and even.** The narrator slows for work and process, and his figures and measures gather there. *(Settled R1121-95.)*
- **A stretch of nothing goes into a sentence:** "Pendant tout ce mois de juin, le temps fut employé à des travaux divers" (I.20 ¶2). *(Settled R1121-95.)*
- **Under stress, a one-line paragraph says the plain fact,** as "Il n’y avait plus d’échelle." (II.5 ¶120). *(Settled R1121-95.)*
- **In 01950:** close, exact detail at the float; short sentences in the squall; almost none at the smooth sand. *(Settled R1121-95.)*
- **Comparisons come from the speaker's world.** The narrator's come from science, geography and the sea. Pencroff's come from ships and the sea, Herbert's from natural history. Nothing modern, and nothing after 1875 (world rule 0). *(Settled R1121-99.)*

## Texture

*White's own numbers on his Part I (`tools/tells.py`). A drafter keeps near them. The voice critic flags a chapter well outside them in either direction. (Settled R1121-94.) In the neighbours, White's sentence breaks are his voice: where Verne's sentences run long, the render (and the revision) splits them toward White's lengths, as White splits them, adding and dropping nothing. (Settled R1121-108.)*

- "and" per sentence about 0.58; White's chapters range from 0.47 to 0.70.
- Median sentence about 18 words, with about a quarter of the sentences long.
- Semicolons are kept. White joins clauses with them, about 4.7 per 1,000 words.
- "as if" at most about 0.5 per 1,000 words. No "the way you … / the way a …" simile: White has none.
- Exclamations about 3 per 1,000 words, most of them in speech.
- None of the stock beats: "It was not X. It was Y."; a list of three abstract clauses; the quiet-realization paragraph; a summing-up tag line that says what the scene showed.
- No paragraph with three or more negations.

## Tells (phase 5)

*Ways a modern model's renders departed from the French, or from White, that White did not. Found by the phase 5 calibration (`plan/archive/task-20261121-07-calibration/tells.md`). The facts and voice critics check for them.*

- **T1, British spelling.** "honour", "labours" and "colours" turned up in three renders of twenty. White's whole text has none of the -our forms. Run the banned list on every draft. *(Settled R1121-85.)*
- **T2 to T6, small words added for idiom.** A word added to make the English plain ("like the bore of a river" for "comme des mascarets"), an intensifier ("whatever" after "sous aucun prétexte"), a filler for idiom ("no longer held together" for "ne tenaient plus"), a verb strengthened ("wrenched away" for "séparée"), a word of speech dropped ("That is not just!" for "Ce n’est pas juste, cela !"). Each is small. Together they make a habit: render what the French says, at its force, without adding words. Where the English needs a word, take the one White would use, and note it for the critic. *(Settled R1121-85.)*
- **T7, a name from another translation.** "Prospect Heights" for White's "Prospect Plateau" in 01900 draft 1: a model's memory of later English versions. Check every name against [White's names](../../../story/voice/names.md). *(Settled R1121-107.)*

## Length

- **Chapter length is a target, never a cap.** The range in a chapter's notes says how big the scene was meant to be and flags drift (a chapter swelling from retelling, not new matter). A draft well over target is reported to the writer with the reason, never cut to fit.

## Things and places

*The setting's defaults a drafter carries in from elsewhere and gets wrong. One line each, with the card that holds the fact.*

- **Check the defaults.** Before drafting, read [what is not true here](../../../world/false-assumptions.md), June 1865: no glass, no candles before 5 June, two knives, no boat, no firearms. *(Settled R1121-98.)*
- **Before a character invents, ask what the locals already do.** If a trade already has a rule of thumb that does it, the character starts from that habit.
- **Report the setup, not the design.** When someone builds something, the narrator sees what is where and what hands do, never what each part is for (unless they would know).
- **Do not invent staging for a device** beyond what the notes give: show what it does, not how it is fed.
- **Name which one.** Where the page has several kinds of a thing (several boats, several keys), each gets a name the page already uses; nothing new is coined.

## People

- **Give each person only their own habits.** Read the Habits section of each sheet and card for everyone in the chapter. A habit that is not on any sheet is a drafter's habit: log it as an invention.
- **Forms of address, as White prints them:** "Mr. Smith" and "Mr. Spilett". Pencroff calls Herbert "my boy", and Neb calls Smith "master". The narrator uses "the engineer", "the sailor", "the reporter", "the lad". Never "Captain Smith". *(Settled R1121-96.)*
- **Oaths no stronger than White's:** "heavens", "confound", "devil". *(Settled R1121-97.)*
- **Ages:** Herbert is fifteen (I.2 ¶24). There are no women on the island. Nothing in the three chapters touches sex or romance (CLAUDE.md rule 16). *(Settled R1121-97.)*

## Places and law

- **The world's rules govern:** [world/rules.md](../../../world/rules.md), checked against every beat.
- **The world's own causes.** Derive a crime, a custom, a price from the setting's rules, never from real history unless the causes carry over.

*When a critic finds the same fault in two batches, the rule it breaks belongs here.*
