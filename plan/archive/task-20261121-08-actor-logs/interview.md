## Ruled (the author, 2026-11-21)

All eleven are answered: 1 to 7, 9 and 10 as recommended (R1121-69 to -75, -77, -78); 8 the surprise (R1121-76); 11 shame, no pact (R1121-79). The float's end: they see it break, and no one finds a piece (R1121-68). Nothing is left open.

## The items as put

1. **Sight lines (L1, C5).** The bar lies in full view of Granite-house's windows. Without glass or candles, the shutters stand open for light: "Toute la baie de l’Union se développait magnifiquement devant eux" (I.19 ¶48); "Nab’s kitchen has a window from which he can watch the shore (II.10 ¶47)" (the card).
   - Plain fix: 2 June dawns in rain ahead of the squall. The shutters are shut against it from first light, with the fire for light, and all three are at indoor work. One household row says so.
   - Other fixes: the three are out hunting inland that morning (I.20 ¶2 has hunting through June); or the bar lies out of the windows' line, round the salient at the Mercy's mouth.
   - **Recommendation:** the plain fix. Pencroff, reading the sky, goes out in the rain, as a sailor would; on 1 June he goes alone to his lines as every day.
2. **The shelter (L2, C6).** The Chimneys are where Verne fears the tides breaking in, and Cyrus guards against them: "il était à craindre que les grandes marées, poussées par les vents du large, n’y fissent encore irruption" (I.20 ¶1).
   - Plain fix: they shelter under the granite wall, out of the surge, where they can still see the float in the surf (R1121-23).
   - **Recommendation:** under the wall, near the foot of the ladder. The Chimneys pick is withdrawn.
3. **The hours (L3).** The 2 June low water is mid-morning, the next is after dark, and sunset is near five.
   - Plain fix: home by early afternoon, once the flood has covered the bar and the squall eases.
   - **Recommendation:** the plain fix. 3 June's low water is again mid-morning, about fifty minutes later.
4. **The knife and the lashing (L6, C3).** In June 1865 there are two knives, the halves of Top's collar ("Voilà deux couteaux, Pencroff !", I.13 ¶26), and a knife could cut the float free.
   - Plain fix: Pencroff has his knife, one of the two. The float's lashing runs under it into the sand, on the hidden side. One clause.
   - **Recommendation:** the plain fix.
5. **The ford (L7).** The channel keeps a gut at low water: on 25 March, near a spring tide, "dont la profondeur ne dépassait pas cinq pieds. Harbert … nageait comme un poisson" (I.3 ¶60). At a neap the gut is deeper still.
   - Plain fix: the bar lies on the coast side of the gut, reached over the bared sands, and "ford" goes from 2 June. The 5 June ford of the five (I.20 ¶20) still crosses over it.
   - **Recommendation:** the plain fix.
6. **Size and range (L8).** The channel is half a mile wide.
   - Plain fix: the float is about a foot across, and the bar lies a cable or two (two to four hundred yards) off the beach.
   - **Recommendation:** the plain fix. The size is also a gap-pass item.
7. **Top (L9).** A dog on the bar would draw the house's eye.
   - **Recommendation:** Top stays in Granite-house with Cyrus. He is Cyrus's dog ("He is Cyrus Smith's dog", the card).
8. **Why they still keep the secret on the evening of 2 June (C1).** The settled reason, "so as not to raise hopes", is spent once the reading dims the hope. Yet they neither tell nor take the picks.
   - **Your answer is wanted.** My pick: Pencroff means to bring it home himself at the next low water and lay it on the table, a surprise. That echoes Harbert at the turtle: "Harbert, voulant faire une surprise à Pencroff, ne lui dit rien" (II.1). So the pride that keeps the secret also loses it.
9. **"Kills their hope" against K8 (C2).** K8, whether a ship is near, is unknown in the stretch (R1121-61).
   - Plain fix: "the reading dims their hope". It leaves the reader unsure, and it leaves some hope alive, which bears on item 8.
   - **Recommendation:** the plain fix.
10. **The lines on the way (C4, L15).** "It is no lie" and the joke hold only if they lift the lines.
    - Plain fix: half a line. They lift the lines on the way out, and the lines are empty.
    - **Recommendation:** the plain fix.
11. **The lifelong silence (C7).** No motive is given for the silence at the 5 June ford or at the chest, where Spilett asks for marks (II.2 ¶63). And Nemo "pénétra tous les secrets de leur existence" (III.16 ¶64), so "never indoors" must hold from June 1865 to III.16.
    - **Your answer is wanted.** My pick: shame at the hour lost. Nothing to show, and a story that tells against them, so they never speak of it. Not a pact, just two men who don't bring it up. The log gets a standing row: never indoors, through III.16, and the other three never learn. The page stays silent (R1121-24).

## Checked: that nothing has to turn up again (R1121-65)

- **The net.** The bar closes over it. Three later events reach this channel:
  - 5 June, the five ford it (I.20 ¶20). Nothing is seen.
  - October 1867, the Speedy blows up "dans le canal" before Granite-house. Its salvage is worked at low water for three days ("À mer basse", III.4 ¶75), and "Ses débris avaient été dispersés" (¶80). Pencroff holds the bed to be "formé d’un lit de sable fin" with no rock (¶44).
  - March 1869, the island's end.
  - Fix: the net lies off the Mercy's mouth, apart from where the brig went down before Granite-house. By 1867 it is rotted to shreds under the sand, nothing a man would know for a net. A log row, *(Proposal.)*. One risk stays: the insert's own logic has storms uncover the bar, so a later storm could. What would show is rotten cord among the wrack, with no float and no mark.
- **The float.** Settled by R1121-66 and R1121-67: the float shatters on the islet's rocks, and no one finds a piece. The shards lie below the low-water line at the islet's point, away from the seal beaches of 5 June. Nothing marked survives.
