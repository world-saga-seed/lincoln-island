### 1. Easter morning (I.14 ¶1–¶3)

### 3. The latitude (I.14 ¶25–¶26)

- Cyrus takes up the instrument of the day before. He measures the opening on a circle divided into three hundred and sixty parts; adding the twenty-seven degrees between alpha and the Antarctic pole and reducing to sea level, the angle is fifty-three degrees. Taken from ninety, this leaves thirty-seven: the island lies on the thirty-seventh parallel of south latitude, or, allowing a five-degree error for the crudeness of his operations, between the thirty-fifth and the fortieth parallel. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: se trouva être de cinquante-trois degrés
  - quote: sur le trente-septième degré de latitude australe
  - quote: entre le trente-cinquième et le quarantième parallèle
- The longitude remains, which he will try that very day at noon, when the sun crosses the meridian. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: C’est ce que l’ingénieur tenterait de déterminer le jour même, à midi

### 6. How far from any land (I.14 ¶54–¶63)

- Cyrus says that they may leave sooner than they think if the island is at a moderate distance from an inhabited archipelago or continent; in an hour they will know. He has no Pacific chart but a clear memory of its southern part: the latitude puts the island across from New Zealand to the west and the Chilean coast to the east, which are at least six thousand miles apart. The longitude will show where on that expanse. *(Drafted.)*

### 8. The result: the island is far from everything (I.14 ¶76–¶80)

- Cyrus announces the result and, allowing for errors as with the latitude, says the island lies between the thirty-fifth and thirty-seventh parallels and between the hundred and fiftieth and hundred and fifty-fifth meridians west of Greenwich. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: entre le trente-cinquième et le trente-septième parallèle, et entre le cent cinquantième et le cent cinquante-cinquième méridien
- The possible error is five degrees each way, which at sixty miles a degree may be three hundred miles in latitude or longitude. It does not change what to do: the island is so far from any land or archipelago that no one could venture across it in a frail boat. *(Drafted.)*
