# The well: the sheer shaft at the end of the great cavern, ninety feet to the sea

*A perpendicular well in the granite at the end of the cavern, by which the lake’s water once left the mass; ninety feet deep to the sea level, silent.*

Keywords: puits, shaft, abyss, gouffre, conduit, underground, sea level, water, silence, issue, exit, cavern

- **What it is.**
  - At the end of the cavern the mouth of a true well opens in the granite; it was by there that the water once engaged in the mass left it (I.18 ¶47). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: l’orifice d’un véritable puits qui s’ouvrait dans le granit
    - quote: C’était bien par là que s’opérait la sortie des eaux
  - It is a perpendicular well, not an oblique and practicable passage, into which it would be impossible to venture (I.18 ¶47). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: un puits perpendiculaire, dans lequel il eût été impossible de s’aventurer
  - A resinous branch thrown in goes out with a slight shudder on reaching the layer of water, which is the level of the sea (I.18 ¶48). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: c’est-à-dire le niveau de la mer
  - Smith reckons its depth from the time of the fall at about ninety feet, so that the cavern floor lies ninety feet above the sea (I.18 ¶49, ¶50). *(Drafted.)*

  - Smith says that whatever being, amphibian or other, occupied the cavern fled by this exit (I.18 ¶53). *(Drafted.)*

  - No sound rises from it, not even that of the water, which the swell should sometimes stir in those depths (I.18 ¶74). *(Drafted.)*
