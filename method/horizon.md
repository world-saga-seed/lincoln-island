# Horizons: outline to a line, render it, read it, then go on

## Outlines before prose

An outline is cheap to change. Prose is not. A fact settled in an outline can change three times in an evening with no cost; the same fact in a drafted chapter means a redraft. Worse, a drafted scene paints the story into a corner: later outlines bend to fit what was drafted instead of what the story needs.

So the story is worked out in outlines first, and progress is measured by outlines, not by drafted words. Drafting starts when the writer says so.

## Staged horizons

Outlining the whole book before any prose has its own risk. A shift found in drafting (one stretch of the story turns out to take a year, not a season) can break every outline after it. So the work moves in stages:

1. **Outline to a horizon:** a volume, an arc, the first third of a novel.
2. **Render it:** draft and refine the chapters inside the horizon.
3. **Read it:** the writer reads it as a reader would, and comments.
4. **Let the story shift.** Fold the read back into the notes.
5. **Outline the next stretch.**

Outlines past the current horizon are provisional. Do not push outline passes beyond it.

## Working inside a horizon

A task on one volume works inside that volume's horizon:

- **It searches the horizon, not the whole tree.** Keep the horizon's file list in a plain text file (`plan/horizon-volume-1.txt`, one path or glob per line) and grep it: `grep -n WORD $(cat plan/horizon-volume-1.txt)`.
- **It writes only inside it.** A change that reaches past the horizon goes on a list for the writer, never into the file.
- **The horizon holds:** the volume's chapter outlines, notes and prose; the arc outline they sit in; and the cards, sheets and voice files, which span all of time.

## The future may shape what is planted, never what is said

Cards span the whole book. A chapter states only what the cards show on the page at or before that chapter. A secret that pays off in volume three can shape what is planted in volume one. It never appears there.
