# S9: III.4 ¶49

¶49

Forward, on both sides of the keel, seven or eight feet before the rise of the stem, the sides of the brig were frightfully torn over a length of at least twenty feet. There gaped two large leaks which it would have been impossible to stop. Not only had the copper sheathing and the planking disappeared, reduced to powder, no doubt, but of the very frames, of the iron bolts and the treenails which bound them, there remained no trace. All along the hull, as far as the after run, the strakes, torn to shreds, no longer held together. The false keel had been wrenched away with an inexplicable violence, and the keel itself, torn from the keelson at several points, was broken throughout its whole length.
