### 1. condensed: the explosive named (III.5 ¶1; White ¶1)
- The French says the cylinder held nitroglycerine, picrate or another explosive of the same kind; White names only nitro-glycerine. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: nitroglycérine, picrate ou autre matière de même nature
  - white: This cylinder, charged with nitro-glycerine

### 2. cut: the ironclad frigate and the impossible refloating (III.5 ¶1; White ¶1)
- The French says the torpedo would have destroyed an ironclad frigate as easily as a fishing boat, so the Speedy could not resist, and that this is why refloating was impossible; White gives the sinking and the shattered hull only. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: À une torpille qui eût détruit une frégate cuirassée aussi facilement qu’une simple barque de pêche, le Speedy n’avait pu résister !
  - white: —

### 3. changed: a castaway "abandoned" becomes one "inhabiting" (III.5 ¶3; White ¶2)
- Smith supposes the being perhaps a castaway like them, abandoned on the island; White says "inhabiting". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: d’un naufragé comme nous peut-être, abandonné sur notre île
  - white: perhaps a castaway like ourselves, inhabiting our island

### 4. cut: Spilett's list of questions (III.5 ¶4; White ¶3)
- Spilett asks whether the being speaks secretly to them by the well of Granite-house and so knows their plans, held out the bottle at the pirogue's first voyage, threw Top out of the lake and killed the dugong, and saved Smith; White gives only that he seems to do supernatural things. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Est-ce lui qui se met en communication secrète avec nous par le puits de Granite-house, et a-t-il ainsi connaissance de tous nos projets ?
  - white: —

### 5. condensed: the supernatural qualified (III.5 ¶4; White ¶3)
- The French has the being's means "tiendraient du surnaturel, si ... le surnaturel était acceptable"; White says flatly that his way of doing things "seems supernatural". *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: disposer de moyens d’action qui tiendraient du surnaturel, si dans les faits de la vie pratique le surnaturel était acceptable
  - white: this unknown friend has a way of doing things which seems supernatural

### 6. cut: the reporter's remark was just (III.5 ¶5; White —)

  - quote: L’observation du reporter était juste, et chacun le sentait bien.
  - white: —

### 7. condensed: means beyond humanity's (III.5 ¶6; White ¶4)
- Smith grants that the being has means of action outside those humanity commands, a further mystery; White has "there is a mystery here". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: je conviens qu’il a à sa disposition des moyens d’action en dehors de ceux dont l’humanité dispose

### 8. cut: Pencroff's esteem for the unknown (III.5 ¶7–¶8; White —)
- Pencroff says whoever he is he is a brave man with his esteem, and Smith answers that this is no answer; White has neither. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: c’est un brave homme, et il a mon estime !
  - white: —

### 9. cut: the portrait of God the Father (III.5 ¶14–¶16; White ¶10)
- Pencroff pictures the unknown fair, tall and strong, with a fine beard and hair like rays, lying on clouds with a great ball in his hand, and Spilett calls it a portrait of God the Father; White keeps only the eye Pencroff would give to see him. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: c’est le portrait de Dieu le père que vous nous faites là !
  - white: —

### 10. changed: the unknown benefactor "alone" and "to be renewed" (III.5 ¶20; White ¶14)
- Ayrton says the benefactor may be alone, may be suffering, and perhaps lives a life to be renewed; White says only that he may be sick or suffering. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Peut-être est-il seul ? Peut-être souffre-t-il ? Peut-être est-ce une existence à renouveler ?
  - white: He may be sick or suffering.

### 11. condensed: Ayrton's reasoning about Tabor (III.5 ¶20; White ¶14)
- The French has Ayrton reason that only the unknown can have come to Tabor Island, found the wretch, and let them know a man there needed saving, and that through him he became a man again; White says "he brought you to save me". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: C’est lui, ce ne peut être que lui qui soit venu à l’île Tabor, qui y ait trouvé le misérable que vous avez connu
  - white: for he brought you to save me

### 12. condensed: what the storerooms hold (III.5 ¶22; White ¶16)
- The French says Granite-house became a general warehouse holding stores, munitions, spare tools and utensils, the whole material of the colony; White says "a receptacle for all the goods". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: renfermant les approvisionnements, les munitions, les outils et ustensiles de rechange
  - white: Granite House thus became a receptacle for all the goods of the colony.

### 13. changed: forged steel becomes cast steel (III.5 ¶30; White ¶24)
- The French has the guns of "acier forgé" here, and "acier fondu" at ¶23; White gives cast steel in both places. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Faits en acier forgé, et se chargeant par la culasse
  - white: They were of cast steel, and breech-loaders

### 14. cut: the flat trajectory (III.5 ¶30; White ¶24)
- The French explains that the useful trajectory must be as flat as possible, which needs a very high muzzle velocity; White says only that the long range comes from the initial velocity. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: la trajectoire décrite par le boulet doit être aussi tendue que possible
  - white: —

### 15. changed: at least four miles becomes about four (III.5 ¶39; White ¶30)

  - quote: à une distance d’au moins quatre milles
  - white: struck the sand about four miles away

### 16. changed: forced "a little" becomes "heavily" (III.5 ¶40; White ¶30)
- Smith forces the charge a little to try the extreme range; White says it was charged heavily. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Cyrus Smith força un peu la charge
  - white: The fourth piece was charged heavily

### 17. condensed: all the pirates of the Pacific (III.5 ¶42; White ¶31)
- Pencroff's boast that all the Pacific pirates may come and none will land without the colonists' leave is reduced to wishing to see a pirate land. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Pas un n’y débarquera maintenant sans notre permission !
  - white: I should like to see a pirate land now!

### 18. changed: jaguars become wild beasts (III.5 ¶44; White ¶33)
- Pencroff calls the six "de vrais jaguars", and Ayrton at ¶46 says he was one of those jaguars; White has "wild beasts" in both places. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Ce sont de vrais jaguars, ces pirates-là
  - white: They are wild beasts, and I think we should treat them as such.

### 19. cut: Spilett honours Ayrton's reserve (III.5 ¶50; White —)

  - white: —

### 20. cut: the wild beasts to be destroyed without remorse (III.5 ¶59; White ¶43)
- The French says Pencroff's rough nature regarded the six as wild beasts to be destroyed without hesitation and without remorse, and that he had never thought his proposal would raise a doubt; White keeps only that he could not admit making terms. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il les regardait comme des bêtes fauves qu’il fallait détruire sans hésitation et sans remords
  - white: —

  - white: “Be it so!” he said.

### 22. condensed: the six would soon be masters of the colony (III.5 ¶62; White ¶46)

  - white: —

### 24. cut: Nab's appeal and Pencroff's "mad dog" (III.5 ¶65–¶66; White —)
- Nab says Pencroff would not shoot one of them at good range; Pencroff answers coldly that he would shoot him like a mad dog; White has neither. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Je tirerais sur lui comme sur un chien enragé, Nab, répondit froidement Pencroff.
  - white: —

### 25. changed: the pirates' amending becomes "coming to terms" (III.5 ¶70; White ¶52)
- The French says the island was large and fertile, that if any honesty remained in them the wretches might mend and make a new life, and that humanity bids them wait; White has them given a chance to "come to terms". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Si quelque sentiment d’honnêteté leur était resté au fond de l’âme, ces misérables pouvaient peut-être s’amender.
  - white: They would give the pirates a chance, which their own interest might induce them to seize upon, to come to terms.

### 26. cut: the loss of safe going and coming (III.5 ¶70; White ¶52)
- The French says the colonists might no longer go about without mistrust, having feared before only wild beasts, and that six convicts, perhaps of the worst kind, on their island would mean security lost for less brave people; White says only that they must be on guard and the situation is serious. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Jusqu’alors ils n’avaient eu à se garder que des fauves, et maintenant six convicts, peut-être de la pire espèce, rôdaient sur leur île.
  - white: But they would have to be constantly on their guard, and the situation was a very serious one.

### 27. changed: the colonists were right "for the present" (III.5 ¶71; White ¶52)

  - quote: Dans le présent, les colons avaient raison contre Pencroff. Auraient-ils raison dans l’avenir ? On le verrait.
  - white: They had silenced Pencroff, but, perhaps, after all, his advice would prove sound.
