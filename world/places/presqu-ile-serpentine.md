# Presqu’île Serpentine: the long tail peninsula at the island's south-west

*The long peninsula that forms the island's tail, curling to a roadstead facing south; named by Spilett on 30 March; its woods are the forests of the Far-West.*

Keywords: presqu’île, Serpentine, tail, queue, alligator, rade foraine, peninsula, south-west, reptile

- **What it is.**
  - The coast ends in a long tail like the caudal appendage of a gigantic alligator; it is a true peninsula running far out to sea (I.11 ¶25). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: Cette queue formait une véritable presqu’île
  - It curves round to make a wide-open roadstead, drawn by the lower coast of the island (I.11 ¶25). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: elle s’arrondissait en décrivant une rade foraine, largement ouverte
  - It projects to the south-west, about thirty miles from the observers on the summit, too far for a dwelling to be made out (I.11 ¶38). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: cette queue qui se projetait au sud-ouest
  - Spilett proposed the name presqu’île Serpentine for it, and promontoire du Reptile for the curved tail that ends it; Smith adopted both (I.11 ¶66). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: je proposerai de donner le nom de presqu’île Serpentine
  - Its woods are named the forests of the Far-West (see [the Far-West](far-west-forests.md)) (I.11 ¶81). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: qui couvraient la presqu’île Serpentine
  - The high forests on the right of the Mercy reach to its end, and are still unexplored (I.22 ¶60). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: jusqu’à l’extrémité de la presqu’île Serpentine
  - Its coast along the west offers refuges that the arid, wild northern coast would refuse to castaways; the exploration is to be continued along it (II.4 ¶36). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: sur toute la presqu’île Serpentine
    - quote: cette partie du littoral offrait des refuges
  - Smith will not count the shore explored until he has gone to the very end of the peninsula (II.4 ¶37). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: jusqu’à l’extrémité même de la presqu’île Serpentine
  - Its end, the promontoire du Reptile, is still two miles off at five in the afternoon of 31 October (II.4 ¶42). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: l’extrémité de la presqu’île Serpentine se trouvait encore à deux milles
  - Its woods are the great forests where the wild beasts take refuge, still the unknown part of the island, which Spilett means to purge of them (II.8 ¶42). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: La portion encore inconnue de l’île Lincoln était toujours celle de ces grands bois qui couvraient la presqu’île Serpentine
    - quote: où se réfugiaient les fauves, dont Gédéon Spilett comptait bien purger son domaine
  - The coast has been fully explored by sea, so that any mysterious being could reside only under the cover of the impenetrable woods of the peninsula, where the colonists have not yet carried their investigations (II.20 ¶32). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: ce ne pouvait être que sous le couvert des bois impénétrables de la presqu’île Serpentine
    - quote: là où les colons n’avaient encore porté leurs investigations
  - The large wooded parts that cover it had not been explored in any way before February 1868 (III.11 ¶26). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: ils n’avaient reconnu en aucune façon les larges portions boisées qui couvraient la presqu’île Serpentine
  - The colonists mean to cut a road with the axe from Granite-house to its end, sixteen to seventeen miles (see [the Far-West road](../things/the-far-west-road.md)) (III.11 ¶29). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: l’extrémité de la presqu’île, sur une longueur de seize à dix-sept milles
  - On 17 February its end is reached and the forest crossed along its whole length, with no sign of the convicts’ or the unknown’s retreat (III.11 ¶65). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: l’extrémité de la presqu’île était atteinte, et la forêt traversée sur toute sa longueur
  - Its forest is three to four miles wide, between the two shores of the peninsula (III.12 ¶1). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: dont la largeur variait de trois à quatre milles, car elle était comprise entre les deux rivages de la presqu’île Serpentine
  - Springs trickling in a thousand threads water the ground of the peninsula (III.13 ¶68). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: s’épanchant par mille filets, abreuvaient le sol de la presqu’île Serpentine
  - After the eruption a single clump of green trees still stands at the end of the peninsula (III.19 ¶62). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: Un seul bouquet d’arbres verts se dressait à l’extrémité de la presqu’île Serpentine
  - The long peninsula is among the places swallowed in the final destruction, though it lay far from the eruptive centre (III.20 ¶2). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: la longue presqu’île Serpentine, si éloignée cependant du centre éruptif
- **Appears in:** I.11, I.22, II.4, II.5, II.8, II.20, III.6, III.8, III.11, III.12, III.13, III.14, III.19, III.20
- **Open:**
  - The lengths: I.11 ¶25 says the peninsula runs "plus de trente milles" to sea from the south-east cape; ¶26 gives the island's greatest length, from the jaw of the north-east to the tail's end, as "pas moins de trente milles"; ¶38 repeats "environ trente milles" from the summit to the extreme points. Card none of the figures.
    - quote: s’allongeait de plus de trente milles en mer, à compter du cap sud-est de l’île
    - quote: ne comptait pas moins de trente milles
    - quote: une distance de trente milles environ séparait les observateurs des points extrêmes
