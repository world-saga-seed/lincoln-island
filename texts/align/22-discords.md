# I.22: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller h. Beads corrected: bead 20 (the first sentence of French ¶23, the rising wind and the sea roaring on the reefs, stands at the end of White ¶18, not in White ¶19); beads 22 and 23 (the last sentence of French ¶26, "rien à craindre", stands at the start of White ¶23); beads 25 and 26 (the first two sentences of French ¶30 end White ¶26); beads 49 and 50 (the last sentence of French ¶54 opens White ¶62).*

### 1. changed: the cold that did not pass the earlier low (I.22 ¶1; White ¶1)
- The French says the cold did not go beyond the lowest Fahrenheit reading yet seen; White says the thermometer never rose above that point, which reads as the reverse. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: sans dépasser toutefois ce maximum de degrés Fahrenheit observé jusqu’alors
  - white: the thermometer never rising above the point hitherto observed

### 2. changed: Pencroff leans over the trap (I.22 ¶16; White ¶12)
- Pencroff bends over the trap to haul the peccary out; White has him "handing over" the trap. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: en se penchant sur la trappe
  - white: handing over the trap

### 3. addition: the "eastern" lookout (I.22 ¶23; White ¶19)
- The French says only that the orientation of Granite House shelters it; White names the aspect, east. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: l’orientation de Granite-house la préservait d’un assaut direct
  - white: the eastern lookout of Granite House preserved it from a direct attack

### 4. cut: no way to stop the wind felling the trees (I.22 ¶25; White —)
- Pencroff's consolation ends on the narrator's remark that there was no means of preventing it; White has nothing. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Et, d’ailleurs, il n’y aurait eu aucun moyen de l’en empêcher.
  - white: —

### 5. condensed: where the osiers grew (I.22 ¶28; White ¶25)
- The French places the osier bed by the point the lake throws out to the north; White says only "the northern part of the lake". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: vers cette pointe que le lac projetait au nord
  - white: at the northern part of the lake

### 6. changed: Nab's stores in the baskets (I.22 ¶28; White ¶25)
- Nab stores rhizomes, pine-kernels and dragon-tree roots; White drops the rhizomes and turns the pine-kernels into pistachio nuts. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ses récoltes de rhizomes, d’amandes de pin-pignon et de racines de dragonnier
  - white: his stock of pistachio nuts and roots of the dragon tree

### 7. changed: snipe become wood-peckers (I.22 ¶30; White ¶27)
- The French names the birds on the frozen lake as ducks, snipe, pintails and guillemots; White gives "ducks and wood-peckers", losing two kinds and putting a wood bird on the ice. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: canards et bécassines, pilets et guillemots
  - white: ducks and wood-peckers

### 8. condensed: the list of restocked provisions (I.22 ¶37; White ¶35)
- The French lists what is renewed: pine-kernels, dragon-tree roots, rhizomes, maple sap, rabbits, agoutis and kangaroos; White says "both animal and vegetable". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: amandes de pin-pignon, racines de dragonnier, rhizomes, liqueur d’érable, pour la partie végétale ; lapins de garenne, agoutis et kangourous, pour la partie animale
  - white: both animal and vegetable

### 9. changed: who made the sand banks round the forge (I.22 ¶39; White ¶39)
- In the French the forge and furnaces were protected by the heaping up of sand (by the sea); White says the colonists had built banks of sand round them. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: protégés qu’ils avaient été tout d’abord par l’entassement des sables
  - white: so carefully protected had they been by the banks of sand which the colonists had built around them

### 10. changed: "not less than" eight degrees (I.22 ¶41; White ¶41)
- The engineer's estimate is that the thermometer would have shown no fewer than eight degrees below zero; White makes it "about eight degrees". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: n’eût pas marqué moins de huit degrés au-dessous de zéro
  - white: would have indicated about eight degrees below zero

### 11. cut: the centigrade figure (I.22 ¶41; White ¶41)
- The French gives the same cold in centigrade; White omits it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: 22°, 22 centigr. au-dessous de glace
  - white: —

### 12. changed: "several times" becomes "once or twice" (I.22 ¶41; White ¶43)
- The French says they went down to the beach several times; White says once or twice. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Plusieurs fois, les uns ou les autres descendirent sur la grève
  - white: Once or twice some of them ventured to the beach

### 13. changed: burning rungs become frostbitten hands (I.22 ¶41; White ¶43)
- In the French the rungs of the ladder burn their fingers with the cold; White has their hands frostbitten and puts the cold on the sides of the ladder. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: les échelons leur brûlaient les doigts
  - white: their hands were frostbitten by holding on to the frozen sides of the ladder

### 14. changed: Nab stirs the sap, White has him skim it (I.22 ¶48; White ¶50)
- In the French scum rises and Nab stirs the thickening sap with a wooden spatula to hasten evaporation and prevent a burnt taste; White has him remove the scum with a ladle. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Nab eut soin de la remuer avec une spatule de bois
  - white: Neb removed carefully with a wooden ladle

### 15. changed: Top's growls become whines (I.22 ¶53; White ¶58)
- Top makes odd growls at the well; White says he whined, which is a weaker, more timid sound. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: Top faisait entendre des grognements singuliers
  - white: Top whined in a most curious manner

### 16. changed: the sea-monster that comes to breathe (I.22 ¶54; White ¶60)
- Cyrus wonders whether some marine monster comes now and then to breathe at the bottom of the well; White has the sea-monsters "come into it from the bottom of these pits", and "respirer" is gone. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Quelque monstre marin ne venait-il pas, de temps en temps, respirer au fond de ce puits ?
  - white: Did any sea-monsters come into it from time to time from the bottom of these pits?

### 17. condensed: Pencroff's vow of no rest (I.22 ¶57; White ¶65)
- The French says Pencroff will give the engineer no peace until his wish is met; White says only that he kept reminding him. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il ne laisserait pas de cesse à l’ingénieur que celui-ci n’eût satisfait son désir
  - white: constantly reminded the engineer that he bad not provided them with guns

### 18. changed: "as one may doubt" becomes "as we suppose" (I.22 ¶58; White ¶66)
- The speaker doubts that the island holds wild beasts; White has him say it holds them "as we suppose". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Si l’île, comme on en peut douter, disait-il, renferme des animaux féroces
  - white: the island contains, as we suppose, wild beasts

### 19. addition: "(mountain goats)" (I.22 ¶59; White ¶67)
- White adds a gloss for "mouflons" that the French does not give. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: puisque les mouflons ne manquaient pas
  - white: as moufflons (mountain goats), were plenty

### 20. cut: the west coast as part of the unexplored country (I.22 ¶60; White ¶68)
- The French names the unknown ground as the forests right of the Mercy and also the whole west coast; White stops at Serpentine Peninsula. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ainsi que sur toute la côte occidentale
  - white: from its mouth to the end of Serpentine Peninsula

### 21. changed: pine-kernels become pistachio nuts (I.22 ¶74; White ¶79)
- The feast includes pine-kernels; White prints pistachio nuts, as at ¶28. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: des amandes de pignon
  - white: pistachio nuts
