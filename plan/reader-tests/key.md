## 01950, the insert

### K1. Why does Pencroff tell only Harbert?

- **Kind:** plain.
- **Text returns:** so as not to raise hopes he cannot keep, the hope being a ship or rescue. Must not return: that he distrusts the others.
- **True:** "He takes only Harbert, so as not to raise hopes." `story/outline/01950-the-float-in-the-bar.md § 2. He takes only Harbert (1 June)` (R1121-34).
- **Planted in:** 01950, beats 1 and 2.

### K2. Why can't the two free it?

- **Kind:** plain.
- **Text returns:** they came without tools, because tools taken would have been asked about. The float is held by its net, buried in the bar and heavy with sand. Both reasons are needed, each from its single line.
- **True:** "the secrecy leaves the tools behind (picks carried out of Granite-house would be asked about), and the float is still on its net, buried in the bar and heavy with sand." `plan/settled/ledger/2026-11-21.md`, R1121-52.
- **Planted in:** 01950, beats 3 and 4.

### K3. What did keeping it secret cost?

- **Kind:** plain. This is the chapter's point (R1121-36).
- **Text returns:** with all five and the picks they would have had it. They lost it because they were two and spent their hour reading it. Must not return: that the sea was simply too strong for anyone.
- **True:** "Five of us and the picks, and we'd have had it." / "We had it, Pencroff. For an hour." `story/outline/01950-the-float-in-the-bar.md § 7. The smooth sand (3 June, the next low water)` (R1121-57, R1121-58).
- **Planted in:** 01950, beats 4 and 7.

### K4. Why is the float lost?

- **Kind:** plain.
- **Text returns:** the squall and the surge shifted the sandbar. It is nature, at a neap tide that gave them only a short low water.
- **True:** "At the next low water the bar has shifted and the float is gone." `story/outline/01950-the-float-in-the-bar.md § 7`; "The insert's tide is a neap" (R1121-53).
- **Planted in:** 01950, beats 3, 5 and 7.

### K5. Whose float is it?

- **Kind:** hidden until III.16. On a reread, the reader should be able to link it to the Nautilus.
- **Text returns:** a fine glass net float, not cork and not American, of a make the sailor has never seen, from somewhere far off. Must not return: that it is the Nautilus's or Captain Nemo's, or that it belongs to anyone on or under the island.
- **True:** "The find is one of the floats that held up the Nautilus's nets, made in the Nautilus's style" (R1121-27); "The page never says the float was Captain Nemo's" (R1121-28).
- **Planted in:** 01950, beat 4. Paid off at III.16 ¶53, ¶62 and III.17 ¶13. Echoed at II.2 ¶64.

### K6. Where did it come from, and how long ago?

- **Kind:** hidden.
- **Text returns:** Harbert's reading of the barnacles: long adrift, from far away, long ago. Must not return: that it came from close by, recently.
- **True:** Nemo "sets a short net off the coast he walks in his diving suit (III.16 ¶62), held up by one of the crew's old floats; the June squall tears the float loose." `plan/settled/ledger/2026-11-21.md`, R1121-29.
- **Planted in:** 01950, beat 4.

### K7. What do the letters say?

- **Kind:** hidden until III.17 ¶13.
- **Text returns:** a broken run, B, L, I, perhaps Latin, as Harbert guesses. Must not return: the word MOBILIS, the motto, or an N.
- **True:** "a broken run, a B, an L, an I, enough for Harbert to say 'Latin, I think' and no more" (R1121-55); "the N lies on the hidden side, toward the sand" (R1121-31).
- **Planted in:** 01950, beat 4. Paid off at III.17 ¶13 ("Mobilis in mobili").

### K8. Is a ship near the island?

- **Kind:** unknown in this stretch. The float gives no answer, and Harbert's reading leans to no. *(Settled R1121-61.)*
- **Text returns:** no answer. A reader may lean to "no, it came from long ago", but must not come back sure either way.
- **True:** world rule 6: from outside, only the harpoon (II.10), the bottle, the Speedy and the Duncan reach the island. `world/rules.md`.
- **Planted in:** 01900 ¶22 (Cyrus's fear), 01950, beat 4.

### K9. Did some hand move the float?

- **Kind:** forbidden. The chapter must not raise an unseen agent behind the loss. The question it leaves is whose the float is, not who lost it. *(Settled R1121-61.)*
- **Text returns:** the question does not arise. The loss is the sea's.
- **True:** "by natural causes, with no sign of Captain Nemo's hand" (R1121-23); world rule 3, "The insert adds no act of his." `world/rules.md`.
- **Planted in:** none.

## 01900, before

### K10. How is Granite-house reached and kept safe?

- **Kind:** plain.
- **Text returns:** by a rope ladder that, once drawn up, makes the house unreachable. The old outlet is sealed and hidden, and lake water is brought in. The precaution is against enemies who "may come from without", not only animals.
- **True:** "une échelle de corde, qui, une fois retirée, rendra impossible l’accès de notre demeure" (I.19 ¶4–¶12). `story/outline/01900-the-facade-the-ladder-and-the-warren.md § 2. The plan, and why a door and a rope ladder (I.19 ¶4–¶12)` and `§ 11. The outlet sealed; water brought in; the finished house and its view (I.19 ¶44–¶48)`. *(Drafted.)*
- **Planted in:** 01900.

### K11. What does Cyrus fear and keep to himself?

- **Kind:** plain.
- **Text returns:** that, far from the ships' route, they may never be rescued, and that a poor boat could not safely carry them away. He tells no one.
- **True:** "en dehors du passage des navires, il pouvait craindre de n’être jamais secouru" (I.19 ¶22). `story/outline/01900-the-facade-the-ladder-and-the-warren.md § 6`.
- **Planted in:** 01900. It sits beside K1: two secrets kept in the house.

## 02000, after

### K12. How are the candles made?

- **Kind:** plain.
- **Text returns:** from seal fat, with lime and sulphuric acid, by Cyrus's chemistry, in under a day.
- **True:** "projet réalisable, puisqu’il avait de la chaux et de l’acide sulfurique, et que les amphibies de l’îlot lui fourniraient la graisse" (I.20 ¶18). `story/outline/02000-candles-and-the-grain-of-wheat.md § 5`.
- **Planted in:** 02000.

### K13. Where did the grain of wheat come from?

- **Kind:** plain.
- **Text returns:** it stayed in the lining of Harbert's jacket from feeding pigeons at Richmond, and was found by chance.
- **True:** "par le plus grand hasard, Harbert trouva un jour dans la doublure de sa veste" (I.20 ¶33); the explanation at ¶37 (N11 on the register).
- **Planted in:** 02000.

### K14. Does anyone remember the float at the ford or at the grain?

- **Kind:** forbidden. 02000 is Verne's, with no words added (`story/outline/02000-candles-and-the-grain-of-wheat.md § The seam with 01950`).
- **Text returns:** the question does not arise from 02000's page. Any link is the reader's own, made from 01950.
- **True:** "the two who saw it say nothing of it at the chest, and the page need not explain their silence" (R1121-24); the seam notes add no words.
- **Planted in:** none.
