# III.8: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller o. Beads corrected: none.*

### 1. cut: six convicts (III.8 ¶2; White ¶2)
- Spilett's reconstruction begins with the six convicts landed on the island; White says only "the convicts", dropping the number. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: les six convicts, débarqués sur l’île
  - white: The convicts had followed the southern coast

### 2. changed: round both shores of Serpentine Peninsula (III.8 ¶2; White ¶2)
- The convicts went along both shores of the peninsula in the French; White has them get over into it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: après avoir parcouru le double rivage de la presqu’île Serpentine
  - white: after getting over into Serpentine Peninsula

### 3. condensed: the corral's stores last a long time (III.8 ¶2; White ¶2)
- The French says the corral's provisions could last a fairly long time; White says only fully provisioned. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dont les approvisionnements, d’ailleurs, pouvaient suffire pendant un assez long temps
  - white: which was fully provisioned

### 4. softened: "abominable projects" (III.8 ¶2; White ¶2)
- The French has the convicts waiting for the moment to carry out their abominable plans; White has them merely installed. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: en attendant le moment de mettre à exécution leurs abominables projets
  - white: They had probably installed themselves there

### 5. changed: venturing into the woods becomes pursuing the convicts (III.8 ¶3; White ¶3)
- The French says that to venture into the woods is to expose oneself to their shots; White says to pursue them. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: et s’y aventurer, c’était s’exposer à leurs coups
  - white: and to pursue them was to be exposed to their fire

### 6. cut: the beat as the object of the great expedition (III.8 ¶4; White ¶4)
- Smith says the general beat will be the object of their great expedition; White drops that clause and leaves "while at the same time——". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Ce sera l’objet de notre grande expédition
  - white: we will beat the island, and have a shot at these rascals; while at the same time——

### 7. cut: the protection failed when most needed (III.8 ¶5; White ¶5)
- Spilett adds that the protector failed them at the very moment he was most needed; White stops at "failed us". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: et au moment même où elle nous eût été le plus nécessaire !
  - white: his protection has failed us.

### 8. changed: tisanes become broths and gruels (III.8 ¶9; White ¶9)
- Harbert has herb teas in the French; White gives broths and gruels. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: mais les tisanes ne lui manquaient pas
  - white: but he had plenty of broths and gruels

### 9. changed: "rarer than" becomes "as rare as" (III.8 ¶10; White ¶10)
- The French says it is perhaps rarer to see a dressing well made than an operation; White says a good dressing is as rare as a good operation. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il est plus rare peut-être de voir un pansement bien fait qu’une opération bien faite
  - white: good dressing is as rare as a good operation

### 10. softened: "no longer entitled to any consideration" (III.8 ¶12; White ¶11)
- Pencroff calls the pirates gentlemen who have no claim to any regard, and offers sentiment in lead; White has "Nice fellows" and sends compliments in a bullet. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: Voilà des gentlemen qui n’ont plus droit à aucun égard !
  - white: “Nice fellows they are,” said he.

### 11. cut: Smith's question and Spilett's answer (III.8 ¶18–¶20; White ¶16)
- Smith asks if Spilett would have hesitated to operate; Spilett says no and thanks God; White gives only that they could not think of it without a shudder. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Et cependant, s’il avait fallu agir, lui répondit un jour Cyrus Smith, vous n’auriez pas hésité ?
  - white: They could not think of it without a shudder.

### 12. cut: men complete one another in society (III.8 ¶21; White —)
- Smith's reflection, that on the island they are alone and men need one another, with the wonder whether a circumstance might defeat them, is dropped. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: les hommes se complètent par l’état de société, ils sont nécessaires les uns aux autres
  - white: —

### 13. condensed: what the island had given (III.8 ¶22; White ¶17)
- The French lists minerals, plants and animals the island had given them and a colony materially complete; White has "they had succeeded in everything". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: L’île leur avait abondamment fourni minéraux, végétaux, animaux
  - white: they had succeeded in everything

### 14. condensed: the convicts' ship and the six who escaped (III.8 ¶24; White ¶17)
- Smith's list, the convicts' ship appeared, the pirates almost miraculously destroyed, six escaped and five survivors nearly impossible to catch, is dropped. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: si ces pirates avaient été pour ainsi dire miraculeusement détruits, six d’entre eux, du moins, avaient échappé à la catastrophe
  - white: Ayrton, doubtless, was dead, and Herbert severely wounded

### 15. softened: massacred becomes dead (III.8 ¶24; White ¶17)
- Ayrton was "massacred by these wretches" in the French and Harbert "struck almost mortally"; White has dead and severely wounded. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: Ayrton avait été, sans aucun doute, massacré par ces misérables
  - white: Ayrton, doubtless, was dead

### 16. changed: "finally strike" becomes "continue to oppress" (III.8 ¶25; White ¶18)
- The French says if adversity were at last to strike them; White says if it continued to oppress them. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: si l’adversité devait enfin les frapper
  - white: if adversity should continue to oppress them
