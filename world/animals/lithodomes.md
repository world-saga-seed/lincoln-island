# Lithodomes: the peppery rock-boring shellfish of the first meal

*Oblong boring molluscs clustered on the shore rocks, eaten like oysters, with a natural pepper taste.*

Keywords: lithodome, shellfish, coquillage, mussel, mollusc, moule, oyster

- **What it is.**
  - They are lithodomes, not mussels, as Harbert says on examining the molluscs on the rocks (I.4 ¶12). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: ce sont des lithodomes
  - They are bivalves swarming on weed-covered rocks that high tide covers (I.4 ¶10). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: des coquillages à double valve
  - They are oblong shells, attached in clusters and very adherent to the rocks; they belong to the species of boring molluscs that dig holes in the hardest stones, and their shell is rounded at both ends, unlike the common mussel (I.4 ¶17). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: attachés par grappes et très-adhérents aux roches
  - They are eaten like oysters and taste strongly peppery, so they spare the castaways any regret for pepper or condiment; but they increase thirst (I.4 ¶18). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: une saveur fortement poivrée
- **On the page.**
  - Pencroff and Harbert fill their pockets and handkerchiefs with them (I.4 ¶19). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: une ample provision de lithodomes
  - A new harvest is made before returning to the Chimneys, in case fire fails (I.5 ¶35). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: une nouvelle récolte de lithodomes
- **Open.**
  - Shellfish eaten on 25 March at the islet are not named; only "coquillages" with which the sand is strewn; likewise the shellfish Harbert offers Nab (I.5 ¶49) and Spilett eats (I.5 ¶51). Not carded as lithodomes (I.3 ¶50). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: quelques coquillages dont le sable était semé
  - Harbert offers Nab a few handfuls of shellfish, named only as "coquillages"; I.5 ¶35 says lithodomes were gathered again before the return to the Chimneys (I.5 ¶49). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: quelques poignées de coquillages
- **Appears in:** I.3, I.4, I.5
