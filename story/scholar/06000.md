## a

After: “The subterranean fires have been brooding for ten weeks,”

Spilett puts the first vapours about the 15th of October, and from then to this day, the 3rd of January, is eleven weeks and more; Herbert’s “two months and a half” agrees with that, and the ten weeks fall short. But chapter LVI dates the first vapours to the 7th of September, when the engineer saw smoke above the crater, and chapter LVII has them unbroken from that day. On the search of the spurs in February there was none. From the 7th of September to this day is nearly seventeen weeks. I take the 7th of September, and both reckonings here for short.
