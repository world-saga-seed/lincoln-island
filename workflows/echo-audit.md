# Precedent audit: real history's outcomes, the story's causes

*Refinement. Add this when an outcome rhymes with real history.*

When an outcome in the story rhymes with real history (a price collapse, a guild war, a famine, an institution's rise and fall), it is plausible by definition, because it happened. The only question is whether the setting gives enough cause for it to happen again here. This audit lists each such outcome, the causes that produced it in history, and the cause in the notes that stands for each, and finds where an outcome runs ahead of its causes.

**Grade.** Mechanical where it can be: each story cause is quoted from a card or ledger row and resolves. Naming the precedent and its causes is a judgment, marked *(My reading, to confirm.)*; the writer confirms it.

**When:** beside the logic check, when an arc's frames are settled, and again before drafting it.

## The table

One heading per outcome (and the chapter where it lands), its **precedent**, and one row per cause:

| Column | Holds |
|---|---|
| **Historical cause** | what produced the outcome there, in plain checkable terms |
| **Story cause** | the cause in the notes that stands for it, quoted as `file:line § heading`; or **missing** |
| **Kind** | same (the cause carries over), substituted (a story cause stands in), blocked (the setting removes it) |
| **On the page** | the chapter where the narrator can see it, "held, lands at N" for a clue late on purpose, or "not yet" |

## The faults

- **A missing cause:** the outcome happens and nothing drives one of its causes. It reads as the writer reaching for history.
- **A blocked cause, outcome kept:** the setting removes a cause history had, yet the story keeps the outcome. Either the outcome goes, or a substitute is found.
- **A hidden substitute:** a story cause stands in, but never reaches the page.
- **A cause too late:** it reaches the page after its outcome. A held clue is late on purpose and never a fault.
- **A dangling cause:** a settled cause with no outcome.
- **Conflicting causes:** two lines give the outcome different causes.

**Strengths are recorded too:** a blocked cause whose outcome is gone, or one that inverts history's outcome, shows the setting truly differs.

## The loop

1. Collect the outcomes in the horizon. 2. Break each into causes. 3. Match each cause in the notes, quoted. 4. Audit for the faults. 5. Pitch fixes, plain first: the smallest beat that puts the cause where the narrator can see it, never narration of the system. 6. Settle with the writer in one batch. 7. Card and ripple.

**The precedent stays in the audit.** The page carries the story's cause and the outcome, never the historical name or comparison. At most two agents, each with one arc and a named file list.
