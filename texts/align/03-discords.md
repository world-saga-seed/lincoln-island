# I.3: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller c. Beads corrected: none.*

### 1. changed: "at most" half a mile becomes "at least" (I.3 ¶13; White ¶11)
- The French says the nearest shore point lay at half a mile at most; White says at least that distance. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: c’était donc à un demi-mille au plus que devait être situé ce point
  - white: it was at least that distance from where they now were

### 2. cut: the east coast (I.3 ¶14; White ¶12)
- The French has them walking north along the east coast of the land; White says only the shore. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: en suivant vers le nord la côte est de cette terre
  - white: followed northward along the shore of that land

### 3. changed: "un îlot" becomes "an island" (I.3 ¶25; White ¶22)
- The French says they were thrown not on a continent, not even on an island, but on an islet; White drops "not even on an island" and calls it an island, and so renders îlot "island" through the chapter. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: non sur un continent, pas même sur une île, mais sur un îlot
  - white: not upon a continent, but upon an island not more than two miles long

### 4. changed: a "might have" becomes a statement (I.3 ¶30; White ¶26)
- The French leaves open that Smith could have saved himself and found refuge; White says he had escaped and was already safe. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Ou l’ingénieur avait pu se sauver seul, et déjà il avait trouvé refuge sur un point de la côte
  - white: The engineer had escaped, and was already safe upon the land

### 5. condensed: why their voices carried (I.3 ¶31; White ¶26)
- The French gives a calm in the air as the reason their voices would carry far; White gives only that the shouts must have sounded into the distance. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: leurs voix devaient se transmettre au loin, car un certain calme régnait alors dans l’atmosphère
  - white: their hallooes must have sounded far into the distance

### 6. changed: "cela prouverait" becomes "that proves" (I.3 ¶33; White ¶27)
- Harbert's conditional inference about a coast to the west is made a certainty in White. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Cela prouverait qu’il existe dans l’ouest une côte assez rapprochée.
  - white: That proves that there is land not far to the west.

### 7. cut: the east side of the islet (I.3 ¶35; White ¶28)
- The French says the immensity over the whole east side of the islet stayed silent; White says only that the silence about the island was unbroken. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: sur toute la partie est de l’îlot, demeura silencieuse
  - white: the silence about the island remained unbroken

### 8. cut: the Southern Cross at the pole (I.3 ¶36; White ¶28)
- The French puts the Southern Cross at the austral pole of the world; White says only that it shone in the heavens. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: la Croix du Sud resplendissait alors au pôle austral du monde
  - white: the Southern Cross shone resplendent in the heavens

### 9. cut: the date, 25 March (I.3 ¶37; White ¶29)
- The French dates the dawn the 25th of March; White gives the hour only. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Vers cinq heures du matin, le 25 mars
  - white: towards 5 o’clock in the morning

### 10. changed: a fair-weather mist becomes a morning mist (I.3 ¶40; White ¶31)
- The French calls the fog a mist of fine weather, which the sun will clear; White says it was nothing more than a morning mist. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Ce n’était qu’une brumaille de beau temps.
  - white: which was nothing more than a morning mist

### 11. cut: the circular plane of the sea (I.3 ¶41; White ¶31)
- The French has the sea show itself on a circular plan; White has the sea simply appear. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: la mer se montra suivant un plan circulaire
  - white: Then the sea appeared, limitless towards the east

### 12. condensed: where Nab landed (I.3 ¶49; White ¶37)
- The French says Nab landed several thousand feet from the spot opposite his start; White says a long distance. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: à plusieurs milliers de pieds de l’endroit qui faisait face au point d’où il était parti
  - white: a place a long distance from the point opposite to that which he had left

### 13. addition: Nab clambers up the wall (I.3 ¶49; White ¶38)
- The French has Nab land at the foot of the granite wall, shake himself and run out of sight; White has him clamber up its side. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Nab prit pied au bas d’une haute muraille de granit et se secoua vigoureusement
  - white: clambered quickly up its side

### 14. condensed: the islet as the carcass of a whale (I.3 ¶51; White ¶40)
- The French says it looked like an enormous whale, of which it was the greatly enlarged carcass; White says only that it lay like a whale. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ressemblait à un énorme cétacé, dont il représentait la carcasse très-agrandie
  - white: like an enormous whale, lay upon the sea

### 15. changed: the "prismatic splinters" of the cliff (I.3 ¶52; White ¶41)
- The French says the cliff crumbles into prismatic fragments; White says it was glistening in the light. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: s’égrenant en éclats prismatiques
  - white: glistening in the light

### 16. changed: the plateau at Cape Town (I.3 ¶53; White ¶42)
- The French likens the plateau to the table that dominates Cape Town, smaller in scale; White says it was as barren as, but less extensive than, the land "around" Cape Town. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: comme celle qui domine Cape-Town
  - white: as barren though not as extensive as that around Cape Town

### 17. changed: the snowy summit, "at least" seven miles (I.3 ¶54; White ¶42)
- The French says seven miles at least; White says about seven. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: à une distance de sept milles au moins
  - white: distant towards the northwest about seven miles

### 18. softened: "they would die there" (I.3 ¶56; White ¶44)
- The French says they would die on the land if it lay off the ships' track; White says they might have to die. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: sur laquelle ils mourraient même, si elle ne se trouvait pas sur la route des navires
  - white: upon which, if out of the track of ships, they might have to die
