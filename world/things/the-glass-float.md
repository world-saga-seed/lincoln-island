# The glass float: the ball of glass on the bar off the Mercy, lost on 2 June 1865

*The glass net float Pencroff finds half buried in the bar on 1 June 1865 and loses in the squall of the 2nd, as the inserted chapter shows it. What it truly is lives in [the outline](../../story/outline/01950-the-float-in-the-bar.md#the-find), never on this card.*

Keywords: float, flotteur, glass ball, boule de verre, net, filet, seal, letters, B L I, barnacles

- **In XIX bis.** *(The inserted chapter, 01950; R1121-132.)*
  - A ball of glass about a foot across, half buried in the bar off the Mercy, a third of it showing above the sand like the back of a turtle (XIX bis ¶5, 6, 29). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: Something lay on the bar, half buried, and it was round.
    - quote: The thing might measure a foot across
    - quote: so that it showed above the sand as the back of a turtle shows above the sea
  - Dark green glass, like a bottle's, under a crust of dead grey-white barnacles over the part that shows, with a few dead goose barnacles among them (XIX bis ¶29, 42). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: there the glass showed through, dark green, like the glass of a bottle
    - quote: these are goose barnacles
  - A rotten, sand-choked net cord runs from it into the bar on the gut side, and a second cord passes under it on the hidden side; it cannot be turned, and the dug sand refills with water (XIX bis ¶29, 31). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: a dark cord came out of the sand, rotten and swollen with water, with the meshes of a net choked with sand along it
    - quote: A cord of the same kind as the other passed under it, on the hidden side
  - Pencroff calls it a float for a net, no cork and never made in America. The narrator: glass net floats are a late thing, new still and rare, and no fishery of the Pacific has yet taken them up (XIX bis ¶33, 37). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: It's a float," said he at last. "A float for a net.
    - quote: Only in late years had fishermen begun to buoy their great nets with globes of blown glass
  - A round seal a palm across is moulded in the glass on the gut side, mostly below toward the sand, with raised capitals inside its rim: B, a place the crust holds, L, I; then the ring goes into the sand (XIX bis ¶51, 53). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: the rim of a round seal about a palm across, which had been moulded in the glass itself
    - quote: Beyond the I, the ring went down into the sand.
  - Lost on 2 June: the squall and flood loosen it, the cord gives, the backwash and the channel current take it to the islet, and it breaks on the rocks; on 3 June the bar is smooth. That day the two tell the others nothing of it (XIX bis ¶64, 65, 75, 78). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: At last it gave way
    - quote: something flashed, once, and flew apart
    - quote: of the net not a thread showed
    - quote: That was all they told, and it was true, as far as it went.
- **Appears in:** XIX bis
