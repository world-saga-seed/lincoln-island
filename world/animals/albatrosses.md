# Albatrosses: the great sea birds of the barren north shore

*Great sea birds seen on the desolate shore toward Cape Mandible-South.*

Keywords: albatros, albatross, oiseau de mer, sea bird, côte, shore, cap Mandibule-Sud

- **What it is.**
  - Great albatrosses frequent the barren shore of sand, shells and lava debris toward the cape that closes Union Bay, with gulls and wild ducks (I.14 ¶38). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: des goëlands, de grands albatros
  - An albatross is a magnificent bird of the family of the great sailers, whose outstretched wings measure ten feet and which can cross seas as wide as the Pacific (II.11 ¶32). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: magnifique oiseau de la famille de ces grands voiliers
    - quote: dix pieds d’envergure
    - quote: qui peuvent traverser des mers aussi larges que le Pacifique
  - One, taken on 30 June after Harbert's shot lightly wounded its foot, heals quickly; Harbert wants to keep and tame it (II.11 ¶32, ¶33). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: le 30 juin, capture fut faite, non sans peine, d’un albatros
    - quote: légèrement blessé à la patte
    - quote: dont la blessure guérit promptement et qu’il prétendait apprivoiser
  - These birds are in the habit of resting on the surface of the sea, so a message bag is tied to the neck of the one freed and not to its foot; it vanishes far off in the mists of the west, and Harbert says it goes towards New Zealand (II.11 ¶35, ¶36, ¶37). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: ces oiseaux ont l’habitude de se reposer à la surface de la mer
    - quote: disparaître au loin dans les brumes de l’ouest
    - quote: Vers la Nouvelle-Zélande
  - The colonists' first attempt to communicate with other men is made with this bird, as a 'rapide courrier de l’air' (II.11 ¶30, ¶35). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: la première tentative faite par les colons pour communiquer avec leurs semblables
    - quote: ce rapide courrier de l’air
  - Albatrosses and frigate birds pass within gunshot of the Bonadventure between Tabor and Lincoln, the only creatures that seem to frequent that part of the ocean (II.13 ¶33). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: quelque grand oiseau, albatros ou frégate, passait à portée de fusil
    - quote: Ces oiseaux étaient les seuls êtres qui parussent fréquenter cette partie de l’océan
- **Appears in:** I.14, II.11, II.13
- **Open.**
  - Whether the notice carried by the albatross reaches anyone is not told in II.11 or II.12 (II.11 ¶35). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: ce ne fut pas sans quelque émotion que les colons le virent disparaître au loin dans les brumes de l’ouest
