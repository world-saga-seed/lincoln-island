# II.7: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller i. Beads corrected: bead 10 (White ¶10, Spilett's reply about the tail, has no French paragraph to pair with; it is an addition). Bead 13 is short on the White side: White ¶13 renders French ¶18–¶20 and ¶22 but not ¶21.*

### 1. changed: pine-nut almonds and rhizome roots become pistachios and sumach (II.7 ¶3; White ¶3)
- The French feeds Jup pine-nut kernels and the roots called rhizomes; White has "pistachio nuts" and "roots of the sumach". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: des amandes de pignon et des racines de rrhyomes
  - white: the pistachio nuts and the roots of the sumach

### 2. changed: "some projects" becomes "three projects" (II.7 ¶4; White ¶4)
- The French says they discussed some urgent projects, then names two; White says "three projects" and then names two. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: discutèrent quelques projets dont l’exécution était urgente
  - white: discussed three projects, the execution of which was urgent

### 3. cut: how the bridge and the corral answer the clothing question (II.7 ¶6; White ¶4)
- The French says the bridge will bring the balloon envelope (linen) and the corral the wool for winter clothes; White says only that the two plans "tended to solve the question of clothing", and does not say that the bridge links the south of the island with Granite-house. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le pont rendrait facile le transport de l’enveloppe du ballon, qui donnerait le linge, et le corral devait fournir la récolte de laine, qui donnerait les vêtements d’hiver
  - white: These two plans tended to solve the question of clothing, which was then the most serious question.

### 4. addition: Spilett's joke about Jup's tail (II.7 ¶11; White ¶10)
- White gives Spilett a reply to Pencroff's fear that Jup will pull up the ladder; the French has none, only Cyrus Smith's answer. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Et si, pendant notre absence, il allait prendre fantaisie à maître Jup de retirer cette échelle
  - white: “We would be dependent on his tail!” answered Spilett.

### 5. cut: Port Ballon found the day before (II.7 ¶14; White ¶11)
- The French dates the find: the port was discovered the day before on the south coast; White gives only the distance. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: de ce point au port Ballon, découvert la veille sur la côte méridionale
  - white: from this point to Balloon Harbor the distance was three miles and a half

### 6. cut: the south side of the plateau (II.7 ¶21; White —)
- The French defends the plateau by water on three sides and lists them as north-west (lake), north (new creek), east (sea) and south (from the Mercy's mouth to its bend, where the bridge is to stand); White gives the lake, the creek and the sea and has no south side. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Au sud, enfin, depuis cette embouchure jusqu’au coude de la Mercy où devait être établi le pont
  - white: —

### 7. cut: the ditch's overflow makes a second fall (II.7 ¶22; White ¶13)
- The French has the ditch's overflow fall into the Mercy by a second fall; White says only that the ditch will "flow into the Mercy". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dont le trop-plein irait se jeter par une seconde chute dans le lit de la Mercy
  - white: which would be filled from the lake, and flow into the Mercy

### 8. cut: Glycerine Creek a tributary of the Mercy (II.7 ¶39; White ¶29)
- The French says the new stream became a tributary of the Mercy, and fixes the ditch from the south angle of Lake Grant to the Mercy's bend; White names the creek and leaves out both. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qui devint un affluent de la Mercy
  - white: forming a small stream, to which they gave the name of Glycerine Creek

### 9. changed: the alectors tamed become alectors that breed (II.7 ¶45; White ¶34)
- The French says the pair of alectors soon grew tame; White says they "soon bred others". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qui ne tardèrent pas à s’apprivoiser
  - white: These soon bred others

### 10. softened: Providence becomes Fortune (II.7 ¶51; White ¶40)
- The French says Providence had a weakness for Pencroff; White says Fortune favored him. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: comme la providence avait un faible pour lui
  - white: as Fortune favored him

### 11. changed: isabel coats become "light bay" (II.7 ¶53; White ¶42)
- The French gives a pale isabel (dun) coat; White says "light bay". *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: pelage isabelle
  - white: of a light bay color

### 12. changed: onaggas become onagers (II.7 ¶54; White ¶43)
- The French names the animal onagga, between zebra and quagga; White calls it onager throughout, which is the name of another animal, the Asiatic wild ass. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: Ce sont des onaggas ! s’écria Harbert
  - white: “They are onagers,” cried Herbert.

### 13. changed: the onaggas bray, White has them whinny (II.7 ¶60; White ¶48)
- The French has them utter sharp brays; White has them whinny. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: jeter quelques braîments aigus
  - white: whinnying and galloping over the grass

### 14. cut: the onagga is harnessed in South Africa and acclimatised in Europe (II.7 ¶62; White ¶50)
- The French adds that the onagga, less rebellious than the zebra, is often harnessed in the mountains of southern Africa and has been acclimatised in Europe; White gives only that they soon submitted. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: l’onagga, moins rebelle que le zèbre, s’attelle fréquemment dans les parties montagneuses de l’Afrique australe
  - white: although it was not long before they submitted to this new service
