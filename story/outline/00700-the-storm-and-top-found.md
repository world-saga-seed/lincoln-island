# The storm and Top's return (outline)

*Chapter 00700: the French I.7 (`texts/fr-1875/07.md`). Before: 00600. After: 00800. Third person, past tense; Verne's narrator. Every beat is marked (Drafted.): it is what the French holds.*

## Outline

### 1. Evening on the shore: how far from the coast? (I.7 ¶1–¶19)

- Spilett stands on the beach with arms crossed, looking at the sea; to the east the horizon merges with a big black cloud rising to the zenith. The wind is strong and rising; a gale is coming. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: un gros nuage noir qui montait rapidement vers le zénith
- Pencroff predicts a bad night, "rain and wind to delight the petrels"; footnote I.7 n1 says petrels are sea birds that love storms. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: De la pluie et du vent à faire la joie des pétrels
  - quote: Oiseaux de mer qui se plaisent surtout au milieu des tempêtes.
- Spilett asks how far from the coast the car took the sea that swept away their companion. Pencroff: two cable-lengths at most; a cable-length is about a hundred and twenty fathoms or six hundred feet. So Smith vanished about twelve hundred feet from shore. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: À deux encâblures, au plus.
  - quote: Cent vingt brasses environ ou six cents pieds.
  - quote: à douze cents pieds au plus du rivage
- Spilett is surprised that neither Top's body nor his master's has been cast ashore. Pencroff: strong sea, and the currents may have carried them farther along the coast. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ni le corps du chien, ni celui de son maître n’aient été rejetés au rivage
- Spilett's view: the complete disappearance of both Cyrus and Top, living or dead, is "inexplicable and improbable". Pencroff's conviction is made. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: a quelque chose d’inexplicable et d’invraisemblable
  - quote: Malheureusement, ma conviction est faite !

### 2. Supper and Nab's absence (I.7 ¶20–¶26)

- A good fire burns; Harbert has thrown on dry wood. Pencroff prepares a main dish: the strings of couroucous are kept for the next day, two tétras are plucked and spitted. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Les chapelets de couroucous furent conservés pour le lendemain
- At seven in the evening Nab has not returned. Pencroff fears an accident or an act of despair; Harbert reasons that something new has kept him: a footprint, a bit of wreck, a trail, perhaps he is with his master. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: À sept heures du soir, Nab n’était pas encore de retour.
  - quote: Peut-être était-il près de son maître ?…
- Spilett approves of Harbert with a gesture; Pencroff thinks Nab simply went farther than the day before. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Seul, le reporter l’approuvait du geste.
- Harbert wants to go to meet Nab; Pencroff says it would be useless in the dark and bad weather, and that if Nab has not come back by morning he will go with Harbert. Spilett agrees that they must not divide. Harbert gives up, two tears fall, and the reporter embraces him. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Si le lendemain Nab n’avait pas reparu, Pencroff n’hésiterait pas à se joindre à Harbert
  - quote: Le reporter ne put se retenir d’embrasser le généreux enfant.

### 3. The gale; the tétras supper (I.7 ¶27–¶31)

- A south-east gale hits the coast; the sea, which is ebbing, roars against the first reefs; rain is pulverised into mist, shingle rattles, sand mixes with the showers; eddies swirl between the river mouth and the cliff, and air escapes up the narrow valley of the river. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Un coup de vent de sud-est passait sur la côte avec une violence sans égale.
  - quote: On entendait la mer, qui baissait alors
- The smoke is driven back by the narrow passage and fills the corridors; Pencroff lets the fire fall once the tétras are cooked and keeps only embers under ash. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Pencroff laissa tomber le feu, et ne conserva plus que des braises enfouies sous les cendres
- At eight Nab has not yet returned; the storm may have kept him, but going to find him is impossible. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: À huit heures, Nab n’avait pas encore reparu
- The game is the only dish; it is excellent; Pencroff and Harbert, hungry after the day's excursion, devour it. Each goes to the corner where he slept the night before; Harbert sleeps beside Pencroff, who lies by the hearth. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Le gibier forma l’unique plat du souper.

### 4. The storm in the night (I.7 ¶32–¶34)

- The tempest rises to formidable proportions, compared to that which carried the prisoners from Richmond. The narrator says such storms are frequent at the equinox, terrible on this wide stretch of sea, and the coast is open to the east. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Tempêtes fréquentes pendant ces temps d’équinoxe
  - quote: une côte ainsi exposée à l’est
- Pencroff crawls twice to the mouth of the corridor; the heap of granite is solid, though some blocks seem to tremble; stones torn from the plateau top fall on the beach and some on the Chimneys, but are not dangerous. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: l’entassement de roches qui formait les Cheminées était solide
- Harbert sleeps through the storm and Pencroff too; only Spilett stays awake, blaming himself for not having gone with Nab, his hope not gone. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Il se reprochait de ne pas avoir accompagné Nab.

### 5. The barking in the night (I.7 ¶35–¶58)

- About two in the morning Spilett shakes Pencroff awake: he thinks he hears a dog barking. Pencroff hears only the gale, then in a lull hears distant barking. Harbert wakes and cries "It is Top!". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il pouvait être deux heures du matin
  - quote: Les aboiements d’un chien !
  - quote: C’est Top ! C’est Top !…
- The three struggle out against the wind; darkness is absolute. They hear the barks again, evidently far away. Pencroff reasons that Top is probably alone, for if Nab were with him he would have come straight to the Chimneys. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: L’obscurité était absolue.
  - quote: Il est plus probable qu’il était seul
- Pencroff goes in, comes out with a lit faggot, throws it into the dark and whistles. Closer barking answers and a dog rushes into the corridor; the three follow. Plate I.7 pl1 (after ¶58) captions the signal. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il le projetait dans les ténèbres, et il poussait des sifflements aigus
  - quote: À ce signal, qui était attendu

### 6. Top is at the Chimneys, alone (I.7 ¶59–¶64)

- Wood is thrown on the embers; the corridor lights up. It is Top, a magnificent Anglo-Norman dog, with the speed and scent of both breeds, Cyrus Smith's dog. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: un magnifique anglo-normand
  - quote: C’était le chien de l’ingénieur Cyrus Smith.
- He is alone; neither his master nor Nab is with him. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Mais il était seul !
- The narrator says it is inexplicable that his instinct led him to the Chimneys, which he did not know, in such a night; and "more inexplicable still", Top is neither tired, nor exhausted, nor even soiled with mud or sand. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Top n’était ni fatigué, ni épuisé, ni même souillé de vase ou de sable !…

### 7. Setting out behind the dog (I.7 ¶65–¶72)

- Harbert caresses Top, who rubs his neck on the boy's hands. Spilett: if the dog is found, the master will be too. Harbert says Top will guide them. Pencroff does not object, feeling that Top's arrival may belie his conjectures. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Si le chien est retrouvé, le maître se retrouvera aussi !
  - quote: l’arrivée de Top pouvait donner un démenti à ses conjectures
- Pencroff covers the embers, puts wood under the ashes to find fire on their return, takes the remains of the supper, and goes out behind Top, who invites him with small barks. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: de manière à retrouver du feu au retour
  - quote: après avoir pris les restes du souper
- The storm is at its maximum. The moon, new and in conjunction with the sun, lets no glimmer through; they must trust Top. Spilett and Harbert walk behind the dog and the sailor last; no talk is possible. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: La lune, nouvelle alors, et, par conséquent, en conjonction avec le soleil
  - quote: Le mieux était de s’en rapporter à l’instinct de Top.
- The south-east wind is at their backs, with the sand; they go faster than they wish. They believe Nab found his master and sent the dog, but wonder whether the engineer lives or Nab calls them only for the last duties. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le vent chassait du sud-est, et, par conséquent, il les poussait de dos
  - quote: Ils ne mettaient pas en doute que Nab n’eût retrouvé son maître

### 8. The march north along the shore (I.7 ¶73–¶81)

- After passing the cut face of the high land, they stop to breathe after a quarter hour's march. Sheltered, they can hear each other; at Harbert's naming Cyrus Smith, Top barks softly as if to say his master is saved. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: après cette marche d’un quart d’heure
  - quote: comme s’il eût voulu dire que son maître était sauvé
- They resume at about half past two. The sea is starting to rise, a spring tide threatening to be very strong. The waves must go over the islet, which is invisible, so that the reef no longer shelters the coast. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Il était environ deux heures et demie du matin.
  - quote: une marée de syzygie
  - quote: elles devaient passer par-dessus l’îlot, absolument invisible alors
- They go north with the surf on their right and an unseen country on their left, which they judge flat because the hurricane now passes overhead without eddying back as it did against the granite. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Ils remontaient au nord, ayant sur leur droite une interminable crête de lames
- At four in the morning they judge they have covered five miles. The clouds lift a little; the squall is drier and colder. They suffer in their clothing without a complaint. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: À quatre heures du matin, on pouvait estimer qu’une distance de cinq milles avait été franchie.
- Dawn begins about five, grey at the zenith, the horizon line, the crests turning tawny; by six the day is made. They are about six miles from the Chimneys on a very flat beach, with reefs offshore, awash at the full of the tide. To the left: dunes with thistles, a sandy region; a few trees leaning west; far back to the south-west, the edge of the last forest. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Vers cinq heures, le jour commença à se faire.
  - quote: à six milles environ des Cheminées
  - quote: Bien en arrière, dans le sud-ouest, s’arrondissait la lisière de la dernière forêt.

### 9. The dunes and the cave (I.7 ¶82–¶87)

- Top shows agitation, runs ahead, returns to urge the sailor on, then leaves the beach and goes in among the dunes without hesitation. They follow; the country seems quite deserted. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: sans montrer une seule hésitation, il s’était engagé entre les dunes
  - quote: Pas un être vivant ne l’animait.
- The dune belt is wide, "a little Switzerland of sand"; only a prodigious instinct could find the way. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: C’était comme une petite Suisse de sable
- Five minutes after leaving the beach they come to a sort of excavation at the back of a high dune. Top stops and gives a clear bark. Spilett, Harbert and Pencroff go into the cave. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Cinq minutes après avoir quitté la grève
  - quote: Top s’arrêta et jeta un aboiement clair.
- Nab is there, kneeling by a body stretched on a bed of grass: the engineer Cyrus Smith. Plate I.7 pl2 (after ¶87) repeats the last line. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Nab était là, agenouillé près d’un corps étendu sur un lit d’herbes…
  - quote: Ce corps était celui de l’ingénieur Cyrus Smith.
