# The last match and the fire (outline)

*Chapter 00500: the French I.5 (`texts/fr-1875/05.md`). Before: 00400. After: 00600. Third person, past tense; Verne's narrator. Every beat is marked (Drafted.): it is what the French holds.*

## Outline

### 1. Making the Chimneys habitable (I.5 ¶1–¶11)

- When the raft is unloaded, Pencroff stops the corridors that make a draught, with sand, stones, interlaced branches and wet earth, and cuts off the upper loop of the &. One narrow winding passage is left on the side for smoke. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Un seul boyau, étroit et sinueux, qui s’ouvrait sur la partie latérale, fut ménagé
- The Chimneys are thus divided into three or four dark chambers; the central one is dry and high enough to stand in, with fine sand on the floor. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: divisées en trois ou quatre chambres
- Harbert and Pencroff talk as they work: perhaps their companions found better quarters; Harbert hopes they bring Smith back; Pencroff says Smith "was a man, and a real one". Harbert asks whether Pencroff despairs of seeing him again; Pencroff answers "God forbid". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: C’était un homme celui-là, et un vrai !
  - quote: Dieu m’en garde !
- The hearth is made of large flat stones at the foot of the first left corridor, at the mouth of the smoke passage; the wood is stored in one chamber. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: De larges pierres plates furent disposées au fond du premier couloir de gauche

### 2. The matchbox is gone (I.5 ¶12–¶34)

- Harbert asks whether Pencroff has matches. Pencroff says he does; without matches or tinder they would be in trouble. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: sans allumettes ou sans amadou, nous serions fort embarrassés !
- Harbert proposes rubbing two dry sticks as islanders of the Pacific do; Pencroff has tried it often and never managed it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: je n’ai jamais pu y parvenir !
- Pencroff's box never left him, for he is a heavy smoker; he searches jacket and trousers and finds nothing. Harbert has no lighter either. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il était un fumeur acharné
- They search the sand, the rocks and the river bank; the box was of copper and could not have escaped their eyes. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: La boîte était en cuivre et n’eût point échappé à leurs yeux.
- Pencroff did not throw the box out of the car, but thinks it vanished in the buffeting; even his pipe is gone. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Ma pipe, elle-même, m’a bien quitté !
- They go to the place where they landed "la veille", about two hundred paces from the Chimneys, and search the shingle and rocks as the sea withdraws; nothing. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le point où ils avaient atterri la veille, à deux cents pas environ des Cheminées
- Harbert says the matches would have been wetted by sea water; Pencroff answers that they were in a copper box that closed well. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Elles étaient dans une boîte en cuivre qui fermait bien !
- Harbert hopes Smith or Spilett have some; Pencroff doubts it: Nab and Smith do not smoke, and he fears Spilett kept his notebook rather than his matchbox. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Nab et M. Smith ne fument pas
  - quote: n’ait plutôt conservé son carnet que sa boîte d’allumettes
- They must give up the egg meal he meant to prepare, and the raw-meat diet does not appeal to him. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le régime de chair crue

### 3. A second gathering of lithodomes; the box is still not found (I.5 ¶35–¶37)

- Before going back they gather more lithodomes in case there is no fire. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: firent une nouvelle récolte de lithodomes
- Pencroff goes on searching, up the left bank to the raft's mooring angle and across the upper plateau and the forest edge, in vain. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le tout vainement
- They return to the Chimneys at five in the evening; the corridors are searched in every corner. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Il était cinq heures du soir

### 4. Nab and Spilett return without the engineer (I.5 ¶38–¶54)

- About six, as the sun sets behind the western heights, Harbert signals the return of Nab and Spilett. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Vers six heures, au moment où le soleil disparaissait derrière les hautes terres de l’ouest
- They are alone; Cyrus Smith has not been found. Spilett sits on a rock, exhausted and starving; Nab's eyes show he has wept. Plate I.5 pl1 (after ¶40): the reporter sat on a rock without a word. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: L’ingénieur Cyrus Smith n’avait pu être retrouvé !
  - quote: Le reporter s’assit sur une roche, sans mot dire.
- Spilett's account: he and Nab covered the coast over more than eight miles, well past the point of the "penultimate fall of the balloon" where the engineer and Top vanished. No trace, no footprint, no one frequents that stretch of coast; the sea was as empty as the shore, and the engineer's tomb must be a few hundred feet out. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: sur un espace de plus de huit milles
  - quote: chute qui avait été suivie de la disparition de l’ingénieur et du chien Top
  - quote: Il était évident qu’aucun habitant ne fréquentait cette portion de la côte.
- Nab refuses to believe Smith is dead, then collapses; he refuses the shellfish Harbert offers and will not eat. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: C’est un homme à revenir de tout !…
  - quote: Nab ne pouvait ou ne voulait plus vivre !
- Spilett eats the shellfish and lies down on the sand, exhausted but calm. Harbert leads him to the Chimneys. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Il était exténué, mais calme.
  - quote: nous avons découvert un abri où vous serez mieux qu’ici

### 5. Not a match among them; one is found (I.5 ¶55–¶70)

- Pencroff asks Spilett, in the most natural tone, whether he has a match; Spilett finds none and says he must have thrown them away. Nab says the same. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: J’en avais, mais j’ai dû tout jeter…
- Pencroff curses; the four castaways stand still, uneasy. Nab says his master would know how to make fire. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: s’il était là, mon maître, il saurait bien vous en faire !
- Harbert tells Spilett to search again; "a single match would do". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Une seule allumette nous suffirait !
- Spilett searches trouser, waistcoat and coat pockets and feels a small piece of wood caught in the lining of his waistcoat that he cannot draw out. Harbert draws it out with skill, unbroken. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: un petit morceau de bois engagé dans la doublure de son gilet
  - quote: Il était intact.
- Pencroff: it is as good as a whole cargo. He takes the match and returns with the others to the Chimneys. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: c’est comme si nous en avions une cargaison tout entière !

### 6. Lighting the fire (I.5 ¶71–¶82)

- Pencroff checks the match is dry and asks for paper; Spilett, after some hesitation, tears a leaf from his notebook. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: après quelque hésitation, déchira une feuille de son carnet
- Pencroff lays grass, leaves and dry moss under the faggots, folds the paper into a cone, and rubs the match gently on a slightly rough pebble. The first stroke does nothing. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Le premier frottement ne produisit aucun effet.
- His hand shakes; he hands over to Harbert. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ma main tremble…
- Harbert strikes it quickly: a small blue flame and acrid smoke; he turns the match to feed the flame and slides it into the paper cone; the paper and moss catch. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: une légère flamme bleuâtre jaillit en produisant une fumée âcre
- The narrator compares Harbert to Prometheus stealing fire from heaven. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Prométhée allant dérober le feu du ciel
- A cheerful flame, fanned by Pencroff, rises in the dark. Plate I.5 pl2 (after ¶80) carries Pencroff's cry: never so moved in his life. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: je n’ai jamais été si ému de ma vie !
- The smoke goes out by the narrow passage and the chimney draws. The fire must never be let go out; some embers must always be kept under the ash. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: conserver toujours quelque braise sous la cendre

### 7. The first supper (I.5 ¶83–¶86)

- Pencroff means to make a meal more nourishing than lithodomes; Harbert brings two dozen eggs. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Deux douzaines d’œufs furent apportées par Harbert.
- Spilett sits in a corner, thinking three questions: whether Cyrus lives, where he might be, and how, if he survived the fall, he has not found a way to make himself known. Nab prowls the beach, "a body without a soul". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: comment expliquer qu’il n’ait pas trouvé le moyen de faire connaître son existence ?
- Pencroff, who knows "fifty-two ways of dressing eggs", can only bury them in hot ashes. They are excellent; this is the first meal on the coast. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Pencroff, qui connaissait cinquante-deux manières d’accommoder les œufs
  - quote: Tel fut le premier repas des naufragés sur cette côte inconnue.
- The narrator says that if the five prisoners escaped from Richmond had all been there they would have only thanks to give; but Cyrus Smith, their undisputed chief, is missing and has not even had a burial. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Si les cinq prisonniers échappés de Richmond eussent été tous là
  - quote: son corps n’avait pu même obtenir une sépulture !

### 8. The night of 25 March (I.5 ¶87–¶90)

- The day is named: 25 March. Night has come; wind whistles, surf beats the coast, shingle rolls with a deafening roar. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Ainsi se passa cette journée du 25 mars.
- The reporter retires to the back of a dark corridor, having noted the day's events: first sight of this land, the disappearance of the engineer, the coast search, the matches. He sleeps. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: après avoir sommairement noté les incidents de ce jour
- Harbert sleeps soon; Pencroff keeps watch by the fire, feeding it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il passa la nuit près du foyer
- Nab alone does not sleep in the Chimneys: all night he wanders the beach calling his master. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: erra sur la grève en appelant son maître !
