# The Balloon in the Hurricane (notes)

## Notes for drafting

- **Where it sits.** The first chapter of Part I, "Les naufragés de l’air"; it opens in the middle of the fall and ends on the beach with one man missing. Nothing precedes it. *(Drafted.)*
- **Cast.** *(Drafted.)* unless marked.
  - The five passengers, first at ¶17 (the voices from ¶1): "cinq passagers". Only one is named here, by address: "monsieur Cyrus" (¶3).
  - A "voix puissante" (¶11) and a "voix mâle" (¶36) lead the throwing-out; the text does not say they are one man, or that either is Cyrus. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - Top, the dog, first at ¶52, named in ¶53; he is "près de son maître" (¶52), the master unnamed. *(Drafted.)*
  - The missing passenger, ¶63, unnamed; the chapter does not say who.
  - Havana and Guadeloupe are named only as earlier disasters (¶14).
- **Places.** *(Drafted.)*
  - The air over the Pacific (¶13); the zone of the storm from the 35th parallel north to the 40th south (¶14); Havana and Guadeloupe (¶14).
  - The land sighted to the south-west, "assez élevée" (¶56), "île ou continent" not known (¶58); the sandy beach where they land (¶61).
- **Things.** *(Drafted.)*
  - The balloon, 50,000 cubic feet (about 1,700 cubic metres), with a tear in its envelope (¶24, ¶32, ¶49, n2).
  - The wicker basket, cut away at about 2 p.m. (¶34, ¶46); the hoop and the net (¶25, ¶47).
  - Ballast, munitions, arms, provisions, pocket things, all thrown (¶5, ¶20, ¶25).
  - A sack of ten thousand francs in gold, thrown (¶38, ¶39).
  - An anchor is mentioned (¶28) but not used.
- **Time.** *(Drafted.)* unless marked.
  - 23 March 1865, about four in the evening: the cry (¶13).
  - The hurricane ran from 18 to 26 March (¶14); at 23 March it had lasted five days (¶18).
  - The night of 23/24 March (¶21). 24 March: dawn (¶21), about eleven (¶22), noon (¶24), one (¶31), two (¶35), four (¶51, ¶59), half past four (¶60, "Une demi-heure plus tard", from four) *(My reading, to confirm.)*, two minutes later the landing (¶61).
  - The chapter covers about a day and a half of story time (from about 4 p.m. on the 23rd to about 4.30 p.m. on the 24th); the hurricane's history runs back to the 18th.
- **Clues.** *(Drafted.)* unless marked.
  - Where the balloon came from and who the passengers are: "D’où venait cet aérostat" (¶18). Answered in I.2.
  - Who is missing: only "Le passager manquant" (¶64). I.2 ¶58 names him.
  - The land to the south-west, and where on the earth it lies: "c’est à peine s’ils savaient vers quelle partie du monde l’ouragan les avait entraînés" (¶58).
  - The balloon, relieved of the passengers' weight, "disparut dans l’espace" (¶62), after the tear that "il était impossible de réparer" (¶49).
  - The sudden bound after the sea blow, "délesté subitement d’une nouvelle partie de son poids" (¶61), which ¶64 explains by the missing man.
- **Length.** 66 numbered paragraphs (¶1–¶66), 2,375 words in them; the summary line ¶0 is 46 words more. Two footnotes, one plate caption (after ¶66). *(Drafted.)*

## Open and flags

- **Time of the 24th: two "four o'clock" lines.** ¶51: "À quatre heures, le ballon n’était plus qu’à cinq cents pieds de la surface des eaux." ¶59: "Or, à quatre heures, il était visible que le ballon ne pouvait plus se soutenir. Il rasait la surface de la mer." The same hour gives 500 feet and skimming the waves. Card neither. *(Drafted.)*
- **Two four o'clocks, two days.** ¶13 puts the opening cry at "vers quatre heures du soir, dans la journée du 23 mars 1865", with the sea "pas à cinq cents pieds" (¶10); ¶51 puts the balloon at five hundred feet at four o'clock on 24 March. *(Drafted.)*
- **When the hurricane ended.** ¶14: "Ce fut un ouragan, sans intermittence, qui dura du 18 au 26 mars." ¶22 (24 March): "Il paraissait s’être tué lui-même." *(Drafted.)*
- **Start in the storm, or before it.** ¶18: "Il n’avait évidemment pas pu partir pendant l’ouragan." Against I.2 ¶55 and ¶57: "le ballon, partant par une direction oblique, disparut" in the hurricane of 20 March (the hurricane began on the 18th by I.1 ¶18 and I.2 ¶20). *(Drafted.)*
- **Wind direction.** ¶14 "coup de vent de nord-est" and ¶30 "du nord-est au sud-ouest"; against I.2 ¶20, "un vent de nord-ouest de moyenne force", which turned to a hurricane. *(Drafted.)*
- **Distance.** ¶18 gives "deux mille milles par vingt-quatre heures" for five days; I.2 ¶1 and ¶57 give seven thousand and "plus de six mille" miles (see the notes of 00200).
- **Wikisource corrections.** None in I.1 (texts/README.md lists none for this chapter; the scan check read page 2, ¶4 to ¶17 and n1, and found a match). The text keeps the print's "très-loin" (¶18). *(Drafted.)*
- **Footnotes.** n1 and n2 are Hetzel's; n1 gives the metric speed, n2 the volume in cubic metres. *(Drafted.)*
