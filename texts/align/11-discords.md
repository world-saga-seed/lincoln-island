# I.11: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller e. Beads corrected: none.*

### 1. softened: imprisoned for life becomes "spend their lives" (I.11 ¶2; White ¶1)
- The French says Cyrus and his people may be imprisoned in the island for life; White says only that they may spend their lives there. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: emprisonnés pour la vie, peut-être
  - white: on which perhaps they were to spend their lives

### 2. changed: the eastern flank becomes the southern side (I.11 ¶5; White ¶4)
- The morning sun lights the whole eastern flank of the mountain in the French; White says the southern side. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: couvrait de ses rayons tout le flanc oriental de la montagne
  - white: the southern side of the mountain was bathed in sunlight.

### 3. cut: the footnote converting the coast to leagues (I.11 ¶17 n1; White —)
- White drops the footnote giving the hundred miles as about 45 leagues of 4 kilometres. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Environ 45 lieues de 4 kilomètres.
  - white: —

### 4. changed: a shark becomes a dog-fish (I.11 ¶22; White ¶15)
- The gulf is like the jaw of a formidable shark (squale) in the French, and the name Shark-gulf follows; White has a dog-fish. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: la mâchoire entr’ouverte de quelque formidable squale
  - white: the half-open jaws of some formidable dog-fish

### 5. changed: a creek at two-thirds of the way, not along two-thirds (I.11 ¶24; White ¶15)
- In the French the coast is cut by a narrow creek at two-thirds of its perimeter; White has the coast bordered by a creek along two-thirds of its length. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: creusé, aux deux tiers de son périmètre, par une étroite crique
  - white: For two-thirds of its length it was bordered by a narrow creek

### 6. changed: the thirty miles become the southern coast (I.11 ¶25; White ¶15)
- In the French the tail curves round into a wide open roadstead drawn by the island's lower coast; White says the thirty miles themselves are the southern coast describing an open bay. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: elle s’arrondissait en décrivant une rade foraine, largement ouverte
  - white: These thirty miles, the southern coast of the island, described an open bay.

### 7. changed: three hundred feet becomes "at least" three hundred (I.11 ¶27; White ¶16)
- Cyrus gives the lake's height as three hundred feet; White says at least three hundred feet. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: l’altitude de cette petite nappe d’eau devait être de trois cents pieds
  - white: it must be at least 300 feet higher

### 8. changed: no settlement of huts becomes "no late settlement" (I.11 ¶38; White ¶24)
- The French finds no cluster of huts, no lone cabin, no fishery on the coast; White has "no late settlement on the beach" and a fisherman's hut. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Pas une agglomération de cases, pas une cabane isolée, pas une pêcherie sur le littoral.
  - white: no late settlement on the beach, not even a lonely cabin or a fisherman’s hut.

### 9. changed: Malay praos become Malay canoes (I.11 ¶40; White ¶24)
- The craft that could cross fifty miles are praos in the French, canoes in White. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: soit par des praos malais, soit par de grandes pirogues polynésiennes
  - white: Malay canoes or by the larger pirogues of the Polynesians

### 10. softened: neighbouring natives become "savage neighbors" (I.11 ¶40; White ¶24)
- The precaution is against a possible landing of the neighbouring natives in the French; White says an attack from savage neighbours (sharpened). *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: prendre certaines précautions contre une descente possible des indigènes voisins
  - white: they must take precautions against an attack from savage neighbors.

### 11. cut: Nab's pledge (I.11 ¶46; White —)
- White gives Herbert's pledge of obedience but drops Nab's line. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Mon maître, toujours et partout !
  - white: —

### 12. addition: the Swiss family Robinson (I.11 ¶58; White ¶40)
- Verne has Pencroff say the names of the Robinsons, whose story Harbert has read to him; White names them the Swiss family Robinson. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Donnons-leur des noms comme faisaient les Robinsons
  - white: Let us give them names like those of the Swiss family Robinson

### 13. changed: the island under their eyes becomes "the inland bay" (I.11 ¶64; White ¶46)
- The island lies before them like an unfolded map in the French; White says the inland bay unrolled like a map. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: L’île était là sous leurs yeux comme une carte déployée
  - white: The inland bay unrolled like a map before their eyes

### 14. changed: the south-east point becomes the south-western (I.11 ¶73; White ¶54)
- Pencroff asks to name the point at the south-east end of the island; White has the south-western extremity, though Harbert's reply makes it the end of Union Bay. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Reste à nommer la pointe à l’extrémité sud-est de l’île
  - white: Now we must name the southwestern extremity of the island

### 15. condensed: the great citizen "who is now fighting" (I.11 ¶89; White ¶63)
- White reduces the man now fighting to defend the unity of the American republic to "the defender of American unity". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: de celui qui lutte maintenant pour défendre l’unité de la république américaine
  - white: of the defender of American unity

### 16. cut: the colonists talk of the war and of the cause of justice (I.11 ¶91; White —)
- White ends at the three hurrahs and drops the evening talk of the war, the certain defeat of the South and the triumph of the North's "cause of justice". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: la cause du Nord, la cause de la justice, ne triomphât, grâce à Grant, grâce à Lincoln !
  - white: —

### 17. cut: the date and Lincoln's death foretold (I.11 ¶92; White —)
- White drops the date, 30 March 1865, and the narrator's notice that sixteen days later Lincoln would fall to a fanatic's bullet on Good Friday. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le vendredi saint, Abraham Lincoln tomberait sous la balle d’un fanatique
  - white: —
