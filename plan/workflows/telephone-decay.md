# Telephone decay: wearing a text until a cold reader cannot see through it

*Speciality, for a book that needs it. Add it when an in-world text (a scripture, a legend, an old record) must hide the truth beneath it.*

How a full, writer-side text (a founder's own account, a document that tells what really happened) is worn into the version the world tells now, so that a cold reader enjoys it and cannot reach what is held back, while every planted clue survives. A text written straight to be vague reads as written to be vague; a text worn down from a true one keeps the true one's shape and grain.

## Setup

- **The source** is the full writer-side text, kept in the reader key, never on the page.
- **Held back:** a short list of truths the reader should not reach, each scored **caught**, **near** or **nebulous** after every round.
- **Clues kept:** a list that is worn but never removed ([never trim a clue](reader-tests.md#how-failures-route-back)); a round that loses one goes back.
- **Reserved words:** the setting's terms of art that would give the game away; a grep checks for them each round.

## Rounds

1. **Retell.** A reteller, given only the previous round, retells it as a later generation would. A retelling that comes back nearly word for word is sent back for real drift.
2. **Elaborate before you trim.** At least one elaboration (accretions, commentary, folk practice) comes before the trims, so the trims blur the details instead of leaving the bare structure. A trim straight after a faithful round exposes the structure.
3. **Cold read.** A fresh reader on the strongest model, given only the new round, answers the open prompt below. The supervisor scores it against the held-back list and logs the reading.
4. **Fix by wear, not by cut.** A truth that keeps leaking is worn into formula (a line becomes a rhyme, a refrain, a ritual phrase) rather than deleted.
5. **Stop** when the held-back items are nebulous, or at the round limit.

## Readers

- **The loaded prompt** ("read it critically, between the lines: what really happened, who wrote it and why") hunts, and hints. Use it to find leaks.
- **The open prompt** ("This is a text from a novel. What do you make of it?") is the test that matters. The text is judged as what its hearers in the story are told, so the last check is a reading as the character who hears it, given only who they are at that point.
- **Several models.** Other models read by genre and add their own herrings; a catch one makes only from the genre is accepted, not fixed.
- **Misreadings** go to the writer: drop, keep as a herring, or track and land.

## After the decay

- A final rewrite in a chosen manner may follow, checked by three critics: rules (no new events, the clues kept, no reserved words), style (the manner), and story (what was lost that hides nothing). The revision restores what the trims took from the story, leaving lost only what would reopen a held-back truth.
- **An in-world scripture must explain its world:** check it against the setting's rules and features, and give each its spun account. A believer's text that is silent on the world's plainest wonder reads as written by an outsider.

## Lessons

- **Scores move slowly and vary.** In the first run, the held-back truths went from caught on most to nebulous on five of six over six rounds. The richest version, 40% longer, held the score once critics restored the story. A single reading varies by one; read twice before acting on a shift.
- **A settled line the world rests on will keep a near catch.** Accept it rather than wear the setting away.
