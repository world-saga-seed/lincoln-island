# I.4: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller c. Beads corrected: none.*

### 1. changed: "pas" become "feet" (I.4 ¶8; White ¶8)
- The French gives the cleft as some hundreds of paces below the landing place; White says hundreds of feet. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: à quelques centaines de pas au-dessous de l’endroit où ils étaient débarqués
  - white: some hundreds of feet below the place where they had been thrown ashore

### 2. condensed: why Smith might be found south (I.4 ¶8; White ¶8)
- The French gives the reason that the current might have pushed Smith that way, and says only that it was not impossible; White says flatly that in that part of the island Smith might be found. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il n’était pas impossible que le courant eût poussé Cyrus Smith de ce côté
  - white: in that part of the island, too, Smith might be found

### 3. cut: the skuas and the gulls that nest in the granite (I.4 ¶9; White ¶9)
- The French has Pencroff name several skuas, a kind of gull also called stercoraires, and small voracious gulls nesting in the granite; White names no such birds here. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Pencroff reconnut plusieurs labbes, sortes de goëlands auxquels on donne quelquefois le nom de stercoraires
  - white: —

### 4. changed: "un grand nombre" becomes "a dozen" (I.4 ¶9; White ¶9)
- A shot into the flock would have brought down a great number in the French; White says a dozen. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: en eût abattu un grand nombre
  - white: would have brought down a dozen

### 5. cut: Harbert's instincts will serve again (I.4 ¶16; White ¶16)
- The French says his naturalist's instincts would be used more than once afterwards and that he did not err in this first test; White has no such sentence. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Aussi ses instincts de naturaliste devaient-ils être plus d’une fois utilisés par la suite, et, pour son début, il ne se trompa pas.
  - white: —

### 6. changed: eaten like oysters, a peppery taste (I.4 ¶18; White ¶18)
- The French says they ate them as oysters and found them strongly peppered; White says they tasted like oysters. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Ils les mangèrent comme des huîtres, et ils leur trouvèrent une saveur fortement poivrée
  - white: They tasted like oysters, with a peppery flavor

### 7. changed: "deux cents pas" become two hundred feet (I.4 ¶20; White ¶20)
- The French says two hundred paces to the cleft; White says feet. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Deux cents pas plus loin
  - white: Two hundred feet further on

### 8. cut: the river's sharp turn and the thicket half a mile up (I.4 ¶20; White ¶20)
- The French has the river turn sharply and vanish under a thicket half a mile away; White stops at the walls declining upstream. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: puis, elle tournait brusquement et disparaissait sous un taillis à un demi-mille
  - white: —

### 9. cut: the meaning of the sign & (I.4 ¶24; White ¶24)
- The French gives the sign & as meaning et cætera in abbreviation; White names the sign only. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: signe typographique &, qui signifie et cætera en abrégé
  - white: the typographical sign, &,

### 10. cut: Pencroff names the Chimneys (I.4 ¶27; White ¶27)
- The French says "Cheminées" was the name Pencroff kept for the provisional dwelling; White gives no such sentence. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ce fut le nom que Pencroff conserva à cette demeure provisoire
  - white: —

### 11. softened: the devil's trumpet becomes a whistle (I.4 ¶27; White ¶27)
- Pencroff's phrase that the devil plays his trumpet through the holes becomes the wind whistling. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: à travers lesquelles le diable joue de sa trompette
  - white: through which the wind whistles so shrilly

### 12. changed: the flood tide drives the wood back, in the French; carries it away, in White (I.4 ¶28; White ¶28)
- The French says the rising tide would push the dead wood back up the river to a considerable distance; White says the returning tide would carry it in the ebb to a great distance. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: devait le refouler avec force jusqu’à une distance assez considérable
  - white: would certainly carry it in the ebb to a great distance

### 13. changed: "deux cents pas" become two hundred feet again (I.4 ¶41; White ¶38)
- The French gives two hundred paces behind the angle of the river; White says feet. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: à deux cents pas en arrière de l’angle formé par la rivière
  - white: Two hundred feet behind the angle of the river

### 14. cut: Spilett and Nab may be out of sight (I.4 ¶42; White ¶39)
- The French adds that the two might be too far off to be seen; White ends at their not being seen. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Mais il était possible qu’en ce moment, tous deux fussent à une telle distance, qu’on ne pût les apercevoir.
  - white: —

### 15. changed: a shore without a cliff becomes a shore without rocks (I.4 ¶46; White ¶43)
- The French says the northern shore was low and flat, without cliff; White says without rocks. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le rivage était bas, plat, sans falaise
  - white: the shore was low and flat, without rocks

### 16. cut: the banks of the river, left and right (I.4 ¶47; White ¶44)
- The French has the left bank stay sheer and the right fall away by stages from rocks to pebbles as far as the point; White has nothing of the banks. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: mais si, sur sa rive gauche, les parois demeuraient nettes et abruptes, sur la rive droite, au contraire, elles s’abaissaient peu à peu
  - white: —

### 17. softened: Providence and Harbert's piety (I.4 ¶52–¶53; White ¶48)
- The French has Pencroff thank Providence and Harbert, of a pious heart, thank the Author of all things; White gives only that they must be grateful. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il faut en remercier la Providence
  - quote: dont le cœur pieux était plein de reconnaissance pour l’Auteur de toutes choses
  - white: For that they must be grateful.

### 18. cut: they cannot guess what the future holds (I.4 ¶54; White ¶48)
- The French says they looked long but could not imagine, after so brief an inspection, what the future held; White has no such sentence. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il était difficile d’imaginer, après une si sommaire inspection, ce que leur réservait l’avenir
  - white: —

### 19. changed: "quelques pas" become a few feet (I.4 ¶64; White ¶57)
- The French says the raft arrived a few paces from the Chimneys; White says feet. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: à quelques pas des Cheminées
  - white: a few feet from the Chimneys

## Outline slips

- Beat 8 of the outline says the raft is at the river mouth "within two hours"; the French says it arrived "before two", that is before two o'clock, the hour being one at the bend. *(Drafted.)*
  - outline: within two hours the raft is at the river mouth
  - quote: avant deux heures, il arrivait à l’embouchure
