# I.15: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller f. Beads corrected: none (bead 11 and bead 12 together carry French ¶10–¶11 against White ¶10–¶11, which is a harmless shift).*

### 1. changed: "what shall we be today?" (I.15 ¶2; White ¶2)
- Pencroff asks what they are to be that day, which sets up the next line (brickmakers and potters now to become metallurgists); White has him ask what they are going to do. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: que serons-nous aujourd’hui
  - white: “Well, what are we going to do to-day?”

### 2. cut: nightfall (I.15 ¶5; White ¶5)
- The French has the party come back to the Chimneys at nightfall; White has them retrace their steps with no hour. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: à la nuit tombante, ils rentraient aux Cheminées
  - white: the colonists retraced their steps to the Chimneys

### 3. cut: Pencroff's formal declaration (I.15 ¶6; White ¶6)
- The French says Pencroff had formally declared that a boat could not make the crossing; White has the judgment but not Pencroff as its author. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Pencroff l’avait formellement déclaré.
  - white: a small boat would certainly not be able to accomplish it

### 4. cut: where the iron ore lies (I.15 ¶7; White ¶7)
- The French places the engineer's deposits in the north-west part of the island; White says only that the engineer had discovered the ore. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dans la partie nord-ouest de l’île
  - white: the iron ore which the engineer had discovered

### 5. cut: the magnetic ore is not carbonated (I.15 ¶8; White ¶8)
- White drops "non carbonaté" from the first sample and "rapide et facile" from the Catalan method. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: du fer magnétique, non carbonaté
  - white: one was magnetic iron, and the other pyrites or sulphuret of iron

### 6. condensed: the blast-furnace method (I.15 ¶8; White ¶8)
- The French says the blast furnace first turns the ore into cast iron (fonte), then the cast iron into iron; White says it "first smelt the ore, and then the iron", with no cast iron named. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qui change d’abord le minerai en fonte, puis la fonte en fer
  - white: which first smelt the ore, and then the iron

### 7. cut: iron, not cast iron (I.15 ¶9; White ¶9)
- The French says what Cyrus needs is iron and not cast iron; White says only that he wanted iron by the shortest way. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: de fer et non de fonte
  - white: The engineer wanted to obtain iron in the shortest way possible.

### 8. cut: Sweden and Norway (I.15 ¶9; White ¶9)
- The French names Sweden and Norway as the source of the first-quality irons made from such ore; White says "in Europe". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dont la Suède et la Norwége sont si abondamment pourvues
  - white: in Europe the best quality of iron is made from it

### 9. cut: the United Kingdom (I.15 ¶9; White —)
- The French closes the paragraph with the wealth of the United Kingdom's works, where coal and ore lie together; White has nothing. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: C’est même ce qui fait la prodigieuse richesse des exploitations du Royaume-Uni
  - white: —

### 10. changed: islet becomes island; the hunt loses its purpose (I.15 ¶11; White ¶11)
- The French has Cyrus say that for the iron they will begin with a seal hunt on the islet (îlot); White says "But first", makes it a pleasure, and puts the hunt on "the island". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: nous commencerons par faire sur l’îlot la chasse aux phoques
  - white: “But first we will do something I think you will enjoy—have a seal hunt on the island.”

### 11. changed: manchots become sphemiscus (I.15 ¶16; White ¶15)
- The French has certain very innocent manchots whose wings are stumps; White calls them "sphemiscus". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: certains manchots très-innocents
  - white: certain innocent-looking sphemiscus

### 12. condensed: why seals are hard to take in the sea (I.15 ¶18; White ¶17)
- The French gives the seal's narrow pelvis, close short coat and spindle shape, and its short webbed feet; White says "owing to their shape". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: avec leur bassin étroit, leur poil ras et serré, leur conformation fusiforme
  - white: owing to their shape

### 13. addition: "huge" bellows (I.15 ¶26; White ¶23)
- The French calls it a blowing machine; White says "a huge pair of bellows". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: une machine soufflante, nécessaire pour le traitement du minerai
  - white: a huge pair of bellows

### 14. changed: Top's collar blades become two knives (I.15 ¶30; White ¶26)
- Cyrus works with the two steel blades from Top's collar; White says "the two knives". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: les deux lames d’acier provenant du collier de Top
  - white: Smith had nothing but the two knives to work with

### 15. cut: several tries (I.15 ¶30; White ¶26)
- The French says the skins had to be redone more than once; White has no repeat. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Il fallut s’y reprendre à plusieurs fois.
  - white: —

### 16. changed: "metallurgical period" becomes "iron age" (I.15 ¶31; White ¶27)
- The reporter's note names the period "la période métallurgique" (and White's own later sentence calls it the "first metallurgic period"); here White gives "the 'iron age'". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: « la période métallurgique »
  - white: the “iron age”

### 17. changed: dragged on a hurdle becomes carried (I.15 ¶32; White ¶28)
- Nab and Pencroff drag the bellows on a hurdle (claie) with vegetable and animal provisions; White has them "carrying" the bellows and "a stock of provisions". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Nab et Pencroff traînaient sur une claie la machine soufflante
  - white: Neb and Pencroff carrying the bellows and a stock of provisions

### 18. changed: leeks become an onion (I.15 ¶33; White ¶29)
- Pencroff calls the dragon trees "poireaux prétentieux" (pretentious leeks), a joke on their size and their family; White gives "overgrown onion", which also sits oddly beside the sentence that follows naming the onion among their relatives. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: que Pencroff traita de « poireaux prétentieux »
  - white: which Pencroff designated as an “overgrown onion,”

### 19. changed: arrows become shooting (I.15 ¶34; White ¶30)
- Harbert and Spilett kill two kangaroos with arrows; White says they "shot" them. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Harbert et Gédéon Spilett tuèrent deux kangourous à coups de flèche
  - white: Herbert and Spilett shot two kangaroos

### 20. changed: koula becomes ai (I.15 ¶38; White ¶34)
- The French gives the animal as a "koula", better known as a sloth; White gives "ai", which is another name for the same beast. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: Ce n’était qu’un « koula », plus connu sous le nom de « paresseux »
  - white: It was an ai, better known as a sloth

### 21. cut: the date and the ancient ground (I.15 ¶41; White ¶37)
- The French dates the search 21 April and says Cyrus looks for the grounds of ancient formation; White has "The next morning" and the place of the specimen. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Le lendemain, 21 avril, Cyrus Smith, accompagné d’Harbert, alla rechercher ces terrains de formation ancienne
  - white: The next morning, Smith, accompanied by Herbert, went to look for the place where they had found the specimen of ore.

### 22. cut: the first metallurgists (I.15 ¶42; White ¶38)
- The French has the cubic heap as the way Tubal-Cain "and the first metallurgists of the inhabited world" worked, and says it still gives good results where ore and fuel are plentiful; White keeps Tubal Cain only. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Tubal-Caïn et les premiers métallurgistes du monde habité
  - white: This was, probably, what Tubal Cain did.

### 23. changed: Adam's grandsons become Adam's grandson (I.15 ¶42; White ¶38)
- The French has Adam's petits-fils (descendants); White has the singular. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ce qui avait réussi avec les petits-fils d’Adam
  - white: a process which gave such good results to Adam’s grandson

### 24. addition: a gloss on refractory earth (I.15 ¶44; White ¶40)
- White glosses "refractory earth" in a parenthesis. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: un tuyau en terre réfractaire
  - white: a pipe of refractory earth (an earth difficult of fusion)

### 25. changed: the bloom becomes a pig of iron (I.15 ¶45; White ¶41)
- The French has a loupe (a bloom of spongy iron), which must be hammered (cingler et corroyer, "c'est-à-dire forger") to drive out the gangue; White calls it "a pig of iron", the cast iron which the engineer has not made, and says it "must be cut and forged". The same word recurs in White ¶42 ("The first pig"). *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: une loupe de fer, réduite à l’état d’éponge, qu’il fallut cingler et corroyer
  - white: a pig of iron in a spongy state, which must be cut and forged

### 26. condensed: the tools of ¶51 (I.15 ¶51; White ¶46)
- The French lists plane blades, axes, hatchets, steel bands for saws, carpenter's chisels, and the iron for pick, spade and mattock; White says "planes and hatchets", drops "axes" and the plane blades become planes. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: lames de rabot, haches, hachettes
  - white: such as planes and hatchets
