## Task 1
### 03200, beat 7
- judgement: confirmed
- reason: The French gives a floor ("pas ... moins de"). The outline's "perhaps" turns it into a guess. The fix is plain: "not less than" or "at least", with no "perhaps". The "eighty feet" part is right.
  - outline: It is a southern whale, eighty feet long, perhaps one hundred and fifty thousand pounds.
  - quote: c’était une baleine australe, longue de quatre-vingts pieds, un géant de l’espèce, qui ne devait pas peser moins de cent cinquante mille livres !
  - at: II.10 ¶51

### 04100, beat 7
- judgement: confirmed
- reason: Verne hedges the claim with "peut-être", and the outline states it flat. The fix is plain: "perhaps without equal anywhere in the world". The rest of the beat is accurate. Two things are not wrong but could be said: the sight is "d’étrangeté", and it is "vu de la mer" because from the summit they were too high ("ils le dominaient de trop haut").
  - outline: From the summit of Mount Franklin they had not seen how grim it is; from the sea it is unmatched.
  - quote: mais, vu de la mer, ce littoral se présentait avec un caractère d’étrangeté, dont l’équivalent ne se rencontrait peut-être pas en aucun coin du monde.
  - at: II.19 ¶47

### 04200, beat 8
- judgement: confirmed
- reason: The beat's point says only that Ayrton "promises to come". The French says he did, about mid-April, and then shared the common life. The quote "Ce qu’il fit vers la mi-avril" is attached to the point, but the point's own words leave the date and consequence out. Nothing else in the beat states them, and the fix is plain. A later chapter depends on this (Ayrton at Granite-house from mid-April). The point also drops that Ayrton is "toujours humble et triste" and takes no part in the others' pleasures.
  - outline: Ayrton is given such clothes; Smith offers him a place at Granite-house for the winter and he promises to come when the corral work is done.
  - quote: Ayrton promit de le faire, dès que les derniers travaux du corral seraient terminés. Ce qu’il fit vers la mi-avril. Depuis ce temps-là, Ayrton partagea la vie commune et se rendit utile en toute occasion ; mais, toujours humble et triste, il ne prenait jamais part aux plaisirs de ses compagnons !
  - at: II.20 ¶73
