# Logic check, 01950 after the read: time and the setting's rules

Four findings: none breaks; two strain; two are nits. Nothing held, open or logged in `plan/state.md` is repeated. Every quote below was checked with grep -F.

## 1. The float's road to the islet runs against the flood, the wind and the surge (strains)

- Outline, beat 5, *(Settled R1121-110.)*: "the backwash draws the freed float off the bar into the gut, and the channel's current, "un courant extrêmement rapide" (I.3 ¶42), takes it onto the islet's point. The wind is onshore, so the sea alone would drive it to the coast."
- Outline, beat 5, *(Proposal.)*: "The flood and the squall come together, and the surge covers the bar (R1121-53)."
- Ledger R1121-110: "The flood sets the current along the channel, not across."
- The channel card, world/places/the-channel.md, *(Drafted.)*: "c’est un courant de jusant" (I.3 ¶47: the rapid current of ¶42 is the ebb); "le flot commençait à monter avec sa violence habituelle" and "les convicts se sentirent entraînés vers la rivière" (III.3 ¶36: the flood carries toward the Mercy, that is, toward the coast the bar lies on).
- The islet lies "séparés par un canal large d’un demi-mille" (I.3 ¶42) to the east of the bar; the wind is east-north-east and the squall "out of the east".

Why it fails: the current the ruling borrows from I.3 ¶42 is the ebb, and the float is freed an hour after low water, on the flood. The French's own flood runs toward the river, and the wind, the surge and the squall all push west, toward the coast. Every force the notes name on 2 June sends the float to the beach or the river mouth, none across half a mile to the islet's point. The islet is where it must land: only there can "no one ever find a piece" (R1121-68) stay true. No line says which way the flood sets in the gut or where the point lies relative to the bar.

Fixes:
1. (Plain; recommended.) Add one settled line, with its mark, to the channel card and R1121-110's reasoning: on the flood the gut's stream, running off the Mercy's bar past the islet's point, takes what the backwash frees. This is an addition to the cards, not a change to the chapter. The writer or the author must supply it, since the French gives the flood only as carrying toward the river (III.3 ¶36); the notes cannot derive it.
2. Keep the page as it is (it says only that the float is gone) and log the geography as a Verne-style discord in rule 2's list, since the page itself never states the route.
3. Free the float on the last of the ebb, before low water. This needs the squall to come before the low, against item 9 of the notes, and R1121-53, so it costs more.

## 2. The low-water hours are not the text's own tide, though rule 9 says they should be (strains)

- Notes, item 7, *(Settled R1121-80.)*: "Pick: low water about 8 in the morning on 1 June, about 9 on the 2nd and about 9:45 on the 3rd" and "The hours are from general knowledge for 35° south in June, not from the French."
- World rule 9, *(Settled R1121-48.)*: "Moon and tide follow the text's own 1865 phases".
- I.3 ¶58-60: "Dans trois heures, nous tenterons le passage" ... "vers dix heures, Gédéon Spilett" wade at low water on 25 March, two days before the new moon. I.7 ¶81 on 27 March, the new moon: "car on était au plein de la mer" at six in the morning.

Why it fails: the two French readings agree with each other. Low water falls about 10 on 25 March, about 11:40 at the new moon, and high water about 6 on the 27th. The new moon of 25 May (27 March plus two lunations) puts 1 June seven days later, about five hours and fifty minutes later in the day's clock. That gives low water about 5:30 on 1 June, about 6:20 on the 2nd and about 7:10 on the 3rd, with the evening low about 6 pm on the 1st. The chapter's 8, 9 and 9:45 sit two and a half to three hours later. On the French's reckoning the flood is already making by the time they go out at eight on the 2nd, and the bar is covered; the "hour of low water" at the float and the squall's surge an hour after low water come before dawn. The hours are the notes' own and nobody can check them on the page. The cost shows only if a reader works the table.

Fixes:
1. (Plain; recommended.) Leave the hours and log the discord: add the chapter's tide hours to the rule-2 list in the timeline (the French's two datums against item 7's), as Verne's discords are listed. Say in item 7 that the hours are the insert's own and depart from the datum of I.3 ¶60 and I.7 ¶81.
2. Re-time the three days to 5:30, 6:20 and 7:10. Pencroff would then see the float in the dark of 1 June, and 2 June's outing would be at first light. This is a large change to beats 1 to 3 and is not worth it for a figure no page states.
3. Name no hour on the page except where the beats need it, so no reading can contradict it.

## 3. A flash across half a mile in a squall is read as the ball breaking (nit)

- Outline, beat 5, *(Settled R1121-119.)*: "They see it break, as R1121-68 has it: the flash flies apart, seen across the channel, not up close."
- Channel card, *(Drafted.)*: "séparés par un canal large d’un demi-mille" (I.3 ¶42); the islet's point lies at least that far from the wall.

Why it fails: they wait "under the granite wall near the foot of the ladder, where they can still see the float" (R1121-70), in a rain squall, and the ball is a foot across. At more than half a mile in spray they can see a glint on wet glass at best, not the ball "fly apart". It works as a witnessed flash that they take for the glass going, which is what the settled wording "a flash" gives.

Fixes:
1. (Plain; recommended.) Keep "a flash in the spray" and let the two infer the break. R1121-68 already says "They know it shattered". Read "see it break" in the outline as "see a flash, and know".
2. Let them see it nearer: not possible without moving the rocks off the islet, so not advised.

## 4. The shelter under a wall that faces the wind (nit)

- Outline, beat 3, *(Settled R1121-127, R1121-128.)*: "The wind is east-north-east, beating on the facade".
- Outline, beat 5, *(Settled R1121-70.)*: "They wait it out under the granite wall near the foot of the ladder, where they can still see the float."
- 01900 outline, seam: "The bad north-east winds hit it only aslant." (I.19 ¶13); the facade "faces east" (I.19).

Why it fails: nothing is said of what shelters them for the hours from the squall to early afternoon. A wall that faces an east-north-east wind with the squall "out of the east" does not give a lee, and the first ladder's landing is forty feet up (I.19 ¶28). The notes lack the line that gives the cause: the salient over the Mercy's mouth (I.19 ¶13), a lee under a rock or the overhang.

Fixes:
1. (Plain; recommended.) Name one cause in a clause on the page, from what the French gives: the salient at the Mercy's mouth, which stands against the north-east, or the rocks at the wall's foot.
2. Leave it unexplained; the reader will not press it.
