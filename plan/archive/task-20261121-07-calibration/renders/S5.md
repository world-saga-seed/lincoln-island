# S5: II.2 ¶14

¶14

The boat therefore followed the shore at a distance of two cables' lengths at most, avoiding the reefs with which these approaches were strewn, and which the rising tide was beginning to cover. The wall went on lowering from the mouth of the river to the point. It was a heap of granite rocks, capriciously distributed, very different from the curtain which formed the plateau of Grande-Vue, and of an extremely wild aspect. One would have said that an enormous cart-load of rocks had been emptied out there. There was no vegetation upon this very sharp projection, which ran out two miles in front of the forest, and the point pretty well represented the arm of a giant coming out of a sleeve of verdure.
