You are judging one English translation of a chapter of Jules Verne's *L'Île mystérieuse* (1875). It was meant to be complete and faithful to the French, nothing cut, added, changed or softened, and written as Stephen W. White's 1876 American translation would read: period American English, American spelling, White's forms of the names (Herbert, Neb, Gideon Spilett, Cyrus Smith). You do not know who or what made it; judge only the text.

The French is first, then the translation, marked TEXT A.

Score each on 1 to 10, and give the evidence for each score (paragraph or a short quote):
1. Completeness: is every sentence of the French there? List every cut, with the French it drops.
2. Accuracy: list every changed fact, figure, date, name or sense, French beside English.
3. Force: list every place the English softens or sharpens the French.
4. Period voice: list every word, sense or idiom later than 1876, every British spelling, every modern turn of phrase.
5. Prose: does it read as a good translation of its time, or as a modern one in costume?
6. Modern-model habits: list each (triplets for rhythm, stated feelings the French leaves implicit, added summaries, stock phrases), with counts.

End with: the six scores; the counts (cuts, changed facts, softenings, anachronisms, habits); and one sentence on the text's main strength and main fault. Be strict and specific; do not praise in general terms.
