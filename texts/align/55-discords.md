# III.13: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller p. Beads corrected: none.*

### 1. cut: the bodies left alone; Ayrton cannot know how they died (III.13 ¶3; White ¶3)
- White drops that the colonists waited all night without going back to where the convicts' bodies lay, and the reasoning that Ayrton could tell nothing of their deaths since he did not know he was in the corral house. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: sans retourner à cette place où gisaient les corps des convicts
  - white: remained all night in the house
  - quote: puisqu’il ne savait pas lui-même être dans la maison du corral
  - white: —

### 2. condensed: a hundred and four days (III.13 ¶4; White ¶3)
- The French gives the length of the separation as a hundred and four days; White says "all these months". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: après cent quatre jours de séparation
  - white: after all these months of separation

### 3. cut: when the convicts found the corral (III.13 ¶11; White ¶10)
- White drops that the convicts found the corral soon after landing on the island. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: les convicts avaient découvert le corral, peu de temps après leur arrivée sur l’île
  - white: Meanwhile the convicts lived upon the stock in the corral, but did not inhabit the place.

### 4. changed: the south coast becomes the west coast (III.13 ¶15; White ¶15)
- The convicts' rare excursions were hunting trips in the interior or as far as the south coast; White says "the west coast" and drops that they were hunting. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: soit à l’intérieur de l’île, soit jusque sur la côte méridionale
  - white: only to a point in the interior or on the west coast

### 5. cut: two days of prostration (III.13 ¶16; White ¶16)
- White drops that Ayrton has been unable to say what happened for two days. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: c’est-à-dire depuis deux jours
  - white: From this moment, he could not say what had happened.

### 6. cut: death that must have been instant; the bodies stiff (III.13 ¶22–¶24; White ¶20–¶21)
- White drops that the position of the bodies was that of a death that must have been like a thunderbolt, and that the bodies were already stiffened by cold. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dans la position où les avait surpris une mort qui avait dû être foudroyante
  - white: in the position in which they had met their deaths
  - quote: déjà raidis par le froid
  - white: —

### 7. changed: the thunderbolt weapon (III.13 ¶29–¶30; White ¶24–¶25)
- Smith calls it a weapon that strikes like lightning, whose secret they lack; White has "a destructive weapon enough, though unknown to us", and Pencroff's "foudroyés" becomes "destroyed". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Une arme foudroyante dont nous n’avons pas le secret !
  - white: A destructive weapon enough, though unknown to us!

### 8. changed: the avenger becomes the ruler (III.13 ¶31; White ¶26)
- Smith names the one who struck the convicts as the island's "justicier", its dispenser of justice; White has "the ruler of the island". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Le justicier de l’île
  - white: The ruler of the island

### 9. cut: the burial, and the fear that Ayrton was dead (III.13 ¶38–¶39; White ¶32)
- White drops that the bodies were carried some distance from the corral and buried deep, and that the colonists no longer hoped to see Ayrton and feared the convicts had massacred him. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: à quelque distance du corral, et ils les enterrèrent profondément
  - white: buried them in the wood
  - quote: ils n’espéraient plus revoir Ayrton et avaient à redouter que les convicts ne l’eussent impitoyablement massacré
  - white: —

### 10. cut: the notice to name where Ayrton is, to take him back (III.13 ¶50; White ¶43)
- Pencroff's notice on Tabor is to say where Ayrton now is, in case the Scottish yacht comes to take him back; White has a notice "indicating our island" only. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: où se trouve actuellement Ayrton, pour le cas où le yacht écossais viendrait le reprendre
  - white: We most put up a notice, indicating our island, in case the Scotch yacht returns.

### 11. changed: the loss to be repaired "au plus tôt" (III.13 ¶64; White ¶57)
- The French agrees the loss must be repaired as soon as possible, and then only the search is thought of; White ties the repair to the end of the search. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il fut convenu que cette perte devrait être réparée au plus tôt
  - white: it was agreed that this loss must be repaired as soon as the search was ended.

### 12. cut: Ayrton was held in the cave until his transport (III.13 ¶65–¶66; White ¶58–¶59)
- White drops that the search might be inside the mountain mass, and that the cave is where Ayrton was held until his transport to the corral. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: peut-être même à l’intérieur du massif du mont Franklin
  - white: and it was here that the explorations must be made
  - quote: dans laquelle il avait été séquestré jusqu’à son transport au corral
  - white: Here was where Ayrton showed them the cavern of the convicts.

### 13. changed: which valleys are wooded and which border the corral's (III.13 ¶68; White ¶60)
- The French says that only three valleys are wooded and rich in pasture, like the corral's valley, which borders the Falls River valley on the west and the Red Creek valley on the east; White puts the Falls River and Red Creek as the bounds of the three valleys, and drops "boisées". White also drops the conifers of the valley by the cave and that the Serpentine Peninsula is watered by springs like the Mercy's. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: trois seulement étaient boisées et riches en pâturages comme celle du corral, qui confinait par l’ouest à la vallée de la rivière de la Chute, et, par l’est, à la vallée du creek Rouge
  - white: three only, bounded on the west by Fall River and on the east by Red Creek, were as rich and fertile as the valley of the corral
  - quote: abreuvaient le sol de la presqu’île Serpentine
  - white: —

### 14. condensed: the northern valleys, and the lava's origin (III.13 ¶71; White ¶62)
- White drops that the two valleys are wide and shallow, the erratic blocks, long moraines, mineral swellings, and the last sentence that the blocks lie as the volcano threw them up when the island emerged. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: larges, peu profondes, sans apparence de verdure, semées de blocs erratiques, zébrées de longues moraines
  - white: two large, arid valleys strewn with lava, sown with huge rocks
  - quote: tels le volcan les avait projetés au-dessus des eaux à l’époque de l’émersion de l’île
  - white: —

### 15. changed: the depression lies between the plateau and the mountain (III.13 ¶84; White ¶76)
- Smith says that between Grande-Vue and the mountain the ground is markedly lower, so lava reaching toward the lake would be thrown on the dunes; White places the depression "between it and the lake". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Entre lui et la montagne, le sol est notablement déprimé
  - white: Between it and the lake there is quite a depression in the land

### 16. changed: the volcano is the chimney, not the valve (III.13 ¶86; White ¶78)
- In Smith's figure the island is the boiler and the volcano the chimney; White calls the volcano "the valve", which is already the name of the crater. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ni le volcan, qui est la cheminée
  - white: nor the volcano, which is the valve

### 17. softened: the sailor's "special god" (III.13 ¶93; White ¶85)
- The narrator calls the island's protector the sailor's "special god"; White has "the guardian of the island". *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: la confiance du marin envers le dieu spécial de son île était absolue
  - white: The confidence of the sailor in the guardian of the island was absolute

### 18. changed: the northern region becomes the western (III.13 ¶94; White ¶86)
- The search from 19 to 25 February spreads to the whole northern region; White says the western portion. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le cercle des investigations fut étendu à toute la région septentrionale de l’île Lincoln
  - white: the investigations were conducted in the western portion of Lincoln Island

### 19. softened: anger becomes annoyance (III.13 ¶97; White ¶89)
- The French has "a sort of anger" in Smith's disappointment; White has them "a trifle annoyed". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il y avait comme une sorte de colère dans la déconvenue de Cyrus Smith et de ses compagnons
  - white: they were a trifle annoyed at their ill success

### 20. softened: the wildest hypotheses (III.13 ¶98; White ¶90)
- The colonists' imaginations are haunted by "les plus folles hypothèses"; White has "strange thoughts floated". *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: les plus folles hypothèses hantèrent leurs imaginations surexcitées
  - white: strange thoughts floated through their over-excited imaginations

### 21. cut: the date of the anniversary (III.13 ¶100; White ¶91)
- White drops the day, 25 March. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: au vingt-cinquième jour de mars
  - white: One month later, they celebrated the third anniversary of their arrival on Lincoln Island.
