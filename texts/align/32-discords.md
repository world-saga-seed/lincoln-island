# II.10: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller j. Beads corrected: none. (Inset lines and notes after ¶61, the footnote n2 at ¶62, are read in `texts/fr-1875/32.md`.)*

### 1. changed: the sloop's sails (II.10 ¶3; White ¶2)
- The French names brigantine, trinquette, fortune, flèche and jib; White lists mainsail, topsail, jib, storm-jib and brigantine, a different set. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: gréé en sloop, avec brigantine, trinquette, fortune, flèche, foc
  - white: sloop-rigged with mainsail, topsail, jib, storm-jib and brigantine

### 2. addition: timbers "bent by steam" (II.10 ¶3; White ¶2)
- The French says the frames are applied hot (à chaud); White says they are bent by steam, which names a means the French does not. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: on l’appliquerait à chaud
  - white: her timbers would be bent by steam

### 3. cut: fir "a little apt to split", and elm and fir abundant (II.10 ¶4; White ¶3)
- The French says elm and fir abound on the island and that fir is a little "fendif" (splits easily) but works easily and bears water as well as elm; White gives only that deal is easier to work and bears immersion better. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: L’orme ou le sapin, qui abondaient dans l’île ?
  - white: whether to use elm or deal

### 4. changed: "between the Chimneys and the cliff" becomes "between Granite House and the Cliff" (II.10 ¶6; White ¶5)
- The yard is in the recess between the Chimneys and the cliff wall; White puts it between Granite House and the Cliff. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: entre les Cheminées et la muraille
  - white: between Granite House and the Cliff

### 5. cut: Pencroff's Brooklyn yard (II.10 ¶7; White ¶6)
- The French says Pencroff had worked some years in a Brooklyn yard; White says only that he had worked as a ship-carpenter. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ayant travaillé quelques années dans un chantier de Brooklyn
  - white: Pencroff, who had worked as a ship-carpenter

### 6. cut: 130,000 grains to the bushel (II.10 ¶11; White ¶9)
- Smith's reckoning of 130,000 grains to the bushel is dropped; White gives only the product. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: à cent trente mille grains par boisseau
  - white: “Five bushels,” answered the engineer, “or 650,000 grains of corn.”

### 7. changed: the reporter's compass becomes the engineer's (II.10 ¶19; White ¶17)
- In the French it is the reporter who never ventures into the Far-West woods without the pocket compass; White says the engineer, who is not there. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le reporter ne s’y hasardait jamais sans emporter la boussole de poche
  - white: the engineer never undertook it without taking with him the pocket compass

### 8. changed: koulas become "ai" (II.10 ¶19; White ¶17)
- The three herbivores are koulas (koalas); White names them "ai", the three-toed sloth, a different animal. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: C’étaient des koulas
  - white: three ai were shot

### 9. cut: the koula seen before, and how they were killed (II.10 ¶19; White ¶17)
- The French says the colonists had already seen one koula north of the lake and that these let themselves be killed stupidly among the branches where they had taken refuge; White omits both. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dont les colons avaient déjà vu un échantillon au nord du lac
  - white: —

### 10. cut: how the tobacco leaves were prepared (II.10 ¶33; White ¶27)
- The French says the thin leaves were dried, chopped and roasted on hot stones; White says only "prepare the leaves". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: sécher les feuilles minces, les hacher, les soumettre à une certaine torréfaction sur des pierres chaudes
  - white: it took to prepare the leaves

### 11. cut: where the Whale-Field lies (II.10 ¶40; White ¶34)
- Harbert says English and American fishermen call this part of the Pacific the Whale-Field, between New Zealand and South America; White has "whalers" and drops the bounds, and the footnote translating the name. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: entre la Nouvelle-Zélande et l’Amérique du Sud
  - white: and just here whales are found in the greatest number

### 12. cut: the whale watched through the telescope (II.10 ¶44; White ¶37)
- The French has Harbert, Spilett and Nab watching the whale with the telescope from the Granite House windows or the plateau; White does not. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ne quittaient pas la lunette et observaient tous les mouvements de l’animal
  - white: —

### 13. cut: the whale's course and speed in the bay (II.10 ¶44; White ¶37)
- The French has the whale ploughing Union Bay from Cape Mandible to Cape Claw, at times at twelve miles an hour, and sometimes coming near the islet; White says it swam about in the bay and approached the shore. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: depuis le cap Mandibule jusqu’au cap Griffe
  - white: He kept swimming about in Union Bay

### 14. cut: the southern whale's flatter head (II.10 ¶44; White ¶37)
- The French says the southern whale's head is flatter than that of the northern whales; White omits it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dont la tête est plus déprimée que celle des baleines du nord
  - white: his black body could be seen perfectly

### 15. addition: "for two days" (II.10 ¶44; White ¶37)
- White gives the whale two days in the bay; the French gives no span. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: la baleine signalée sembla ne point vouloir abandonner les eaux de l’île
  - white: He kept swimming about in Union Bay for two days

### 16. cut: whether it blows vapour or water (II.10 ¶45; White ¶37)
- The French says naturalists and whalers disagree on whether the whale spouts vapour or water; White says only "spouting vapor". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: les naturalistes et les baleiniers ne sont pas encore d’accord à ce sujet
  - white: spouting vapor to a vast height in the air

### 17. cut: Pencroff dreams aloud and would have gone after it (II.10 ¶46; White ¶37)
- Pencroff dreams aloud of the whale at night and would not have hesitated to chase it had the boat been fit; White says only that he longed like a child. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: La nuit, il en rêvait à voix haute
  - white: Pencroff was like a child longing for some forbidden object

### 18. cut: "three miles" and the route to Wreck Point (II.10 ¶49; White ¶39)
- The French puts the stranding three miles from Granite House and has the party cross the Mercy bridge and go down the right bank; White gives neither the distance nor the bank. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: à trois milles de Granite-house
  - white: It had grounded on Jetsam Point at high water

### 19. changed: eighty feet becomes forty-five (II.10 ¶51; White ¶41)
- The whale's length is eighty feet in the French, forty-five in White, while the weight is the same. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: longue de quatre-vingts pieds
  - white: measuring forty-five feet in length

### 20. cut: the footnote on the Vineyard (II.10 ¶62 n2; White —)
- The French footnote glosses the Vineyard as a port of New York State; White has no note. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Port de l’état de New York.
  - white: —

### 21. cut: nobody flinched, and Spilett "a very good castaway" (II.10 ¶66; White ¶56)
- The French says no colonist was put off by the work, not even Spilett, who Pencroff says will make a very good castaway; White omits it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: finirait par faire « un très-bon naufragé »
  - white: —

### 22. cut: why the blubber is rendered away from the plateau (II.10 ¶67; White ¶56)
- The vats are brought to the spot so as not to foul the approaches of Prospect Heights; White gives no reason. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: car on ne voulait pas empester les abords du plateau de Grande-Vue
  - white: large earthen vats, which had been brought on to the ground

### 23. cut: the lower lip's four thousand pounds (II.10 ¶67; White ¶56)
- The lower lip gives four thousand pounds of oil besides the tongue's six thousand; White omits the lip. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: la langue seule donna six mille livres d’huile, et la lèvre inférieure quatre mille
  - white: the tongue alone yielded 6,000 pounds of oil

### 24. cut: the baleen described (II.10 ¶67; White ¶56)
- The French gives eight hundred horny elastic blades on the upper jaw with teeth six feet long, like two combs; White has "the whalebone" only. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: huit cents lames cornées, très-élastiques
  - white: there was, besides, the whalebone

### 25. changed: Aleut hunters of Russian America become inhabitants of the Aleutian islands (II.10 ¶75; White ¶63)
- The device is used by Aleut hunters in Russian America; White says the inhabitants of the Aleutian islands. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: chasseurs aléoutiens dans l’Amérique russe
  - white: the inhabitants of the Aleutian islands

### 26. softened: "divine providence, sacred author of all things" becomes "Heaven be praised" (II.10 ¶95; White ¶79)
- Pencroff's invocation of divine providence and the sacred author of all things is reduced to a mild exclamation. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: Oh ! divine providence ! Auteur sacré de toutes choses !
  - white: “Heaven be praised!”

### 27. changed: "for life and death" becomes "eternally grateful" (II.10 ¶101; White ¶84)
- Pencroff's closing "from now it is for life and death" is replaced by gratitude. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Maintenant, c’est à la vie, à la mort !
  - white: Meanwhile I am eternally grateful!
