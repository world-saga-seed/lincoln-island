# II.13: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller k. Beads corrected: bead 25 and bead 26 (French ¶32 belongs with White ¶25, so bead 25 is French ¶31 with White ¶24, and bead 26 is French ¶32–¶33 with White ¶25); beads 32 to 34 (French ¶41–¶43, Pencroff's cry and the joy of the crew, are White ¶32–¶34; bead 32 is French ¶40 with White ¶31 alone); beads 45 to 47 (French ¶61 is White ¶45, French ¶62 is White ¶46, French ¶63 has no White, French ¶64 is White ¶47).*

### 1. changed: "some hundreds of miles" becomes "some hundred miles" (II.13 ¶1; White ¶1)
- The French says the castaway is some hundreds of miles away; White says "some hundred". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: à quelques cents milles de nous
  - white: some hundred miles from us

### 2. changed: the castaway's seamanship becomes being "well informed" (II.13 ¶6; White ¶6)
- Smith's first conclusion in the French is that the castaway has fairly advanced knowledge of seamanship; White says only that he is a man well informed. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: un homme ayant des connaissances assez avancées en marine
  - white: a man well informed

### 3. condensed: the coordinates match "to a minute" (II.13 ¶6; White ¶6)
- The French says the latitude and longitude agree with the colonists' own to within a minute; White says only that he gives them "exactly", and drops the comparison with the colonists' own reckoning. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: conformes à celles que nous avons trouvées, et jusqu’à une minute d’approximation
  - white: he gives the latitude and longitude of his island exactly

### 4. cut: "and besides, we shall know" (II.13 ¶14; White ¶14)
- White drops Smith's promise that the age of the paper will be learned. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: et, d’ailleurs, nous le saurons !
  - white: —

### 5. changed: the hour of anchoring, four becomes two (II.13 ¶16; White ¶15)
- The French has the Bonadventure anchor about four o'clock; White says two. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: vers quatre heures à l’embouchure de la Mercy
  - white: By 2 o’clock Claw Cape was doubled

### 6. cut: the crossing of a hundred and fifty miles and arrival on the 13th (II.13 ¶17; White ¶16)
- White drops the distance, 150 miles, and the date, the 13th, on which they might arrive, and keeps only "forty-eight hours". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ils pourraient arriver le 13 dans la journée
  - white: they would reach the island, supposing the wind continued, in forty-eight hours

### 7. cut: Spilett's farewell words at the first separation (II.13 ¶22; White ¶18)
- White turns Spilett's exclamation into narration and drops his "Nos amis!". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: « Nos amis ! s’écria Gédéon Spilett.
  - white: They were Smith and Neb, from whom they were separating for the first time in fifteen months.

### 8. condensed: the stores loaded (II.13 ¶19; White ¶18)
- The French lists bedding, utensils, arms, ammunition, a compass and provisions for about eight days; White has "bedding, arms, munitions, provisions, etc.", dropping the utensils, the compass and the eight days. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: une boussole, des vivres pour une huitaine de jours
  - white: bedding, arms, munitions, provisions, etc.

### 9. cut: the emotion of the farewells (II.13 ¶20; White ¶18)
- White drops the emotion on both sides at parting. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: non sans une certaine émotion de part et d’autre
  - white: —

### 10. changed: south-west becomes south-east (II.13 ¶20; White ¶18)
- The French has Pencroff take the south-west route after Cape Claw; White says south-east. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: la route du sud-ouest
  - white: the route to the southeast

### 11. changed: "a quart of wine per watch" becomes "an extra allowance of grog" (II.13 ¶28; White ¶22)
- Pencroff's promised reward is a quart of wine per watch in the French; White makes it grog. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: d’un quart de vin par bordée
  - white: an extra allowance of grog

### 12. cut: the moon's first quarter on the 16th (II.13 ¶29; White ¶23)
- White drops that the crescent moon would not reach its first quarter until the 16th, and that it soon went out. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qui ne devait être dans son premier quartier que le 16
  - white: —

### 13. cut: the 12th of October (II.13 ¶32; White ¶25)
- White drops the date of the second day, 12 October. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: la journée du 12 octobre s’écoula dans les mêmes conditions
  - white: The night and the next day passed quietly and safely.

### 14. changed: south-west becomes south-east again (II.13 ¶32; White ¶25)
- The French holds a strict south-west course all day; White says "southeast". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: La direction au sud-ouest fut strictement maintenue
  - white: The Good Luck held her southeast course

### 15. cut: Spilett's last chronicle and the sea's only visitors (II.13 ¶33; White ¶25)
- White drops Spilett wondering whether he had entrusted his last chronicle for the New-York Herald to one of the great birds, and the remark that the birds were the only creatures on that stretch of ocean. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qu’il avait confié sa dernière chronique adressée au New-York Herald
  - white: —

### 16. changed: 120 miles in 36 hours becomes 130 miles (II.13 ¶39; White ¶30)
- The French has about a hundred and twenty miles in thirty-six hours, a speed of three and a third miles an hour; White says 130 miles for the same speed, and drops the thirty-six hours. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: cent vingt milles depuis son départ de l’île Lincoln, c’est-à-dire depuis trente-six heures
  - white: they calculated the distance traversed at 130 miles

### 17. cut: Pencroff could not be mistaken (II.13 ¶42; White ¶32)
- White drops the sentence that it was inconceivable Pencroff should be wrong, so the land was there. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il était inadmissible que Pencroff se fût trompé
  - white: —

### 18. cut: "between New Zealand and the American coast" (II.13 ¶51; White ¶39)

  - quote: entre la Nouvelle-Zélande et la côte américaine
  - white: all this part of the Pacific

### 19. changed: Pencroff and two companions become "his companion" (II.13 ¶52; White ¶40)
- The French has Pencroff and his two companions climb the shore; White says "his companion". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Pencroff et ses deux compagnons
  - white: Pencroff and his companion

### 20. cut: Harbert's comparison with Smith on Mount Franklin (II.13 ¶54–¶55; White ¶41)

  - quote: ce que M. Cyrus a fait tout d’abord à l’île Lincoln, en gravissant le mont Franklin
  - white: —

### 21. cut: the pigeons and swallows "like those of Lincoln" (II.13 ¶56; White ¶41)

  - white: —

### 23. condensed: Tabor's wooded face against Lincoln's arid and fertile halves (II.13 ¶59; White ¶43)

  - quote: aride et sauvage sur une partie, mais fertile et riche sur l’autre

  - white: —

### 25. changed: "a pair or two" becomes "two or three pairs" (II.13 ¶73; White ¶54)
