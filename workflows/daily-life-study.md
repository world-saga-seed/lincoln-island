# Daily-life study: how people live under the setting's rules

*Refinement. Add this when scenes default to the reader's own world.*

Sets each occupation of daily life against the setting's core rules: what the setting makes **cheap**, what it **changes**, and what stays **hard**. Useful for any book whose setting departs from the reader's world (a period, a place, an invented world), because the texture of a scene comes from who does what work and what it costs. Rerunnable for a new trade, or after a core rule changes.

**Why it pays.** A setting's rules shift what is cheap and what is dear. When they do, the social forms follow the prices. A study that re-prices daily work stops the page from defaulting to the reader's own world.

## Steps

1. **Study** (one agent, read-only except its report, [prompt](#prompt)).
2. **Questions** to the writer in batches of five, recommendation first, for every row the notes do not decide.
3. **Card** the settled rows: a topic file (`world/daily-life.md` or similar), linked from the cards for the people and places it touches.

## Prompt

> You are working in {REPO}, the notes of a novel. Read `README.md`, `plan/README.md` and the setting's core rules, {RULES}, whole. Edit nothing except your report, {REPORT}. Run no git commands; do not launch agents. **The question:** set each occupation of daily life against the core rules and say whether the setting makes it cheap, changes it, or leaves it hard. **The list:** start from a broad list of the period's occupations (field and herd, water, fuel and heat, light, food, laundry and cleaning, building and repair, carrying, messages, writing and records, keeping time, medicine, burial, the watch, soldiering, hunting and fishing, mining, the crafts, entertainment, inns, markets, money, schooling, religion, service in a great house), add the ones the setting invents, and drop or merge the ones it lacks, saying why. **For each row:** the occupation; the verdict; the rule or card that decides it (`file:line`, quoted); who does it; how it changes by season or place; what it costs and who pays; what the narrator would see of it. Mark every row that rests on your reading. End with the questions the notes leave open.
