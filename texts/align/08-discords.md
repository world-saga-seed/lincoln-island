# I.8: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller d. Beads corrected: none.*

### 1. condensed: the two or three hundred yards without a landing (I.8 ¶19; White ¶15)
- The French measures the stretch with no trace of a landing at two or three hundred yards; White says only that there was no sign of a landing. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Sur un espace de deux à trois cents yards
  - white: There was no sign of a landing having ever been made there.

### 2. cut: footnote n1, the yard (I.8 ¶19 n1; White —)
- The French footnote gives the yard as an American measure of 0.9144 m; White has no note and no yards. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Le yard est une mesure de longueur américaine qui vaut 0, 9144 m.
  - white: —

### 3. changed: a quarter of a mile becomes a quarter of an hour (I.8 ¶27; White ¶22)
- The French has Nab follow the footprints a quarter of a mile; White says a quarter of an hour. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Je les suivis pendant un quart de mille
  - white: I followed them for a quarter of an hour

### 4. addition: "wild with joy" (I.8 ¶27; White ¶22)
- The French says only that Nab became like a madman at the sight of the prints; White adds that it was with joy. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: je devins comme fou
  - white: made me wild with joy

### 5. changed: Pencroff says Smith came alone, White says he could not (I.8 ¶34; White ¶28)
- The French has Pencroff say it is plain that Smith came by himself; White has him say Smith could not have come alone, which reverses the sense and makes the reporter's reply in White ¶29 ("But he must have done it") answer a different point. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Il est bien évident que M. Smith y est venu seul
  - white: He certainly could not have come alone

### 6. condensed: forty minutes becomes "some little time" (I.8 ¶50; White ¶43)
- The French times the making of the litter at about forty minutes; White gives no figure. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Ce fut l’affaire de quarante minutes environ
  - white: This work occupied some little time

### 7. changed: colour in his cheeks becomes colour in his lips (I.8 ¶51; White ¶44)
- The French has the colour return to Smith's cheeks, which had been death-pale; White says his lips. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: La coloration revenait à ses joues
  - white: The color had come back to his lips

### 8. cut: "whatever it was, island or continent" (I.8 ¶58; White ¶51)
- In the reporter's account the French says the unknown land seemed deserted, whether island or continent; White drops the open question and says only "its deserted appearance". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: quelle qu’elle fût, soit une île, soit un continent
  - white: its deserted appearance

### 9. changed: "about half a mile" becomes "at least half a mile" (I.8 ¶64; White ¶57)
- The French has Pencroff give the distance from the grotto to the reefs as about half a mile; White says at least. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: À un demi-mille environ
  - white: At least half a mile

### 10. condensed: "a current like lightning" becomes "a strong current" (I.8 ¶67; White ¶60)
- The French gives the current that seized Smith as a current of lightning, that is of extreme speed; White says a strong current. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: un courant de foudre le saisit
  - white: a strong current seized him

### 11. changed: "inexplicable things" becomes "a thing passing belief" (I.8 ¶75; White ¶68)
- The French has Smith call what happened inexplicable (and the reporter repeats "inexplicables"); White gives Smith "a thing passing belief" and then has the reporter say "Inexplicable". *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: Il s’est passé là des choses inexplicables !
  - white: That is a thing passing belief.

### 12. changed: "one will agree" becomes "it was agreed" (I.8 ¶82; White ¶73)
- The French is the narrator's remark to the reader that there was no other explanation; White makes it an agreement among the characters. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: On conviendra qu’il n’y avait pas d’autre explication à donner
  - white: It was agreed that there was no other way to account for the rescue
