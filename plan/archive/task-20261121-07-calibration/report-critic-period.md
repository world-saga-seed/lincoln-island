# Phase 5: the period critic

*Saved by the main session from the critic's final message.*

Period critic report. No git commands were run and nothing was written. I read the renders, the flags file, White I.5, and searched Webster and White by grep. Most renders are close to White's wording. I found no clear anachronism of word, sense or science.

## A1
- cosmographical: period
  - render: "the cosmographical order established by God Himself"
  - reason: Webster 1864 has "Cosmographic" (from Fr. cosmographique) and "cosmography"; the -ical form is an ordinary derivative.
- hyperborean: period
  - render: "the hyperborean winds"
  - reason: Webster has "Hyperborean" (Lat. hyperboreus); the script missed it, probably through OCR.
- harbert: name
  - render: "asked Harbert" (twice)
  - reason: Verne's spelling of the boy's name; White writes "Herbert" (voice sample ¶2). Harbert is not an error, but it departs from the voice.
- ice-pack: unsure
  - render: "the presence of ice or of ice-packs"; "the nearness of the ice-pack"
  - reason: "Pack-ice" and "ice-pack" were in use by the 1850s to 1870s (Arctic-voyage writing), so the word is probably fine. Webster has "iceberg" but I could not confirm "ice-pack".
- rational mechanics: period
  - render: "the laws of rational mechanics"
  - reason: This was a standard 18th and 19th century term.
- Idiom check: none found. "badly served in the sharing", "as much as to say" and "Friend Pencroff" are Gallicisms or archaisms in keeping with White.
- Science (question 4): none from after 1875. The passage uses the sea as a heat store, floating ice reaching lower latitudes in the south, and a southern hemisphere colder through the sun's distance (apogee and perigee). These are Newton and Kepler. The Coriolis force (1835, applied to the oceans and winds later), ocean conveyor belts, and the circumpolar current are not named.
- Science: Verne's own error stays. The render has "The earth occupies one of the foci of the ellipse", where the sun is at the focus. That is wrong, but it is a period error, not later science.
