# A5: the French, the render, White

## ¶28

**French:** Le jour même, les colons se rendirent au corral avec les outils nécessaires, et la semaine ne s’était pas écoulée que la maison était prête à recevoir son hôte. Elle avait été élevée à une vingtaine de pieds des étables, et, de là, il serait facile de surveiller le troupeau de mouflons, qui comptait alors plus de quatre-vingts têtes. Quelques meubles, couchette, table, banc, armoire, coffre, furent fabriqués, et des armes, des munitions, des outils furent transportés au corral.

**Render:** The same day the colonists repaired to the corral with the necessary tools, and the week had not gone by before the house was ready to receive its guest. It had been raised some twenty feet from the stables, and from thence it would be easy to watch over the flock of mouflons, which then numbered some forty head. Some furniture, a bed, a table, a bench, a wardrobe, and a chest, was made, and arms, ammunition, and tools were carried to the corral.

**White** (¶26): (¶26) The same day the colonists went, with the necessary tools, to the place, and before the week was out the house was ready for its guest. It was built twenty feet from the sheds, at a place where the herd of moufflons, now numbering twenty-four animals, could be easily overlooked. Some furniture, including a bed, table, bench, clothes-press, and chest was made, and some arms, ammunition, and tools, were carried there.

## ¶59

**French:** « Il est temps de dire que cet Ayrton était un traître. C’était, en effet, le contre-maître du Britannia ; mais, à la suite de discussions avec son capitaine, il avait essayé d’entraîner son équipage à la révolte et de s’emparer du navire, et le capitaine Grant l’avait débarqué, le 8 avril 1852, sur la côte ouest de l’Australie, puis il était reparti en l’abandonnant — ce qui n’était que justice.

**Render:** “It is time to say that this Ayrton was a traitor. He was, in fact, the boatswain of the Britannia; but, in consequence of disputes with his captain, he had tried to draw the crew into revolt and to seize the ship, and Captain Grant had landed him, on the 8th of April, 1852, on the west coast of Australia, and had then sailed away, abandoning him—which was no more than justice.

**White** (¶56): (¶56) “It is time to say that this Ayrton was a traitor. He was, indeed, the boatswain’s mate of the Britannia; but, after some dispute with his captain, he had tried to excite the crew to mutiny and seize the ship, and Captain Grant had put him ashore, the 8th of April, 1832, on the west coast of Australia, and had gone off, leaving him there, which was no more than right.
