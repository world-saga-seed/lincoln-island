### 2. The decision to winter (I.15 ¶5–¶9)

- Twelve hundred miles to the Pomotou archipelago is a great distance; a boat could not make it, especially at the approach of the bad season, as Pencroff formally declared. To build even a simple boat they need tools (hammers, axes, adzes, saws, augers, planes) which would take time. It is decided to winter on Lincoln Island and to look for a more comfortable dwelling than the Chimneys. *(Drafted.)*

### 5. Skins and the bellows (I.15 ¶27–¶30)

- It is pointless to carry the two heavy carcasses, so Nab and Pencroff skin them on the spot while Cyrus and the reporter finish exploring the islet. Three hours later Cyrus has two sealskins, to be used as they are, without tanning. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Nab et Pencroff résolurent de les dépouiller sur place

- They wait for the sea to ebb again, cross the channel and go home. *(Drafted.)*

### 6. To the ore through the Jacamar Wood (I.15 ¶31–¶40)

- On 20 April, at dawn, "the metallurgical period", as the reporter calls it in his notes, begins. ### 7. Smelting the iron (I.15 ¶41–¶47)

- On 21 April Cyrus with Harbert looks for the ancient formations where he had found an ore sample. He finds the deposit at ground level almost at the creek's very sources, at the foot of one of the north-east buttresses; rich in iron, in a fusible gangue, it suits the catalan method, but simplified as used in Corsica. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Le lendemain, 21 avril, Cyrus Smith, accompagné d’Harbert
  - quote: presque aux sources même du creek
  - quote: la méthode catalane, mais simplifiée, ainsi qu’on l’emploie en Corse
- The true catalan method needs furnaces and crucibles; Cyrus would save those, forming a cubic mass of ore and charcoal, into whose centre he would direct the bellows' wind, as Tubal-Cain and the first metallurgists did. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: une masse cubique au centre de laquelle il dirigerait le vent de son soufflet
  - quote: Tubal-Caïn et les premiers métallurgistes du monde habité
- The coal is gathered at the surface nearby; the ore is broken into small pieces and cleaned by hand; ore and coal are piled in successive layers, like a charcoal-burner's wood. Under the blast, the coal would turn to carbonic acid then to carbon monoxide, which reduces the iron oxide. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: la houille fut récoltée, sans peine et non loin, à la surface du sol
  - quote: charbon et minerai furent disposés en tas et par couches successives
  - quote: oxyde de carbone, chargé de réduire l’oxyde de fer
- The sealskin bellows, with a fireclay pipe made earlier at the pottery kiln, is set by the heap, worked by a mechanism of frames, fibre cords and counterweights. The plate: "The operation was difficult." *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: un tuyau en terre réfractaire, qui avait été préalablement fabriqué au four à poteries

### 8. Steel and the tools; back to the Chimneys (I.15 ¶48–¶52)

- Iron is of most use as steel, a combination of iron and carbon, taken either from pig iron by removing carbon (natural or puddled steel) or from iron by adding it (cementation steel). *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: l’acier est une combinaison de fer et de charbon
  - quote: l’acier de cémentation

  - quote: en chauffant le métal avec du charbon en poudre dans un creuset fait en terre réfractaire
