# The Search for a Dwelling and the Dugong (notes)

## Notes for drafting

- **Where it sits.** The French I.16. It follows I.15's close on 5 May (the smiths back at the Chimneys) and covers 6 May: the decision to find a better dwelling, the search of the granite wall, the circuit of Lake Grant, and the dugong's fight at five o'clock. It ends with the colonists going back to the Chimneys. *(Drafted.)*
- **Cast.** *(Drafted.)* Pencroff (¶4), Cyrus Smith (¶5), the reporter, Gédéon Spilett (¶6, named ¶11), Harbert (¶8), Nab (¶18), Top (¶29). The "indigènes" of the Malay seas and the unseen "quelque puissant animal" (¶62) are not on the page.
- **Places.** *(Drafted.)*
  - The Chimneys (¶3). The granite wall of the coast, from the river's mouth to its northern angle, about two miles (¶20, ¶21). The talus west of that angle and the sand plain below it (¶23).
  - The upper plateau and its edge (¶26–¶28); the Red Creek's mouth at the lake, left bank (¶28, ¶30); the lake's north-east point (¶32); the east shore (¶34); the south-east angle and the plateau of Grande-Vue (¶47); the small beach at the lake's south angle (¶63). The lake is called Lake Grant (¶12); the creek "Creek-Rouge" (¶24).
  - Not yet seen: the west coast of the island, where no stream was seen from Mount Franklin (¶12).
- **Things.** *(Drafted.)* Arms: bows, arrows, iron-pointed sticks, iron-shod spears (¶29, ¶59). The snake of fourteen to fifteen feet (¶29). The coot (¶33). The dugong, fifteen to sixteen feet, three to four thousand pounds, with a blade-like wound in its neck (¶64).
- **Time.** *(Drafted.)* 6 May, "jour qui correspond au 6 novembre" (¶1); the half hour from the lake's east shore to the south-east angle (¶47); five in the evening at the second plunge (¶53). The chapter covers one day. Temperature "de dix à douze degrés" and the rainy season near (¶1, ¶2). The island is "très-vraisemblablement entre le trente-cinquième et le quarantième parallèle" (¶1), a guess.
- **Clues.** *(Drafted.)* Each is a mystery Cyrus says he cannot explain.
  - Top's behaviour at the lake before the attack.
    - quote: quelque être invisible qui se serait glissé sous les eaux du lac
  - Top thrown ten feet into the air "par quelque force inconnue" (¶61).
    - quote: Lancé en l’air par quelque force inconnue
  - The dugong's neck wound, "une lame tranchante" (¶64), and the narrator's closing question (¶65).
    - quote: Quel était donc l’amphibie qui avait pu, par ce coup terrible, détruire le formidable dugong
  - The lake's missing outlet: the creek's flow is "assez considérable" yet no overflow is found (¶30, ¶47–¶48).
    - quote: l’ingénieur n’avait pu découvrir par où et comment s’opérait la décharge des eaux
  - The possibility of Malay pirates and of natives (¶7, ¶9).
    - quote: souvent fréquentée par les pirates malais
  - The island's thirty-fifth to fortieth parallel is a guess, and Cyrus's "I fear dangerous animals abound" (¶7) is unproved; the beasts seen so far are few.
- **Length.** 65 paragraphs (¶1–¶65), 2,989 words in the ¶ lines. *(Drafted.)*

## Open and flags

- The trade wind. I.16 says the east-facing coast is not exposed to the trade winds "qui soufflent du nord-ouest dans cet hémisphère". I.19 calls the north-east winds "les mauvais vents" for the facade and speaks of "grands vents d’est" at the chimney. Card neither version. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qui soufflent du nord-ouest dans cet hémisphère
- The text says Harbert's "lamantin" is wrong and the animal is a "dugong" (¶57); later chapters name it "dugong". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Ce n’était pas un lamantin, mais un spécimen de cette espèce
- Lake's south end is called "angle sud" (¶63), "pointe sud" in I.17 ¶2, and the party's arrival point "angle sud-est" (¶47). The text does not say whether the south-east angle and the south angle are one place. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
- The snake is "serpents-diamants ... Nouvelle-Galle du Sud" in the print's spelling (¶29). *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dans la Nouvelle-Galle du Sud
- Cyrus says the opposite coast may hold a cavern (¶11, Spilett) and the party chooses not to explore it; the chapter ends without a decision to settle there. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
- No Wikisource correction touches this chapter's facts (texts/README.md lists I.12, II.20 and III.10 only). *(Drafted.)*
