# III.19: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller r. Beads corrected: none.*

### 1. cut: the island is not made to last as long as the globe (III.19 ¶3; White ¶3)
- Smith says Lincoln Island is not one of those that last as long as the globe itself; White drops the clause and gives only that it is doomed. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: l’île Lincoln n’est pas de celles qui doivent durer autant que le globe lui-même
  - white: Lincoln Island is doomed to destruction sooner or later

### 2. changed: Pencroff's retort is reversed (III.19 ¶9; White ¶9)
- Pencroff says that dead as he is Nemo will do them still other services; White has him say that they think Nemo will help them no more, which reverses his meaning. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Vous verrez que, tout mort qu’il est, il nous en rendra d’autres encore !
  - white: You think, because he is dead, that he will help us no more!

### 3. changed: "a particular arrangement" becomes "a particular event" (III.19 ¶11; White ¶11)
- The French has a particular disposition (arrangement) of the island's structure; White has "event", which gives a reader of White a happening, not a feature of the island. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: une disposition particulière que m’a fait connaître le capitaine Nemo
  - white: a particular event, made known to me by Captain Nemo

### 4. cut: the narrator's note on extinguishing volcanoes (III.19 ¶19; White —)
- The French paragraph, in which the narrator says Smith does not exaggerate and that water meeting a closed space at thousands of degrees would burst any casing, is wholly absent from White. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Bien des gens ont déjà eu l’idée qu’on pourrait peut-être éteindre les volcans
  - white: —

### 5. cut: the night of 23 to 24 January (III.19 ¶25; White ¶25)
- The French dates the night of the first great explosion 23 to 24 January; White says only "the night of the 23d". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: pendant la nuit du 23 au 24
  - white: during the night of the 23d

### 6. changed: "a thousand feet" becomes "thousands of feet" (III.19 ¶37; White ¶36)
- The French says the burning rocks were thrown up more than a thousand feet; White says thousands of feet. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: projetées à plus de mille pieds
  - white: thrown up thousands of feet

### 7. changed: the forest is ablaze, not "surrounded" (III.19 ¶40; White ¶39)
- The French says all that part of the forest was on fire; White says it was "surrounded". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Toute cette partie de la forêt était embrasée
  - white: All that part of the forest was surrounded

### 8. softened: death that the explosion "will not make us wait for" (III.19 ¶43; White ¶41)
- Smith says the explosion of the island will not make them wait for their death; White says it "may anticipate" it, which leaves open what the French states as certain. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qu’une mort que l’explosion de l’île ne nous fera pas attendre !
  - white: a death which the explosion of the island may anticipate!

### 9. changed: a rise in the ground becomes "a depression" (III.19 ¶46; White ¶44)
- The French says there was a certain rise (exhaussement) of the ground at the lake's edge which, if higher, might have held the torrent; White says a depression, which could not hold anything. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Là existait un certain exhaussement du sol
  - white: There was a certain depression in the ground

### 10. changed: the dike's length, "hundreds of paces" becomes "hundreds of feet" (III.19 ¶50; White ¶47)
- The French gives the dike's length as some hundreds of paces; White gives some hundreds of feet. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: sur quelques centaines de pas de longueur
  - white: some hundreds of feet long

### 11. changed: the vapours fall on the sea, not "to the ground" (III.19 ¶54; White ¶51)
- The French says the steam, carried by the wind, fell as rain on the sea; White says it fell to the ground. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: retombaient en pluie sur la mer
  - white: fell to the ground in rain

### 12. cut: the frost of twenty degrees (III.19 ¶54; White ¶51)
- The French compares the lake to a storm-tossed water suddenly solidified by a cold of twenty degrees; White drops the figure. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: subitement solidifiées par un froid de vingt degrés
  - white: suddenly solidified by cold

### 13. cut: the dates 25 to 30 January and the "twenty men" (III.19 ¶57; White ¶54)
- The French dates the six days, 25 to 30 January, and says the colonists worked as twenty men could; White gives neither. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Pendant les six jours qui suivirent, du 25 au 30 janvier, les colons travaillèrent au navire autant que vingt hommes eussent pu le faire.
  - white: During the next six days, the colonists worked on the ship with all their might.

### 14. condensed: the list of animals that fled (III.19 ¶60; White ¶57)
- The French names the fleeing animals, jaguars, boars, capybaras and koulas, and says the colonists paid no heed even to the most formidable; White says only "the beasts and birds of the woods" and "these animals". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: jaguars, sangliers, cabiais, koulas, gibier de poil et de plume
  - white: The beasts and birds of the woods

### 15. changed: the Mercy valley becomes Red Creek (III.19 ¶62; White ¶59)
- The French says the valleys of Fall River and the Mercy no longer gave a drop of water to the sea; White says the valleys of Fall River and Red Creek held no water. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Les vallées de la rivière de la Chute et de la Mercy ne versaient plus une seule goutte d’eau à la mer
  - white: The valleys of Fall River and Red Creek contained no water

### 16. changed: the lake "dried up" becomes "filled up" (III.19 ¶62; White ¶59)
- The French says the colonists would have had no way to quench their thirst had Lake Grant been wholly dried up; White says it had been completely filled up. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: si le lac Grant eût été entièrement asséché
  - white: if Lake Grant had been completely filled up

### 17. changed: "until 7 February" becomes "8th of February" (III.19 ¶67; White ¶65)
- The French dates the end of the eight further days 7 February; White says the 8th. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: jusqu’au 7 février
  - white: until the 8th of February

### 18. cut: Pencroff shares the intention to launch early (III.19 ¶68; White ¶66)
- The French makes the intention to launch as soon as the hull is tight that of Pencroff and Smith together; White gives it to Smith alone. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: L’intention de Pencroff et de Cyrus Smith était de procéder au lancement du navire
  - white: It was Smith’s intention to launch her

### 19. changed: "about ten days" becomes "twelve days" (III.19 ¶69; White ¶67)
- The French says the launch could be done in about ten days from 3 March; White says twelve days. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dans une dizaine de jours
  - white: in twelve days

### 20. addition: Nab is called "the negro" (III.19 ¶76; White ¶74)
- The French says Pencroff whispered in Nab's ear; White says he whispered "to the negro", which names Nab by his race where the French uses his name. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: murmura Pencroff à l’oreille de Nab
  - white: whispered Pencroff to the negro

### 21. changed: the lava goes beyond Glycerine Creek, not "along" it (III.19 ¶78; White ¶76)
- The French says the stream went beyond Glycerine Creek and invaded the plateau; White says it flowed along the creek. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: se porta au delà du creek Glycérine
  - white: flowed along Glycerine Creek

### 22. cut: the night of 8 to 9 March (III.19 ¶81; White ¶79)
- The French dates the night of the explosion 8 to 9 March; White says "that night". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: pendant cette nuit du 8 au 9
  - white: during that night
