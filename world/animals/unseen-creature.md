# The unseen creature: what the colonists suppose fought the dugong and left the cavern

*An unnamed amphibian or sea monster, never seen, that the colonists wonder about after the lake fight and the empty cavern.*

Keywords: monstre marin, sea monster, amphibie, amphibian, mystère, mystery, inconnu, unknown, lac Grant, caverne, cavern, puits, well, fuite

- **What it is.**
  - After the dugong's death the narrator asks which amphibian could have destroyed the formidable dugong by that terrible blow, and says no one could tell (I.16 ¶65). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: Quel était donc l’amphibie qui avait pu, par ce coup terrible, détruire le formidable dugong ?
    - quote: Personne n’eût pu le dire
  - Cyrus Smith wonders which relation of the mastodons or other sea monsters made the dugong so strange a wound (I.17 ¶3). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: quel congénère des mastodontes ou autres monstres marins avait fait au dugong une si étrange blessure
  - In the great cavern the colonists find no animal and no living being (I.18 ¶42). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: pas un animal, pas un être vivant
  - The reporter holds that the cavern was occupied by some being; the engineer answers that this being, "amphibie ou autre", fled by the well and ceded its place (I.18 ¶52, ¶53). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: elle était occupée par un être quelconque
    - quote: l’être quelconque, amphibie ou autre, s’est enfui par cette issue
  - The narrator says that if some sea monster had been surprised by the withdrawal of the waters, it had by now regained the open sea by the conduit running under the beach (I.18 ¶74). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: Si quelque monstre marin avait été inopinément surpris par le retrait des eaux
    - quote: il avait maintenant regagné le large par le conduit souterrain qui se prolongeait sous la grève
  - The text does not itself say that the powerful animal of the lake fight and the being supposed to have left the cavern are one (I.16 ¶62, I.18 ¶53). *(My reading, to confirm.)*
    - quote: quelque puissant animal
    - quote: l’être quelconque
  - Gédéon Spilett supposes the well to be in direct communication with the sea, so that some marine animal comes from time to time to breathe at the bottom; Pencroff says there is no other explanation to give (II.11 ¶70, ¶71). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: ce puits est en communication directe avec la mer
    - quote: quelque animal marin vient de temps en temps respirer au fond
    - quote: il n’y a pas d’autre explication à donner
  - The incident darkens the engineer's brow (II.11 ¶73). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: assombrit le front de l’ingénieur
  - Cyrus Smith finds the well's wall solid, with no mark or break on its projections to suggest a climb, and no side passage opening at the water or elsewhere; only a conduit always under water, joining the sea under the rock of the shore, could be used, and only by marine animals; where it ends and at what depth cannot be settled (II.11 ¶85, ¶88). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: il ne trouva aucune empreinte, aucune cassure
    - quote: ne s’ouvrait aucun couloir latéral
    - quote: cela n’était possible qu’à des animaux marins
    - quote: on ne pouvait la résoudre
  - He concludes: 'Je n’ai rien vu, et pourtant il y a quelque chose !'; the reporter, told of the exploration, shares his opinion that, though nothing was found, a secret remains to be discovered (II.11 ¶90; II.12 ¶10). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: Je n’ai rien vu, et pourtant il y a quelque chose
    - quote: il restait toujours un secret à découvrir
  - The text does not say that the animal supposed at the well is the one that fought the dugong or the one supposed to have left the cavern (II.11 ¶70). *(My reading, to confirm.)*
    - quote: quelque animal marin
- **Appears in:** I.16, I.17, I.18, II.11, II.12
