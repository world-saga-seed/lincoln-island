# The Search for a Dwelling and the Dugong (outline)

*Chapter 01600: the French I.16 (`texts/fr-1875/16.md`). Before: 01500. After: 01700. Third person, past tense; Verne's narrator. Every beat is marked (Drafted.): it is what the French holds.*

## Outline

### 1. 6 May: winter is near, the Chimneys will not do (I.16 ¶1–¶4)

- The date is 6 May, which answers to 6 November in the northern hemisphere. The sky has been hazy some days and winter quarters must be thought of. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: On était au 6 mai, jour qui correspond au 6 novembre
  - quote: il importait de prendre certaines dispositions en vue d’un hivernage
- A centigrade thermometer would still read ten to twelve degrees above zero. The island lies "very likely" between the thirty-fifth and fortieth parallels, so its climate is Sicily's or Greece's, with hard cold in the depth of winter. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dix à douze degrés au-dessus de zéro
  - quote: très-vraisemblablement entre le trente-cinquième et le quarantième parallèle
- The rainy season is near; the island, exposed to the open Pacific, will have frequent and probably terrible storms. A better dwelling than the Chimneys must be thought out and quickly decided. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: la saison des pluies était prochaine
- Pencroff is fond of the Chimneys he found, but sees another place is needed: the sea has already visited them, "in circumstances which are remembered". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Déjà les Cheminées avaient été visitées par la mer

### 2. Cyrus on precautions: animals and Malay pirates (I.16 ¶5–¶9)

- Cyrus says more precautions are needed. The island is probably uninhabited, though not fully explored; if no man is here he fears dangerous animals abound. He wants shelter from attack, so that no one need watch each night to keep a fire in. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: je crains que les animaux dangereux n’y abondent
  - quote: ne pas obliger l’un de nous à veiller chaque nuit pour entretenir un foyer allumé
- He adds that this part of the Pacific is often visited by Malay pirates. Harbert asks if that is possible so far from any land; Cyrus answers that they are bold seamen as well as dangerous criminals. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: souvent fréquentée par les pirates malais
  - quote: Ces pirates sont de hardis marins

### 3. Where to live: the lake side, or a place in the rock (I.16 ¶10–¶19)

- Pencroff says they will fortify against savages "on two legs and on four" and asks whether to explore the whole island first; the reporter agrees and wonders if the opposite coast has a cavern. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: nous nous fortifierons contre les sauvages à deux et à quatre pattes
  - quote: sur la côte opposée une de ces cavernes
- Cyrus gives reasons for staying on this side: they must be near running water, and from the top of Mount Franklin no stream or river was seen to the west. Here they lie between the Mercy and Lake Grant, and the east-facing coast is not exposed to the trade winds, which "blow from the north-west in this hemisphere". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: nous n’avons aperçu vers l’ouest ni ruisseau ni rivière
  - quote: entre la Mercy et le lac Grant
  - quote: qui soufflent du nord-ouest dans cet hémisphère
- Pencroff proposes a house by the lake: bricks and tools are no longer lacking, and having been brickmakers, potters, founders and smiths they can be masons. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Ni les briques, ni les outils ne nous manquent maintenant
- Cyrus prefers to search first: a dwelling made by nature would save work and be safer against enemies within and without. The reporter says the whole granite mass of the coast has been examined, "not a hole, not even a crack". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Une demeure dont la nature aurait fait tous les frais
  - quote: pas un trou, pas même une fente
- Pencroff pictures five or six rooms cut in the wall, high up, facing the sea. Harbert laughs and adds windows; Nab adds a stair. Pencroff asks whether picks and pickaxes are lacking, and whether Cyrus will not make powder to blast, "the day it is needed". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: cinq ou six chambres…
  - quote: Avec des fenêtres pour les éclairer
  - quote: Et un escalier pour y monter
  - quote: N’est-il pas vrai, monsieur Cyrus, que vous ferez de la poudre le jour où il nous en faudra

### 4. The granite wall searched again (I.16 ¶20–¶22)

- Cyrus judges attacking the granite, even by mine, a herculean labour. He proposes only to examine the wall more closely, from the river's mouth to its northern angle. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: c’était un travail herculéen
  - quote: depuis l’embouchure de la rivière jusqu’à l’angle qui la terminait au nord
- They go out and search about two miles with extreme care; the wall, smooth and straight, shows no cavity anywhere. The rock pigeons' nests on the crest are only holes in the irregular edge of the granite. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: sur une étendue de deux milles environ
  - quote: n’étaient, en réalité, que des trous forés à la crête même
- The narrator says that by chance Pencroff had found the only provisionally habitable shelter on all that shore, the Chimneys, which they must nevertheless leave. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le seul abri provisoirement habitable

### 5. Up the slope and along the plateau's edge; the snake (I.16 ¶23–¶29)

- At the wall's northern angle it ends in long slopes; from there west it is only a talus at forty-five degrees, of stones, earth and sand bound by plants, with granite points showing, clumps of trees, and a long plain of sand at its foot. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: incliné sous un angle de quarante-cinq degrés seulement
  - quote: une longue plaine de sables
- Cyrus thinks the lake's overflow must pour out on this side as a cascade, since the surplus from the Red Creek must be lost somewhere; he has found no such place on the banks already explored, from the creek's mouth on the west to Grande-Vue. *(Drafted.)*

  - quote: l’ingénieur ne l’avait encore trouvé sur aucune portion des rives déjà explorées

  - quote: kakatoès bruyants

### 6. The creek's mouth; round the lake; the coot (I.16 ¶30–¶33)

- They reach the Red Creek's mouth at the lake and recognise on the far bank the place they saw coming down from Mount Franklin. Cyrus notes the creek's flow is considerable, so nature must have given the lake an outlet, probably a fall whose mechanical power could be used. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le débit d’eau du creek était assez considérable
  - quote: une chute dont il serait possible d’utiliser la puissance mécanique
- They go round the lake's very steep bank. The waters seem full of fish, and Pencroff resolves to make fishing gear. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Les eaux semblaient extrêmement poissonneuses
- At the sharp north-east point the lake's end nearly touches the plateau's edge; the outflow is not there, and the shore curves back parallel to the coast. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: doubler la pointe aiguë du nord-est
  - quote: Mais il n’en était rien
- The reporter and Harbert shoot at birds raised by Top; Harbert hits one, which falls in marsh grass. Top retrieves a slate-coloured swimming bird, a "coot", the size of a large partridge, poor game, and it is decided that it will be Top's supper. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: un bel oiseau nageur, couleur d’ardoise
  - quote: C’était un « foulque »
  - quote: il fut convenu que le foulque servirait à son souper

### 7. The east shore: Top's strange behaviour (I.16 ¶34–¶46)

- Along the east shore, near ground already known, the engineer is surprised to see no sign of the lake's overflow, and tells the reporter and the sailor so. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il ne voyait aucun indice d’écoulement du trop-plein des eaux
- Top, calm until now, grows agitated: he runs along the bank, halts, stares at the water with a paw raised, barks furiously and falls silent. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: donna des signes d’agitation
  - quote: une patte levée
- The barking grows so frequent that Cyrus asks what is the matter; Top leaps toward him, shows real uneasiness, then suddenly plunges into the lake. Cyrus calls him back, not wishing him to venture on "suspect" waters. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il se précipita dans le lac
  - quote: ces eaux suspectes
- Pencroff asks what is under the surface. Harbert suggests an amphibian; the reporter an alligator; Cyrus doubts it, since alligators are found only in lower latitudes. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Top aura senti quelque amphibie
  - quote: Les alligators ne se rencontrent que dans les régions moins élevées en latitude
- Top returns, but cannot rest; he leaps in the tall grass as though following some invisible being gliding under the water along the edge, while the surface stays calm. The colonists watch and see nothing: "there was some mystery". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: quelque être invisible qui se serait glissé sous les eaux du lac
  - quote: Il y avait là quelque mystère
- Cyrus, much intrigued, says to carry the exploration to the end. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Poursuivons jusqu’au bout cette exploration

### 8. The south-east angle: no outlet found (I.16 ¶47–¶52)

- Half an hour later they are at the lake's south-east angle, on the plateau of Grande-Vue itself. The survey of the shores is finished and Cyrus still has not found how the waters are discharged. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Une demi-heure après, ils étaient tous arrivés à l’angle sud-est du lac
  - quote: l’ingénieur n’avait pu découvrir par où et comment s’opérait la décharge des eaux
- Cyrus insists the outlet exists, and since it is not outside, it must be hollowed inside the granite mass of the coast. Asked by the reporter why it matters, he says that if the flow runs through the massif, a cavity may lie there which could be made habitable once the waters were diverted. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il faut qu’il soit creusé à l’intérieur du massif granitique de la côte
  - quote: il est possible qu’il s’y trouve quelque cavité
- Harbert asks whether the waters might leave by the lake's bottom, by an underground conduit to the sea. Cyrus says it may be; if so they must build their house themselves. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: par un conduit souterrain
  - quote: nous serons obligés de bâtir notre maison nous-mêmes

### 9. Five o'clock: Top and the dugong (I.16 ¶53–¶65)
