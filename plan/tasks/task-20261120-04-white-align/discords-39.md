# II.17: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller A. Beads corrected: none (bead 81, White ¶80, breaks off mid-sentence and White ¶81 finishes it; the pairing is right).*

### 1. changed: "Une demande de l’inconnu" in the summary (II.17 ¶0; White ¶0)
- The French summary says "a request" of the Unknown; White's summary says "a bequest". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Une demande de l’inconnu.
  - white: A BEQUEST OF THE UNKNOWN’S

### 2. changed: "these last words" become "these last events" (II.17 ¶1; White ¶1)
- The French says it was the unknown's last words that confirmed the colonists' presentiments; White says "events". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Ces dernières paroles justifiaient les pressentiments des colons.
  - white: These last events justified the presentiments of the colonists.

### 3. changed: who thinks he will speak (II.17 ¶2; White ¶2)
- The French leaves it to the future whether he will speak; White says the colonists believed he would. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: C’est ce que l’avenir apprendrait.
  - white: The colonists thought so

### 4. cut: the need to be on their guard (II.17 ¶3; White ¶3)
- The French adds that it was necessary to be on their guard in the forest; White gives only the prudence about the boy. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: et il fallait se tenir sur ses gardes
  - white: —

### 5. changed: the mouflon flock, over eighty head against twenty-four (II.17 ¶28; White ¶26)
- The French counts more than eighty mouflons; White counts twenty-four. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qui comptait alors plus de quatre-vingts têtes
  - white: now numbering twenty-four animals

### 6. changed: the Grant children (II.17 ¶42; White ¶39)
- The French has "a girl and a boy" (jeune fille, jeune garçon); White has "a little boy, and a little girl", reversing the order and making Mary little. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: une jeune fille et un jeune garçon
  - white: a little boy, and a little girl

### 7. changed: both latitude and longitude "given" (II.17 ¶44; White ¶41)
- The French says the document gave the latitude only (the longitude was washed out); White says the latitude and longitude were given, then says the longitude was illegible. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dont le document donnait la latitude, mais dont la longitude, effacée par l’eau de mer, n’était plus lisible
  - white: a land of which the latitude and longitude was given

### 8. changed: America becomes Patagonia (II.17 ¶50; White ¶47)
- The French says Glenarvan meant to cross Australia as he had crossed America; White says Patagonia. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: comme il avait traversé l’Amérique
  - white: as he had crossed Patagonia

### 9. softened: "Dieu" becomes "Heaven" (II.17 ¶52; White ¶49)
- The French has "louez et remerciez Dieu"; White has "Heaven be praised". *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: Milord, louez et remerciez Dieu.
  - white: ‘Heaven be praised, my lord.

### 10. changed: the man is "Ayrton", not one who "called himself" Ayrton (II.17 ¶55; White ¶52)
- The French says "Cet homme s’appelait Ayrton"; White says he "called himself" Ayrton, which suggests an alias. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: Cet homme s’appelait Ayrton.
  - white: This man called himself Ayrton.

### 11. cut: "as his document indicates" (II.17 ¶56; White ¶53)
- Ayrton's words in the French tie Grant's survival to what "son document" says; White drops the clause. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: et si le capitaine Grant est vivant encore, comme l’indique son document
  - white: and if the Captain is still living

### 12. changed: the date of departure, 1854 becomes 1861 (II.17 ¶58; White ¶55)
- The French gives 23 December 1854 for the start of the crossing; White prints 1861. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Ils partirent le 23 décembre 1854.
  - white: They left on the 23d of December, 1861.

### 13. changed: the date of the landing on the west coast, 1852 becomes 1832 (II.17 ¶59; White ¶56)
- The French says Grant put Ayrton ashore on 8 April 1852; White prints 1832. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le 8 avril 1852
  - white: the 8th of April, 1832

### 14. changed: why the Duncan went to Melbourne (II.17 ¶63; White ¶60)
- The French says the Duncan was sent to Melbourne to be repaired; White says to await instructions (which is what ¶57 of the French says of the destination). *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le Duncan avait été envoyé à Melbourne pour s’y réparer
  - white: the Duncan had been taken to Melbourne to await instructions

### 15. changed: Ayrton took the letter on himself, or was ordered to carry it (II.17 ¶63; White ¶60)
- The French says Ayrton had undertaken to carry the letter to the second; White says he was ordered to deliver it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: une lettre qu’il s’était chargé de porter
  - white: a letter which he was ordered to deliver

### 16. softened: God stops the criminal (II.17 ¶65; White ¶62)
- The French says God was to stop him at the end of his designs; White says "Heaven". *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: Dieu devait l’arrêter au dénouement de ses funestes desseins.
  - white: Heaven stopped him in the consummation of these dark designs.

### 17. changed: "the next day" becomes "the second day out" (II.17 ¶66; White ¶63)
- The French has Ayrton learn the ship's course the day after sailing; White says the second day out. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: quand, le lendemain de l’appareillage, il apprit
  - white: when, the second day out, he learned

### 18. changed: Austin shows the letter, or his order (II.17 ¶66; White ¶63)
- In the French Austin shows Ayrton the letter; White says the mate showed him "his order". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Austin lui montra la lettre !…
  - white: but the mate showed him his order.

### 19. addition: "in irons" (II.17 ¶67; White ¶64)
- The French says only that Ayrton was shut up; White says he was put in irons. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: On l’enferma.
  - white: They put him in irons

### 20. changed: the caronades become a salute (II.17 ¶68; White ¶65)
- The French says the detonations were the Duncan's caronades firing; White calls it a salute. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: C’étaient les caronades du Duncan qui faisaient feu
  - white: It was a salute from the Duncan

### 21. changed: "by the will of Heaven" becomes "by the bounty of Heaven" (II.17 ¶72; White ¶69)
- The French has the Duncan found "par la volonté du Ciel"; White says "by the bounty of Heaven". *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: par la volonté du Ciel
  - white: by the bounty of Heaven

### 22. cut: Grant found "precisely on this thirty-seventh parallel" (II.17 ¶76; White ¶73)
- The French says Grant and his two men were found precisely on the thirty-seventh parallel; White says only that they were found. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: on retrouva le capitaine Grant et ses deux hommes, précisément sur ce trente-septième parallèle
  - white: they found Captain Grant and his two companions

### 23. cut: the editor's note on the dates (II.17 ¶79 n1; White —)
- The French has a footnote saying these events come from *Les Enfants du capitaine Grant*, warning of a discord in the dates, and promising the reason later; White has no footnote. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: une certaine discordance dans les dates
  - white: —

### 24. cut: the tools (II.17 ¶80; White ¶77)
- The French says Ayrton lacked neither munitions, arms, tools nor seeds; White omits the tools. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ni les munitions, ni les armes, ni les outils, ni les graines ne lui manquaient
  - white: he lacked neither ammunition nor arms nor seeds
