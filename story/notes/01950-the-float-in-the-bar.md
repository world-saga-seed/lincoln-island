## The gap pass

*Items 1 to 22 are the executor's picks, accepted by the author (Settled R1121-80). Item 23 is the author's (R1121-81), and item 24 is the author's own (R1121-82).*

### The float and the net

1. **Colour of the glass.** Pick: dark green, like bottle glass, clear where the barnacles are chipped away. *(Settled R1121-80.)*
2. **The device.** Pick: a round seal moulded on the glass, a palm across, with a ring of capital letters about a letter in the centre. It is the form of the Nautilus's marked things, "une lettre entourée d’une devise en exergue" (VML I.8). The two bare part of the ring's lower arc: B, a gap, L, I. *(Settled R1121-80.)*
3. **How much shows.** Pick: the upper third of the ball, above the sand. *(Settled R1121-80.)*
4. **The barnacles.** A grey-white crust of dead acorn barnacles, cemented flat to the glass, which stay after death, thickest on the upper side. Among them are a few dead goose barnacles: their shells, and the dried stumps of their stalks. Only these tell Herbert the float rode at the surface. *(Settled R1121-80, amended R1121-120.)*
5. **The net.** Pick: what shows is a dark, rotten cord, the mesh choked with sand, running down into the bar. Pencroff calls it "a net", and no more. *(Settled R1121-80.)*
6. **The lashing.** Pick: a cord of the same, running under the ball into the sand on the hidden side (R1121-72). *(Settled R1121-80.)*

### Time, tide, light, weather

7. **Hours.** Pick: low water about 8 in the morning on 1 June, about 9 on the 2nd and about 9:45 on the 3rd, about fifty minutes later each day. Sunrise about 7:15, sunset about 5. The hours are from general knowledge for 35° south in June, not from the French. *(Settled R1121-80.)*
8. **1 June.** Pick: grey after the night's squalls, the wind dropping, the sea still running. Pencroff is out at first light. *(Settled R1121-80.)*
9. **2 June.** Pick: dawn in rain (R1121-69). The wind comes onshore, from the east-north-east into the bay: I.20 ¶1 fears "les grandes marées, poussées par les vents du large". The squall arrives about an hour after low water, with the flood. It eases by early afternoon (R1121-71). *(Settled R1121-80.)*
10. **The flash.** Pick: in a rift of light under the squall, the wet glass on the islet's rocks catches it for an instant (R1121-68). *(Settled R1121-80.)*
11. **3 June.** Pick: grey and still, the wind gone round to the south-west, the sea down. *(Settled R1121-80.)*
12. **Cold.** Pick: raw, not yet freezing. The great cold comes at the end of June, "about 20 °F" on 29 June (I.21 ¶2; the timeline). The water is cold enough to numb the hands. *(Settled R1121-80.)*

### What they wear and carry

13. **Clothes.** They have only the clothes they came in: "Les colons n’avaient d’autres habits que ceux qu’ils portaient" (I.20 ¶3). Pick: they go barefoot on the sands, trousers rolled, and leave their worn shoes at the foot of the wall. The sealskin shoes come after the hunt of 5 June (I.20 ¶21). *(Settled R1121-80.)*
14. **What they carry.** Pencroff's knife (R1121-72). Pick: the stone they chip with is picked up on the bar. Harbert carries nothing. *(Settled R1121-80.)*

### Places

15. **The bar.** Off the Mercy's mouth, on the coast side of the gut, a cable or two out (R1121-73, R1121-74). *(Settled.)*
16. **The lines.** Pick: on the Mercy's left bank near the mouth, by the Chimneys. *(Settled R1121-80.)*
17. **The way down and up.** Pick: the rope ladder of 28 May, wet on the climb home. *(Settled R1121-80.)*

### People and talk

18. **Who does what at the float.** Pick: Harbert chips and reads, and Pencroff kneels, holds the glass and watches the water. *(Settled R1121-80.)*
19. **Pencroff's word to Harbert.** Pick: he calls him "my boy", as in the French ("mon garçon", I.4 and elsewhere). His request is short: help with the lines at the morning ebb. *(Settled R1121-80.)*
20. **Does Harbert know the barnacles by name?** Pick: yes, and he names them, as he names the tinamous "avec raison" (II.3). *(Settled R1121-80.)*

### The page

21. **Length.** Pick: about 2,500 words. Its neighbours run 3,064 (I.19) and 2,659 (I.20). *(Settled R1121-80.)*
22. **The heading.** White prints a summary under each chapter number, in phrases set off by dashes (his chapter XX: "THE RAINY SEASON—WHAT TO WEAR—A SEAL-HUNT—…"). Pick: the insert has one too, in his manner. *(Settled R1121-80.)*
23. **The number.** "XIX bis", between White's XIX and XX, with a note that it has no translation (BRIEF phase 9). *(Settled R1121-81.)*

### The author's addition

24. **Pencroff's sum.** While Harbert chips, Pencroff counts in his head what the glass is worth: light for the shuttered house, a float for his lines. Never aloud. The reader does the sum when it breaks. *(Settled R1121-82.)* It rests on the shutters kept "en attendant que l’ingénieur eût eu le temps de fabriquer du verre à vitre" (I.19 ¶46) and on Pencroff's lines (R1121-60).
