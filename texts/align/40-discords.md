# II.18: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller m. Beads corrected: none.*

### 1. cut: the drawplate named in Smith's plan (II.18 ¶33; White ¶33)
- The French has Smith name the tool that will make the wire; White says only that he thinks they can succeed. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: mais au moyen d’une filière, je pense que nous en viendrons à bout
  - white: but I think we can succeed

### 2. condensed: the drawplate becomes "a steel plate" (II.18 ¶36; White ¶36)
- White never names the "filière" (drawplate); a reader of White meets a steel plate with holes and does not learn its name or the metallurgist's phrase on tempering. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Cyrus Smith commença par fabriquer une filière, c’est-à-dire une plaque d’acier
  - white: Smith began by making a steel plate

### 3. changed: length of the drawn wires (II.18 ¶38; White ¶38)
- The French gives wires of forty to fifty feet; White gives thirty or forty. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: des fils longs de quarante à cinquante pieds
  - white: wires 30 or 40 feet long

### 4. condensed: "a few days" becomes "a little while" (II.18 ¶39; White ¶39)
- The time the wire-drawing took, a few days, is lost. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Il ne fallut que quelques jours pour mener à bien cette besogne
  - white: It took but a little while to get this work under way

### 5. cut: how retort carbon is made (II.18 ¶40; White ¶40)
- White drops the clause that the carbon is what is left once the coal has been dehydrogenated, and renders "charbon de cornue" as "coke". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: après que la houille a été déshydrogénée
  - white: that hard deposit obtained from gas retorts

### 6. changed: the cloth that holds the clay plug (II.18 ¶43; White ¶43)
- The French has a clay plug held by a cloth, with the glass tube closed at its lower end; White has plugs "connected with threads" and nothing of the closed end. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: au moyen d’un tampon d’argile maintenu par un linge
  - white: stopped: with clay plugs, connected with threads

### 7. condensed: the direction of the current (II.18 ¶44; White ¶44)
- The French says the current runs from the flask plate to the tube plate; White says only that a circuit is produced between tube and jar. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qui alla de la lame du flacon à celle du tube
  - white: thus produced a circuit between the tube and jar

### 8. cut: the narrator's summing up of the apparatus (II.18 ¶45; White —)
- White has nothing for the sentence that closes the battery passage and says what it will let Smith do. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: appareil qui allait lui permettre d’établir une communication télégraphique entre Granite-house et le corral
  - white: —

### 9. cut: the earth carries the current back; the line follows the corral road (II.18 ¶46; White ¶45)
- White drops that the wire follows the corral road and that the earth is charged with bringing the current back to its start. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: que la terre se chargerait de ramener à son point de départ
  - white: ready to produce the electric current, which travels with the speed of 100,000 kilometres a second

### 10. changed: the electromagnet when the current is cut (II.18 ¶48; White ¶47)
- The French says the electromagnet is demagnetised the moment the current is interrupted; White says "The current closed", which reverses the sense. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Le courant était-il interrompu, l’électro-aimant se désaimantait aussitôt.
  - white: The current closed, the attraction of the electro-magnet ceased.

### 11. cut: Smith can correspond from one station to the other (II.18 ¶48; White ¶47)
- White's account of the dial ends with the letters on its face; the French adds that this lets him correspond between the stations. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: et, de cette façon, correspondre d’une station à l’autre
  - white: which bore the letters of the alphabet upon its face

### 12. changed: grains counted per hour (II.18 ¶52; White ¶51)
- The French says three hundred a minute is nine thousand an hour; White says 18,000 an hour (which is what 300 a minute comes to; the French figure is Verne's slip). *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: soit neuf mille à l’heure
  - white: or 18,000 an hour

### 13. cut: Pencroff gives up the count (II.18 ¶52; White ¶51)
- White does not say that the sailor gave up counting. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le brave marin crut devoir y renoncer
  - white: it would take him 5,500 years to accomplish his undertaking

### 14. cut: the cart also carries coal (II.18 ¶54; White ¶53)
- The French has the onagers draw wood and coal to Granite-house; White has wood and minerals only. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le bois et la houille
  - white: to drag wood or bring the minerals

### 15. cut: Harbert has become an excellent rider (II.18 ¶54; White ¶53)
- White does not say that Harbert became an excellent horseman under the reporter. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: devenu un excellent cavalier sous la direction du reporter
  - white: ridden by Spilett and Herbert

### 16. changed: the dog barks at the well; White has it growl (II.18 ¶58; White ¶56)
- The dog "aboya" (barked) at the mouth of the well; White has it growl. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le chien aboya à l’orifice de ce puits
  - white: the dog had growled more than once again above that pit

### 17. cut: the photographic apparatus came from the box (II.18 ¶59; White ¶57)
- White drops that the apparatus was found in the box. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qui avait été trouvé dans la caisse
  - white: by means of the photographic apparatus, which had not been used until now

### 18. condensed: the photographic supplies and their uses (II.18 ¶60; White ¶58)
- The French gives each substance with its use (collodion for the glass plate, silver nitrate to sensitise it, and so on) and the paper's treatment; White lists five chemicals, leaves out collodion, and gives no uses. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: collodion pour préparer la plaque de verre, nitrate d’argent pour la sensibiliser
  - white: the nitrate of silver, the hyposulphata of soda, the chloride of ammonium, the acetate of soda, and the chloride of gold

### 19. changed: Jup's portrait looks about to grimace (II.18 ¶66; White ¶63)
- Pencroff says Jup looks about to make a face; White has him say Jup was laughing. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: On dirait qu’il va faire la grimace !
  - white: One would say he was laughing!

### 20. cut: March matches September in the north (II.18 ¶68; White ¶65)
- White drops the equivalence of March to September in northern latitudes. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qui correspond au mois de septembre des latitudes boréales
  - white: and the month was not as pleasant as they had expected

### 21. condensed: Centigrade equivalent of 58 degrees (II.18 ¶75; White ¶72)
- White gives 58° with no Centigrade figure. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: cinquante-huit degrés (14° centig. au-dessus de zéro)
  - white: “But the thermometer stands at 58°,” said Spilett.

### 22. softened: Pencroff's oath (II.18 ¶77; White ¶74)
- "Mille diables" becomes "The deuce". *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: « Mille diables ! s’écria Pencroff
  - white: “The deuce!” cried the sailor

### 23. changed: how many birds, and where they came from (II.18 ¶81; White ¶78)
- The French says the birds had settled by hundreds of thousands on the islet and coast; White says they had come from hundreds of miles around, which the French does not say. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Ils s’étaient abattus par centaines de mille sur l’îlot et sur la côte
  - white: They had come from hundreds of miles around on to the islet and the coast

### 24. changed: who was thrown on the island (II.18 ¶82; White ¶79)
- The French says "the castaways of the air" were thrown on the island; White says the balloon was. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il y avait deux ans que les naufragés de l’air avaient été jetés sur l’île Lincoln
  - white: Two years had passed since the balloon had been thrown upon Lincoln Island.
