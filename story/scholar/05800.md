## a

After: “The Frenchman whom chance threw on board my vessel sixteen years ago?”

The Captain speaks in October of 1868, so his sixteen years put the Frenchman on board in 1852. A few pages on, the same account gives the night as the 6th of November, 1866, and the escape as the 22nd of June, 1867. Neither will stand with the rest of his history. He was in Bundelkund in 1849 and in the revolt of 1857, and built his vessel only after it; he sailed “during many years” before the three men were thrown on his deck, and “for a long time” after. Yet he had lain six years in his port beneath the island when the balloon fell in March of 1865, which brings him there about 1859. The year 1852 falls before the vessel was built, and the years 1866 and 1867 fall while she lay in her port under the island and went nowhere. I find no year that the history allows, and leave it open.
