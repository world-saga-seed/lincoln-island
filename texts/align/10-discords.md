# I.10: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller e. Beads corrected: 40 and 41 (French ¶56 is rendered at the head of White ¶40, not in White ¶39).*

### 1. cut: the reporter's answer and the source of the heat (I.10 ¶5; White ¶5)
- The French says Spilett's answer was exact and that it was the sun that gave the heat Pencroff marvelled at; White goes straight to the sailor's stupefaction. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: La réponse de Gédéon Spilett était exacte. C’était le soleil qui avait fourni cette chaleur dont s’émerveillait Pencroff.
  - white: The sailor could not believe his eyes, and was too stupefied to question the engineer.

### 2. softened: a god becomes a magician (I.10 ¶9; White ¶9)
- Pencroff's thought that Cyrus is not a god but certainly more than a man loses its religious word. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Si, pour lui, Cyrus Smith n’était pas un dieu, c’était assurément plus qu’un homme.
  - white: If Smith was not a magician to him, he was certainly more than a man.

### 3. condensed: why the question comes before all others (I.10 ¶14; White ¶13)
- White reduces "island or continent" to an "urgent question" and drops that Cyrus rightly put it before every other. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: à laquelle il donnait, non sans raison, le pas sur toutes les autres
  - white: He hoped, therefore, for an easy solution of the urgent question, “Island or continent?”

### 4. changed: pine kernels become pistachio-nuts (I.10 ¶15; White ¶14)
- The French has the almonds of the pine-nut pine; White has pistachio-nuts (and again at ¶46, White ¶34). *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Les sargasses et les amandes de pin pignon complétèrent ce repas
  - white: the sargassum and pistachio-nuts completed the repast.

### 5. cut: Cyrus's methodical mind (I.10 ¶16; White ¶14)
- White drops the narrator's remark that Cyrus was evidently a methodical mind, the reason he only shakes his head. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qui était évidemment un esprit méthodique
  - white: Once or twice Pencroff talked of some project for the future, but Smith shook his head.

### 6. cut: the date, 29 March (I.10 ¶18; White ¶16)
- The French gives the date of the morning of the climb; White does not, and drops Top among the sleepers and the quiet night. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le lendemain, — 29 mars, — frais et dispos
  - white: The morning found them fresh and eager for the expedition which was to settle their fate.

### 7. changed: flint is probable in the French, certain in White (I.10 ¶19; White ¶17)
- Pencroff's thought that the flint "should not be lacking" in plutonic ground becomes a statement of fact in volcanic ground. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Quant au silex, il ne devait pas manquer dans ces terrains d’origine plutonienne.
  - white: and flint abounded in this volcanic region.

### 8. changed: the western edge of the forest becomes the northern (I.10 ¶20; White ¶18)
- The French has them reach the west edge of the forest at nine o'clock; White says "soon" and names the northern border. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: à neuf heures, Cyrus Smith et ses compagnons atteignaient la lisière occidentale de la forêt
  - white: and soon reached the northern border of the forest.

### 9. addition: paws and hoofs (I.10 ¶27; White ¶21)
- The French says only tracks of large animals, "fauves ou autres"; White says the marks were of paws and hoofs. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: des empreintes qui indiquaient le passage récent de grands animaux, fauves ou autres
  - white: the recent marks of huge paws and hoofs on the ground.

### 10. changed: the tragopan is the size of a cock, not a hen (I.10 ¶32; White ¶25)
- The French compares the pairs to a cock in size; White to a hen. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: de la taille d’un coq
  - white: which were about the size of a hen

### 11. changed: fifty paces become fifty feet (I.10 ¶35; White ¶27)
- The distance to the mouflons is given in paces by Verne and in feet by White. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: à cinquante pas d’une demi-douzaine d’animaux de grande taille
  - white: fifty feet from half-a-dozen large animals

### 12. changed: solfataras become sulphur springs (I.10 ¶42; White ¶32)
- The French has small solfataras (vents) that cut the way and must be skirted; White has sulphur springs. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: De petites solfatares coupaient parfois la route suivie par les ascensionnistes, et il fallait en prolonger les bords.
  - white: little sulphur springs intercepted their route.

### 13. condensed: the pozzolana and the ash of crystals (I.10 ¶42; White ¶32)
- White reduces the pozzolana with coarse, strongly baked grains and the ash made of countless feldspar crystals to "sand and whitish cinders of feldspar". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: pouzzolanes à grains irréguliers et fortement torréfiés
  - white: in the midst of the sand and whitish cinders of feldspar

### 14. changed: two miles become two thousand feet (I.10 ¶44; White ¶33)
- The five hundred feet grow by zigzags to more than two miles in the French; White has 2,000 feet. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ces cinq cents pieds s’accrurent de plus de deux milles par les zigzags qu’il fallut décrire
  - white: but these 500 were increased to 2,000 and more by the tortuous route.

### 15. changed: brushwood becomes thistles (I.10 ¶45; White ¶34)
- The fuel at the camp is moss and dry brushwood in the French; White has moss and dry thistles (and a load of thistles). *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: au moyen des mousses et des broussailles sèches qui hérissaient certaines portions du plateau
  - white: yet the moss and dry thistles, so abundant on the plateau, would serve their turn.

### 16. changed: pine kernels become pistachio-nuts again (I.10 ¶46; White ¶34)
- The supper has a few dozen pine almonds in the French; White has pistachio-nuts. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: quelques douzaines d’amandes de pin pignon
  - white: a few dozen pistachio-nuts.

### 17. cut: why Cyrus must go round the plateau (I.10 ¶47; White ¶35)
- White gives only that Cyrus wished to know whether the cone's base could be passed; he drops that the hat leans north, where the plateau may be impassable, and that the aim of the climb would then be partly lost. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il était possible que, du côté où le chapeau s’inclinait, c’est-à-dire vers le nord, le plateau ne fût pas praticable
  - white: Before taking rest, he was anxious to know whether the base of the cone could be passed, in case its flanks should prove too steep for ascent.

### 18. changed: seventy degrees become seventy-five (I.10 ¶49; White ¶36)
- The slope that stops them is near seventy degrees in the French; White has seventy-five. White also turns the two cones' slopes meeting flush, with no shoulder between, into a slant that was "equal". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: La contourner sur des pentes inclinées à près de soixante-dix degrés devenait impraticable.
  - white: To walk around the mountain upon an acclivity whose angle was nearly seventy-five degrees was impossible.

### 19. changed: the circle of sky widens, not the light (I.10 ¶55; White ¶39)
- The French says the radius of the circular piece of sky framed by the cone's rim grew; White says the light from the sky increased. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Le rayon de cette portion circulaire du ciel, encadrée par les bords du cône, s’accrut sensiblement.
  - white: The light from the outer sky visibly increased.

### 20. cut: the Southern Cross replaces the Pole Star (I.10 ¶55; White ¶39)
- White ends the sentence at the Southern Cross and drops that it takes the place of the northern Pole Star. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qui remplace la Polaire de l’hémisphère boréal
  - white: the glowing Southern Cross.

### 21. changed: a continent to the west becomes the eastern end of a continent (I.10 ¶57; White ¶40)
- The French asks whether the land joins, to the west, some continent of the Pacific; White asks whether it is the eastern extremity of a continent. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ou cette terre se rattachait-elle, dans l’ouest, à quelque continent du Pacifique ?
  - white: Was the land an island, or the eastern extremity of a continent?
