# I.20: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller g. Beads corrected: beads 34 and 35. The pairing is French ¶46 with White ¶33, French ¶47 with White ¶34 ("Faith, I don't know"), and French ¶48 with White ¶35.*

### 1. cut: Cyrus's precautions at the Chimneys (I.20 ¶1; White ¶1)
- The French says Cyrus took some precautions against the tides, to preserve the forge and furnaces set up at the Chimneys; White says only that they feared the tides would destroy the furnaces and foundry. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Cyrus Smith prit même quelques précautions, en prévision de cette éventualité, afin de préserver, autant que possible, la forge et les fourneaux qui y étaient installés
  - white: they feared even lest the high tides driven by the sea-wind should pour in and destroy their furnaces and their foundry

### 2. changed: the amphibians of the islet become those of the island (I.20 ¶18; White ¶16)
- The French has the fat come from the amphibians of the islet; White has "the amphibia of the island". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: les amphibies de l’îlot lui fourniraient la graisse nécessaire
  - white: the amphibia of the island would furnish the necessary fat

### 3. cut: the prayers that rise to heaven (I.20 ¶19; White ¶16)
- The French says all work was suspended and prayers rose to heaven, now prayers of thanks; "they no longer asked, they thanked". White says they kept it "as a day of rest and thanksgiving" and has them "colonists" in place of the French's wretched castaways of the islet. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Tous travaux furent suspendus, et des prières s’élevèrent vers le ciel.
  - white: which they kept as a day of rest and thanksgiving

### 4. cut: the uncertain weather of 5 June (I.20 ¶20; White ¶16)
- The French has them set out for the islet in rather uncertain weather; White gives no weather. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: par un temps assez incertain
  - white: On the next day, June 5, they started for the islet.

### 5. changed: the glycerine resolved into the fatty acids (I.20 ¶23; White ¶17)
- In the French, after the glycerine is isolated, Cyrus would separate the oleine, margarine and stearine from the new combination with boiling water; White has the glycerine itself resolved by boiling water into the three acids. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: de la combinaison nouvelle, il eût facilement séparé l’oléine, la margarine et la stéarine, en employant l’eau bouillante
  - white: separate the glycerine, which again could be resolved, by means of boiling water, into oleine, margarine, and stearine

### 6. changed: the candles are unbleached and unpolished (I.20 ¶25; White ¶18)
- The French says the candles lacked only whitening and polishing; White says they were "white and smooth". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: auxquelles il ne manqua que le blanchiment et le polissage
  - white: They were real stearine candles, made by hand, white and smooth.

### 7. cut: the wicks, the snuffers and the esteem for the candles (I.20 ¶25; White ¶18)
- The French says the wicks, unlike those soaked in boric acid, did not vitrify and burn away; that Cyrus made a fine pair of snuffers; and that the candles were much prized in the evenings at Granite-house. White ends at "white and smooth". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: mais Cyrus Smith ayant fabriqué une belle paire de mouchettes, ces bougies furent grandement appréciées pendant les veillées de Granite-house
  - white: —

### 8. condensed: the forests still to be searched (I.20 ¶30; White ¶21)
- The French says the wooded part from the Mercy to the Reptile Promontory, searched to its most secret recesses, would yield new treasures; White says "the western forests". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: sur toute cette partie boisée qui s’étendait depuis la Mercy jusqu’au promontoire du Reptile
  - white: a minute exploration of the western forests

### 9. softened: Providence becomes "a little incident" (I.20 ¶33; White ¶23)
- The French says Providence was to come directly to the colonists' help, in an infinitesimal measure, and that Harbert found the grain in the lining of his jacket, which he was mending; White says only that a little incident occurred. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: la Providence devait, en cette circonstance, venir directement en aide aux colons
  - white: a little incident occurred which brought about what Smith, with all his ingenuity, could not have achieved

### 10. cut: "We shall make bread of it" and Pencroff's cakes and tarts (I.20 ¶40–¶42; White ¶29)
- The French has Pencroff ask what they can do with one grain, Cyrus answer "We shall make bread of it", and Pencroff reply "Bread, cakes, tarts!"; White gives Pencroff's joke about the bread alone and drops Cyrus's answer and the cakes and tarts. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Nous en ferons du pain, répondit Cyrus Smith.
  - white: —

### 11. cut: "yet they were exact" (I.20 ¶49; White ¶36)
- The French says the figures stupefied them and that they were nevertheless exact; White leaves out that they were exact. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Ils étaient exacts, cependant.
  - white: The figures stupefied them.
