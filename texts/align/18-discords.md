# I.18: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller g. Beads corrected: beads 72, 73 and 74 are one paragraph out. The pairing is French ¶78 with White ¶71, French ¶79 with White ¶72, and French ¶80 with the opening of White ¶73 ("Forward!" said Smith).*

### 1. condensed: the materials for powder (I.18 ¶9; White ¶9)
- Cyrus names the four materials for making ordinary powder; White says only "the material". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: puisque nous avons l’acide azotique, le salpêtre, le soufre et le charbon
  - white: as we have the material

### 2. condensed: the traces that the water could not wash out (I.18 ¶19; White ¶19)
- The French says the granite was worn by the water through endless time, and that the traces of eruptive work were such as the washing of the waters had not wholly effaced; White gives only that the traces were still visible. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: un travail éruptif que le lavage des eaux n’avait pu totalement effacer
  - white: the traces of igneous action were still visible upon its surface

### 3. changed: the octopus becomes "pulp" (I.18 ¶20; White ¶20)
- The French fears an octopus or other giant cephalopod; White prints "pulp", so that a reader of White gets a word that names no animal (a misprint for "polypus", my reading). *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: quelque poulpe ou autre gigantesque céphalopode
  - white: some pulp or other gigantic cephalopod

### 4. cut: how they know they are a third of the way down (I.18 ¶29; White ¶28)
- Cyrus agrees that they are about a third down because they have gone down about a hundred feet from the orifice; White gives only "But 100 feet" and drops "about a third" and the reason. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Au tiers environ, répondit Cyrus Smith, car nous avons descendu une centaine de pieds depuis l’orifice
  - white: “But 100 feet,” responded Cyrus

### 5. cut: Pencroff's guess that Top went on (I.18 ¶32; White ¶31)
- Pencroff's remark that the dog has probably gone on is left out. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Il aura probablement continué sa route, dit Pencroff.
  - white: —

### 6. changed: sixty feet become sixteen (I.18 ¶40; White ¶36)
- The colonists slide sixty feet lower to reach Top; White gives sixteen feet. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: soixante pieds plus bas, ils eurent rejoint Top
  - white: Sixteen feet lower they came up with the dog.

### 7. changed: why the burning branch shines brighter (I.18 ¶48; White ¶43)
- In the French the resin shines the more because of the speed of its fall; White ascribes it to the wind of the fall fanning it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dont le pouvoir éclairant s’accrut encore par la rapidité de sa chute
  - white: fanned by the wind of its fall, burned brightly

### 8. changed: who hears Cyrus murmur of Top (I.18 ¶55; White ¶49)
- The French says that any companion who had come near would have heard him (a conditional: it is not said that any did); White says that those near him did hear. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: celui de ses compagnons qui se fût approché de lui l’eût entendu murmurer ces paroles
  - white: those who were near him heard him murmur

### 9. softened: "I think so" becomes "I am convinced" (I.18 ¶56; White ¶50)
- Cyrus says "Yes, I quite believe" that Top knows more than they do; White makes it a conviction. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: Oui, je crois bien que Top en sait plus long que nous sur bien des choses
  - white: Yes, I am convinced that Top knows more than we do about many things!

### 10. changed: the two hours of work (I.18 ¶63; White ¶56)
- In the French the work "had already lasted two hours" in all, after the half-hour and the relays; White has it continue "two hours longer". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Ce travail durait depuis deux heures déjà
  - white: The work continued, two hours longer

### 11. cut: the width of the left-hand cavern, and the size of the hole (I.18 ¶67; White ¶60)
- The French says the left part is no more than thirty feet high and wide; White gives the height and the length only. It also leaves out that the hole was fairly wide because the rock had crumbled much. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: elle ne mesurait pas plus de trente pieds de haut et de large sur une longueur de cent pieds
  - white: it measured only thirty feet in height and one hundred in length

### 12. cut: the footnote to Granite-house (I.18 ¶72 n1; White ¶65)
- The French carries a footnote glossing "Granite-house" as "Palais de granit", with "house" used for palaces and houses alike, as in Buckingham-house; White has no note. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Palais de granit. Le mot house s’applique également aux palais et aux maisons.
  - white: —

### 13. changed: the well is not silent but has the sound of water (I.18 ¶74; White ¶67)
- The French says no sound came, not even that of the water; White says there was no sound save the water's. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Aucun bruit ne se produisit, pas même celui des eaux
  - white: But there was no sound from these depths save that of the water agitated by the undulations of the surge.

### 14. cut: the conduit that took the lake's overflow (I.18 ¶74; White ¶67)
- Cyrus's reasoning is cut short: the French adds that the underground conduit under the beach was the one the lake's overflow followed before a new outlet was offered, and that the well sank perpendicularly to sea level. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: et que suivait le trop-plein du lac, avant qu’une nouvelle issue lui eût été offerte
  - white: —

## Outline slips

- Beat 3 of the outline gives the plate (I.18 pl1) caption "Les colons descendaient lentement." as the French of ¶20; the text's own words are "Les colons ne descendaient que fort lentement". The plate caption is a separate line after ¶20, so this is a point of citing rather than a contradiction. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - outline: Les colons descendaient lentement.
  - quote: Les colons ne descendaient que fort lentement.
