# The ladder gone and the orang (notes)

## Notes for drafting
- **Where it sits.** The French II.6, directly after II.5 ¶120 ("There was no longer any ladder."): the colonists, back from the west-coast excursion, find the ladder of Granite-house gone, spend the night in the Chimneys, and next day retake the dwelling from a band of apes; the chapter ends with the orang, Jup, taken into service. *(Drafted.)*
- **Cast.** Cyrus Smith (¶1), Pencroff (¶2), Nab (¶3), Harbert (¶4), Gédéon Spilett (¶6), Top the dog (¶16). The "hunter with the lead shot" is named only as a hypothesis (¶8). The apes, "a band" (¶43, ¶87), three or four seen at once (¶43); one shot dead on the beach (¶45); more killed or fallen (¶74); one large orang survives, bound (¶89, ¶96), named Jupiter, Jup (¶116). Buffon is mentioned as owner of an orang (¶100). *(Drafted.)*
- **Places.** The foot of Granite-house in the dark (¶1); the Chimneys, where they sleep (¶14, ¶17); the shore at the edge of the reefs (¶21); the Mercy's left bank and the Grande-Vue plateau, for the planned descent by the old overflow of the lake (¶65, ¶68); the corridor, hall and storeroom of Granite-house (¶87, ¶89). *(Drafted.)*
- **Things.** The ladder: the upper ladder stays "stretched from the landing to the door" (¶24); the lower is first missing, then seen drawn up to the threshold (¶24), then pulled in with the rope (¶37), the rope breaking (¶53), and finally lowered from inside (¶81). Bows and arrows and some fathoms of light hibiscus cord kept in a passage of the Chimneys (¶33). Rifle and carbine (¶34, ¶45). Picks and mattocks (¶68). The old overflow's mouth, closed by a cemented stone wall (¶67). Plates: pl1 (after ¶16), pl2 (after ¶45), pl3 (after ¶51). *(Drafted.)*
- **Time.** The chapter opens at night after the return (¶1, "that deep night"); "depuis sept mois" (¶12); dawn light in the east, the windows visible "avant cinq heures" (¶21); two hours' wait (¶55); breakfast and a half-hour hunt (¶57–¶58); two hours later (¶60); "past noon" (¶68); the afternoon recapture. The chapter covers one night and the following day, to evening. The date is not given here: II.7 ¶9 and ¶11 place the next day at 3 November, with the ladder returned "hier"; so the apes' day is 2 November and the night before it 1 to 2 November. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
- **Clues.** The ladder gone with no wind (`Il n’a pas pourtant fait de vent`, ¶4); the snigger heard from Granite-house (`a sort of snigger whose origin they could not make out`, ¶11); the influence Cyrus cannot name (¶18); the apes' sudden terror from "quelque cause inconnue" (¶74); the ladder lowered from inside at once after Cyrus's remark (¶81); the storeroom untouched (¶87); who sent the ladder (¶88); Cyrus's odd voice when he says the orang threw it (¶95). The chapter does not say who withdrew the ladder, what frightened the apes, or who lowered it; Pencroff and Cyrus leave it open (¶82–¶83, ¶88). *(My reading, to confirm.)*
- **Length.** 117 paragraphs (¶1–¶117), 3,513 words; the summary ¶0 adds 40; 3 plate captions; no footnotes. *(Drafted.)*

## Open and flags
- Seam with II.5 ¶120: the last line of 02700 is "There was no longer any ladder." and ¶1 here opens with Cyrus Smith stopping "without saying a word" and finding "the ladder had absolutely disappeared". The first beat follows. *(Drafted.)*
- The text disagrees with itself over the ladder: ¶24 says `l’échelle inférieure avait été retirée et relevée jusqu’au seuil`, while ¶74 has the narrator say of the apes `Ils ne cherchaient même pas à replacer l’échelle, par laquelle il leur eût été facile de descendre`, and ¶42 has Pencroff say the apes climbed `par l’échelle pendant notre absence`. Card neither reading. *(Drafted.)*
- ¶1 says the ladder had possibly been lifted `jusqu’au premier palier, à mi-paroi`; ¶24 says the lower ladder is raised to the threshold of the door. Both are the text's. *(Drafted.)*
- ¶43 gives `trois ou quatre quadrumanes` at the windows; ¶74 gives `cinq ou six` in position to be shot, and ¶89 a further ape in the passage; the count is never given. *(Drafted.)*
- ¶101 gives the orang as six feet tall, with a "small curly beard, hazel in colour"; ¶46 says Harbert declared it an orang-outang, and the narrator (¶46) is unsure between chimpanzee, orang, gorilla and gibbon. *(Drafted.)*
