# II.3: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller h. Beads corrected: none.*

### 1. changed: preserved meat becomes dried meat; the gallons are gone (II.3 ¶4; White ¶4)
- The French has tinned or preserved meat and some gallons of beer and fermented liquor; White has "dried meat" and no quantity. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: conserves de viande et de quelques gallons de bière et de liqueur fermentée
  - white: enough dried meat, beer, and fermented liquor

### 2. condensed: the caps would be used up (II.3 ¶6; White ¶6)
- The French says the caps would be quickly exhausted by frequent use; White says they could not be replaced. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: des amorces fulminantes, qu’un fréquent usage eût promptement épuisées
  - white: whereas the caps could not be replaced

### 3. cut: the moon will be full in three days (II.3 ¶9; White ¶8)
- The French gives the moon's phase as a reason for the strong flood; White omits it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: la lune devait être pleine trois jours après
  - white: The current was strong, and they did not need to row

### 4. changed: the river bends toward the southwest, or flows from it (II.3 ¶11; White ¶8)
- The French says the river curves and slants toward the southwest; White says it flows from the southwest. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: obliquait vers le sud-ouest
  - white: flowing from the southwest

### 5. changed: the hackberry's almond becomes its root (II.3 ¶12; White ¶8)
- The oil comes from the kernel of the hackberry; White says the root, and leaves the tree's name in French. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dont l’amande produit une huile fort utile
  - white: the micocouliers, the root of which produces a useful oil

### 6. cut: tobacco only in the pipe-bowl (II.3 ¶15; White ¶11)
- The French says Pencroff had seen his favourite plant only in the bowl of his pipe; White omits the joke. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: n’avait jamais vu sa plante de prédilection que dans le fourneau de sa pipe
  - white: had evidently never seen the plant which he fancied so much

### 7. changed: two pairs of tinamous become "a couple" (II.3 ¶22; White ¶18)
- Spilett catches two pairs of live birds; White says a couple. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: deux couples de gallinacés vivants
  - white: a couple of birds

### 8. cut: Pencroff's shot goes off in spite of him (II.3 ¶24; White ¶20)
- The narrator says Pencroff's shot went off against his will; White has only his cry. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: et on peut dire que son coup partit malgré lui
  - white: “I know it,” cried Pencroff.

### 9. condensed: the dozen touracos (II.3 ¶28; White ¶24)
- The French says a dozen touracos, green with crimson wings and a white-edged crest; White gives "some touracolories" and the size only. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: une douzaine de « touracos-loris », sortes de grimpeurs de la grosseur d’un pigeon, tout peinturlurés de vert
  - white: some touracolories, climbing birds, the size of pigeons

### 10. cut: no axe or knife had ever touched the forest (II.3 ¶30; White ¶26)
- The French says the explorers found that no woodman's axe had ever cut the trees and no pioneer's knife the creepers; White says only that they found no trace of man. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: jamais la hache du bûcheron n’avait entaillé ces arbres, que jamais le couteau du pionnier n’avait tranché ces lianes
  - white: but no where could they detect the presence of man

### 11. changed: "at least five miles" becomes "five miles or less" (II.3 ¶31; White ¶27)
- The engineer reckons the west coast at five miles at the least; White says five miles or less. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: de cinq milles au moins
  - white: about five miles or less

### 12. changed: the river runs toward the mountain, not the shore (II.3 ¶31; White ¶27)
- The French says the Mercy ran not toward the shore but toward Mount Franklin; White says it ran sometimes to the shore, oftener to the mountain. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: non vers le littoral, mais plutôt vers le mont Franklin
  - white: sometimes towards the shore, was oftener towards the mountain

### 13. changed: the flood fails; White says the tide had reached its height (II.3 ¶32; White ¶27)
- The French says the flood failed altogether, because the tide was falling or no longer felt so far up; White says the tide had attained its height. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Mais bientôt le flux manqua tout à fait, soit que la marée baissât
  - white: Soon, the tide having attained its height

### 14. cut: the latitude a botanist would name (II.3 ¶34; White ¶29)
- The French says the trees would let a botanist name the parallel of the island; White omits the sentence. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: leur présence eût suffi à un botaniste pour qu’il nommât sans hésitation le parallèle que traversait l’île Lincoln
  - white: —

### 15. changed: bark "five inches" thick becomes "five fingers" (II.3 ¶36; White ¶29)
- The bark is up to five inches thick; White says five fingers. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: comptait jusqu’à cinq pouces d’épaisseur
  - white: their bark, five fingers in thickness

### 16. changed: the eucalypts are themselves myrtles (II.3 ¶36; White ¶29)
- The French says these huge trees belong to the myrtle family; White sets "the enormous specimens of myrtle" beside them as a separate wonder. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ces énormes échantillons de la famille des myrtacées
  - white: Equally wonderful were the enormous specimens of myrtle

### 17. cut: the grass and the small birds (II.3 ¶37; White ¶29)
- The French describes fresh grass at the foot of the trees and flights of small birds glittering like winged carbuncles; White omits it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: des volées de petits oiseaux
  - white: —

### 18. condensed: Harbert's list of the myrtle family (II.3 ¶41; White ¶33)
- Harbert names guava, clove, pomegranate, a wine eugenia, ugni, cinnamon, allspice, pepper-myrtle, manna and beer eucalypts, and gives forty-six genera and thirteen hundred species; White says only that he "enumerated many species". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dont on compte quarante-six genres et treize cents espèces
  - white: the young naturalist enumerated many species of the plant and their uses

### 19. changed: the engineer's reply is given to the reporter (II.3 ¶56; White ¶47)
- Cyrus answers that it will come and be found; White gives the reply to the reporter. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: cela se trouvera, répondit l’ingénieur
  - white: we will find it,” answered the reporter

### 20. cut: the eucalyptus belt (II.3 ¶57; White ¶48)
- The French says the party went on for two miles at least through country covered with eucalypts that dominated the woods; White says "through the woods". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: pendant deux milles au moins, au milieu d’une contrée couverte d’eucalyptus
  - white: They continued on through the woods two miles further

### 21. condensed: tall grass and sharp rocks in the river bed (II.3 ¶57; White ¶48)
- The French says the bed was often choked with tall grass and sharp rocks that hampered the oars; White says the river was winding, shallow and narrow. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Ce lit était souvent obstrué de hautes herbes et même de roches aiguës
  - white: the river becoming more winding, shallow, and so narrow that Pencroff pushed along with a pole

### 22. cut: Pencroff's temptation to shoot the monkeys (II.3 ¶58; White ¶49)
- The French says the useless slaughter tempted Pencroff a little; White keeps only that Smith opposed it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qui tentait un peu l’enragé Pencroff
  - white: but Smith was opposed to the useless slaughter

### 23. cut: why it was prudent not to provoke the monkeys (II.3 ¶58; White ¶49)
- The French adds that it was prudent, since the monkeys were strong and agile and might be dangerous; White omits it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: car ces singes, vigoureux, doués d’une extrême agilité, pouvaient être redoutables
  - white: —

### 24. condensed: the waterfall a few hundred paces ahead (II.3 ¶68; White ¶58)
- The French says the fall was heard a few hundred paces upstream; White says "some little distance further on". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: à quelques cents pas en amont
  - white: some little distance further on

### 25. changed: moored to a trunk becomes grounded on a rock (II.3 ¶69; White ¶59)
- The canoe is tied to a tree trunk near the right bank; White says it grounded on a rock. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il était amarré à un tronc, près de la rive droite
  - white: then grounded on a rock near the right bank

### 26. cut: the rainbow spray (II.3 ¶70; White ¶60)
- The French says the spray of the little fall shone in the colours of the prism; White omits it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dont l’humide poussière resplendissait des couleurs du prisme
  - white: —

### 27. softened: roars become growling (II.3 ¶72; White ¶62)
- The French says suspect roars were heard at nightfall; White says growling. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: quelques rugissements de nature suspecte
  - white: some suspicious growling
