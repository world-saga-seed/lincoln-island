# III.18: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller r. Beads corrected: beads 83, 84 and 85 are one paragraph out. The pairing is French ¶86 with White ¶82; French ¶87 with White ¶83; French ¶88–¶89 with White ¶84.*

### 1. cut: the basalt pier of the arch (III.18 ¶1; White ¶1)
- The French says the waves beat the basaltic pier of the arch; White says only that the piers were washed by the waves. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dont le flot battait le pied-droit basaltique
  - white: whose piers were washed by the waves

### 2. cut: the date of the night (III.18 ¶5; White ¶5)
- The French dates the night of the storm and the Nautilus, 15 to 16 October; White says only "this night". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Les divers incidents de cette nuit du 15 au 16 octobre
  - white: The incidents of this night

### 3. cut: the places they might sail to (III.18 ¶7; White ¶7)
- The French names the two possible destinations, a Polynesian archipelago of the Pacific or the coast of New Zealand; White drops both. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: soit un archipel polynésien du Pacifique, soit les côtes de la Nouvelle-Zélande
  - white: If, when it was finished, the colonists decided not to leave the island

### 4. cut: the notice concerns Ayrton (III.18 ¶7; White ¶7)
- The French says the notice to be left on Tabor Island is the one about Ayrton; White says only "a notice". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: afin d’y déposer la notice relative à Ayrton
  - white: leave a notice there

### 5. condensed: who works on the ship (III.18 ¶8; White ¶8)
- The French names Smith, Pencroff and Ayrton as the workers, helped by Nab, Spilett and Herbert when free; White says "all". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Cyrus Smith, Pencroff et Ayrton, aidés de Nab, de Gédéon Spilett et d’Harbert
  - white: All worked at it without ceasing

### 6. cut: the ship must be ready by the beginning of March (III.18 ¶8; White ¶8)
- The French turns "five months" into a date, the beginning of March; White leaves the five months without the date. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: c’est-à-dire pour le commencement de mars
  - white: finished in five months

### 7. changed: the Speedy's rigging becomes "sails" (III.18 ¶8; White ¶8)
- The French says the whole rigging of the Speedy was saved, so that only the hull remains to be finished; White says the sails were saved and drops that the hull is what remains. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: celui du Speedy avait été sauvé en entier
  - white: All the sails of the Speedy had been saved

### 8. condensed: how the ship will behave at sea (III.18 ¶9; White ¶9)
- The French says the plans show that the ship will behave well at sea; White says only that the plans were excellent. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le navire se comporterait bien à la mer
  - white: they were able to see that Smith’s plans were excellent

### 9. changed: a plume becomes "a black cloud" (III.18 ¶12; White ¶12)
- The French calls it an enormous plume; White calls it an immense black cloud, though the French gives the column as greyish. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: un énorme panache
  - white: an immense black cloud

### 10. changed: the interior boiling is denied (III.18 ¶15; White ¶15)
- The French says the earlier vapours came from an interior boiling of mineral matter; White says there had been no ebullition of mineral matters. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: mais qui ne provenaient encore que d’une ébullition intérieure des matières minérales
  - white: but there bad been no ebullition of mineral matters

### 11. cut: the size of the smoke column (III.18 ¶15; White ¶15)
- The French gives a greyish column over three hundred feet wide at its base, spreading seven to eight hundred feet above the peak; White gives no figure and no colour. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: large de plus de trois cents pieds à sa base, et qui s’épanouissait comme un immense champignon à une hauteur de sept à huit cents pieds au-dessus de la cime du mont
  - white: a thick column of smoke rose, like an immense mushroom, above the summit of the mountain

### 12. changed: loose soil in the south becomes "mixed soil inland" (III.18 ¶27; White ¶26)
- The French sets lavas in the north against loose soil in the south; White says "a mixed soil inland". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: laves au nord, sol meuble au midi
  - white: lavas to the north, a mixed soil inland

### 13. cut: no dislocation (III.18 ¶29; White ¶28)
- The French concludes that, the escape being wide enough, no dislocation and no explosion is to be feared, and states the width as a fact; White drops the dislocation and makes the width a condition. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: la soupape étant assez large, aucune dislocation ne se produirait, aucune explosion ne serait à craindre
  - white: if the escape-pipe was sufficiently large, no explosion need be feared

### 14. cut: the work Pencroff sets for them (III.18 ¶30; White ¶29)
- Pencroff says they are fitting the wales and that a dozen arms are not too many; White drops both. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Nous allons ajuster les précintes, et une douzaine de bras ne seront pas de trop.
  - white: we want all hands to-day!

### 15. cut: what the wales are, and the date (III.18 ¶31–¶32; White ¶30)
- The French says they set the wales, thick strakes that girdle the hull and bind the frames, and dates the day 3 January; White says only that they worked steadily all day. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: procédèrent à la pose des précintes, épais bordages qui forment la ceinture d’un bâtiment
  - white: All the colonists went down to the shipyard and worked steadily all day

### 16. cut: the wind carried the smoke west (III.18 ¶32; White ¶30)

  - quote: Le vent, soufflant du large, emportait toutes ces vapeurs dans l’ouest.

### 17. cut: the cause of the danger (III.18 ¶45; White ¶42)

  - quote: dont l’éruption serait la cause directe ou indirecte
