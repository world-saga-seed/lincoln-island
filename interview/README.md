# The intake and the interview

Before any card or outline, Claude has to learn the book that is in the writer's head. The writer may have years of it there. The interview is how it gets onto paper without Claude inventing what it does not know.

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| [intake.md](intake.md) | The first look at the existing work: what is there and what kind of material each piece is |
| [questions.md](questions.md) | The interview script, by section. Each question says why it is asked and where the answer lands |

## The rules of the interview

1. **Look before asking.** Read the existing work first. Never ask what the manuscript already answers.
2. **Ask by structure, not by recall.** "What has to be true for the ending to work?" is easy. "What was the name of the inn in your second draft?" is hard. The files hold the details; the writer holds the shape.
3. **A few questions at a time.** Three to five. Wait for the answers. Fold each answer in before asking the next batch, because answers change the questions.
4. **Each question comes with context and a recommendation,** when the existing work or earlier answers support one. Say what you would pick and why. When nothing supports a recommendation, ask open.
5. **Never invent a gap.** When the writer does not know yet, mark the question open and move on. An open question is a good result. A confident guess written as fact is the worst result.
6. **Write the ruling, not the words.** Each answer becomes one plain sentence on the ledger, with the file it lands in. The writer can phrase loosely; the ledger is clean.
7. **Read back what you heard.** At the end of each section, say back in three lines what you now believe, and let the writer correct it.
8. **The writer can skip anything.** No question is required. Some writers do not want to name their dislikes or their reading history. That is fine.
9. **Nothing personal goes in the repo unless the writer puts it there.** Accessibility needs, for example, go in CLAUDE.md only as working preferences ("mark every jump in time"), never as a diagnosis.

## How long it takes

One or two sessions of an hour or so. A writer with a lot of existing material may need a third, spread across the carding stage, because the cards will raise questions the interview did not.

## What comes out

- `plan/interview.md`: the record. Each answer as a ruling, with where it landed (see the example's [interview record](../example/interview-record.md)).
- Rows on today's ledger, one per ruling.
- The first lines of `CLAUDE.md`'s "About the writer", `plan/roadmap.md`, the voice sheet, and the arc outline's head.
- A list of open questions, held in `plan/state.md`.
