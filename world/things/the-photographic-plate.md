# The photographic plate: Harbert’s plate of 17 October that shows a speck on the horizon

*A glass plate taken by Harbert from the great hall of Granite-house on 17 October 1867, showing a small, almost imperceptible spot on the sea horizon, which proves to be a ship.*

Keywords: cliché, plaque, négatif, défaut, point, tache, horizon, navire, lentille, Harbert, 17 octobre

- **What it is.**
  - Harbert takes the whole bay of the Union, facing the Grande-Vue plateau from cape Mandibule to cape Griffe, at about three o’clock on 17 October, tempted by the purity of the sky (II.20 ¶82). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: le 17 de ce mois d’octobre, vers trois heures du soir
    - quote: toute la baie de l’union qui faisait face au plateau de Grande-Vue
  - The horizon is admirably drawn and the sea, undulating under a soft breeze, has at the back the stillness of a lake, flecked with luminous spangles (II.20 ¶83). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: L’horizon était admirablement dessiné
    - quote: piquetées çà et là de paillons lumineux
  - The lens stands at one of the windows of the great hall, so that it dominates the beach and the bay, and the plate is fixed with the substances kept in a dark closet (see [the dark closet](../places/the-dark-closet.md)) (II.20 ¶84). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: L’objectif avait été placé à l’une des fenêtres de la grande salle de Granite-house
    - quote: dans un réduit obscur de Granite-house
  - In full light Harbert sees on it a small, almost imperceptible point that stains the sea horizon; repeated washing cannot remove it (II.20 ¶85). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: un petit point presque imperceptible qui tachait l’horizon de mer
    - quote: il ne put y parvenir
  - He first takes it for a defect in the glass, then examines it with a strong lens unscrewed from one of the telescopes, and cries out (see [the telescope](telescope.md)) (II.20 ¶86, ¶87, ¶88). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: C’est un défaut qui se trouve dans le verre
    - quote: une forte lentille qu’il dévissa de l’une des lunettes
  - Smith examines the spot, then looks for it with his long-glass from the window; the spot is a ship (see [the ship in sight](the-ship-in-sight.md)) (II.20 ¶89, ¶90, ¶91). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: Cyrus Smith examina ce point
    - quote: saisissant sa longue-vue
- **Appears in:** II.20
