# Bamboo: the clumps at the promontoire du Reptile, good for much and for noise

*Thick clumps of bamboo where the last trees of the Far-West forest die at the promontory; Harbert lists its uses, and Smith burns some at the cave mouth, where it bursts like fireworks.*

Keywords: bambou, bamboo, pétarade, detonation, asperges, paniers, paper, canes, Marco Polo, Tartars, promontoire du Reptile

- **What it is.**
  - Thick clumps of bamboo stand at the promontoire du Reptile, among the last trees of the forest of the Far-West (II.4 ¶44). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: d’épais bouquets de bambous
  - Harbert says its bark, cut in flexible slats, serves for baskets and hampers, and its bark, reduced to paste and macerated, makes Chinese paper (II.4 ¶47). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: découpée en latte flexible, sert à faire des paniers ou des corbeilles
    - quote: sert à la fabrication du papier de Chine
  - Its stems give, by their thickness, canes, pipe stems and water pipes; the big ones make excellent light, strong building material never attacked by insects (II.4 ¶47). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: des cannes, des tuyaux de pipe, des conduites pour les eaux
    - quote: ne sont jamais attaqués par les insectes
  - Sawn between the nodes with a part of the cross partition left for a bottom, it gives solid, handy vessels much used by the Chinese (II.4 ¶47). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: on obtient ainsi des vases solides et commodes
  - In India its young shoots are eaten like asparagus (II.4 ¶49, ¶51). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: dans l’Inde, on mange ces bambous en guise d’asperges
    - quote: de jeunes pousses de bambous
  - The pith of the new stems, preserved in vinegar, is a much-prized condiment (II.4 ¶53). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: la moelle des tiges nouvelles, confite dans du vinaigre
  - It exudes between its nodes a sugary liquor from which a very pleasant drink is made (II.4 ¶55). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: exsudent entre leurs nœuds une liqueur sucrée
  - It cannot be smoked (II.4 ¶59). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: Ça ne se fume pas
  - Smith cuts a quantity and mixes it with the fuel of the fire at the cave mouth; struck by the flame, the bamboos burst like fireworks, enough to frighten the boldest beasts (II.4 ¶78, ¶80). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: une certaine quantité, qu’il mêla au combustible du foyer
    - quote: qui détonaient comme des pièces d’artifice
  - Smith did not invent this: according to Marco Polo the Tartars have long used it to keep off the fierce beasts of Central Asia (II.4 ¶81). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: suivant Marco Polo, les Tartares, depuis bien des siècles, l’emploient
- **Appears in:** II.4
