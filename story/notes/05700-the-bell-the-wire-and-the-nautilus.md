# The bell, the wire and the Nautilus (notes)

## Notes for drafting
- **Where it sits.** The French III.15, `texts/fr-1875/57.md`; after the smoke over the crater (III.14 ¶56), through the autumn work to the night of 15 October, the call, the wire, the cavern, and the first sight of the Nautilus; it ends on Smith's greeting to Captain Nemo. *(Drafted.)*
- **Cast.** Cyrus Smith (¶5), Gédéon Spilett (¶24), Harbert (¶14), Ayrton (¶22), Pencroff (¶13), Nab (¶13), Top and Jup (¶21; left behind ¶39); the unknown sender of the message, then Captain Nemo (¶147), whose name Smith murmurs to Spilett (¶135). *(Drafted.)*
- **Places.** Mount Franklin and the crater (¶1); the shipyard (¶9); Granite-house hall (¶23); the grove road: left bank of the Mercy, the plateau, the bridge of Glycerine Creek, the forest (¶42); the corral and its house (¶52–¶57); the spur between the corral valley and the Falls River valley (¶80); the south-west spur and the barren plateau with the basalt wall (¶82); the high edge above the western ocean (¶84); the ravine (¶86); the shore rocks and ledge (¶89–¶90); the sea cavern, lake and the Nautilus (¶103–¶132); the platform, hatch, passage, room, library, salon (¶140–¶144). Reference caves: Fingal's Cave, Morgat, Bonifacio, Lyse-Fjord, Mammoth Cave (¶116). *(Drafted.)*
- **Things.** The hydraulic sawmill (¶8); the schooner's frame (¶9); the electric bell and the dial (¶23, ¶36); Smith's two messages (¶35, ¶37); the lantern (¶46, ¶58); the notice "Follow the new wire." (¶70); the insulated wire (¶76); the iron boat with two oars (¶112); the Nautilus, spindle-shaped, 250 feet long (¶132); the electric lighting (¶129). *(Drafted.)*
- **Time.** End of September: schooner's frame stands (¶9). A week's interruption for the harvest (¶10). 15 October, 9 p.m.: the bell (¶23); new moon that day (¶40). 9:12 p.m.: they leave Granite-house; 9:47: three of five miles done (¶49). "At a quarter past nine" at the corral gate (¶52). Slightly before 10: the high edge above the ocean (¶84). Around midnight: Smith goes down with the lantern; two hours of falling tide (¶102). About an hour later: the opening passable (¶105, ¶110). Three hours of falling tide: fifteen feet (¶110). The boat enters; a quarter of an hour, then another quarter; half a mile (¶117, ¶123). Ayrton's last visit to the corral: four days before (¶67). The chapter covers about five hours of night. *(Drafted.)*
- **Clues.** The unknown who sends the message and has laid the extra wire; the notice in English (¶69–¶70); the unknown knows their habits (¶43); the insulated cable (¶76); the boat waiting at the cave's mouth (¶112); the electric light (¶129); the name Smith murmurs (¶135) and the reporter's reply "a man outside the law" (¶137). Smith's earlier reasoning that the way will open at low tide (¶96–¶99). The new wire and the retreat link the volcano's early eruption talk to the mystery (¶73, ¶83). *(Drafted.)*
- **Length.** 147 paragraphs (¶1–¶147, plus the summary ¶0); 4,247 words in the ¶ lines. *(Drafted.)*

## Open and flags
- The hours. ¶49 says they left Granite-house at 9:12 and at 9:47 had done three of five miles; ¶52 says "At a quarter past nine" a flash showed the palisade, as they reached the gate. 9:15 is earlier than 9:47. Card neither. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Cyrus Smith et ses compagnons avaient quitté Granite-house à neuf heures douze minutes.
  - quote: À neuf heures un quart, un vif éclair leur montrait l’enceinte palissadée
- The bell. ¶23 puts the first ring at nine in the evening and the departure at 9:12 (¶49). Pencroff "had just made for his bed". The text gives "nine in the evening" only once. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Il était neuf heures du soir.
- The distance. ¶49 gives five miles from the mouth of the Mercy to the corral; III.9 (not mine) gives the corral road as five miles; ¶85 gives a mile and a half from the corral to the sea. Not otherwise reconciled. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: sur les cinq qui séparaient l’embouchure de la Mercy du corral
  - quote: la distance d’un mille et demi depuis le corral
- Tide. ¶96 says the sea is high at about 10 p.m.; ¶102 says two hours of falling tide at midnight (so the ebb began about 10); ¶110 says "In three hours the tide had fallen fifteen feet" around 1 a.m. and also that the arch's top stands eight feet above the water. The cavern entrance's own height is not given. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: Il y avait déjà deux heures de mer baissée.
  - quote: En trois heures, la marée avait baissé de quinze pieds.
- The Nautilus's height above the water: ¶132 says "ten to twelve feet" above the sea; III.17 ¶42 says the platform rises "seven or eight feet" above the water. Both are in the text. Card neither. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: s’élevait de dix à douze pieds au-dessus du niveau de la mer
- ¶112 as printed runs two sentences together with no space: "Les colons étaient dans le canot.C’était un canot…" and the next sentence ¶114 repeats that the colonists are in the boat. A printing slip in the working text; the quote is given as the text reads. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Les colons étaient dans le canot.C’était un canot
- ¶117's cavern "did it then extend to the centre of the island?" is Smith's question; the boat then goes "half a mile" (¶123). The text does not say which direction the cavern runs, though the wire "ran towards the west" (¶76). *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: s’étendait-elle donc jusqu’au centre de l’île
- The sender of the message. ¶54 and ¶71: Smith concludes the message did not start at the corral. The text does not say how the unknown knew Ayrton's last visit or the wire's route. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: la dépêche n’était pas partie du corral
- ¶3 says the earlier eruptions' lava streaks the northern slopes; ¶6 says a new crater in the south wall would threaten the corral. Whether this meets later events is for later chapters. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: si quelque nouveau cratère s’ouvrait dans les parois sud du mont Franklin
- No Wikisource correction touches this chapter; none of its facts rests on one. *(Drafted.)*
