## a

After: “About a hundred and fifty miles to the north-east,” replied the engineer.

A few lines up, the engineer gives the islet’s place from the atlas: two degrees and a half farther to the west and two degrees farther to the south than Lincoln Island. That is south-west, not north-east. The voyage decides it. In chapter XXXV the Bonadventure holds her south-west course all the way and makes the islet; in chapter XXXVI she comes home on a course east-north-east, and sees the fire on Lincoln Island to the north-east. In chapter LXII the yacht, too, goes from Tabor Island to Lincoln Island to the north-east. I read south-west.
