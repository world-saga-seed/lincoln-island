# The Float in the Bar (outline)

*Chapter 01950, the inserted chapter. Before: [01900](01900-the-facade-the-ladder-and-the-warren.md) (Granite-house fitted out, late May 1865). After: [02000](02000-candles-and-the-grain-of-wheat.md) (June: the winter opens with squalls; Whit Sunday, 4 June; the grain of wheat). Verne's narrator, past tense, kept with Pencroff and Harbert (R1121-28). Checked against [the world's rules](../../world/rules.md).*

*Stage: outlined around the author's frames (R1121-54 to -60).*

## What the chapter is

- *(Settled R1121-23.)* A storm chapter. A castaway sees something half buried near the shore and takes one other into his confidence, keeping it from the rest. A squall drives them off. When it has passed, the bar has shifted and the thing is gone. The cause is natural, with no sign of Captain Nemo's hand.
- *(Settled R1121-36.)* The point is the cost of not acting quickly. *(Settled R1121-37.)* That cost is the secrecy: five could have dug the float out in the hour of low water, two cannot, and they spend the time they have reading it. The scene is kept short.
- *(Settled R1121-24.)* It foreshadows the chest (II.2). At the chest the two say nothing of the float, and the page does not explain their silence.
- *(Settled R1121-38.)* 1 to 3 June 1865.

## The find

- *(Settled R1121-26, R1121-27.)* The thing is one of the floats that held up the Nautilus's nets, made in the Nautilus's style. Nemo lost it in his ordinary work and never learns it came near them. It is never recovered.
- *(Settled R1121-29.)* Nemo, alone, still sets a short net from the light boat off this shore, held up by one of the dead crew's old floats. The June squall tears it loose.
- *(Settled R1121-33.)* It is glass, a blown ball with the device moulded in it.
- *(Settled R1121-32.)* It is thick with barnacles. *(Proposal.)* They are goose barnacles, the kind that hang from things kept at the surface.
- *(Settled R1121-31.)* They bare only a few letters of the motto's ring, too few to make a word. The N lies on the hidden side, toward the sand.
- *(Settled R1121-28.)* The page never says it was Nemo's.

## The beats

*Marks: **(Settled …)** is the author's, a frame or a ruling. **(Resolved by judgment, overturnable.)** is a gap the author left to the executor. **(Proposal.)** is the executor's tween.*

### 1. Pencroff sees it (1 June, morning low water)

- *(Settled R1121-54.)* Pencroff sees it on his way to his lines, reading the waterline the way a sailor does.
- *(Settled R1121-60.)* His lines are set in the Mercy near its mouth, with iron hooks from the forging (I.15). His way to them runs along the beach past the channel to the islet. The French has fishing that month, "ni la chasse, ni la pêche" (I.20 ¶2), and later Pencroff's "lignes de fond, armées d’hameçons de fer" in the Mercy (II.8 ¶15, in the pluperfect).
- *(Proposal.)* At the neap low water, the sands of the channel bed show for a short while (R1121-53; "la plus grande partie des sables, formant le lit du canal, avait découvert", I.3 ¶59). On the bar a round thing, half buried, catches his eye, the wrong shape for a stone.
- *(Proposal.)* The flood is already making, and he does not go out alone.

### 2. He takes only Harbert (1 June)

- *(Settled R1121-34.)* He takes only Harbert, so as not to raise hopes.
- They speak of it only out of doors (world rule 4).
- *(Resolved by judgment, overturnable.)* The pretext is the lines themselves. Pencroff asks the boy to help him lift and reset them at the next low water. It is his daily errand, so no one asks, and it is no lie.
- *(Proposal.)* The wait for the next low water is the first cost of the secrecy (R1121-37, delay c).

### 3. Out at the next low water, without tools (2 June)

- *(Settled R1121-52.)* The first cause, in a line: the tools stay behind, because picks carried out of Granite-house would be asked about. They have their knives.
- *(Proposal.)* They ford the channel to the bar, as the whole party fords it three days later ("franchir à gué le canal", I.20 ¶20, 5 June).

### 4. At the float

- *(Settled R1121-52.)* The second cause, in a line: the float is still on its net, buried in the bar and heavy with sand.
- *(Settled R1121-30.)* It is glass, stuck fast, and they cannot turn it.
- *(Settled R1121-32.)* It is thick with barnacles. They chip some away with a stone. *(Proposal.)* They go carefully, for fear of the glass.
- *(Proposal.)* Pencroff: a net float, but not cork and not American. He has never seen the like.
- *(Proposal.)* Harbert: goose barnacles, the kind on a thing long adrift. So it came from far off, long ago. The reading is wrong, and it kills their hope of a ship nearby.
- *(Settled R1121-55.)* The frame: a broken run, a B, an L, an I. Harbert says "Latin, I think," and no more. *(Proposal.)* What it rests on is the -bili- of the Latin ending -bilis, which a schooled boy knows. It is part of MOBILIS, but not the word. The N lies toward the sand (R1121-31).
- *(Settled R1121-37.)* The time they have goes in the reading. The scene is short.

### 5. The squall (2 June)

- *(Settled R1121-23.)* The squall comes in. The waves are too high to stay, but they can see the float in the surf. They go back to safety and wait it out. *(Proposal.)* The flood and the squall come together, and the surge covers the bar (R1121-53). The weather is the text's own: "des averses et des rafales qui se succédèrent sans relâche" (I.20 ¶1).
- *(Proposal.)* They wait in the Chimneys, now the forge. On these same squalls, I.20 ¶1 has the tides that might "burst in again".

### 6. Home, wet (2 June, evening)

- *(Settled R1121-56.)* Pencroff makes a joke of the soaking.
- *(Settled R1121-59.)* The joke: "The lines took nothing, and the sea took the pair of us."
- *(Proposal.)* The joke is all they tell. It is true, and it is less than the whole.

### 7. The smooth sand (3 June, the next low water)

- *(Settled R1121-23.)* The bar has shifted, and the float is gone.
- *(Settled R1121-57.)* Pencroff, at the smooth sand: "Five of us and the picks, and we'd have had it."
- *(Settled R1121-58.)* Harbert: "We had it, Pencroff. For an hour." It turns Pencroff's "would have" back on the hour they spent reading.
- *(Settled R1121-34.)* The grain of wheat later that month (I.20 ¶34) is the pair. The chapter does not point to it.
