# The Island Seen and Named Lincoln (notes)

## Notes for drafting

- **Where it sits.** The French I.11. It opens at the camp on the first plateau on the night of 29 March, covers the second ascent and survey on 30 March, and ends with the island's naming as Lincoln and the narrator's note on Lincoln's death. *(Drafted.)*
- **Cast.** *(Drafted.)* Cyrus Smith (¶1), Harbert (¶1), Pencroff (¶3), Gédéon Spilett, "the reporter" (¶3), Nab (¶3). Top is not named in this chapter. The narrator names Abraham Lincoln and Grant (¶89, ¶91).
- **Places.** *(Drafted.)*
  - The camp on the first plateau, "this hole of basalt" at 2,500 feet (¶1); the crevasse and crater (¶5–¶8); the summit, on the crater's northern rim (¶9).
  - The island and its features as seen: the east bay (¶22), the south-east cape (¶22), the north-east gulf and its two capes (¶22), the north-west "skull" (¶23), the creek on the west coast (¶24, ¶26), the south-west peninsula and its tail (¶25), the lake (¶27), the small river (¶30), the northern sands and marshes (¶32).
  - The Chimneys (¶26, ¶55), the river (¶78), the islet (¶79), the plateau above the Chimneys (¶80), the forest of the south-west (¶81).
  - The names given: baie de l’Union, baie Washington, mont Franklin, lac Grant (¶63, ¶65); presqu’île Serpentine, promontoire du Reptile (¶66); golfe du Requin, cap Mandibule-Nord, cap Mandibule-Sud (¶68–¶71); cap de la Griffe (¶75); la Mercy, l’îlot du Salut, le plateau de Grande-Vue, les forêts du Far-West (¶78–¶81); Lincoln Island (¶89); Les Cheminées kept (¶57).
  - Proposed and not adopted: baie Providence, pointe des Cachalots, cap de l’Espoir trompé (¶58); port Nab, cap Gédéon (¶61).
- **Things.** *(Drafted.)* The reporter's map of the island (¶21, ¶64); his notebook (¶86); nothing else is made or found. The island's measures: coast over 100 miles (¶17, n1); length 30 miles, width 10 miles at the narrowest (¶26); lake at 300 feet (¶27); crater inclination 35 to 40 degrees (¶7).
- **Time.** *(Drafted.)*
  - Night of 29 March: half an hour after Cyrus and Harbert left the summit they are back at camp (¶1).
  - "The next day, 30 March" (¶2); about seven o'clock departure (¶3); before eight at the crater's top (¶9); an hour on the summit (¶34); "30 March 1865" (¶92); "sixteen days later" (¶92).
  - The plan to fix the north "the next day" (¶83) is 31 March. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - The chapter covers the night of 29 March and the day of 30 March to evening.
- **Clues.** *(Drafted.)*
  - Whether the island is visited by natives from nearby islands; Cyrus says precautions are needed.
    - quote: il était donc convenable de prendre certaines précautions contre une descente possible des indigènes voisins
  - The island's position is unknown and cannot easily be fixed without instruments.
    - quote: Cyrus Smith parviendrait-il sans instruments à relever plus tard sa position en latitude et en longitude ?
  - The island is "off the routes" of ships, by Cyrus's reasoning.
    - quote: il est à craindre qu’elle ne soit située en dehors des routes ordinairement suivies
  - The lake unseen before, with an outlet that is only inferred.
    - quote: il est probable que du côté de la mer il existe un déversoir par lequel s’échappe le trop-plein des eaux
  - The forest hides the valley floors and gorges.
    - quote: échappaient seuls à leurs investigations
  - The island is shaped like a fantastic sleeping animal.
    - quote: elle ressemblait à quelque fantastique animal
  - Lincoln's death, told ahead of the characters by the narrator.
    - quote: ils ne savaient guère que, seize jours après
- **Length.** *(Drafted.)* 92 paragraphs (¶1–¶92, plus the summary ¶0), 3,636 words in the ¶ lines. One footnote (n1 after ¶17) and two plate captions (pl1 after ¶30, pl2 after ¶64).

## Open and flags

- **The length of the peninsula and of the island.** ¶25 says the tail peninsula runs more than thirty miles to sea from the south-east cape; ¶26 says the greatest length of the island, from the north-east jaw to the end of the tail, is not less than thirty miles. Card neither. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: s’allongeait de plus de trente milles en mer, à compter du cap sud-est de l’île
  - quote: sa plus grande longueur, de la mâchoire du nord-est à l’extrémité de la queue du sud-ouest, ne comptait pas moins de trente milles
  - ¶38 repeats "a distance of about thirty miles" to the extreme points.
- **The forest.** ¶32 says the forest covers two-thirds of the island; ¶81 names "forêts du Far-West" the woods covering the Serpentine peninsula; ¶27 puts the forest across the whole south "from the mountain to the shore". The three descriptions overlap and do not say how much of the forest the new name covers. Card neither. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: qui faisaient des deux tiers de l’île une forêt immense
  - quote: ce massif d’impénétrables bois qui couvraient la presqu’île Serpentine
- **The lake's name.** ¶63 has Cyrus propose "lac Grant" among the principal names; ¶65 lists only "baie de l’Union, baie Washington et mont Franklin" as named at once, and ¶64 says the proposal was "unanimement admise". The lake is not mentioned again in the chapter. *(My reading, to confirm.)* The name is adopted with the others in ¶64.
  - quote: à ce lac qui s’étend sous nos regards, celui de lac Grant
  - quote: Tout d’abord, on nomma baie de l’Union, baie Washington et mont Franklin, les deux baies et la montagne
- **The north.** ¶83 has Cyrus place the east by the sun's height and position, to be corrected tomorrow; the names given in the chapter (east bay, south bay, north-east gulf) therefore rest on a rough orientation. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: l’ingénieur l’avait déterminée approximativement par la hauteur et la position du soleil
- **The latitude.** I.9 ¶87 says the pines suggest a latitude higher than Cyrus supposed; I.11 ¶43 says the island is "too far south" for ships to the Pacific archipelagos and "too far north" for those bound for Australia. The text gives no figures. Not reconciled here.
  - quote: cette contrée se trouvait plus élevée en latitude que ne le supposait l’ingénieur
  - quote: trop au sud pour les navires qui fréquentent les archipels du Pacifique
- **The date of Lincoln's death.** ¶92 says that "sixteen days later" 30 March, on Good Friday, Lincoln falls; 30 March plus sixteen days is 15 April. *(My reading, to confirm.)* Outside the text, Good Friday 1865 was 14 April and Lincoln was shot that night and died on 15 April; the chapter's count lands on the day of death, not of the shot. This is not reconciled.
  - quote: seize jours après, un crime effroyable serait commis à Washington
  - quote: le vendredi saint, Abraham Lincoln tomberait sous la balle d’un fanatique
- **The unit in n1.** The text gives "more than a hundred miles" and the footnote "About 45 leagues of 4 kilometres each", that is about 180 km. *(My reading, to confirm.)* The footnote's league and the text's mile are not tied by the text; 100 nautical miles is about 185 km, which fits the note, but the text does not say which mile it means.
  - quote: un développement de plus de cent milles
  - quote: Environ 45 lieues de 4 kilomètres.
- **The lake's level.** ¶27 says the lake "seemed to be at the same level as the sea" and then "trois cents pieds". Not a discord: the narrator gives the correction. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Vu de cette hauteur, le lac semblait être au même niveau que la mer
- **Seam from I.10.** I.10 ends at the summit at about eight in the evening of 29 March; I.11 ¶1 returns to camp half an hour later and says Cyrus told his friends only that it was an island. The seam holds. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: L’ingénieur se bornait à dire à ses compagnons que la terre sur laquelle le hasard les avait jetés était une île
- No fact in this chapter rests on a Wikisource correction listed in texts/README.md. *(Drafted.)*
