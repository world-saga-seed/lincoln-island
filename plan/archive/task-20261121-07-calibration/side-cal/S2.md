# S2: the French, the render, White

## ¶41

**French:** Pencroff avait trouvé dans les herbes une demi-douzaine de nids de tétras, ayant chacun de deux à trois œufs. Il eut grand soin de ne pas toucher à ces nids, auxquels leurs propriétaires ne pouvaient manquer de revenir. Ce fut autour d’eux qu’il imagina de tendre ses lignes, — non des lignes à collets, mais de véritables lignes à hameçon. Il emmena Harbert à quelque distance des nids, et là il prépara ses engins singuliers avec le soin qu’eût apporté un disciple d’Isaac Walton[n1]. Harbert suivait ce travail avec un intérêt facile à comprendre, tout en doutant de la réussite. Les lignes furent faites de minces lianes, rattachées l’une à l’autre et longues de quinze à vingt pieds. De grosses épines très-fortes, à pointes recourbées, que fournit un buisson d’acacias nains, furent liées aux extrémités des lianes en guise d’hameçon. Quant à l’appât, de gros vers rouges qui rampaient sur le sol en tinrent lieu.

**Render:** Pencroff had found in the grass half a dozen grouse's nests, each holding from two to three eggs. He took great care not to touch these nests, to which their owners could not fail to return. It was around them that he resolved to stretch his lines,—not snare-lines, but true lines with hooks. He led Harbert to some distance from the nests, and there he prepared his singular tackle with the care which a disciple of Isaac Walton[n1] would have brought to it. Harbert followed this work with an interest easy to understand, though he doubted of its success. The lines were made of slender creepers, fastened one to another, and from fifteen to twenty feet long. Large, very strong thorns, with curved points, furnished by a bush of dwarf acacias, were tied to the ends of the creepers by way of hooks. As for the bait, some large red worms which were crawling on the ground served the purpose. [n1] A celebrated author of a treatise on angling.

**White** (¶28–¶29): (¶28) Pencroff had found in the grass half-a-dozen tetras nests, with two or three eggs in each. / (¶29) He was very careful not to touch these nests, whose owners would certainly return to them. Around these he purposed to draw his lines, not as a snare, but with hook and bait. He took Herbert to some distance from the nests, and there made ready his singular apparatus with the care of a true disciple of Isaac Walton. Herbert watched the work with a natural interest, but without much faith in its success. The lines were made of small lianas tied together, from fifteen to twenty feet long, and stout thorns with bent points, broken from a thicket of dwarf acacias, and fastened to the ends of the lianas, served as hooks, and the great red worms which crawled at their feet made excellent bait. This done, Pencroff, walking stealthily through the grass, placed one end of his hook-and-line close to the nests of the tetras. Then he stole back, took the other end in his hand, and hid himself with Herbert behind a large tree. Herbert, it must be said, was not sanguine of success.
