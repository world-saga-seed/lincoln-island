# A3: I.14 ¶74–¶75

¶74

Nothing remained but to work out the operation in figures. Nothing could be easier. There was, as is seen, in round numbers, a difference of five hours between the meridian of Washington and that of Lincoln Island; that is to say, it was noon at Lincoln Island when it was already five o'clock in the evening at Washington. Now the sun, in its apparent movement round the earth, traverses one degree in four minutes, or fifteen degrees in an hour. Fifteen degrees multiplied by five hours gave seventy-five degrees.

¶75

Then, since Washington is in 77° 3′ 11″, as much as to say seventy-seven degrees reckoned from the meridian of Greenwich—which the Americans, concurrently with the English, take for the starting-point of longitudes—it followed that the island was situated seventy-seven degrees plus seventy-five degrees west of the meridian of Greenwich, that is to say, on the hundred and fifty-second degree of west longitude.
