# I.12: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller e. Beads corrected: 45 (French ¶64 is not cut: White ¶43 renders it, in part, at the end of its paragraph).*

*Not a discord: I.12 ¶41's "trop haute" is Wikisource's correction; the print reads "trop basse" (texts/README.md, page 108), and White's "too low" follows the print. Removed by the main session.*

### 1. changed: pine kernels become pistachio-nuts (I.12 ¶13; White ¶8)
- The reserve of game and almonds (of the pine) is used up in the French; White has meat and pistachio-nuts. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: la réserve de gibier et d’amandes fut totalement épuisée
  - white: that little was left of the meat and pistachio-nuts

### 2. changed: Top's small portion becomes a feast (I.12 ¶13; White ¶8)
- The French says Top's portion had been very scanty, which is why he must find game; White says he had not been forgotten in the feast. White also drops that they would restock on the way. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Top, dont la portion avait été fort congrue
  - white: Top, who had not been forgotten in the feast

### 3. cut: the footnote on "creek" (I.12 ¶14 n1; White —)
- White drops the footnote that "creek" is the Americans' name for small watercourses. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Nom que les Américains donnent aux cours d’eau peu importants.
  - white: —

### 4. changed: the smoke had worried Cyrus, with reason (I.12 ¶32; White ¶22)
- The French says the smell told him what the smoke was, the smoke that had at first been bound to worry him, and not without reason; White says only that the smell reassured him. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ce qu’était cette fumée qui, tout d’abord, avait dû l’inquiéter, et non sans raison
  - white: This odor, unmistakable as it was, had been sufficient to reassure the engineer.

### 5. changed: a sodic sulphide spring becomes a spring of sulphate of soda (I.12 ¶35; White ¶25)
- The spring is "sulfurée sodique" in the French (sodium sulphide, giving off hydrogen sulphide); White has a spring of sulphate of soda. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: une source sulfurée sodique
  - white: a spring of sulphate of soda

### 6. condensed: the Centigrade figure (I.12 ¶36; White ¶25)
- White gives only the Fahrenheit figure and drops the Centigrade equivalent. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: (35° centig. au-dessus de zéro)
  - white: Its temperature he estimated at 95° Fahrenheit

### 7. changed: "no longer the conifers" becomes "belong to those conifers" (I.12 ¶41; White ¶28)
- The trees by the creek belong to Australian species and no longer to those conifers of the part of the island already explored; White says they "belong to those conifers". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: et non plus à celles de ces conifères qui hérissaient la portion de l’île déjà explorée
  - white: and belong to those conifers which clothed the portion of the island already explored

### 8. cut: the start of autumn (I.12 ¶41; White ¶28)
- White drops that April in the south is the start of autumn. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: c’est-à-dire au début de l’automne
  - white: a month which corresponds in that hemisphere to our October, yet their leaves had not begun to fall.

### 9. changed: tussac grass becomes moss (I.12 ¶41; White ¶28)
- The cedars rise in clearings covered with tall grass the New-Hollanders call "tussac"; White says moss. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: haut gazon que l’on appelle « tussac » dans la Nouvelle-Hollande
  - white: covered high with that sort of moss which the New-Hollanders call tussocks

### 10. addition: birds of paradise (I.12 ¶43; White ¶30)
- The "rois" are green birds crowned with red in the French; White calls them king-birds, birds of paradise, and drops the "blues-mountains". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: « rois », d’un vert éclatant et couronnés de rouge, loris bleus, « blues-mountains »
  - white: king-birds, birds of paradise, of brilliant green, with a crowd of red, and blue lories

### 11. condensed: the kangaroo "is worth the best venison" (I.12 ¶49; White ¶33)
- The reporter's stewed kangaroo is as good as the best venison in the French; White says only a delicious stew. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: cela vaut la meilleure venaison !…
  - white: “They make a delicious stew,” said the reporter.

### 12. softened: bows and arrows that bloodied the world (I.12 ¶54; White ¶38)
- Bows and arrows sufficed for centuries to bloody the world in the French; White has them sufficient for the warfare of mankind. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: Les arcs et les flèches ont suffi, pendant des siècles, à ensanglanter le monde.
  - white: Bows and arrows were sufficient for many centuries for the warfare of mankind.

### 13. cut: the easier kangaroos and the dozen species (I.12 ¶57; White ¶41)
- White breaks off at the kangaroo-rats and drops that these are easier to take and that a dozen species are counted. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dont il est plus aisé de s’emparer. On en compte une douzaine d’espèces…
  - white: kangaroo-rats—

### 14. cut: the name "maras" and what sets them from agoutis (I.12 ¶64; White ¶43)
- White says the rodents were a sort of agouti, but drops that the citizens of the Union named them "maras" and that the five molars on each side are what distinguish them from agoutis. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: C’étaient des « maras », sorte d’agoutis
  - white: They were a sort of agouti, a little larger than their tropical congeners

### 15. changed: American rabbits become American hares (I.12 ¶64; White ¶43)
- The French calls them true rabbits of America; White has hares. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: véritables lapins d’Amérique
  - white: true American hares

### 16. changed: the western shore becomes the left bank (I.12 ¶68; White ¶46)
- The explorers reach the lake's western shore in the French; White puts them on its left bank. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Les explorateurs étaient arrivés sur la rive occidentale du lac Grant.
  - white: They were now on its left bank

### 17. cut: the philedons and the lyre-bird (I.12 ¶68; White ¶46)
- White's list of water birds stops at the red-beaks and drops the philedons with brush-shaped tongues and the one or two lyre-birds. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: des philédons, munis d’une langue en forme de pinceau, et un ou deux échantillons de ces menures splendides
  - white: wild ducks, pelicans, water-hens and red-beaks.

### 18. cut: the footnote on the lake's area (I.12 ¶68 n2; White —)
- White drops the footnote giving the area as about 200 hectares. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Environs 200 hectares.
  - white: —

### 19. changed: an outlet through the granite becomes a river through a gorge (I.12 ¶73; White ¶50)
- Cyrus thinks an outlet (déversoir) probably exists, doubtless through a cut in the granite; White says a river pouring through a gorge. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qu’un déversoir existait quelque part, et sans doute à travers une coupée du granit
  - white: It was probable that a river existed somewhere pouring through a gorge in the granite.

### 20. changed: a mile and a half and another mile become one mile (I.12 ¶73; White ¶50)
- The search north runs a mile and a half and then a mile more in the French; White gives a tramp of one mile. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: l’exploration fut prolongée sous les arbres pendant un mille et demi vers le nord
  - white: So they walked up the plateau, following the banks of Lake Grant, but after a tramp of a mile, they could find no outlet.

### 21. cut: the outlet "which had to exist" (I.12 ¶73; White ¶50)
- White says only that they found no outlet; the French says Cyrus had not found the outlet, which nevertheless had to exist. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Cyrus Smith n’avait pu découvrir le déversoir, qui devait exister cependant.
  - white: they could find no outlet.

### 22. changed: coal becomes charcoal (I.12 ¶77; White ¶52)
- Among nature's gifts Cyrus shows a sample of "charbon", with iron ore, pyrite, clay and lime (the outline reads it as coal); White has charcoal. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ceci du charbon
  - white: this charcoal
