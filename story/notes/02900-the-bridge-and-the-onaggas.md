# The bridge and the onaggas (notes)

## Notes for drafting
- **Where it sits.** The French II.7, after the retaking of Granite-house (II.6). It runs from the evening of the apes' day, through the building of the Mercy bridge and the closing of the Grande-Vue plateau, to the capture of the onaggas and the first trip with the cart to Port Ballon at the end of December. *(Drafted.)*
- **Cast.** Cyrus Smith (¶4), Pencroff (¶3), Nab (¶2), Gédéon Spilett (¶28), Harbert (¶43), Jup (¶3), Top (¶30). The colonists as a body (¶1). Two onaggas, male and female (¶53). *(Drafted.)*
- **Places.** Granite-house (¶2); the left bank of the Mercy and its bend, where the bridge is built (¶12–¶13); Port Ballon on the south coast, three and a half miles from the bend (¶14); Lake Grant, its old overflow, its east-shore cut (¶18); the creek and fall to the sea (¶19); the Grande-Vue plateau, an irregular pentagon of about four miles' perimeter (¶40); the Far-West forest (¶32, ¶61); the Chimneys (¶52); the poultry-yard on the south-east shore of the lake (¶44); sources of the Red Creek, the planned corral site (¶7). *(Drafted.)*
- **Things.** The bridge: fixed on the right bank, movable on the left, counterweights, two arches, piles, a pile-driver, about eighty feet across, a twenty-foot gap when raised (¶26–¶31). Two small bridges (ponceaux) already at the fall, two more to build (¶23). The ditch: twelve feet wide, six deep (¶39); nitro-glycerine (¶39); the Creek-Glycérine (¶39). The second wheat field, 750 grains, palisade, turnstiles, dummies (¶36–¶38). The poultry-yard, two hundred square yards, with tinamous, ducks, alectors, pelicans, kingfishers, water-hens (¶44–¶45). The dovecote, a dozen pigeons (¶46). The stable (¶59). Harness and traces of plant fibre, the cart (¶61). The balloon envelope and rigging brought from Port Ballon (¶63). Plates: pl1 (after ¶3), pl2 (after ¶26), pl3 (after ¶38). *(Drafted.)*
- **Time.** The apes' day is called "hier" on the morning of `Le lendemain, — 3 novembre` (¶9, ¶11), so it is 2 November (my reading). Bridge: begun 3 November, "trois semaines" (¶29), finished `Le 20 novembre` (¶31). The ditch is begun `Le 21 novembre` (¶39), done "en moins de quinze jours" (¶39), all works ended "la première quinzaine de décembre" (¶40). Heat in December (¶41). Onaggas caught `le 23 décembre` (¶52); "quelques jours après la capture" the cart is ready and the road cut; the first try "vers la fin de décembre" (¶61); the trip that day, back at `huit heures du soir` (¶64). The chapter covers about eight weeks, 2 November to the end of December 1865. The year is not stated in this chapter. Seven months on the island are counted in ¶28. *(Drafted; the year and the 2 November date are my reading, to confirm.)*
- **Clues.** The apes' sudden unexplained terror, again given as unexplained (¶1). Jup's behaviour with the ladder is watched (¶11). Nothing new is offered as a mystery in this chapter. The remark that the plateau is closed against `quadrupèdes ou de quadrumanes` (¶15). The onaggas enter the plateau because the small bridges were open (¶53), a chance of a kind the text does not explain. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
- **Length.** 64 paragraphs (¶1–¶64), 3,312 words; summary ¶0 adds 50; 3 plate captions; no footnotes. *(Drafted.)*

## Open and flags
- Seam with II.6: ¶1 opens with the colonists having `reconquis leur domicile, sans avoir été obligés de suivre l’ancien déversoir`, which follows II.6 ¶81 to ¶94. *(Drafted.)*
- The text disagrees with itself over Port Ballon's discovery: ¶14 says `de ce point au port Ballon, découvert la veille sur la côte méridionale`, but on the day before 3 November (2 November) II.6 has the colonists at Granite-house and the Chimneys from dawn, and returning by night on the 1st. Both sides are the text's; no earlier chapter was supplied to check when the port was found. Card neither. *(Drafted.)*
- Duration of the bridge: ¶29 says `dura trois semaines`; ¶9 gives the start at 3 November and ¶31 the end at 20 November, which is seventeen days. *(Drafted.)*
- ¶61 says `quelques jours après la capture des onaggas` the cart was ready and the road cut, and `vers la fin de décembre` the first trial: capture on 23 December, so the trial falls between about 26 and 31 December. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
