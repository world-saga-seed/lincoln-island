# II.15: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller k. Beads corrected: none.*

### 1. cut: the windmill, the harvest, the first flour and bread in the chapter summary (II.15 ¶0; White ¶0)
- White's chapter summary leaves out the third harvest, the windmill, and the first flour and first bread that the French summary lists. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: La troisième récolte. — Un moulin à vent. — La première farine et le premier pain.
  - white: —

### 2. cut: the date, 20 October, and the four days of the voyage (II.15 ¶1; White ¶1)
- White drops the date and the length of the voyage. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Le lendemain, — 20 octobre, — à sept heures du matin, après quatre jours de voyage
  - white: At 7 o’clock the next morning

### 3. cut: only the three are on deck (II.15 ¶6; White ¶3)
- White drops the sentence that Pencroff, Spilett and Harbert were alone on the deck. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Pencroff, Gédéon Spilett et Harbert étaient seuls sur le pont du Bonadventure
  - white: —

### 4. changed: "only three as at the start" becomes "all alone" (II.15 ¶10; White ¶6)
- Smith argues that the party is only three, as when it left; White has him say "you are all alone". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: puisque vous n’êtes que trois comme au départ
  - white: since you are all alone

### 5. changed: "a few months ago" becomes "in a few months" (II.15 ¶24; White ¶12)
- The French says that a few months ago the man was a man like Smith and the others; White puts it in the future ("in a few months, he will be as much a man as any of us"). Harbert's next question and Smith's answer, that the paper was recently written, turn on the past reading. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il y a quelques mois à peine, ce malheureux était un homme comme vous et moi
  - white: in a few months, he will be as much a man as any of us

### 6. changed: "a soul" becomes "any reason" (II.15 ¶32; White ¶20)
- Smith says the man still has a soul, and that soul they will save; White has "any reason", and "save it". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: puisqu’il a encore une âme en lui, cette âme, nous la sauverons
  - white: if he still has any reason, we will save it

### 7. changed: who murmurs "Poor abandoned one" (II.15 ¶35; White ¶22)
- The French has the engineer murmur it, and calls the man "abandonné" (abandoned); White gives the line to the reporter and says "shipwrecked sailor". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: « Pauvre abandonné ! » murmura l’ingénieur.
  - white: “Poor shipwrecked sailor,” murmured the reporter.

### 8. softened: "nothing human" becomes "little of the human" (II.15 ¶36; White ¶23)
- The French says the man seemed to have nothing human left; White says "little". *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: ce misérable être n’avait plus rien d’humain
  - white: this miserable creature had little of the human left in him

### 9. cut: the powder store outside Granite House (II.15 ¶46; White ¶32)
- The French offers a small powder store either outside Granite House or in the upper cavern; White gives only the upper cavern. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: soit en dehors de Granite-house, soit même dans la caverne supérieure
  - white: in the upper cavern of Granite House

### 10. cut: the Bonadventure's conduct in the gale (II.15 ¶50; White ¶36)
- White drops Pencroff's praise that she behaved admirably in the gale on the return. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qui s’est admirablement comportée pendant ce coup de vent qui nous a assaillis si violemment au retour
  - white: —

### 11. cut: Cape Claw on the way to Port Balloon (II.15 ¶60; White ¶46)
- White drops that the sea wind carried the boat quickly to Cape Claw. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: que le vent du large conduisit rapidement au cap Griffe
  - white: —

### 12. changed: "the soul coming back to the body" becomes "reason returning" (II.15 ¶61; White ¶47)
- The French asks whether the soul was returning to the body; White asks whether his reason was returning. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: L’âme, enfin, revenait-elle au corps ?
  - white: Was not, in short, his reason returning to him?

### 13. changed: "formerly" becomes "sometimes" (II.15 ¶64; White ¶50)
- The French says that when intelligence lit his face in the past, it must have had a kind of beauty; White says "sometimes, when intelligence was visible". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: quand l’intelligence l’éclairait autrefois
  - white: sometimes, when intelligence was visible

### 14. cut: the proof seen in the storm; the soul kindled (II.15 ¶65; White ¶51)
- White drops "On l'avait bien vu, pendant la tempête" and turns the soul that a flash might rekindle into "his reason". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: On l’avait bien vu, pendant la tempête, à bord du Bonadventure !
  - white: —

### 15. changed: Smith "watched for his soul" becomes "his reason" (II.15 ¶68; White ¶53)
- The French has Smith lying in wait for the man's soul, "if one may say so"; White says "reason". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Comme il guettait son âme, si l’on peut parler ainsi !
  - white: How he laid in wait for his reason, so to speak

### 16. changed: "It is only the sea" becomes "It is not the ocean" (II.15 ¶84; White ¶66)
- The French has Spilett say it is only the sea so far, which may not tempt him to flee; White has "It is not, indeed, the ocean". *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: Ce n’est encore que la mer
  - white: It is not, indeed, the ocean

### 17. cut: the route up the left bank of the Mercy (II.15 ¶89; White ¶71)
- White drops that they went to the mouth of the Mercy and up the left bank of the river to the plateau. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: remontant la rive gauche de la rivière
  - white: And thereupon all proceeded towards Prospect Plateau.

### 18. changed: one large tear becomes tears (II.15 ¶92; White ¶73)
- The French has a single large tear fall from his eyes; White has tears coursing down his cheeks. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: une grosse larme coula de ses yeux
  - white: tears coursed down his cheeks

### 19. changed: "you have become a man again" becomes "you will be a man again" (II.15 ¶93; White ¶74)
- Smith's cry in the French says the man has become a man again, since he weeps; White has him say "will be". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: te voilà donc redevenu homme, puisque tu pleures
  - white: you will be a man again, since you weep
