# II.2: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller h. Beads corrected: bead 43 and 44 (French ¶48, "Si seulement il y avait…", pairs with White ¶43, "If it should turn out that there was—", not with White ¶42); beads 47 and 48 (the first list in French ¶53, Outils, pairs with White ¶48, TOOLS, and the rest of French ¶53 with White ¶49–¶53).*

### 1. cut: the launching (II.2 ¶1; White ¶1)
- The French describes the canoe carried to the sand before Granite House, lifted by the rising tide, and Pencroff sculling it and finding it fit; White stops at its length and weight. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le flot montant la souleva
  - white: —

### 2. condensed: "all five" becomes "all of us" (II.2 ¶4; White ¶4)
- Pencroff wants to see whether the canoe can carry all five; White says "all of us". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: s’il peut nous porter tous les cinq
  - white: if it will carry all of us

### 3. changed: "it was agreed" becomes Pencroff's confidence (II.2 ¶5; White ¶5)
- The French says it was agreed to try the canoe that very day; White says Pencroff was confident they could. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il fut convenu qu’on ferait, ce jour même, l’essai de la pirogue
  - white: he was confident that they could at once make a trial trip of the craft

### 4. changed: swell the canoe barely felt (II.2 ¶11; White ¶11)
- The long swell was barely felt by the heavily laden canoe; White says it rose and fell with it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Quelques longues ondulations que la pirogue sentait à peine, car elle était lourdement chargée
  - white: the canoe rose and fell with the long undulations of the sea

### 5. cut: the load of rocks (II.2 ¶14; White ¶12)
- The French says the point looked as if an enormous cart had tipped out its load of rock; White omits the image. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: On eût dit qu’un énorme tombereau de roches avait été vidé là.
  - white: —

### 6. changed: "strange" country becomes "suspicious" land (II.2 ¶16; White ¶13)
- Cyrus looks as if he were observing some strange country; White says suspicious land. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il semblait toujours qu’il observât quelque contrée étrange
  - white: as if he was observing some suspicious land

### 7. cut: three-quarters of an hour (II.2 ¶17; White ¶14)
- The French gives the time taken to reach the end of the point; White omits it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: après trois quarts d’heure de navigation
  - white: The canoe had reached the end of the point

### 8. changed: the tide reaches the wreck (II.2 ¶42; White ¶38)
- The French says the tide had begun to reach the wreck, which would float at high water; White has the sailor already working at it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: La marée commençait déjà à atteindre l’épave
  - white: The sailor was already working at the prize, which would doubtless float at high water.

### 9. cut: the hour and a half for three miles (II.2 ¶42; White ¶38)
- The French says it took the whole hour and a half to cover three miles; White gives the hour and a half without the distance. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il avait fallu tout ce temps pour franchir cette distance de trois milles
  - white: in an hour and a half the canoe touched the bank before Granite House

### 10. changed: Nab's cry is given to Pencroff (II.2 ¶46; White ¶41)
- Nab asks whether it could be preserves; White gives the line to Pencroff, and in the French it is the sailor who then hopes for something else. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: s’écria Nab, est-ce que ce seraient des conserves qu’il y a là dedans
  - white: cried Pencroff, “they must be preserves which are inside.

### 11. softened: "danced like a negro" (II.2 ¶51; White ¶46)
- The French has Nab dancing "like a negro"; White has only that he danced. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: Nab dansait… comme un nègre
  - white: Neb danced.

### 12. changed: the bedane becomes an axe, two adzes become one (II.2 ¶53; White ¶48)
- The French lists 2 adzes and 1 bedane; White prints "I adzes, l axe", losing the bedane and the number of adzes (the figures look misprinted). *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: 2 herminettes. | 1 besaiguë.
  - white: I adzes, l axe

### 13. changed: powder kegs of twenty-five pounds become fifteen (II.2 ¶53; White ¶49)
- The kegs hold twenty-five pounds each; White prints "l5 pounds". The later chapter says "environ cinquante livres" for the two. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: pouvant contenir chacun vingt-cinq livres
  - white: holding l5 pounds each

### 14. changed: six table knives become five (II.2 ¶53; White ¶52)
- The French lists six table knives; White has five. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: 6 couteaux de table
  - white: 5 table knives

### 15. cut: the dictionary in six volumes (II.2 ¶53; White ¶53)
- The dictionary of natural sciences is in six volumes; White omits the number. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: 1 dictionnaire des sciences naturelles, en six volumes
  - white: 1 dictionary of the Natural Sciences

### 16. cut: the Bible with both Testaments (II.2 ¶53; White ¶53)
- The French says the Bible holds the Old and the New Testament; White lists only "Bible". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: 1 Bible contenant l’Ancien et le Nouveau Testament
  - white: l Bible, 1 atlas

### 17. cut: "by pirates" (II.2 ¶57; White ¶57)
- Pencroff's supposition is that the owner was taken prisoner by pirates; White drops the pirates. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: n’eût été fait prisonnier par des pirates…
  - white: “Unless its owner had been taken prisoner,” said Pencroff.

### 18. cut: no date of publication, and the quarto (II.2 ¶65–¶67; White ¶65)
- The French says the dictionaries bear neither publisher nor date, and likewise the atlas; White speaks only of editor or publisher. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ils ne portaient aucun nom d’éditeur, ni aucune date de publication
  - white: neither in it, nor in any of the others, did the name of the editor or publisher appear

### 19. cut: the Bible is a quarto remarkable for its type (II.2 ¶66; White ¶65)
- The French says the English Bible is a quarto remarkable typographically; White gives only that it had been often read. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: in-quarto remarquable au point de vue typographique
  - white: the latter showed that it had been often read

### 20. cut: the laugh at Pencroff's tobacco (II.2 ¶74; White ¶70)
- The French says none could help laughing at the sailor's remark; White omits the line. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: On ne put s’empêcher de rire à l’observation du marin.
  - white: —

### 21. changed: "feared" becomes "doubtless" (II.2 ¶75; White ¶71)
- The French says it was to be feared that castaways on the west coast were without resources; White states that they were doubtless without. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il était à craindre qu’ils fussent sans ressource
  - white: they were doubtless without resources

### 22. cut: it was a Sunday (II.2 ¶77; White ¶72)
- The French says 29 October was a Sunday; White gives the date without the day. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: était précisément un dimanche
  - white: that evening—the 29th of October—Herbert

### 23. changed: the Gospel becomes the Bible (II.2 ¶77; White ¶72)
- Harbert asks for a passage from the Gospel, and Cyrus opens "l'Évangile"; White says Bible in both places. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: lire quelque passage de l’Évangile
  - white: read some passages from the Bible
