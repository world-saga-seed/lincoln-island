# The Britannia: the wrecked ship named on the Tabor plank

*A ship known only from half a name on a plank of the Tabor hut, whose hull supplied the hut’s planks; her flag and her fate are not told.*

Keywords: Britannia, navire, ship, wreck, naufrage, épave, pavois, Tabor, English, American

- **What it is.**
  - The planks of the Tabor hut come from the hull or deck of a ship, and a plank, probably from her bulwarks, bears half-effaced letters (see [the Britannia plank](the-britannia-plank.md); [the Tabor dwelling](../places/the-tabor-dwelling.md)) (II.14 ¶22, ¶23). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: ces planches provenaient de la coque ou du pont d’un navire
    - quote: probablement une de celles qui formaient les pavois du navire naufragé
  - Her name is read "Britannia" (II.14 ¶24). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: Britannia !
  - Pencroff says it is a name common to many ships, and he could not say whether she was English or American (II.14 ¶24). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: c’est un nom commun à bien des navires, et je ne pourrais dire si celui-ci était anglais ou américain
  - Smith agrees with his friends that the unknown must be English or American, since the name Britannia suggests it (II.15 ¶38). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: que l’inconnu devait être anglais ou américain, car le nom de Britannia le donnait à penser
  - She is a three-masted English ship, whose captain is Grant, the father of Mary and Robert (II.17 ¶42, ¶50). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: un trois-mâts anglais, le Britannia
    - quote: les enfants du capitaine Grant, dont le navire le Britannia
  - Ayrton was her boatswain, put ashore by Captain Grant on 8 April 1852 on the west coast of Australia after trying to raise the crew in revolt and to seize the ship (II.17 ¶55, ¶59). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: le contre-maître du Britannia
    - quote: il avait essayé d’entraîner son équipage à la révolte et de s’emparer du navire
- **Appears in:** II.14, II.15, II.17
- **Open:**
  - When she was lost: Ayrton’s tale says the Britannia "avait péri corps et biens, une année auparavant" (II.17 ¶42) and Glenarvan asks whether she was lost "depuis moins de deux ans" (II.17 ¶50), while Ayrton was landed on 8 April 1852 (II.17 ¶59); Hetzel’s note speaks of "une certaine discordance dans les dates" (II.17 n1); card none (II.17 ¶42, ¶50, ¶59, n1). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: avait péri corps et biens, une année auparavant
    - quote: se fût perdu depuis moins de deux ans
    - quote: une certaine discordance dans les dates
