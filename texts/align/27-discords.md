# II.5: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller i. Beads corrected: none. (Beads 13 and 14 are off by a clause: Nab's "Mais…" in French ¶16 opens White ¶13, and French ¶17 is the rest of White ¶13.)*

### 1. changed: two thirds of the horizon becomes "either hand" (II.5 ¶2; White ¶1)
- The French says the horizon was visible over two thirds of its circumference from the tip of the promontory; White says it was visible "on either hand". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: cet horizon, qui était visible sur les deux tiers de sa circonférence
  - white: scrutinizing the horizon visible on either hand

### 2. cut: the date, 2 November (II.5 ¶4; White ¶2)
- The French names the day as 2 November; White says "this day". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: cette journée du 2 novembre
  - white: give up this day to it

### 3. changed: what Smith thought the west shore might shelter (II.5 ¶5; White ¶2)
- The French has Smith expect a ship in distress or a ship on a regular course; White has a wreck or a passing ship. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: soit à un bâtiment en détresse, soit à un navire en cours régulier de navigation
  - white: sheltered either a wreck or a passing ship

### 4. changed: what they failed to find on the west becomes persons (II.5 ¶5; White ¶2)
- The French says to look on the south shore for what could not be found on the west; White says to search for "those whom they had failed to discover". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il fallait chercher sur celui du sud de l’île ce qu’on n’avait pu trouver sur celui de l’ouest
  - white: it became necessary to search the south side of the island for those whom they had failed to discover on the west

### 5. condensed: the canoe already left alone one day (II.5 ¶12; White ¶9)
- Spilett's argument is that the canoe has already stood alone one day without harm; White says only that it can stay "a day or two". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: La pirogue est restée seule pendant un jour aux sources de la Mercy
  - white: The canoe can stay where it is for a day or two

### 6. addition: "State Engineer" (II.5 ¶23; White ¶19)
- The French has Pencroff say Smith is an engineer by trade; White makes him "State Engineer". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: est-ce que M. Smith n’est pas ingénieur de son état ?
  - white: isn’t Mr. Smith State Engineer?

### 7. cut: the forty-mile stage (II.5 ¶24; White ¶20)
- The French says a forty-mile stage is long; White says only that "the tramp was long". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: une étape de quarante milles était longue
  - white: for the tramp was long

### 8. condensed: encounters with beasts and men (II.5 ¶25; White ¶20)
- The French loads the guns with ball against animals of two legs or four; White says "in case of an encounter". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: En prévision de mauvaises rencontres, animaux à deux ou à quatre pattes
  - white: the guns loaded with ball in case of an encounter

### 9. condensed: the signs of landing they look for (II.5 ¶26; White ¶21)
- The French lists no landing, wreck, camp remains, ashes of a fire or footprint; White says only no sign of any human being. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ni une épave, ni un reste de campement, ni les cendres d’un feu éteint, ni une empreinte de pas
  - white: not the slightest sign of any human being was seen

### 10. condensed: a hull of several hundred tons (II.5 ¶33; White ¶27)
- The French says the hull of a ship of several hundred tons; White says "many tons". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: la coque d’un navire de plusieurs centaines de tonneaux
  - white: the hull of a ship of many tons measurement

### 11. changed: "even in that case" becomes "as now" (II.5 ¶35; White ¶29)
- Pencroff says it would be surprising, even after time or storm, for no spars to be cast beyond the sea's reach; White's "as now" turns the general claim into a remark on this shore. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: il serait surprenant, même dans ce cas, que des débris de mâture, des espars n’eussent pas été jetés sur le rivage
  - white: it would be surprising, as now, that no spars or timbers were thrown upon the shore

### 12. changed: the tide at slack becomes receding (II.5 ¶39; White ¶32)
- The French has the tide at slack water and soon to uncover the reefs; White has the waves receding. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: que la marée, étale en ce moment, ne devait pas tarder à découvrir
  - white: above the undulations of the receding waves

### 13. changed: the granite wall rises becomes sinks (II.5 ¶40; White ¶32)
- The French says the granite wall tended to rise higher and higher; White says it sank more and more. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: La muraille de granit tendait aussi à s’exhausser de plus en plus
  - white: The granite wall sunk more and more

### 14. cut: Pencroff and Nab among the reefs, and the false alarms (II.5 ¶41; White ¶33)
- The French has Pencroff and Nab venture among the reefs whenever an object caught the eye, and be fooled by odd rock shapes; White has only a general lookout. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Pencroff et Nab s’aventurèrent même au milieu des écueils, toutes les fois qu’un objet attirait leur regard
  - white: on the lookout for any sign of a wreck, but without success

### 15. changed: edible shellfish become mussels (II.5 ¶41; White ¶33)
- The French says edible shellfish in general; White says mussels. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: les coquillages comestibles abondaient sur cette plage
  - white: edible mussels were plenty on this beach

### 16. cut: a wreck's hull or debris would have shown, as the chest did (II.5 ¶42; White —)
- White drops the paragraph: a hull would have been visible, or its wreckage carried ashore as the chest was, found less than twenty miles away. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ou ses débris eussent été portés au rivage, comme l’avait été cette caisse, trouvée à moins de vingt milles de là
  - white: —

### 17. changed: "might be less than ten miles" becomes "proved to be ten miles" (II.5 ¶44; White ¶34)
- The French makes the plateau's distance from Cape Claw a guess (could be less than ten miles); White states ten miles as found. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qui pouvait être situé à moins de dix milles du cap Griffe
  - white: which proved to be ten miles from Claw Cape

### 18. condensed: seven or eight minutes (II.5 ¶71; White ¶56)
- The French gives the walk after Top as seven or eight minutes; White says "a few minutes". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Après sept à huit minutes de marche
  - white: In a few minutes the dog stopped.

### 19. changed: the balloon's copper fittings become leather (II.5 ¶89; White ¶73)
- The French has copper fittings on the envelope and a retaining ring; White has leather mountings and an iron ring. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ses ressorts, sa garniture de cuivre
  - white: its valve, springs, and leather mountings

### 20. changed: the cave with no key becomes one that "does not kick" (II.5 ¶95; White ¶79)
- Pencroff's joke is that the cupboard has no key; White has "it does not kick". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: mais comme elle ne ferme pas à clef
  - white: but since it does not kick

### 21. changed: a moving object becomes motionless (II.5 ¶105; White ¶86)
- The French has Pencroff see a moving object in the dark (the drifting canoe); White has it "resting motionless". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: un objet mobile qui apparaissait confusément dans l’ombre
  - white: an object resting motionless in the gloom
