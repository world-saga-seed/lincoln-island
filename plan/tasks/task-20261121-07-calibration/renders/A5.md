# A5: II.17 ¶28, ¶58–¶59, ¶79 and its note

¶28

The same day the colonists repaired to the corral with the necessary tools, and the week had not gone by before the house was ready to receive its guest. It had been raised some twenty feet from the stables, and from thence it would be easy to watch over the flock of mouflons, which then numbered more than eighty head. Some furniture, a bed, a table, a bench, a wardrobe, and a chest, was made, and arms, ammunition, and tools were carried to the corral.

¶58

“They set out on the 23rd of December, 1854.

¶59

“It is time to say that this Ayrton was a traitor. He was, in fact, the boatswain of the Britannia; but, in consequence of disputes with his captain, he had tried to draw the crew into revolt and to seize the ship, and Captain Grant had landed him, on the 8th of April, 1852, on the west coast of Australia, and had then sailed away, abandoning him—which was no more than justice.

¶79

“It was the 18th of March, 1855.[n1]

[n1] The events which have just been briefly related are taken from a work which some of our readers have doubtless read, and which is entitled The Children of Captain Grant. They will remark on this occasion, as also later, a certain discordance in the dates; but later, too, they will understand why the true dates could not be given at first. (Note of the Publisher.)
