## Task 1
### 07, discord 7: the ebbing sea "which was out"
- judgement: drop
- reason: "Was out" is the ordinary English way to say the tide is low, and in this stormy passage it carries the same sense as the French "falling". The ebbing is not a fact the story depends on.
  - french: "On entendait la mer, qui baissait alors, mugir contre la lisière des premières roches"
  - white: "They beard the sea, which was out, roaring upon the reef."

### 13, discord 14: pot-au-feu becomes soup, and the capybara ham a spiced ham
- judgement: drop
- reason: "Soup" for pot-au-feu and "spiced" for "aromatisé" are conventional approximations, and the dish and the sense stay the same. The entry's claim that the chapter heading calls it "the first" does not appear in the passage.
  - french: "Nab avait préparé un pot-au-feu d’agouti, un jambon de cabiai aromatisé"
  - white: "Neb had prepared some agouti soup, a spiced ham"

### 19, discord 2: "no natives" made certain
- judgement: drop
- reason: The French is already flat: "ne l'est pas!". "Certainly" adds emphasis only. Smith's reply ("En êtes-vous bien sûr?") is kept in White ¶8, so the exchange reads the same.
  - french: "Quant à être habitée par des indigènes, notre île ne l’est pas !"
  - white: "and there are certainly no natives"

### 21, discord 6: billions of billions become millions upon millions
- judgement: drop
- reason: Both phrases are hyperbole for "countless" in Smith's speech. Neither is meant as a count, so a reader of White learns the same thing.
  - french: "des milliards de milliards d’animalcules travaillent à bâtir"
  - white: "millions upon millions of animalculæ are building"

### 22, discord 15: Top's growls become whines
- judgement: stands
- reason: A growl is a threatening sound and a whine is a timid one, so a reader of White hears a different dog. White does keep the anger in the later "yelped", but the opening sound is changed.
  - french: "Top faisait entendre des grognements singuliers"
  - white: "Top whined in a most curious manner"

### 33, discord 24: the wall is compact granite
- judgement: stands
- reason: The material of the wall is named in the French and is lost in White. This is a real cut, though a small one. It is not a rendering, because "mass" tells the reader nothing about what the rock is.
  - french: "C’était un granit compacte, à travers lequel nul être vivant ne pouvait se frayer un chemin."
  - white: "It was a compact mass, through which no human being could make his way."

### 43, discord 7: Pencroff's oath on guarding the island
- judgement: stands
- reason: "Mille diables!" and "foi de Pencroff" are dropped, and "believe me" is a weaker asseveration. "Softened" is accurate, since the sense is kept but the sailor's force is not.
  - french: "Mille diables ! s’écria le marin, j’y resterai plutôt tout seul pour la garder, et, foi de Pencroff, on ne me la volerait pas"
  - white: "“I would sooner stay here alone to guard it,” cried the sailor, “and, believe me, they would not steal it from me"

### 44, discord 24: fear that the islet was invaded
- judgement: recut
- reason: The kind is right (condensed), but the wording is off, and f2's reading is wrong. White does keep the fear of a descent on the island (that is French ¶81, in White ¶54). What is lost from French ¶82 is the two-part question and the silence. The detonations had ceased, neither Ayrton nor Pencroff reappeared, "was the islet invaded?" and "ought we to run to help?". Reword to say that the silence and the two questions are condensed to "they could not go to the assistance", and drop the "gives only that they could not cross" wording.
  - french: "les détonations avaient cessé, et ni Ayrton ni Pencroff ne reparaissaient. L’îlot était-il donc envahi ? Ne fallait-il pas courir au secours d’Ayrton et de Pencroff ?"
  - white: "They could not go to the assistance of Ayrton or Pencroff, for the boat had been taken, and the high tide forbade them crossing the channel."

### 51, discord 10: the plateau, plantations and poultry-yard at risk
- judgement: stands
- reason: Pencroff's wish and his fear are cut, and the "White —" is correct. White ¶47's "devastation and ruin" is the narrator's comment after Neb's note, not Pencroff's list of plateau, plantations and poultry-yard. White ¶34 renders only Harbert's impatience, French ¶45, and says nothing of Pencroff's farmer's attachment.
  - french: "si les coquins ne peuvent rien tenter contre notre demeure, du moins peuvent-ils saccager le plateau, nos plantations, notre basse-cour !"
  - white: "The convicts on Prospect Plateau meant disaster, devastation and ruin!" (White ¶47, a different matter)

### 55, discord 11: the loss to be repaired "au plus tôt"
- judgement: stands
- reason: The French sets urgency ("as soon as possible"), after which the search is the only business. White drops the urgency and gives a different timing, a repair deferred until the search ends. The two readings differ, though only a little.
  - french: "il fut convenu que cette perte devrait être réparée au plus tôt. Ceci bien arrêté, on ne s’occupa plus que de mener à bonne fin l’exploration"
  - white: "it was agreed that this loss must be repaired as soon as the search was ended."

### 62, discord 7: Bonadventure becomes Good Luck
- judgement: drop
- reason: White renders the name as Good Luck in every place it occurs (chapters 34, 35, 37, 41, 56 and 62), so it is a settled English form of a name. "Bonadventure" means good luck, and the sense is kept. This falls under the labellers' exclusion of settled forms of names.
  - french: "c’était donc lui qui avait pris notre Bonadventure"
  - white: "it was he who took our Good Luck"
