# Captain Nemo tells his life (notes)

## Notes for drafting
- **Where it sits.** The French III.16, `texts/fr-1875/58.md`; right after Smith's greeting at the end of III.15; Nemo's recognition, his life story as told by the narrator, the list of his help to the colony, his plea to be judged, and Harbert's kiss. *(Drafted.)*
- **Cast.** Captain Nemo, Prince Dakkar (¶1; named ¶38); Cyrus Smith (¶4); Gédéon Spilett (¶8); Ayrton, Pencroff, Harbert, Nab (¶8); Nemo's father, mother, wife and two children, killed (¶45, ¶49); Tippoo-Sahib (¶38); his twenty companions (¶51); the French professor, his servant and the Canadian fisherman (¶55); Top and Jup, named in the list (¶65). *(Drafted.)*
- **Places.** The salon of the Nautilus (¶1); Bundelkund (¶38); Europe (¶38); England, never visited (¶41); Seringapatam (¶46); India (¶47); a deserted Pacific island and its yards (¶53); Vigo Bay (¶54); the Maelstrom, Norway, the Lofoden Isles (¶58); the coral cemetery (¶59); the port under Lincoln Island (¶61); the crypt's entrance (¶63); the inner well of Granite-house (¶64). *(Drafted.)*
- **Things.** The Nautilus (¶15); the book Twenty Thousand Leagues under the Sea (¶24); electric power, used by "by means that will one day be known" (¶53); the Spanish galleons' millions (¶54); the diving dress (¶62); the torpedo, electric bullets, quinine sulphate, rope, chest, canoe, bottle (¶65); the lettered apparatus on the Nautilus (¶66). *(Drafted.)*
- **Time.** Prince Dakkar goes to Europe at ten; from ten to thirty he studies (¶38–¶39); return to Bundelkund in 1849 (¶45); the revolt of the sepoys in 1857 (¶47); the Vigo Bay treasure dates from 1702 (¶54); the night of 6 November 1866 (¶55); seven months aboard (¶57); 22 June 1867 (¶58); at sixty, alone (¶60); six years at the port under Lincoln Island (¶62). Nemo says thirty years without contact with the inhabited world (¶19) and sixteen years since the Frenchman (¶21). The chapter covers the telling of a life; the scene itself is one night. *(Drafted.)*
- **Clues.** The loop of the mystery is closed: every incident of the list in ¶65 is given a cause. Left open or only touched: the "means that will be known one day" of his electricity (¶53); why the interviews with the French professor ended as they did (¶70–¶71); what the frigate did (¶73); the "last thought" (¶68) is the question of judgment. The footnote n1 refers readers to an earlier note on the discord in dates (¶34). *(Drafted.)*
- **Length.** 86 paragraphs (¶1–¶86, plus the summary ¶0); 3,258 words in the ¶ lines. *(Drafted.)*

## Open and flags
- Dates and the years. Nemo says the Frenchman was thrown aboard "sixteen years ago" (¶21); ¶55 gives the night of 6 November 1866 and ¶62 gives six years at the island; the colony's own date is 1868 (three years after March 1865, III.14 ¶1). Sixteen years from 1866 does not fit 1868. The Publisher's note n1 itself names "a discrepancy in some dates". Card neither. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Ce Français que le hasard jeta à mon bord il y a seize ans
  - quote: sur la discordance de quelques dates
  - at: III.16 n1
- "Thirty years". Nemo says thirty years without contact with the inhabited world (¶19). The text has him return to Bundelkund in 1849 (¶45), revolt in 1857 (¶47) and vanish after that; thirty years back from 1868 gives 1838, before 1849. Both are in the text. Card neither. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Il y a pourtant trente années que je n’ai plus aucune communication avec le monde habité
  - quote: revint-il au Bundelkund dans l’année 1849
- His age. ¶60 says he was sixty when he became alone; ¶39 says he studied from ten to thirty and ¶45 has him back in India in 1849. The year of his birth is not given. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: Le capitaine Nemo avait alors soixante ans.
- Six years at the island. ¶62 says six years at the port when the balloon fell. The balloon's fall was in March 1865 (III.14 ¶1 counts three years from the escape); the Nautilus therefore came to the island about 1859, which is before the 6 November 1866 night of ¶55 and the seven months of ¶57. The text's own order has the island port after the Frenchman. Both lines are in the text. Card neither. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: Depuis six ans, le capitaine était là, ne naviguant plus
  - quote: pendant la nuit du 6 novembre 1866
- The frigate. ¶71 says the Nautilus sank an English frigate "in the north of the Atlantic" a few days before the Frenchman's flight, while ¶73 has Nemo say he was "hemmed in a narrow and shallow bay". Both lines are in the text. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dans le nord de l’Atlantique
  - quote: J’étais resserré dans une baie étroite et peu profonde !…
- The uncle. ¶38 calls him "nephew of the hero of India, Tippo-Saïb"; ¶42 calls him "of the family of Tippo-Saïb". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: neveu du héros de l’Inde, Tippo-Saïb
- Footnote n2 names the Candiotes' rising as one of the causes he served; it is Verne's own note. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Il s’agit du soulèvement des Candiotes
  - at: III.16 n2
- The brig and the torpedo. ¶65 says Nemo blew up the brig "by the shock of a torpedo placed at the bottom of the channel"; earlier text (not mine) is where the explosion is first told. Not checked. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qui fit sauter le brick par le choc d’une torpille
- The inner well. ¶64 says he reached the top of the inner well "climbing by the projections of the rock"; III.14 ¶54 says Top and Jup no longer prowled the well in the quiet months. Both lines are in the text. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: s’élevant par les saillies du roc jusqu’à son orifice supérieur
- No Wikisource correction touches this chapter; none of its facts rests on one. *(Drafted.)*
