# New Zealand: the land named as the farthest the wind might have carried them

*The country Smith names as the farthest possible landfall, where English or Maoris would be met.*

Keywords: Nouvelle-Zélande, Maoris, English, colony, landfall

- **What it is.**
  - Smith named it as the farthest landfall the wind might have carried them to, where English or Maoris would be met (I.9 ¶70). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: jusqu’aux terres de la Nouvelle-Zélande
  - The island lies abreast of it to the west, and between New Zealand and the Chilean coast the distance is at least six thousand miles (I.14 ¶54). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: par le travers de la Nouvelle-Zélande à l’ouest
  - If the island lay only two or three hundred miles from it, Smith would build a boat (I.14 ¶61). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: au lieu de faire une maison, nous ferons un bateau
  - The island lies more than eighteen hundred miles from it (I.14 ¶79). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: à plus de dix-huit cents milles de la Nouvelle-Zélande
  - Its natives call "kauris" the superb conifers of the forest by the Mercy, near two hundred feet high (II.1 ¶48). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: auxquels les indigènes donnent le nom de « kauris » dans la Nouvelle-Zélande
  - It lies on the same latitude as Lincoln Island, and has the congeners of the eucalyptus found in the forest (II.3 ¶36). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: toutes deux situées sur la même latitude que l’île Lincoln
  - The eucalyptus are called there "fever trees" (II.3 ¶48, ¶50). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: Savez-vous comment on les appelle dans l’Australie et la Nouvelle-Zélande
    - quote: les « arbres à fièvre »
  - The Whale-Field lies between New Zealand and South America, where the southern whales are met in greatest number (II.10 ¶40). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: entre la Nouvelle-Zélande et l’Amérique du Sud, que les baleines de l’hémisphère austral se rencontrent en plus grand nombre
  - Harbert says the albatross, vanished in the west, goes towards it (II.11 ¶37). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: Vers la Nouvelle-Zélande
  - The letter Ayrton obtained, drafted by the French geographer, by an error named the east coast of New Zealand as the Duncan’s destination, so that the yacht was taken there instead of to Twofold Bay (II.17 ¶66). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: la côte est de la Nouvelle-Zélande était indiquée comme lieu de destination
  - The Duncan cruises on that coast until 3 March (1855), and Glenarvan, who crossed the country on the thirty-seventh parallel from the west coast, finds her waiting on the other coast (II.17 ¶68, ¶72). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: Le Duncan resta à croiser sur cette côte jusqu’au 3 mars
    - quote: la traversa sur le trente-septième parallèle
  - It lies to the west of the island, a voyage the colonists put aside as too long for a boat of theirs (II.19 ¶34). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: jusqu’à la Nouvelle-Zélande, dans l’ouest
- **Appears in:** I.9, I.14, II.1, II.3, II.10, II.11, II.17, II.19, III.14
