# III.10: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller p. Beads corrected: none.*

*White follows the 1875 print at ¶52 ("7th"); the working French reads "7 décembre". Not a discord (see texts/README.md).*

### 1. cut: who stands at the wall, who takes the cart back (III.10 ¶2; White ¶2)
- The French names Smith, Spilett and Pencroff at the foot of the wall after ten minutes, and Nab sent back with the cart to Grande-Vue; White gives only "within a few minutes" and does not say where Neb goes. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Dix minutes après, Cyrus Smith, Gédéon Spilett et Pencroff étaient au pied de la muraille, laissant à Nab le soin de reconduire le chariot sur le plateau de Grande-Vue.
  - white: within a few minutes Herbert was lying on his bed in Granite House.

### 2. changed: the creek becomes a bridge; the dark night cut (III.10 ¶8; White ¶5)
- Nab fires at a convict about to cross the watercourse; White has him crossing "the bridge". White also drops the dark night that kept Nab from knowing whether he hit. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qui se disposait à traverser le cours d’eau
  - white: who was crossing the bridge

### 3. cut: nineteen days (III.10 ¶10; White ¶7)
- White gives the dates 11th and 29th but drops the count of nineteen days. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Il y avait donc dix-neuf jours que Nab n’avait eu d’autres nouvelles
  - white: In that time all the information that Neb had received was the disastrous news brought by Top.

### 4. cut: why Jup, and why unseen (III.10 ¶12; White ¶9)
- White drops that Jup's intelligence had often been tried, that he had often driven the cart to the corral with Pencroff, and that the convicts would take him for a native of the woods. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: bien souvent il y avait conduit le chariot en compagnie de Pencroff
  - white: He knew the intelligence of the orang. Jup knew the word “corral.”
  - quote: dont les convicts, d’ailleurs, devraient le croire un des habitants naturels
  - white: The agile brute could slip through the woods unperceived.

### 5. cut: Jup unnoticed by the convicts (III.10 ¶15; White ¶11)
- White drops that the convicts' attention was not aroused at all as Jup went. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: sans que l’attention des convicts eût été aucunement éveillée
  - white: and disappeared in the darkness.

### 6. condensed: why the convicts kept off the shore (III.10 ¶18; White ¶13)
- The French gives the convicts' reasoning: they might think Granite-house strongly held and remembered the gunfire from the rocks at the brig. White gives only "doubtless fearing the inhabitants". White also drops that they believed the colonists still shut up at the corral, and "doing evil for evil's sake". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Ils devaient se rappeler que, pendant l’attaque du brick, de nombreux coups de feu les avaient accueillis
  - white: doubtless fearing the inhabitants of Granite House, whose number they did not know.
  - quote: qu’ils devaient croire encore confinés au corral
  - white: and they had left but half-an-hour before the colonists returned.

### 7. changed: the burning buildings become an "inclosure", Nab's struggle in vain (III.10 ¶19; White ¶14)
- The French has the poultry-yard buildings burning and Nab fighting the fire in vain until the cart appeared; White has the "inclosure to the poultry-yard" burning and drops "inutilement". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: les bâtiments de la basse-cour
  - white: the inclosure to the poultry-yard
  - quote: il avait lutté, mais inutilement, contre le feu
  - white: Ho was engaged in this work when the others returned.

### 8. cut: Spilett stays with Harbert (III.10 ¶21; White ¶15–¶16)
- White does not say that Spilett stays in Granite-house with Harbert and Pencroff while Smith goes. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Gédéon Spilett demeura à Granite-house près d’Harbert et de Pencroff
  - white: Smith, accompanied by Neb, went to see for himself, the extent of the injury done.

### 9. cut: the Chimneys spared (III.10 ¶22; White ¶16)
- White drops the remark that it was lucky the convicts did not reach the foot of Granite-house, for the Chimneys' workshops would not have escaped, and that this loss would have been more easily repaired than the plateau's. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Les ateliers des Cheminées n’auraient pas échappé à la dévastation.
  - white: —

### 10. condensed: the two likelihoods (III.10 ¶23–¶26; White ¶16)
- The French gives two cases: the convicts saw the colonists pass on the corral road, and went back to the corral, which holds precious resources for them; or they went into the Jacamar wood along the Mercy, not knowing of the return, and then back to camp. White gives one sentence; the Jacamar wood, their ignorance of the return and the corral's resources are lost. White also drops the search of the far bank. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ils s’étaient enfoncés dans le bois de Jacamar, en suivant le cours de la Mercy, et ils ignoraient ce retour
  - white: or that they had gone back to their camp to await an occasion to renew the attack.
  - quote: qui renfermait des ressources précieuses pour eux
  - white: had gone back to the corral, now undefended

### 11. cut: why no expedition is possible (III.10 ¶27; White ¶17)
- White keeps the dependence on Herbert's state but drops that Smith would need all his strength and that no one could leave Granite-house. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Cyrus Smith n’aurait pas trop de toutes ses forces, et personne ne pouvait, en ce moment, quitter Granite-house.
  - white: At present, however, all attempts to rid the island of these pests were subject to the condition of Herbert.

### 12. condensed: the plateau's damage (III.10 ¶28–¶29; White ¶18)
- White's list drops the ripening of the wheat, the other plantations, the overturned vegetable garden and the reserve of seed at Granite-house that could repair it; it also drops the mill and says "stables" for the onagga stable. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Heureusement, Granite-house possédait une réserve de graines qui permettait de réparer ces dommages.
  - white: —
  - quote: Quant au moulin et aux bâtiments de la basse-cour, à l’étable des onaggas, le feu avait tout détruit.
  - white: the stables and other buildings burned

### 13. cut: Cyrus Smith's silent anger (III.10 ¶31; White ¶18–¶19)
- White drops the whole paragraph: Smith paler than usual, holding down an inner anger, silent, his last look at the ruined fields and the smoke, and his return to Granite-house. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dénotait une colère intérieure qu’il ne dominait pas sans peine, mais il ne prononça pas une parole
  - white: —

### 14. cut: Harbert's graver illness feared (III.10 ¶32; White ¶19)
- White drops Spilett's foreboding that a graver illness, a result of the deep physical shock, might declare itself and that he would be powerless against it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Gédéon Spilett pressentait une telle aggravation dans son état, qu’il serait impuissant à la combattre !
  - white: Herbert became more and more feeble.

### 15. cut: the fever's first fit, 6 December (III.10 ¶33–¶35; White ¶19)
- White drops the date 6 December, the account of the three stages (chill, heat, sweat), the five hours of the fit, and "à tout prix". He says only that the fever became intermittent. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Gédéon Spilett le reconnut le 6 décembre.
  - white: Meantime, the fever became intermittent
  - quote: L’accès avait duré cinq heures environ.
  - white: —

### 16. condensed: the other substitutes for quinquina (III.10 ¶40; White ¶24)
- The French lists horse-chestnut, holly leaf and serpentaria beside willow bark and says the bark is worth less than quinquina; White drops the list and the comparison, and says the bark has been "partly" considered a substitute where the French says "justement". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: aussi bien que le marronnier de l’Inde, la feuille de houx, la serpentaire, etc.
  - white: Indeed, willow bark has been partly considered succedaneous to cinchonia
  - quote: bien qu’elle ne valût pas le quinquina
  - white: —

### 17. cut: the tertian fever possibility (III.10 ¶43; White ¶26)
- White drops that Spilett's silence rested on the chance of a tertian fever, with the fits not daily, returning the next day. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Il pouvait se faire que les intermittences ne fussent pas quotidiennes, que la fièvre fût tierce, en un mot, et qu’elle revînt le lendemain.
  - white: but Spilett, who knew that the fever was intermittent, looked forward to the next day with anxiety.

### 18. cut: the brain taken (III.10 ¶44; White ¶27)
- White keeps the congested liver and the delirium but drops that the delirium showed the brain was also taken. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: bientôt un délire plus intense démontra que son cerveau se prenait aussi
  - white: and soon a more marked delirium manifested itself.

### 19. softened: the germ caught "without doubt" (III.10 ¶48; White ¶31)
- Spilett is sure of the germ from the island's marshes ("sans doute", "et cela suffit"); White makes it a possibility. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Harbert aura sans doute contracté ce germe dans les marais de l’île, et cela suffit.
  - white: Herbert may have caught the germ in the marshes.

### 20. cut: the day and night of anguish (III.10 ¶52; White ¶35)
- White drops the sentence that the engineer and reporter spent the 7th, and the night that followed it, in anguish. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: On comprend dans quelles inquiétudes furent l’ingénieur et le reporter pendant cette journée du 7 décembre et pendant la nuit qui la suivit.
  - white: —

### 21. cut: what Harbert said in delirium (III.10 ¶55; White ¶36)
- White drops that what Harbert said in his delirium broke his companions' hearts, and that he raved. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Dans son délire, Harbert disait des choses qui fendaient le cœur de ses compagnons ! Il divaguait
  - white: In his delirium, Herbert wrestled with the convicts

### 22. cut: the date 8 December (III.10 ¶56; White ¶37)
- White gives "The next day" and not the date 8 December. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: La journée du lendemain, 8 décembre
  - white: The next day passed with only a continuation of the lad’s feebleness.

### 23. changed: the night of 8 to 9 December, and the third fit (III.10 ¶59–¶60; White ¶40)
- White drops the date of the night (8 to 9 December), the horribly congested liver and attacked brain, and that Herbert is "comme inanimé" between crises. The French asks whether he will live to the third fit the next day, which would surely carry him off; White says only that he would hardly live "till morning". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Vivrait-il jusqu’au lendemain, jusqu’à ce troisième accès qui devait immanquablement l’emporter ?
  - white: It was not even probable that he would live till morning.
  - quote: Son foie était horriblement congestionné, son cerveau attaqué
  - white: During the night Herbert became frightfully delirious.
