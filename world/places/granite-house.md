# Granite-house: the colonists' dwelling, with an inner well

*The great cavern found after the blast at the end of the old outlet and named Granite-house by Smith; in the last chapter, the colonists' home, with a well inside it that Captain Nemo climbed from below.*

Keywords: home, house, shaft, rock, rope, orifice, dwelling

- **What it is.**
  - Smith found the great cavern, after the blast, at the end of the old outlet; it is vast and magnificent, and empty: not an animal, not a living being (I.18 ¶41, ¶42). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: une vaste et magnifique caverne
    - quote: pas un animal, pas un être vivant
  - Smith at once judges it their dwelling (I.18 ¶51). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: Voici notre demeure
  - Its floor lies ninety feet above sea level, over the well (I.18 ¶50). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: quatre-vingt-dix pieds au-dessus du niveau de la mer
  - The waters had abandoned it and could not come back (I.18 ¶57). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: Les eaux l’avaient abandonnée et n’y pouvaient plus revenir
  - It could be divided into rooms by brick partitions and fitted up as a spacious apartment, though by torchlight its capacity could not be estimated (I.18 ¶57). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: au moyen de cloisons de briques
  - Its difficulties are light and access; a great thickness of granite roofs it, so light must come through the front wall facing the sea, thought to be thin, by windows, a door and an outside ladder (I.18 ¶58). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: percer une porte que des fenêtres, et d’établir une échelle extérieure
  - The front wall is only three feet thick where the pick went through, and the opening stands eighty feet above the ground (I.18 ¶65, ¶66). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: trois pieds d’épaisseur
    - quote: dominait le sol de quatre-vingts pieds
  - Through the opening one sees the strip of shore, the islet, and beyond them the immense sea (I.18 ¶66). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: la lisière du rivage, l’îlot, et, au delà, l’immense mer
  - The left part is not more than thirty feet high and wide by a hundred long; the right part is enormous, its vault rounding more than eighty feet high (I.18 ¶67). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: ne mesurait pas plus de trente pieds de haut et de large sur une longueur de cent pieds
    - quote: plus de quatre-vingts pieds de hauteur
  - Granite pillars, irregularly set, support the vault like a cathedral nave; it mixes Byzantine, Romanesque and Gothic forms, yet is only the work of nature (I.18 ¶67). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: des piliers de granit, irrégulièrement disposés
    - quote: ce n’était que l’œuvre de la nature
  - Smith means to place chambers, stores and offices in the left part, and to keep the splendid cavern as study room and museum (I.18 ¶70). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: notre salle d’étude et notre musée
  - It was named Granite-house by Smith; the note says "house" applies to palaces and houses alike, and glosses it "palace of granite" (I.18 ¶72, n1). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: Granite-house
    - quote: Palais de granit
    - quote: Le mot house s’applique également aux palais et aux maisons
  - It is reached only by climbing back up the old outlet to the plateau’s top (see [the old outlet](the-old-outlet.md)) (I.18 ¶73). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: en remontant le couloir
  - The colonists are called the colonists of Granite-house, so it is their dwelling (III.16 ¶66). *(My reading, to confirm.)*
    - quote: les colons de Granite-house
  - It has an inner well (a shaft) that can be reached from the sea floor (III.16 ¶64). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: arriver au fond du puits intérieur de Granite-house
  - The well has an upper opening, reached by climbing projections of the rock (III.16 ¶64). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: s’élevant par les saillies du roc jusqu’à son orifice supérieur
- **On the page.**
  - The well is at the cavern’s end (see [the well](the-well.md)); Top’s barking finds nothing in the cavern, and Smith murmurs that Top knows more than they do about many things (I.18 ¶56). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: Top en sait plus long que nous sur bien des choses
  - From the upper opening of the well Nemo heard the colonists talk of the past, the present and the future (III.16 ¶64). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: il entendait les colons raconter le passé, étudier le présent et l’avenir
  - A rope was thrown from the top of Granite-house during the attack of the monkeys (III.16 ¶65). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: la corde du haut de Granite-house
  - Nemo summoned the colonists of Granite-house by a wire linking the corral to the Nautilus (III.16 ¶66). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: il manda, comme on sait, les colons de Granite-house
- **Appears in:** I.18, III.16
- **Open:**
  - Chapter I.13 knows no Granite-house: there the engineer still hopes to find or build a better dwelling than the Chimneys (I.13 ¶40). Nothing in these chapters says where Granite-house stands, how big it is, or how the well opens inside it.
    - quote: une demeure plus convenable
