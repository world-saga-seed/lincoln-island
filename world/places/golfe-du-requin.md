# Golfe du Requin: the narrow gulf of the north-east, like a shark's open jaw

*The narrow gulf between the two capes of the north-east, named Shark-gulf by Harbert on 30 March; the lava causeway of Mont Franklin runs down to it.*

Keywords: golfe du Requin, Shark-gulf, mâchoire, jaw, north-east, gulf, squale, Mandibule

- **What it is.**
  - Between the two north-east capes there is a narrow gulf that looks like the half-open jaw of a formidable shark (I.11 ¶22). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: un étroit golfe qui ressemblait à la mâchoire entr’ouverte de quelque formidable squale
  - Harbert named it golfe du Requin (Shark-gulf) (I.11 ¶68). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: appelons-le golfe du Requin (Shark-gulf)
  - Pencroff completed the image by naming the two parts of the jaw cap Mandibule; there being two, they became cap Mandibule-Nord and cap Mandibule-Sud (see [Mandibule-Nord](cap-mandibule-nord.md), [Mandibule-Sud](cap-mandibule-sud.md)) (I.11 ¶69). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: nommant cap Mandibule (Mandible-cape) les deux parties de la mâchoire
  - The narrow gulf between the two capes is framed by a bizarre and capricious border formed of the mineral matter vomited by the volcano (I.15 ¶5). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: cet étroit golfe compris entre les deux caps
    - quote: formés des matières minérales vomies par le volcan
  - Before the voyage the colonists know little of the narrow bay hollowed between the capes "like a shark’s jaw" (II.19 ¶35). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: l’étroite baie qui se creusait entre eux comme une mâchoire de requin
  - Smith holds that it deserves to be explored with care (II.19 ¶60). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: cette baie, qui mérite d’être explorée avec soin
  - It is entered by a narrow pass, past cape Mandibule-Nord, into waters framed in the strangest setting of lavas (II.20 ¶6). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: entrait prudemment dans la passe
    - quote: ces eaux, enfermées dans le plus étrange cadre de laves
  - Pencroff thinks it would make an admirable roadstead where fleets could manoeuvre at ease (II.20 ¶7). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: un bout de mer qui ferait une rade admirable, où des flottes pourraient évoluer à leur aise
  - Smith says it was formed by two lava flows vomited by the volcano and heaped up by successive eruptions, so that it is sheltered all round and, he thinks, calm as a lake even in the worst winds (II.20 ¶8). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: ce golfe a été formé par deux coulées de laves, vomies par le volcan
    - quote: la mer y est calme comme un lac
  - The wind enters only by the narrow neck between the two capes, and the north cape covers the south one so as to keep out the squalls (II.20 ¶9). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: n’a que cet étroit goulet creusé entre les deux caps
    - quote: le cap du nord couvre-t-il celui du sud
  - Nab calls it "la gueule du Requin", the shark’s mouth (II.20 ¶12). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: Nous sommes dans la gueule du Requin
  - The sounding line, about fifty fathoms long, with an iron block, runs out to its end without touching bottom (see [the sounding line](../things/the-sounding-line.md)) (II.20 ¶18). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: Cette ligne mesurait environ cinquante brasses
    - quote: sans heurter le sol
  - Smith calls it a true abyss, and in view of the island’s plutonian origin finds such depressions in the sea floor unsurprising (II.20 ¶20). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: c’est un véritable abîme que ce golfe
    - quote: l’origine plutonienne de l’île
  - Harbert judges that its walls seem cut sheer, and that even a line five or six times longer would find no bottom at their foot (II.20 ¶21). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: ces murailles ont été coupées à pic
  - Its high lava walls, very steep, offer along the whole perimeter not one place fit for landing, like an impassable curtain wall recalling, but more arid, the fiords of Norway (II.20 ¶25). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: n’offraient pas sur tout le périmètre du golfe un seul endroit propice à un débarquement
    - quote: C’était une infranchissable courtine
    - quote: les fiords de la Norwége
  - The Bonadventure, grazing the walls, finds not even a ledge from which passengers could leave the boat (II.20 ¶25). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: ne trouva pas même une saillie qui pût permettre aux passagers de quitter le bord
  - Spilett says it lacks a cut or trench giving access to the interior of the island (II.20 ¶24). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: Une coupée, une tranchée quelconque, qui donne accès à l’intérieur de l’île
  - Pencroff holds that with a mine the wall could be breached when needed (II.20 ¶26). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: la mine aidant, on saurait bien éventrer cette muraille
  - The dunes end at its half-open mouth (III.11 ¶25). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: la gueule entr’ouverte du golfe du Requin
  - Its high walls are of lava, and it is extremely difficult to reach the level of the gulf itself (III.13 ¶96). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: les hautes murailles laviques du golfe du Requin
    - quote: il fût extrêmement difficile d’atteindre le niveau même du golfe
