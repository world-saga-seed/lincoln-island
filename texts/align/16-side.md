WH ¶38 “What’s going on under there?” asked the sailor examining the surface of the lake.

## Bead 43: French ¶44–¶45, White ¶42 (2-1)

FR ¶44 Cependant, Top était revenu à l’appel de son maître, et avait regagné la berge ; mais il ne pouvait rester en repos ; il sautait au milieu des grandes herbes, et, son instinct le guidant, il semblait suivre quelque être invisible qui se serait glissé sous les eaux du lac, en en rasant les bords. WH ¶42 Meantime, Top came ashore at the call of his master, but he could not be quiet; he rushed along the bank, through the tall grass, and, guided by instinct, seemed to be following some object, invisible under the water, which was hugging the shore. Nevertheless the surface was calm and undisturbed by a ripple. Often the colonists stood still on the bank and watched the water, but they could discover nothing. There certainly was some mystery here, and the engineer was much perplexed.

## Bead 44: French ¶46, White ¶43 (1-1)

FR ¶46 « Poursuivons jusqu’au bout cette exploration, » dit-il.

WH ¶43 “We will follow out this exploration,” he said.

## Bead 45: French ¶47, White ¶44 (1-1)

FR ¶47 Une demi-heure après, ils étaient tous arrivés à l’angle sud-est du lac et se retrouvaient sur le plateau même de Grande-Vue. WH ¶44 In half an hour all had arrived at the southeast angle of the lake, and were again upon Prospect Plateau. They had made the circle of the bank without the engineer having discovered either where or how the surplus water was discharged.

## Bead 46: French ¶48, White ¶45 (1-1)

FR ¶48 « Pourtant, ce déversoir existe, répétait-il, et puisqu’il n’est pas extérieur, il faut qu’il soit creusé à l’intérieur du massif granitique de la côte !

WH ¶45 “Nevertheless, this outlet exists,” he repeated, “and, since it is not outside, it must penetrate the massive granite of the coast!”

## Bead 47: French ¶49, White ¶46 (1-1)

FR ¶49 — Mais quelle importance attachez-vous à savoir cela, mon cher Cyrus ? demanda Gédéon Spilett.

WH ¶46 “And why do you want to find that out?” asked Spilett.

## Bead 48: French ¶50, White ¶47 (1-1)

FR ¶50 — Une assez grande, répondit l’ingénieur, car si l’épanchement se fait à travers le massif, il est possible qu’il s’y trouve quelque cavité, qu’il eût été facile de rendre habitable après avoir détourné les eaux.

WH ¶47 “Because,” answered the engineer, “if the outlet is through the solid rock it is possible that there is some cavity, which could be easily rendered habitable, after having turned the water in another direction.”

WH ¶48 “But may not the water flow into the sea, through a subterranean outlet at the bottom of the lake?” asked Herbert.

WH ¶49 “Perhaps so,” answered Smith, “and in that case, since Nature has not aided us, we must build our house ourselves.”

WH ¶50 As it was 5 o’clock, the colonists were thinking of returning to the Chimneys across the plateau, when Top again became excited, and, barking with rage, before his master could hold him, he sprang a second time into the lake. Every one ran to the bank. The dog was already twenty feet off, and Smith called to him to come back, when suddenly an enormous head emerged from the water.

## Bead 52: French ¶55, White ¶51 (1-1)

WH ¶51 Herbert instantly recognized it, the comical face, with huge eyes and long silky moustaches.

WH ¶54 The huge monster threw himself upon the dog. His master could do nothing to save him, and, before Spilett or Herbert could draw their bows, Top, seized by the dugong, had disappeared under the water.

WH ¶55 Neb, spear in hand, would have sprung to the rescue of the dog, and attacked the formidable monster in its own element, had he not been held back by his master.

## Bead 57: French ¶61, White ¶56 (1-1)

FR ¶61 Cependant, une lutte se passait sous les eaux, lutte inexplicable, car, dans ces conditions, Top ne pouvait évidemment pas résister, lutte qui devait être terrible, on le voyait aux bouillonnements de la surface, lutte, enfin, qui ne pouvait se terminer que par la mort du chien ! Mais soudain, au milieu d’un cercle d’écume, on vit reparaître Top. Lancé en l’air par quelque force inconnue, il s’éleva à dix pieds au-dessus de la surface du lac, retomba au milieu des eaux profondément troublées, et eût bientôt regagné la berge sans blessures graves, miraculeusement sauvé.

WH ¶56 Meanwhile a struggle was going on under the water—a struggle which, owing to the powerlessness of the dog, was inexplicable; a struggle which, they could see by the agitation of the surface, was becoming more terrible each moment; in short, a struggle which could only be terminated by the death of the dog. But suddenly, through the midst of a circle of foam, Top appeared, shot upward by some unknown force, rising ten feet in the air, and falling again into the tumultuous waters, from which he escaped to shore without any serious wounds, miraculously saved.

WH ¶57 Cyrus Smith and his companions looked on amazed. Still more inexplicable, it seemed as if the struggle under water continued. Doubtless the dugong, after having seized the dog, bad been attacked by some more formidable animal, and had been obliged to defend itself.
