# III.12: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller p. Beads corrected: none.*

### 1. condensed: the missing traces on the west coast (III.12 ¶3; White ¶3)
- The French lists what is no longer found: footprints, broken branches, cold ashes, abandoned camps; White says "no further traces of any kind". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Plus d’empreintes de pas, plus de brisées aux arbres, plus de cendres refroidies, plus de campements abandonnés.
  - white: On the west coast they found no further traces of any kind.

### 2. changed: the corral as a supply store (III.12 ¶6; White ¶6)
- Smith says the corral is for the convicts only a place to draw supplies, not a settled camp; White has it "provisional", which does not say that it is a store. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Le corral n’est pour eux qu’un lieu d’approvisionnement, et non un campement définitif.
  - white: The corral is only a provisional and not a permanent retreat for them.

### 3. changed: an act of gratitude becomes a discovery (III.12 ¶9; White ¶10)
- Smith names the second aim of the search as an act of gratitude to be done; White has "to make a discovery". The outline and the next paragraphs read the French as thanks owed to the unknown. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: nous avons un acte de reconnaissance à accomplir !
  - white: to punish crime and to make a discovery.

### 4. cut: fortifying the corral, and the colonists' approval (III.12 ¶14–¶15; White ¶15–¶16)
- If the corral is empty the plan is to fortify themselves there and make it the centre for exploring Mount Franklin; White says only that it is to be "used as the point". White also drops that the plan was unanimously approved and that they longed to have their island back. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: s’il ne l’était pas, s’y retrancher et en faire le centre des opérations
  - white: but if it should be empty, it was to be used as the point from which the explorations of Mount Franklin would be made.
  - quote: Ce plan fut unanimement approuvé des colons, et il leur tardait, vraiment, d’avoir repris possession entière de leur île !
  - white: —

### 5. cut: Harbert's wound as the reason to wait (III.12 ¶18; White ¶18)
- White drops the reminder that a shot at the corral already struck Harbert. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ainsi qu’il était arrivé à Harbert
  - white: but to do this in the day-time was to run the risk of being shot

### 6. changed: feet become paces (III.12 ¶30; White ¶28)
- Under the trees the darkness hides objects beyond thirty to forty feet; White says thirty or forty paces. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: au delà d’un rayon de trente à quarante pieds
  - white: thirty or forty paces distant

### 7. cut: walking apart, expecting a shot (III.12 ¶31; White ¶28)
- White drops that Spilett and Pencroff walked apart to offer less of a mark, and that they expected a report at every instant. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Ils marchaient l’un écarté de l’autre, afin d’offrir moins de prise aux coups de feu.
  - white: —

### 8. cut: no light seen at the gate (III.12 ¶41; White ¶34)
- White drops that they reached the gate without a single gleam of light in the dark. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: sans que l’ombre eût été sillonnée d’un seul trait de lumière
  - white: crept cautiously forward to the gate of the corral

### 9. cut: that the convicts may not know of the expedition (III.12 ¶47; White ¶35)
- White drops the possibility that the convicts might not know of the expedition against them, which is the reason not to risk the chance of surprise. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: s’ils n’avaient pas connaissance de l’expédition tentée contre eux
  - white: And, if there was now a chance of surprising the convicts, should they risk that chance in this way?

### 10. cut: the cart as the store of munitions and food (III.12 ¶55; White ¶42)
- Smith gives two reasons for taking the cart: it holds the munitions and the food, and it can serve as a breastwork; White gives only the defense. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: c’est notre fourgon de munitions et de vivres
  - white: it may serve as a defense in case of need.

### 11. cut: one of the convicts may have just gone out (III.12 ¶64–¶65; White ¶49)
- White drops the second question, whether one of the convicts had just gone out, and the colonists' inability to answer. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Y étaient-ils encore, ou un des leurs venait-il de sortir ?
  - white: Were they still there?

### 12. cut: Jup and Top tied up (III.12 ¶74; White ¶58)
- White drops that Jup and Top were tied to the cart as a precaution. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qu’on y avait attachés par prudence
  - white: The wagon was left under charge of Top and Jup

### 13. cut: the two groups, and the dark corral empty (III.12 ¶75–¶76; White ¶58–¶59)
- White drops that the colonists went in two groups along the palisade, and that they saw the dark part of the corral absolutely empty. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Cyrus Smith, Pencroff, Gédéon Spilett, d’un côté, Harbert et Nab, de l’autre, en longeant la palissade, observèrent cette portion du corral qui était absolument obscure et déserte.
  - white: In a few moments they were before the closed door of the house.
