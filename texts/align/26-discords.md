### 1. condensed: the tree list and the mastic bushes (II.4 ¶4–¶5; White ¶3)
- The French names the first bushes (mastic) and eight kinds of tree and says the trees are mostly of middling size because crowding hurt their growth; White gives none of the names and no reason for the slow going. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: C’étaient des déodars, des douglas, des casuarinas, des gommiers, des eucalyptus, des dragonniers, des hibiscus, des cèdres et autres essences, généralement de taille médiocre, car leur nombre avait nui à leur développement.
  - white: The forest was, for the most part, composed of such trees as had already been recognized about the lake and on Prospect Plateau.

### 2. condensed: the crayfish (II.4 ¶27; White ¶24)
- The French says the catch was miraculous because the crayfish swarmed, and describes the cobalt-blue shell and the rostrum armed with a small tooth; White says only "little blue crustaceæ". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dont le test présentait une couleur bleu cobalt, et qui portaient un rostre armé d’une petite dent
  - white: It took but five minutes to fill a sack with these little blue crustaceæ

### 3. cut: the tide would have turned the creek back if the mouth were near (II.4 ¶29; White ¶26)
- The French reasons that the rising tide would have turned the creek back had its mouth been only a few miles off, and it does not; White drops the "few miles" and makes the distance follow "because" the tide was rising. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: aurait dû rebrousser le cours du creek, si son embouchure n’eût été qu’à quelques milles seulement
  - white: because at this time the tide was rising, and its’ effect would have been visible near the mouth of the creek

### 4. changed: the right bank is as thick as the left becomes much denser (II.4 ¶30; White ¶26)
- The French says the trees of the right bank were as close as those of the left; White says the right bank was much denser than the left. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Les arbres de sa rive droite étaient aussi pressés que ceux de sa rive gauche
  - white: The growth of trees on the right bank was much denser than on the left

### 5. condensed: the finest trees in the world (II.4 ¶34; White ¶28)
- The French calls the fringe made of the most beautiful trees in the world; White says "beautiful trees". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: une admirable lisière faite des plus beaux arbres du monde
  - white: a fine border of beautiful trees

### 6. changed: the cove becomes a hollow by a rivulet (II.4 ¶35; White ¶29)
- The French puts the colonists at the notch of a small cove, too small for two or three fishing boats, which serves as the outlet of the new creek; White has a hollow by a tiny rivulet that is the "neck" of the other stream. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: à l’échancrure d’une petite crique sans importance, qui n’eût même pas pu contenir deux ou trois barques de pêche
  - white: stood in a hollow by a tiny rivulet, which served as a neck to the other stream

### 7. cut: the Pacific tides never reach the river; millions of years (II.4 ¶35; White ¶29)
- The French adds that the Pacific tides at their highest never reach the river's level, which forms an upper reach, and that millions of years must pass before the water wears through the granite sill; White has only the fall and the untouched current. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: des millions d’années, sans doute, s’écouleraient encore avant que les eaux eussent rongé ce radier de granit
  - white: —

### 8. cut: the exploration is to go on over the whole Serpentine peninsula (II.4 ¶36; White ¶30)
- The French says the exploration must continue over the whole Serpentine peninsula, between Falls-river and Reptile promontory; White says "this part of the coast" and leaves the peninsula unnamed here. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: sur toute la presqu’île Serpentine, que l’exploration devait être continuée
  - white: on this part of the coast that the exploration must be continued

### 9. cut: the hour of departure (II.4 ¶38; White ¶32)
- The French fixes the start after lunch at half past eleven; White gives no hour. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: à onze heures et demie, Cyrus Smith donna le signal du départ
  - white: After a hurried breakfast he gave the signal to start.

### 10. cut: they need twice as long, eight hours (II.4 ¶39; White ¶32)
- The French says the twelve miles, four hours on a clear beach, took twice that; White says only that obstacles multiplied. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: mais il leur fallut le double de ce temps pour atteindre leur but
  - white: and these hindrances multiplied as they proceeded

### 11. changed: why a wreck might leave no trace (II.4 ¶40; White ¶32)
- The French has Spilett say the sea may have carried everything out to the open; White has him say that because the tide was up they could not be certain. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: la mer avait pu tout entraîner au large
  - white: as the tide was up, they could not say with certainty that there had not been one

### 12. cut: why they must camp at the promontory (II.4 ¶42; White ¶34)
- The French says that after the promontory they could not get back before sunset to the camp near the sources of the Mercy, and that provisions were not lacking; White gives the night camp with no reason and no word of the provisions. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: n’auraient plus le temps de revenir, avant le coucher du soleil, au campement qui avait été établi près des sources de la Mercy
  - white: it was evident that the colonists would have to camp for the night on the promontory of Reptile End

### 13. changed: furred game gone from the fringe becomes game as plentiful as on the other coast (II.4 ¶42; White ¶34)
- The French says the furred game no longer showed on this wooded fringe and only the birds swarmed; White says game was as plentiful as on the other coast. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le gibier de poil ne se montrait plus sur cette lisière
  - white: game was as plenty here as on the other coast

### 14. condensed: the birds (II.4 ¶42; White ¶34)
- The French lists jacamars, trogons, tragopans, grouse, lories, parrots, cockatoos, pheasants, pigeons and "a hundred others", and a nest in every tree; White says "birds of different kinds abounded". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: jacamars, couroucous, tragopans, tétras, loris, perroquets, kakatoès, faisans, pigeons et cent autres
  - white: birds of different kinds abounded

### 15. condensed: the uses of bamboo (II.4 ¶47; White ¶38)
- Harbert's list loses the canes and pipe stems, the Chinese paper pulp, the timber that insects never attack, and the vessels cut from the joints as the Chinese use them. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: que les grands bambous forment d’excellents matériaux de construction, légers et solides, et qui ne sont jamais attaqués par les insectes
  - white: that the larger ones make excellent building material and strong jars

### 16. condensed: the rocks broken by the southwest seas (II.4 ¶60; White ¶51)

  - quote: devaient être violemment battus par la mer sous l’influence des vents du sud-ouest
  - white: much worn by the action of the sea

  - quote: ce féroce rival du tigre, bien autrement redoutable que le couguar, qui n’est que le rival du loup
