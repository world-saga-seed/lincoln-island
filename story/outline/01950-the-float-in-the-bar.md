# The Float in the Bar (outline)

*Chapter 01950, the inserted chapter. Before: [01900](01900-the-facade-the-ladder-and-the-warren.md) (Granite-house fitted out, late May 1865). After: [02000](02000-candles-and-the-grain-of-wheat.md) (June: the winter opens with squalls; Whit Sunday, 4 June; the grain of wheat). Verne's narrator, past tense, kept with Pencroff and Harbert (R1121-28). Checked against [the world's rules](../../world/rules.md).*

*Stage: outlined around the author's frames (R1121-54 to -60).*

## What the chapter is

- *(Settled R1121-23.)* A storm chapter. A castaway sees something half buried near the shore and takes one other into his confidence, keeping it from the rest. A squall drives them off. When it has passed, the bar has shifted and the thing is gone. The cause is natural, with no sign of Captain Nemo's hand.
- *(Settled R1121-36.)* The point is the cost of not acting quickly. *(Settled R1121-37.)* That cost is the secrecy: five could have dug the float out in the hour of low water, two cannot, and they spend the time they have reading it. The scene is kept short.
- *(Settled R1121-24.)* It foreshadows the chest (II.2). At the chest the two say nothing of the float, and the page does not explain their silence.
- *(Settled R1121-38.)* 1 to 3 June 1865.

## The find

- *(Settled R1121-26, R1121-27.)* The thing is one of the floats that held up the Nautilus's nets, made in the Nautilus's style. It was lost in the Nautilus's ordinary work, years before. Nemo never learns it came near them. It is never recovered.
- *(Settled R1121-62, R1121-63.)* The net is old. The crew laid it as a set net off the Union Bay shore while the Nautilus still sailed. It fouled in the bar and they could not get it back, and it has been coming apart there ever since. It has lain six years and more under the sand, and this June's squalls uncover it. The page never dates it. No act of Nemo's, then or now. *(Settled R1121-64.)* It fouled below the low-water mark, where the crew's divers could not dig it out from under a bar the sea had moved over it, and over the years the shifting sands have worked it inshore.
- *(Settled R1121-33.)* It is glass, a blown ball with the device moulded in it.
- *(Settled R1121-32.)* It is thick with barnacles. *(Settled R1121-62.)* They grew while the float rode at the surface, and they died under the sand. *(Settled R1121-120.)* The crust is acorn barnacles, cemented flat to the glass. Among them are a few goose barnacles, the kind that hang from things kept at the surface, now dead shells and dried stumps of stalk.
- *(Settled R1121-31.)* They bare only a few letters of the motto's ring, too few to make a word. The N lies on the hidden side, toward the sand.
- *(Settled R1121-28.)* The page never says it was Nemo's.

## The beats

*Marks: **(Settled …)** is the author's, a frame or a ruling. **(Resolved by judgment, overturnable.)** is a gap the author left to the executor. **(Proposal.)** is the executor's tween.*

### 1. Pencroff sees it (1 June, morning low water)

- *(Settled R1121-54.)* Pencroff sees it on his way to his lines, reading the waterline the way a sailor does.
- *(Settled R1121-60.)* His lines are set in the Mercy near its mouth, with iron hooks from the forging (I.15 ¶51, "etc."; named at II.8 ¶15). His way to them runs along the beach past the channel to the islet. The French has fishing that month, "ni la chasse, ni la pêche" (I.20 ¶2), and later Pencroff's "lignes de fond, armées d’hameçons de fer" in the Mercy (II.8 ¶15, in the pluperfect).
- *(Settled R1121-62.)* This June's squalls have uncovered it. Storms reshape this shore: after the March storm "l’effroyable tempête de la veille avait modifié l’aspect des lieux" (I.8 ¶90).
- *(Proposal.)* At the neap low water, the sands of the channel bed show for a short while (R1121-53; "la plus grande partie des sables, formant le lit du canal, avait découvert", I.3 ¶59). On the bar a round thing, half buried, catches his eye, the wrong shape for a stone. *(Settled R1121-74.)* It is about a foot across, and the bar lies a cable or two off the beach.
- *(Proposal.)* The tide is on the turn (low water about 8, notes item 7), and he does not go out alone.
- *(Proposal, from the draft, [01950](../prose/01950-the-float-in-the-bar.md).)* The lines are his own errand, visited every morning; the narrator likens it to a captain's round of his ship.
- *(Proposal, from the draft, [01950](../prose/01950-the-float-in-the-bar.md).)* He holds back because he crossed the channel on the first day and knows its current; he resets his lines watching the bar, and the sea has covered it before he is back at the ladder.

### 2. He takes only Harbert (1 June)

- *(Settled R1121-34.)* He takes only Harbert, so as not to raise hopes.
- They speak of it only out of doors (world rule 4).
- *(Settled R1121-75.)* Top stays in Granite-house with Smith.
- *(Resolved by judgment, overturnable.)* The pretext is the lines themselves. Pencroff asks the boy to help him lift and reset them at the next low water. It is his daily errand, so no one asks, and it is no lie.
- *(Proposal, from the draft, [01950](../prose/01950-the-float-in-the-bar.md).)* He says nothing at breakfast, and speaks to Harbert later that morning, finding him alone on the beach. He tells of a thing on the bar that does not belong there, and hedges that it may be a stone.
- *(Settled R1121-109.)* Harbert's cry at "made by men's hands" is the hope of a ship, not of men on the island. Pencroff's answer stands: it may have come a long way.
- *(Proposal, from the draft, [01950](../prose/01950-the-float-in-the-bar.md).)* Pencroff names the three he will not raise hopes in, Smith, Spilett and Nab, all tumbling down the ladder for what may be a stone.
- *(Proposal.)* The wait for the next low water is the first cost of the secrecy (R1121-37, delay c).
- *(Proposal, from the draft, [01950](../prose/01950-the-float-in-the-bar.md).)* The evening ebb falls after dark, so the sailor, no patient man, waits the night through.

### 3. Out at the next low water, without tools (2 June)

- *(Settled R1121-69.)* The day dawns in rain ahead of the squall. The shutters of Granite-house are shut from first light, the house lit by the fire, and the other three are at indoor work, so no one sees the bar.
- *(Settled R1121-127, R1121-128.)* The wind is east-north-east, beating on the facade, and the kitchen chimney smokes, as I.19 ¶46 says it would in such winds. Stated as fact, with no nod back.
- *(Settled R1121-52, R1121-72.)* The first cause, in a line: the tools stay behind, because picks carried out of Granite-house would be asked about. Pencroff carries his knife, one of the two halves of Top's collar.
- *(Proposal, from the draft, [01950](../prose/01950-the-float-in-the-bar.md).)* Indoors that morning Top lies at Smith's feet.
- *(Proposal, from the draft, [01950](../prose/01950-the-float-in-the-bar.md).)* Pencroff thinks of the picks and shovels in the storeroom; no one carries a pick down the ladder to lift lines, and the engineer would have asked what it was for.
- *(Settled R1121-130.)* Toward eight, as the ebb nears its lowest, Pencroff turns up the collar of his jacket, says aloud that the fish do not mind the rain if he does, and goes out by the door, Harbert after him. Nab calls them two madmen and goes back to his pots.
- *(Proposal, from the draft, [01950](../prose/01950-the-float-in-the-bar.md).)* At the foot of the wall they leave their much-worn shoes under the rock (the words as R1121-119) and roll their trousers to the knee.
- *(Settled R1121-78.)* On the way out they lift the lines, and the lines are empty.
- *(Proposal, from the draft, [01950](../prose/01950-the-float-in-the-bar.md).)* The hooks come up with the bait untouched (the words as R1121-120), and Pencroff jokes that the fish have the sense to keep indoors.
- *(Settled R1121-73.)* The bar lies on the coast side of the channel's gut, reached over the bared sands. They do not ford. The five ford the channel itself three days later ("franchir à gué le canal", I.20 ¶20, 5 June).

### 4. At the float

- *(Settled R1121-52.)* The second cause, in a line: the float is still on its net, buried in the bar and heavy with sand.
- *(Settled R1121-130.)* A tangle of wrack, torn loose by the squalls, lies over it; Pencroff drags it away.
- *(Settled R1121-30.)* It is glass, stuck fast, and they cannot turn it. *(Settled R1121-72, R1121-129.)* Its lashing runs under it into the sand on the hidden side. Pencroff works his blade down beside the glass and saws blind at it a long while; the sand closes on the blade, and he cannot tell whether he has cut it half through or not at all.
- *(Settled R1121-32, R1121-134.)* It is thick with barnacles, over the part that shows: they grew where the float rode under water, and the ball has turned in the bar since, so never "thickest on the top". They chip some away with a stone. *(Proposal.)* They go carefully, for fear of the glass.
- *(Proposal, from the draft, [01950](../prose/01950-the-float-in-the-bar.md).)* The upper third shows above the bar as a turtle's back shows above the sea; the rotten cord comes out of the sand on the side toward the gut.
- *(Proposal.)* Pencroff: a net float, but not cork and not American. He has never seen the like.
- *(Proposal, from the draft, [01950](../prose/01950-the-float-in-the-bar.md).)* He raps the bared glass with a knuckle, and says he has put into many ports and never seen one.
- *(Settled R1121-130, R1121-131.)* "I have never seen the like", and "The sailor might well say so." Then two paragraphs in the narrator's voice: glass net floats had only in late years come into the Norway cod fisheries, a novelty of the northern seas; and the sea opens strange roads to floating bodies, the South Pacific's waters turning in a great round. They speak of the sea, never of this float's origin (R1121-92).
- *(Settled R1121-82.)* While Harbert chips, Pencroff counts in his head what the glass is worth: light for the shuttered house, a float for his lines. Never aloud. The reader does the sum when it breaks.
- *(Proposal, from the draft, [01950](../prose/01950-the-float-in-the-bar.md).)* In the sum, Pencroff is sure Smith could make a window of the ball, having seen the lens Smith made of two watch glasses (the words as R1121-120).
- *(Proposal, from the draft, [01950](../prose/01950-the-float-in-the-bar.md).)* Pencroff knows barnacles from scraping ships' bottoms; Harbert tells him these few are another kind, that hang by a stalk.
- *(Proposal; the kinds Settled R1121-120.)* Harbert: among the common barnacles, goose barnacles, long dead, the kind on a thing kept at the surface. So it has been in the sea a long time, and it came from far off. "Long ago" is true. "Far off" is wrong, since the net was set close in. *(Settled R1121-77.)* The reading dims their hope of a ship nearby; it does not kill it.
- *(Proposal, from the draft, [01950](../prose/01950-the-float-in-the-bar.md).)* Pencroff keeps the hope alive after a pause: far off is a long word, and ships go a long way.
- *(Settled R1121-55, R1121-129, R1121-134.)* The frame: a broken run, a B, an L, an I. Harbert says "Latin, I think," and adds only that a word in Latin may be read a dozen ways and there are not letters enough to choose among them. *(Proposal.)* What it rests on is the -bili- of the Latin ending -bilis, which a schooled boy knows. It is part of MOBILIS, but not the word. The N lies toward the sand (R1121-31).
- *(Settled R1121-37, R1121-118.)* The time they have goes in the reading. The scene is short. Even Pencroff forgets to watch the water, and "The hour of low water went by in this way" is on the page before the squall (K3). The lapse is Verne's kind, and the rescue is its cost (R1121-134).

### 5. The squall (2 June)

- *(Settled R1121-23.)* The squall comes in. The waves are too high to stay, but they can see the float in the surf. They go back to safety and wait it out. *(Proposal.)* The flood and the squall come together, and the surge covers the bar (R1121-53). The weather is the text's own: "des averses et des rafales qui se succédèrent sans relâche" (I.20 ¶1).
- *(Proposal, from the draft, [01950](../prose/01950-the-float-in-the-bar.md).)* The squall comes out of the east across the bay, a grey wall of rain and wind. A wave breaks over Pencroff's knees. *(Settled R1121-129.)* In the run back the bottom goes from under Harbert in a hollow scoured toward the gut; the current turns him over and draws him seaward, and Pencroff catches him, loses him, catches him again and hauls him out, and half carries him to the wall. There the lad coughs up the sea water and laughs; Pencroff does not.
- *(Settled R1121-70.)* They wait it out under the granite wall near the foot of the ladder, where they can still see the float.
- *(Settled R1121-65, R1121-66, R1121-68.)* In the squall, what is left of the rotten mooring breaks. The surge throws the float on the islet's rocks: from under the wall, a flash in the spray, and it is gone. They know it shattered. The bar closes over the net. *(Settled R1121-119.)* They see it break, as R1121-68 has it: the flash flies apart, seen across the channel, not up close.
- *(Settled R1121-110.)* The path, in one clause: the backwash draws the freed float off the bar into the gut, and the channel's current, "un courant extrêmement rapide" (I.3 ¶42), takes it onto the islet's point. The wind is onshore, so the sea alone would drive it to the coast.
- *(Settled R1121-129.)* Before the cord gives, the sand they dug from about the ball no longer holds it, it rocks under each sea, and the cord the knife worked on, if not cut through, has been weakened.

### 6. Home, wet (2 June, early afternoon)

- *(Settled R1121-71.)* Home by early afternoon, once the flood has covered the bar and the squall eases.

- *(Settled R1121-56.)* Pencroff makes a joke of the soaking.
- *(Settled R1121-59.)* The joke: "The lines took nothing, and the sea took the pair of us."
- *(Proposal, from the draft, [01950](../prose/01950-the-float-in-the-bar.md).)* The joke answers Spilett, who asks what the sea has given them. Nab laughs and makes up the fire, the engineer smiles, and the two dry their clothes on their backs at the hearth, as sailors do.
- *(Proposal.)* The joke is all they tell. It is true, and it is less than the whole.
- *(Settled R1121-134, R1121-135.)* Before they climb, at the foot of the ladder, Harbert stops: "It was nothing, Pencroff. And no one need know that I took a ducking." He spares the sailor; that is his own reason. Pencroff looks at him and says nothing, and they go up. Pencroff's guilt is shown and never told: he does not laugh under the wall, and on 3 June he keeps the lad on his landward side. The fire paragraph of the final (R1121-130, -131) and the pride of R1121-76 are gone.

### 7. The smooth sand (3 June, mid-morning low water)

- *(Proposal, from the draft, [01950](../prose/01950-the-float-in-the-bar.md).)* The pretext is the lines once more; they go out by the lines, and the chapter ends as they go back across the sand to them.
- *(Settled R1121-129, R1121-134.)* This time Pencroff keeps the lad on his landward side and does not take his eyes off the gut: the guilt shown, never told.
- *(Settled R1121-23, R1121-65.)* The bar has shifted. There is nothing to find, not even the net. *(Settled R1121-68.)* No one ever finds a piece of the glass.
- *(Settled R1121-57.)* Pencroff, at the smooth sand: "Five of us and the picks, and we'd have had it."
- *(Settled R1121-123, R1121-134.)* Harbert: "Yes," answered Herbert; "and it lay under our hands the whole time the sea was out." Then the sailor shakes himself, as Top does coming out of the water, and says to the lad: "Bah! The sea has taken, and the sea will give us something else." His hand on the lad's shoulder, they go back to the lines. The narrator does not look ahead (R1121-91).
- *(Settled R1121-34.)* The grain of wheat later that month (I.20 ¶34) is the pair. The chapter does not point to it.

## After

- *(Settled R1121-79, R1121-134, R1121-135.)* Afterward the two never bring it up, with no pact between them: Harbert to spare the sailor, Pencroff from what the day cost the boy. It is never spoken of indoors, through III.16, and the other three never learn of it. The page names no reason; at the chest and after it says nothing (R1121-24).
