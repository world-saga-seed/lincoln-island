# The Lens Fire and the Ascent (notes)

## Notes for drafting

- **Where it sits.** The French I.10. It opens on the afternoon of 28 March, when the hunters return to a fire made by Cyrus's lens, and runs through the climb of 29 March to the moment Cyrus sees the sea from the summit at night. *(Drafted.)*
- **Cast.** *(Drafted.)* Pencroff (¶1), Cyrus Smith (¶1), Gédéon Spilett, "le reporter" (¶1), Harbert (¶6), Nab (¶12), Top (¶18).
- **Places.** *(Drafted.)*
  - The Chimneys with the hearth and rebuilt partitions (¶1, ¶13); the plateau above the Chimneys where Cyrus climbed (¶14).
  - The river's left bank and the bend to the south-west, the forest's western edge (¶20); the slope rising inland (¶21).
  - The mountain "à six milles environ dans le nord-ouest" (¶14), of two cones (¶22–¶23); the buttresses (¶22), the midday halt with a cascading stream (¶30), the tragopan copse (¶32), the hundred-foot bank (¶33), the mouflons' ground (¶35), the solfataras (¶42).
  - The first plateau, the camp among rocks (¶44–¶45); the circular base of the upper cone (¶47); the breach, "l’égueulement" (¶51); the crater's inner slopes (¶53); the summit (¶56).
- **Things.** *(Drafted.)*
  - Two watch glasses, water and clay, forming a lens (¶8); the glasses then put back in the watches (¶19).
  - Burnt linen as tinder (¶19, ¶45); flint, "silex" (¶19, ¶45); a "briquet" (¶45).
  - The capybara on a spit (¶12); the tragopan, killed with a thrown stone and kept uncooked (¶32, ¶46); sargassum and pine almonds (¶15, ¶46).
  - Sticks as staffs (¶20).
- **Time.** *(Drafted.)*
  - "Le lendemain, — 29 mars" (¶18).
  - 29 March: departure at 7:30 a.m. (¶20); the forest's western edge at 9 (¶20); halt at 10 (¶22); lunch at noon (¶30); resumption at 1 (¶32); the last trees at about 4 (¶43); at the plateau "presque nuit" after a seven-hour ascent (¶44); supper done before 6:30 (¶46); twenty minutes' walk (¶49); the summit at nearly 8 (¶56).
  - *(My reading, to confirm.)* The opening scene is the afternoon of 28 March, because I.9 ¶117 puts the hunters at the river bend about 2:30 and ¶1 follows "quelques instants après". The chapter covers from about 3 p.m. on 28 March to about 8 p.m. on 29 March.
- **Clues.** *(Drafted.)*
  - Tracks of large animals on the lower slopes.
    - quote: Harbert fit remarquer des empreintes qui indiquaient le passage récent de grands animaux, fauves ou autres.
  - The mouflons seem never to have seen men.
    - quote: comme s’ils voyaient pour la première fois des bipèdes humains
  - The crater is in a "complete extinction", with the breach on the north.
    - quote: c’était sa complète extinction
  - The moon's reflection on a liquid surface to the west, the first sign of the sea.
    - quote: son image tremblotante se refléter un instant sur une surface liquide
  - The southern stars: the sky's constellations are given, a rough indication of latitude and hemisphere.
    - quote: Les magnifiques constellations de ce ciel austral resplendissaient.
- **Length.** *(Drafted.)* 61 paragraphs (¶1–¶61, plus the summary ¶0), 3,209 words in the ¶ lines. No footnotes; two plate captions (pl1 after ¶32, pl2 after ¶61).

## Open and flags

- **The pine nut's name.** I.10 ¶15 reads "pin pignon"; I.9 ¶97 read "pin pigeon". Card neither.
  - quote: les amandes de pin pignon complétèrent ce repas
- **Direction of the mountain.** I.10 ¶14 says north-west; I.9 ¶60 had "dans l’ouest". Card neither.
  - quote: Le mont, situé à six milles environ dans le nord-ouest
- **The altitude of the plateau.** ¶22 gives the first cone's truncation at about 2,500 feet; ¶43 speaks of "trois mille pieds"; ¶44 puts five hundred feet still below the plateau. *(My reading, to confirm.)* ¶43's three thousand feet is a conditional about a strong breeze, not a measure of where they stand; the numbers do not agree on their face.
  - quote: tronqué à une hauteur de deux mille cinq cents pieds environ
  - quote: une violente brise, à une altitude de trois mille pieds, eût gêné leurs évolutions
  - quote: Cinq cents pieds seulement séparaient alors les explorateurs du plateau
- **The "briquet".** I.9 ¶21 says they had no steel, "le morceau d’acier dans le briquet usuel"; I.10 ¶45 says "Le briquet fut battu". The text does not say what served as steel. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: n’avaient pas la consistance de celles qui s’échappent du morceau d’acier dans le briquet usuel
  - quote: Le briquet fut battu, le linge brûlé recueillit les étincelles du silex
- **The seven-hour ascent.** ¶44 counts seven hours of climbing; from the forest's edge at 9 (¶20) to nightfall is not seven hours of clock time. *(My reading, to confirm.)* The text may count from 7:30 with the halts removed or loosely; no arrival time is given beyond "presque nuit".
  - quote: très-fatigués par une ascension de sept heures
- **Time of the supper.** ¶46 says all was over before half past six, yet ¶44 says it was almost night on arrival. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Il n’était pas encore six heures et demie que tout était terminé.
  - quote: il était presque nuit
- **Stars.** The text puts Antares "au zénith". *(Drafted.)* It is given without a latitude and is not checked against the island's latitude in a later chapter.
  - quote: Au zénith, brillaient d’un pur éclat la splendide Antarès du Scorpion
- **Seam to I.11.** ¶61 ends with "Une île !" at the summit about 8 p.m.; I.11 ¶1 opens half an hour later at camp. The seam holds. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: « Une île ! » dit-il, au moment où le croissant lunaire s’éteignait dans les flots.
- **Seam from I.9.** I.9 ends with smoke over the rocks; I.10 opens with the hearth lit by the sun. The seam holds. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
- No fact in this chapter rests on a Wikisource correction listed in texts/README.md. *(Drafted.)*
