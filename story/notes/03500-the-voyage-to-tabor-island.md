# The voyage to Tabor Island (notes)

## Notes for drafting

- **Where it sits.** The French II.13, from the evening of 10 October to the evening of 13 October 1866 (dates my reading from II.12 ¶87 and this chapter's days). It opens on the bottle's message (II.12 ¶131) and ends on the empty dwelling on Tabor. *(Drafted.)*
- **Cast.**
  - Pencroff (¶1), Cyrus Smith, "the engineer" (¶2), Gédéon Spilett (¶7), Harbert (¶8). *(Drafted.)*
  - Nab (¶21): stays at Granite House and waves from the heights with Smith. *(Drafted.)*
  - The castaway: unnamed, unseen, "a single castaway" (¶76). *(Drafted.)*
  - Top and Jup do not appear in this chapter. *(Drafted.)*
- **Places.**
  - Tabor island: low, hardly out of the waves (¶44); a cone of 250 to 300 feet at half a mile from the landing (¶52); six miles round, an elongated oval (¶58); wooded, two or three low hills, a stream crossing a meadow to the west coast (¶59). *(Drafted.)*
  - Cape Claw (¶15), the mouth of the Mercy (¶16), Granite House (¶21), Reptile End (¶25), Mount Franklin (¶24). *(Drafted.)*
  - Landing: a sandy beach, about noon (¶50). South point, west coast (¶68). The dwelling in the wood (¶92). *(Drafted.)*
- **Things.**
  - The document (¶5–¶6): in English, latitude and longitude matching the colonists' own to a minute. *(Drafted.)*
  - The box, "the box", said to be explained by a castaway (¶7). *(Drafted.)*
  - Stores: bedding, utensils, arms, ammunition, a compass, provisions for about eight days (¶19). *(Drafted.)*
  - The topsail (¶26); the telescope (¶49); a mooring for the ebb (¶52). *(Drafted.)*
  - Goats and pigs of European kind (¶73); old clearings with potatoes, chicory, sorrel, carrots, cabbages and turnips (¶80). *(Drafted.)*
  - The dwelling: planks under thick tarred cloth, door half closed (¶92–¶93). *(Drafted.)*
- **Time.**
  - 10 October, about four in the afternoon: anchors at the Mercy mouth (¶16); that evening the plan is made and the boat loaded (¶17, ¶19). *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - 11 October, 5 a.m.: departure (¶20). About 1 p.m.: Reptile End passed, ten miles out; about 4 p.m.: Lincoln lost to sight (¶25). Evening: the crescent moon (¶29). *(Drafted.)*
  - 12 October: a day at sea (¶32); by evening about 120 miles in 36 hours (¶39). Night of 12 to 13: no one sleeps (¶40). *(Drafted.)*
  - 13 October: 6 a.m. land (¶41); 11 a.m. two miles off (¶46); about noon the landing (¶50); an hour to the south point (¶68); four hours round the islet (¶69); a quick dinner on board; 5 p.m. under the trees (¶72); nightfall and twilight (¶88, ¶92). *(Drafted.)*
  - Planned return on the 17th (¶17); first quarter of the moon on the 16th (¶29). *(Drafted.)*
  - The chapter covers about three days. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
- **Clues.**
  - The bottle: Smith asks "And does that not seem strange to you?" (¶9) and "and yet…" (¶11). *(Drafted.)*
  - Spilett: "the document appears to have been written recently" (¶13), and Smith's "we shall know" (¶14). *(Drafted.)*
  - The castaway is to be an educated seaman (¶6). Spilett's link of the document to the arrival of the box (¶7). *(Drafted.)*
  - The empty dwelling, tarred cloth, planks: whose it was is not told (¶92–¶94). *(Drafted.)*
  - Old paths and rotted trunks "overturned many years ago" (¶74); cultivation long abandoned (¶82–¶83). *(Drafted.)*
  - Seals that fear men (¶67). *(Drafted.)*

## Open and flags

- **Recent or old.** Spilett finds the document recently written; later the colonists conclude it is old. Card neither. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le document paraît avoir été récemment écrit
  - quote: Il faut donc admettre que le document a une date déjà ancienne ?
- **Distance.** ¶1 puts the castaway "a few hundred miles from us"; ¶17 says "a hundred and fifty miles". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: abandonné à quelques cents milles de nous
  - quote: cette traversée de cent cinquante milles
- **Hours of the crossing.** ¶17 says the crossing needs no more than forty-eight hours from a start on 11 October; the landing is at noon on the 13th (¶50), about fifty-five hours after the 5 a.m. departure (¶20). *(My reading, to confirm.)* Card neither. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il ne fallait pas plus de quarante-huit heures pour faire cette traversée de cent cinquante milles
  - quote: Enfin, à midi à peu près, le Bonadventure vint heurter de son étrave une grève de sable.
- **Fifteen months.** Spilett says "first separation in fifteen months" (¶22); the chapter's own dates (second winter, II.12 ¶15; "the winter of 1866-67", II.11 ¶9) set the day at 11 October 1866. Card neither; earlier chapters were not given to me. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Voilà notre première séparation depuis quinze mois !…
- **The language of the document.** ¶6 says the note is in English; the print in II.12 ln3 is in French. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: puisque le document est écrit en langue anglaise
- **Where the castaway is.** The document names the island; the island is thought to have no sister island: ¶51 "there was no other island in that part of the Pacific". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il n’existait aucune autre île sur cette portion du Pacifique
- **Moon.** ¶29 says the evening of 11 October has a crescent moon that sets quickly, with first quarter on the 16th. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qui ne devait être dans son premier quartier que le 16
- **Wikisource corrections.** None of the three that change a fact falls here. I was not given the places of the other five. *(Drafted.)*
