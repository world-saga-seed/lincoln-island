# II.6: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller i. Beads corrected: none.*

### 1. changed: a search for "the broken part" of the ladder (II.6 ¶1; White ¶1)
- The French has them search the wall in case the wind moved the ladder and the ground in case it had come loose; White has them search for "the broken part of the ladder", torn off by wind. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: pour le cas où elle se fût détachée
  - white: for the broken part of the ladder, supposing it had been torn off by the wind

### 2. softened: Pencroff swears (II.6 ¶9; White ¶9)
- The French has Pencroff answer swearing, impatience beginning to win; White has him merely getting angry. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: répondit Pencroff en jurant, car l’impatience commençait à le gagner
  - white: replied Pencroff, beginning to get angry

### 3. cut: chance or a human hand (II.6 ¶17; White ¶17)
- The French says they were anxious whether the event was chance with natural causes to be seen by day, or the work of a human being; White gives only the uncomfortable beds and the occupied house. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: soit qu’il fût le résultat d’un hasard dont les causes naturelles leur apparaîtraient au jour, soit, au contraire, qu’il fût l’œuvre d’un être humain
  - white: Not only were their beds uncomfortable, but it was certain that their house was occupied at present

### 4. condensed: what Granite House holds (II.6 ¶18; White ¶17)
- The French lists arms, instruments, tools, munitions and stores of food, and says pillage would force them to remake arms and tools; White says "Everything they possessed". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: armes, instruments, outils, munitions, réserves de vivres
  - white: Everything they possessed was stored there.

### 5. changed: Harbert would have liked to question Smith (II.6 ¶18; White ¶17)
- The French says Harbert, not knowing what to imagine, would have liked to question Smith; White says he often questioned him. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: eût aimé à interroger Cyrus Smith
  - white: and often questioned Smith

### 6. softened: Nab is no longer "the brave negro" (II.6 ¶18; White ¶17)
- The French calls Nab "le brave nègre"; White calls him "the brave fellow". *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: le brave nègre aurait dormi cette nuit-là
  - white: the brave fellow would have slept this night

### 7. changed: the speaker of "I don't see how we get in" (II.6 ¶48; White ¶37)
- In the French Pencroff answers Nab's praise of the beast; White makes the reporter answer, so the reporter speaks twice running. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: répondit Pencroff, mais je ne vois pas encore comment nous pourrons rentrer chez nous
  - white: answered the reporter, “but I don’t see yet how we are going to get in!”

### 8. changed: three or four times becomes two or three (II.6 ¶55; White ¶44)
- The French has a muzzle or paw show three or four times in the two hours; White says two or three. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: trois ou quatre fois, un museau ou une patte se glissèrent par la porte ou les fenêtres
  - white: two or three times, a muzzle or paw slipped by the door or the windows

### 9. softened: Pencroff's oath (II.6 ¶66; White ¶54)
- The French has "mille et mille diables"; White has "Why in the mischief". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Ah ! mille et mille diables ! s’écria le marin.
  - white: “Why in the mischief didn’t I think of that!” cried the sailor.

### 10. changed: "Too much" becomes "Too lucky" (II.6 ¶82–¶83; White ¶66–¶67)
- Pencroff's "Voilà qui est fort" and the engineer's "Trop fort" mean that it is too much to believe; White has "lucky" and "Too lucky", which turns the engineer's unease into luck. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: Trop fort ! murmura l’ingénieur
  - white: “Too lucky!” muttered Smith

### 11. changed: "sagouins" becomes "sojourners" (II.6 ¶84; White ¶68)
- Pencroff fears a few more of those sagouins (small monkeys) inside; White has "sojourners". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: s’il y a encore quelques-uns de ces sagouins
  - white: if there should be any sojourners

### 12. changed: the orang's lack of faults is reassigned (II.6 ¶100; White ¶81)
- The French says the orang has neither the baboon's ferocity, the macaque's thoughtlessness nor the sagouin's uncleanliness; White gives the ferocity to "the baboon nor the macauco" and the thoughtlessness to the sagoin, and drops the uncleanliness. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ni la férocité du babouin, ni l’irréflexion du macaque, ni la malpropreté du sagouin
  - white: neither the ferocity of the baboon nor the macauco, nor the thoughtlessness of the sagoin

### 13. condensed: the facial angle of sixty-five degrees (II.6 ¶101; White ¶82)
- The French gives the angle as sixty-five degrees; White says "a sharp facial angle". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: angle facial atteignant soixante-cinq degrés
  - white: with a sharp facial angle

### 14. changed: the service to come becomes a service done (II.6 ¶116; White ¶96)
- The French says the new member would render more than one service; White says he "had already done them a service". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: d’un nouveau membre, qui devait lui rendre plus d’un service
  - white: one who had already done them a service

## Outline slips

- Beat 4 of the outline makes the hibiscus cord "some twenty fathoms"; the French has several twenties of fathoms (quelques vingtaines), which is more. *(Drafted.)*
  - outline: with some twenty fathoms of light hibiscus cord
  - quote: quelques vingtaines de brasses d’une légère corde d’hibiscus
