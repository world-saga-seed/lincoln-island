1. **Why does Pencroff tell only Herbert about what he has seen?**
* **Answer:** He doesn't want to risk mortifying himself in front of Cyrus Smith, Gideon Spilett, and Neb if the object turns out to be something worthless like a round stone or a rotten gourd, nor does he want to provoke questions by taking tools, so he takes only Herbert as an extra pair of hands to help secure the discovery before presenting it to the rest.
* **Line:** "To go back up the ladder and announce to Cyrus Smith, Gideon Spilett, and the rest that he had spied something strange on the bar, only to find, when they had all come down in expectation, that it was nothing better than a round stone or a rotten gourd, would have mortified him beyond measure." / "...to have carried a pick or a spade out of the storehouse at that hour would have provoked a dozen questions from the engineer and the reporter, and Pencroff was determined that whatever lay on the bar should be his own discovery first."


2. **Why can't the two of them free the object?**
* **Answer:** The glass float is bound to an old, heavy net buried deep beneath the packed sand of the bar, and they brought no digging tools with them—only bare hands and a six-inch iron knife.
* **Line:** "To cut it free without digging away half a ton of wet sand was manifestly impossible with a six-inch blade." / "Two of them, with nothing but a short iron knife and their bare hands, were helpless against the packed sand and the rising sea."


3. **What did keeping it to themselves cost them?**
* **Answer:** It cost them the manpower and tools (such as the five colonists and the pickaxe or spade) needed to dig the float free before the tide came in, resulting in the loss of the object.
* **Line:** "Had they possessed two picks, or even one, they might have dug the float out in twenty minutes when they first reached the bar; five men working together could have wrenched it free, net and all, before the tide turned."


4. **How is the object lost?**
* **Answer:** During a sudden squall and rising flood tide, a massive wave snaps its rotten hemp moorings and hurls the glass sphere against the black basalt rocks of Safety Islet, shattering it.
* **Line:** "A moment later, during a brief rift in the driving rain-squall, both Pencroff and Herbert saw a sharp, bright flash of green light leap up amidst the white spray against the black basalt rocks of Safety Islet—a single, brilliant sparkle as the glass sphere was hurled against the reef..."


5. **Whose was the object?**
* **Answer:** The chapter does not say.
* **Guess:** Based on Pencroff’s comment about glass floats being used by "the Norwegians and the Portuguese," and the Latin letters on the seal, it likely belonged to an unknown fisherman or seafarer from a European vessel (or, knowing the broader context of Verne's novel, could be connected to Captain Nemo's *Nautilus*, whose motto *Mobilis in mobili* contains the letters B, L, and I).


6. **Where had it come from, and how long had it been in the sea?**
* **Answer:** The chapter does not say specifically where it came from or the exact number of years it was in the sea, only that it drifted across the open Pacific from "some unknown shore thousands of miles away" and had been in the sea "for years" (with Pencroff casually guessing "ten years").
* **Line:** "It must have been drifting in the sea for years, Pencroff, and come from a great distance." / "...it was a waif of the wide, empty Pacific, drifted from some unknown shore thousands of miles away."
* **Guess:** Given the Pacific currents and the Latin inscription, it may have come from a deep-sea vessel or submarine operating in the Pacific Ocean several years prior to the colonists' arrival on Lincoln Island.


7. **What did the letters on it say?**
* **Answer:** The three visible capital letters on the broken seal were **B**, **L**, and **I** (with a pitted space between the B and the L), which Herbert suspected were part of a Latin inscription.
* **Line:** "“There is a B,” he said slowly, “and then a space where the glass is pitted—then an L—and an I.”" / "“Latin, I think,” he said simply."


8. **Is there a ship near the island?**
* **Answer:** No; Herbert’s examination of the dead deep-water barnacles proves the float had been drifting in the empty ocean for years rather than coming from a nearby or recently wrecked ship.
* **Line:** "Herbert’s quiet observation, spoken without emphasis, dimmed that hope at once. This globe was no relic of yesterday’s storm; it was a waif of the wide, empty Pacific, drifted from some unknown shore thousands of miles away."


9. **Did someone or something cause its loss, other than the weather and the sea?**
* **Answer:** Only Pencroff and Herbert's own delay in lingering to scrape and decipher the letters during the hour of low tide—combined with their decision not to bring tools or the other men—contributed to its loss; no third person or external force was involved.
* **Line:** "They lingered over it nevertheless, turning the three letters over in their minds, scraping a little further to the left and right in the vain hope of uncovering another character, until nearly an hour had slipped away—the whole precious hour of dead low water." / "Even now, though the float was gone, Pencroff could not bring himself to speak of a prize he had spotted and lost through his own delay."


10. **What would the object have been worth to them, had they kept it?**
* **Answer:** Pencroff thought the thick green glass could either be set into a hall shutter as a window pane to let in daylight during winter storms, or be re-slung in fresh cordage to serve as a durable buoy for his deep-water fishing lines.
* **Line:** "...even a single pane of curved green bottle-glass let into the hall shutter would admit the daylight during the long winter storms! Or, better still, freed from its rotten net and slung in fresh cordage, what a buoy it would make for his deep-water lines off the capes..."


11. **What do Pencroff and Herbert tell the others?**
* **Answer:** Pencroff tells Gideon Spilett only that they went down to check the fishing lines, caught nothing, and got soaked by the sea; neither he nor Herbert says a word about the sandbar or the glass float.
* **Line:** "“And so we did, Mr. Spilett,” answered the sailor, shaking the water from his cap with a forced laugh, “and a fine morning’s work we made of it! The lines took nothing, and the sea took the pair of us.”"


12. **Will either of them ever mention it again? How do you know?**
* **Answer:** No, neither of them will ever speak of it again to anyone—not even to each other—because the narrator explicitly states this in the final sentence of the chapter.
* **Line:** "And from that day forward, neither the man nor the boy ever spoke of what they had found on the sandbar—not to Cyrus Smith, not to Spilett, not to Neb, nor even to one another."
