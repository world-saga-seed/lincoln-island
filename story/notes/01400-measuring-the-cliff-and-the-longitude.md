# Measuring the Cliff and the Longitude (notes)

## Notes for drafting

- **Where it sits.** The French I.14, the whole of Easter Sunday, 16 April. It finishes the latitude (the granite wall measured by similar triangles), then a walk north with the oyster bed and the talk of guns and the balloon, and ends with the noon shadow, the longitude, and the conclusion that the island is far from all land. *(Drafted.)*
- **Cast.** *(Drafted.)* Cyrus Smith (¶1), Harbert (¶4), Pencroff (¶6), Nab (¶6), Gédéon Spilett, "the reporter" (¶6), Top (not named in this chapter). Jonathan Forster and his companions are named by Pencroff (¶47); "the Southerners" (¶51).
- **Places.**
  - The Chimneys (¶1, ¶21); the beach and the granite wall, 500 feet from the stake (¶8, ¶20). *(Drafted.)*
  - The canal's edge, the Salvation Islet opposite (¶28); the oyster bed about four miles from the Chimneys (¶30). *(Drafted.)*
  - The dunes and the barren shore up to South Mandible Cape, the point closing Union Bay (¶38). *(Drafted.)*
  - The halt six miles from the Chimneys, near the part of the dunes where the engineer was found, with a brook close by (¶65). *(Drafted.)*
  - Far places in speech: New Zealand, Chile, the Pomotou archipelago, Tahiti (¶54–¶56, ¶79). *(Drafted.)*
  - Washington (¶70, ¶74–¶75). *(Drafted.)*
- **Things.**
  - A twelve-foot pole (ten feet above the sand), a stone plumb line, a stake (¶7–¶9, ¶18). *(Drafted.)*
  - A slate stone scratched with a shell, for the sum (¶22). *(Drafted.)*
  - The wooden compass of I.13, used again for the angle (¶25). *(Drafted.)*
  - A hibiscus-fibre net, Nab's, holding the food and oysters (¶36). *(Drafted.)*
  - A six-foot rod, tilted south, wooden pegs, the reporter's chronometer (¶66–¶70). *(Drafted.)*
  - Measures given: wall 333 feet, English feet of 30 cm (¶24, n1); angle 53 degrees; latitude 37° S (¶25); longitude 152° W (¶75); Washington 77° 3′ 11″ (¶75); reading 5 h 01 (¶73). *(Drafted.)*

## Open and flags

- **The latitude's bounds.** ¶25 gives "between the thirty-fifth and fortieth parallels", an error of five degrees. ¶76 gives "between the thirty-fifth and thirty-seventh parallels". Card neither. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: entre le trente-cinquième et le quarantième parallèle
  - quote: entre le trente-cinquième et le trente-septième parallèle
- **Five degrees against the bounds.** ¶77 says the error is "five degrees either way". The stated limits (35–37 in latitude; 150–155 in longitude) are not five degrees either side of 37 and 152. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: L’écart possible qu’il attribuait aux erreurs d’observation était, on le voit, de cinq degrés dans les deux sens
- **"Washington" or Richmond time.** ¶70 says the reporter's watch gives "the true time it would then be at Washington"; I.12 ¶7–¶11 says it keeps Richmond time, "nearly" Washington's meridian. The chapter's arithmetic uses Washington's longitude (¶75). *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: l’heure donnée par Gédéon Spilett serait l’heure vraie qu’il serait alors à Washington
- **The islet.** ¶28–¶29 call it "l’îlot du Salut" with birds and seals, and say nothing of the party having been there. I.15 ¶16 says the balloon threw them on the islet first. Not a conflict inside this chapter; recorded because the earlier chapters were not given to me. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
- **Distances to the oyster bed, the halt and the cape.** ¶30 "about four miles from the Chimneys" (oysters); ¶65 "at a distance of six miles from the Chimneys" (halt); I.15 ¶5 "nearly seven miles from the Chimneys" (the cape). Consistent in order; no conflict. *(Drafted.)*
- **Distances to land.** ¶54 says the gap between New Zealand and Chile is "at least six thousand miles"; ¶79 gives over 1,800 miles to New Zealand and over 4,500 to the American coast, together 6,300. The Pomotou are "more than twelve hundred miles" (¶56) and "at least twelve hundred miles from Tahiti and the islands of the Pomotou archipelago" (¶79). *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: la distance est au moins de six mille milles
- **Wikisource corrections and scan notes.** None of the three that change a fact are in this chapter. The scan check (p. 126, ¶22–¶25, the table, n1) found one slip: the print's "très-exactement" in ¶25 lost its hyphen on Wikisource. Nothing here rests on it. *(Drafted.)*
- **The proportion's table.** ¶23 is a table line (`15 : 500 : : 10 : x | 500 × 10 = 5000 | 5000 | ――― | 15 = 333,33.`). The result is correct for the numbers given. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
