# Report, round 1

## A1 (III.14 ¶30–¶41)

- "la Nouvelle-Bretagne" (¶30): rendered "New Britain", the period English name for British North America. It is not the Melanesian island. I kept the name rather than putting "Canada", which would be a gloss.
- "les contrées méditerranéennes" (¶31): rendered "inland countries". Verne means lands in the middle of a continent, set against "les îles et les régions du littoral". "Mediterranean countries" would mislead an English reader, and Lombardy against Scotland bears out "inland".
- "glaces ou de banquises" (¶36), "la banquise" (¶37): rendered "ice or of ice-packs" and "the ice-pack". "Ice-fields" was the other choice.
- "son apogée … son périgée" (¶39): kept as "apogee … perigee", as Verne has them, though "aphelion … perihelion" are the correct terms for the earth and the sun. "La terre occupe un des foyers de l'ellipse" is also kept as written ("The earth occupies one of the foci"), though it is the sun that stands at the focus. Both are Verne's own errors, and I left them in on purpose.
- "si mal partagé" (¶38): rendered "so badly served in the sharing". "Ce n'est pas juste, cela !" became "That is not just!", which drops the trailing "cela". That is a small departure.
- "en fronçant le sourcil" (¶38): rendered "knitting his brows".
- ¶41: the French has no exclamation mark ("ce qu'on ne sait pas, »"), and the English has none either.

## A4 (III.16 ¶27, ¶38–¶50, ¶62)

- "mis peut-être au ban de l'humanité" (¶27): rendered "put perhaps under the ban of humanity", which keeps Verne's figure rather than "an outlaw".
- "dès l'âge de dix ans" (¶38): rendered "from the age of ten years".
- "supérieurement doué" (¶39): rendered "endowed in a superior degree".
- "dont le Royaume-Uni n'a pu que nominalement assurer la servitude" (¶42): rendered "whose servitude the United Kingdom has been able to secure only in name". I kept Verne's present perfect "has", not "had".
- "élevé dans les idées de revendication et de vengeance" (¶42): rendered "ideas of reclamation and of vengeance". "Revendication" means a claim to recover what is one's own. "Reclamation" is the nearest single English word of the period, but it is not a perfect fit.
- "ayant l'inéluctable amour de son poétique pays chargé des chaînes anglaises" (¶42): rendered "bearing within him an ineluctable love for his poetic country, laden with English chains". "Bearing within him" is my phrasing for "ayant". "Laden" agrees with "country", as "chargé" agrees with "pays".
- "Il n'en était rien." (¶44): rendered "It was nothing of the kind."
- "Aussi, … revint-il" (¶45): rendered "Thus it was that … returned".
- "Le prince Dakkar emprunta la voix des mécontents." (¶46): rendered literally, "borrowed the voice of the discontented". The sense is unclear: he either spoke through them or spoke for them. I kept Verne's words and did not resolve it.
- "populations indoues" (¶46): rendered "Hindoo populations", the 1876 spelling.
- "Il paya de sa personne" (¶47): rendered "He spared not his own person", which is the idiom's sense. A literal "paid with his person" would not read as English.
- "c'en était fait peut-être en Asie de l'influence et de la domination" (¶48): rendered "it would perhaps have been all over in Asia with the influence and the dominion".
- "s'il ne se rencontra pas un traître" (¶49): rendered "though there was not found a traitor". I used "though" for the concessive "si".
- "la civilisation ne recule jamais, et il semble qu'elle emprunte tous les droits à la nécessité" (¶50): rendered "civilization never recedes, and it seems as though it borrowed every right from necessity". Verne's force is kept and nothing is softened.
- "Un bon mouvement entraîna le capitaine…" (¶62): rendered "A good impulse carried the captain away . . .", which keeps Verne's ellipsis. The ellipsis at the end of ¶49 is kept too.
- "prisonniers des sudistes" (¶62): rendered "the prisoners of the Southerners".
- "Tippo-Saïb" is spelled as the French spells it.

## A6 (I.15 ¶26)

- "machine soufflante" rendered "blowing-machine", which is literal. It means a bellows, and the context gives "soufflets de forge".
- "ces amphibies" rendered "these amphibious animals".

## S1 (I.4 ¶9)

- "labbes, sortes de goëlands auxquels on donne quelquefois le nom de stercoraires": rendered "skuas, a sort of gull to which the name of stercoraries is sometimes given". "Stercoraries" anglicizes Stercorarius, the skua genus. "Mouettes" became "sea-mews", to keep them apart from "goëlands" (gulls).
- "palmipèdes" kept as "palmipedes", Verne's technical term, rather than "web-footed birds".
- "que le flot n'avait jamais rongé": rendered "which the waves had never eaten away".

## S2 (I.6 ¶41 and n1)

- "tétras": rendered "grouse", since the rule asks for English where the French gives a common noun. The chapter elsewhere (¶38, outside this passage) discusses the name "tétras" as used in the United States. If the book keeps "tétras" there, this paragraph may need to match.
- "Isaac Walton": the French spelling, not "Izaak", is kept.
- The note is marked "[n1]" at its place in the sentence and given at the paragraph's end. "La pêche à la ligne" became "angling".
- "lignes à collets": rendered "snare-lines".
- "engins singuliers": rendered "singular tackle".
- "lianes": rendered "creepers".

## S3 (I.17 ¶49)

- "d'une disposition particulière": rendered "of a particular arrangement".
- "les plus adroits ouvriers du monde": rendered "the most adroit workmen in the world".

## S4 (I.22 ¶41)

- "(22°, 22 centigr. au-dessous de glace)": rendered "(22°.22 centigrade below freezing-point)". This gives the figure as 22.22 degrees, as the French comma decimal means. It is not 22 degrees 22 minutes.
- "le vent sauta au sud-est, et, subitement, le froid devint": "subitement" is kept with the cold, not the wind.
- "une bise aiguë": rendered "a piercing wind". "Bise" usually means a cold north wind, but the wind here is south-east, so I did not write "north wind".
- "le strict passage au renouvellement de l'air": rendered "the strict passage needed for renewing the air". "Needed" is implied by the French, not stated in it.
- "les colons ne s'éclairèrent souvent qu'avec la flamme des foyers": rendered "the colonists often had no other light than the flame of the hearths".
- "aux bâtons de l'échelle" … "les échelons": rendered "the rounds of the ladder" … "the rungs", to keep Verne's two words distinct.

## S5 (II.2 ¶14)

- "très-différents de la courtine, qui formaient le plateau de Grande-Vue": the plural "formaient" leaves it unclear what forms the plateau, the curtain or the granites. I rendered it "the curtain which formed the plateau of Grande-Vue", following the card lines.
- "Grande-Vue" is kept as the French names it.
- "La muraille allait en s'abaissant": rendered "The wall went on lowering".
- "tombereau": rendered "cart-load". A tombereau is a tip-cart.
- "Le canot" rendered "The boat".

## S6 (II.3 ¶70)

- "Les divers rios": rendered "The various streams". Verne's Spanish word is not kept. That is a departure if the Spanish colour is wanted.
- "mais alors ce n'était plus qu'un ruisseau": "alors" (at that point, there) is rendered "here".
- "taillis": rendered "thickets".
