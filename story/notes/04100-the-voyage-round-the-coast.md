# The voyage round the coast (notes)

## Notes for drafting

- **Where it sits.** The French II.19; follows 04000's 26 March 1867 and runs from early April to the afternoon of 18 April 1867 at sea off the Shark Gulf. *(Drafted.)*
- **Cast.**
  - Gédéon Spilett (¶6), Harbert (¶7), Pencroff (¶8), Nab (¶11), Cyrus Smith (¶17). *(Drafted.)*
  - Ayrton, spoken of from ¶11 and left at Granite-house (¶39); Jup, left with him (¶39); Top, aboard (¶40). *(Drafted.)*
  - Lord Glenarvan and Captain Grant, only in talk (¶16–¶17, ¶22). *(Drafted.)*
- **Places.**
  - Granite-house's hall (¶4); Tabor Island and the hut (¶22); Port Balloon (¶38); the Reptile End promontory (¶40). *(Drafted.)*
  - The west coast, the Falls River mouth (¶46), the basalt coast of eight or nine miles (¶48), the north-west marshy shore (¶52); the north shore anchorage (¶53); North Mandible Cape and the Shark Gulf (¶55, ¶58). *(Drafted.)*
- **Things.**
  - The launch Bonadventure (¶38). The "mares' tails", cirrus clouds (¶56–¶57). The barometer at 29.9 inches, 759 mm 45 (¶37). *(Drafted.)*
  - The photographic apparatus, used again on the coast (¶45). *(Drafted.)*
  - The planned notice for Tabor (¶22). *(Drafted.)*
- **Time.**
  - First week of April, the talk (¶4); in the same week a fall in the barometer and a west gale of five or six days (¶37). *(Drafted.)*
  - 16 April, departure, a day's tacking, anchor at night (¶40–¶43); 17 April, west coast; midday at the Falls River mouth (¶44, ¶46); evening anchorage on the north shore (¶53). *(Drafted.)*
  - Morning of the next day, which the text does not date, two hours' hunt, 8 a.m. under way (¶54–¶55); this is 18 April. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - 10 a.m. (¶69); full moon "aujourd’hui" (¶73); cape expected between noon and one (¶72); flood at seven p.m. (¶77). *(Drafted.)*
  - Ayrton has been with them "depuis cinq mois" (¶27); twelve years since his abandonment (¶18); the fire of the night of 19 to 20 October (¶83). *(Drafted.)*
  - The chapter's talk covers a day in early April; the voyage covers two and a half days. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
- **Clues.**
  - The basalt coast: Top's barks "had something strange about them, like the ones the dog had given at the mouth of the Granite-house well" (¶49); no cave seen (¶51). *(Drafted.)*
  - The fire on the night of 19 to 20 October, which Smith says he never lit (¶88). *(Drafted.)*
  - Smith feigns agreement ("It was a happy idea I had there!", ¶84) and says the truth in private (¶88). *(Drafted.)*
  - The question of who was to be warned of Ayrton's and the colonists' presence at Tabor (¶21–¶27). *(Drafted.)*
- **Length.** 88 paragraphs (¶1–¶88), about 3,300 words of paragraph text. Two plates (pl1 after ¶54, pl2 after ¶88). *(Drafted.)*

## Open and flags

- Pencroff's prediction (¶72): "we shall be abreast of the cape between noon and one o'clock". II.20 ¶1: "it was about six o'clock when the Bonadventure was abeam of the gulf". Card neither. *(Drafted.)*
- ¶77 Pencroff says the flood comes "until seven in the evening"; II.20 ¶1 says at about six "the ebb was making itself felt". Card neither. *(Drafted.)*
- ¶27 spells "Lord Glenarwan" (with a w); elsewhere in the chapter ¶16–¶17 "Glenarvan". This is the print's reading; it is not among Wikisource's listed corrections. *(Drafted.)*
- ¶64: Harbert says "what we saw on the south coast of the golfe du Requin", at a point when they have not yet entered the gulf. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
- ¶39 has Ayrton coming to Granite-house while the others are away; II.20 ¶73 says Ayrton moved to Granite-house "towards mid-April" and II.20 ¶31 has him waiting on the beach on their return. Card neither. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
- ¶73: "pleine lune" on 18 April; III.1 ¶97 has a new moon on 17 October. The text does not state the lunar phase on the days between. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
- ¶18 and ¶27: the interval, "douze ans" since the abandonment, and "cinq mois" with the colonists. *(Drafted.)*
- No Wikisource correction touches this chapter. *(Drafted.)*
