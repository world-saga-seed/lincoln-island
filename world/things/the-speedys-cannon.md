# The Speedy’s cannon: the four breech-loading guns of the pirate brig

*Four modern breech-loading cannon throwing balls of eight to ten pounds, trained on the island on the morning of 18 October and, after the wreck, hauled ashore by Ayrton and Pencroff.*

Keywords: canons, cannon, artillerie, guns, culasse, breech-loading, boulets, batterie, Speedy, arsenal

- **What it is.**
  - The brig carries pieces of artillery, shown by the flash and report of a shot in the night (III.1 ¶99, ¶100). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: il y avait des pièces d’artillerie à bord
  - There are four, of a calibre to throw balls of eight to ten pounds (III.2 ¶58). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: armé de quatre canons, qui devaient lancer des boulets de huit à dix livres
  - Ayrton finds by touch that they load at the breech, so that they are modern pieces, easy to use and terrible in effect (III.2 ¶58). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: ces canons se chargeaient par la culasse
    - quote: des pièces modernes, d’un emploi facile et d’un effet terrible
  - Smith’s fear is that against them the colonists have only a few guns (III.2 ¶3). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: Quelques fusils seulement
  - By day the four pieces that make up the ship’s artillery are trained on the island, ready to fire at the first signal (III.3 ¶18). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: les quatre canons composant l’artillerie du bord avaient été braqués sur l’île
  - They are sunk with the brig; Ayrton and Pencroff, diving several times to the bed of the channel before the sands swallow the heavy things, find them and bring them ashore, lightened by empty casks, between 19 and 21 October (III.4 ¶75). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: jusqu’aux quatre canons, qui, soulagés au moyen de barriques vides, purent être amenés à terre
  - They enrich the colony’s arsenal; Pencroff speaks of building a battery to command the channel and the mouth of the river (III.4 ¶76). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: l’arsenal de la colonie avait non moins gagné au sauvetage
  - Smith judges them of excellent make; being breech-loaders of forged steel, they should bear a heavy charge and so carry very far (III.5 ¶30). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: ces canons étaient de fabrication excellente

  - On Pencroff’s urging they are hoisted with tackle and cranes to the landing of Granite-house, and embrasures are cut between the windows, so that the guns thrust their muzzles through the granite wall and command the whole Union Bay, like a small Gibraltar (III.5 ¶23). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: sur les instances de Pencroff, furent hissés au moyen de caliornes et de grues jusqu’au palier même de Granite-house

    - quote: C’était comme un petit Gibraltar
  - Pencroff has cleaned, greased and polished the four since they came out of the water, the breech-plug, bolt and pressure screw included, until they shine as if on a frigate of the United States Navy (III.5 ¶33). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: le marin s’était donné la tâche de les astiquer consciencieusement

    - quote: aussi brillantes que si elles eussent été à bord d’une frégate de la marine des États-Unis
  - They are tried on 8 November, loaded with pyroxyle instead of ordinary powder, and fire a cylindro-conical projectile (see [the pyroxyle](pyroxyle.md)) (III.5 ¶24, ¶27, ¶34). *(Drafted.)*

  - The first shot, fired seaward, passes over the islet and is lost far out, at a distance that could not be exactly judged (III.5 ¶36). *(Drafted.)*
