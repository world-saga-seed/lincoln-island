# I.9: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller d. Beads corrected: none.*

### 1. changed: the north-east wind that chills the night becomes a north-west wind that had raised the temperature (I.9 ¶17; White ¶14)
- The French says the temperature, changed by a shift of the wind to the north-east, turned seriously cold; White says the northwest wind had raised the temperature before it grew cold again. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: modifiée par une saute du vent dans le nord-est, se refroidit sérieusement
  - white: which the northwest wind had raised, again became very cold

### 2. changed: "very bearable" becomes "excellent" (I.9 ¶18; White ¶15)
- The French says the castaways found the sargassum's taste very bearable; White says they agreed it was excellent. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: auxquelles ils trouvèrent un goût très-supportable
  - white: which they agreed was excellent

### 3. cut: Nab's part in the attempts at fire (I.9 ¶21–¶22; White ¶17)
- The French has Nab help Pencroff in striking the stones and in rubbing the wood; White has Pencroff alone. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Nab l’aida même dans cette opération.
  - white: The sailor, much worried, tried every possible means of procuring a fire.

### 4. changed: "after an hour" becomes "half an hour" (I.9 ¶23; White ¶17)
- The French has Pencroff in a sweat after an hour's work; White says half an hour. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Après une heure de travail
  - white: After half an hour’s work

### 5. changed: "dying of exhaustion" becomes "dying of hunger" (I.9 ¶38; White ¶32)
- The French has Smith say he was dying chiefly of exhaustion and that a little food will set it right; White has him say he is dying of hunger. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Je mourais surtout d’épuisement
  - white: I am dying of hunger

### 6. changed: "chemical" matches become "friction" matches (I.9 ¶45–¶46; White ¶39–¶40)
- The French has the matches Smith will make be chemical ones; White calls them friction matches, which is the sort Pencroff has just failed to make. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Chimiques ?
  - white: Friction matches?

### 7. changed: "perhaps deplorable" becomes "doubtless deplorable" (I.9 ¶67; White ¶60)
- The French has Smith say the situation is perhaps deplorable; White says doubtless. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: notre situation est peut-être déplorable
  - white: our situation is, doubtless, deplorable

### 8. changed: an uninhabited island becomes a barren one (I.9 ¶70; White ¶63)
- The French has Smith consider an uninhabited island of a Micronesian archipelago, which bears on the question of inhabitants asked in ¶67; White says barren. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: quelque île déserte d’un archipel micronésien
  - white: some barren island in the Micronesian Archipelago

### 9. changed: departure at ten becomes six o'clock (I.9 ¶80; White ¶72)
- The French has the hunters leave about ten in the morning, which fits the signal at two in the afternoon; White says six o'clock. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Ils partirent donc, vers dix heures du matin
  - white: So, at 6 o’clock, the party left

### 10. changed: the "pin pigeon" becomes the pistachio pine (I.9 ¶97; White ¶89)
- The French names the pin pigeon, which bears an almond; White names the pistachio pine and its nut. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: C’était le pin pigeon
  - white: It was the pistachio pine

### 11. cut: the capybara that perhaps sees men for the first time (I.9 ¶105; White ¶95)
- The French notes that the capybara does not fight and perhaps sees men for the first time, which points to an unvisited land; White has the rolling eyes and nothing more. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Peut-être voyait-il des hommes pour la première fois.
  - white: —
