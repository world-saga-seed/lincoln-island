# The barnacles: acorn and goose barnacles on the glass float

*The dead barnacles crusting the glass float, which Herbert names and reads (the inserted chapter only).*

Keywords: barnacles, anatifes, balanes, goose barnacles, stalk, Lepas, crust, shells

- **In XIX bis.** *(The inserted chapter, 01950; R1121-132.)*
  - A grey-white crust of dead acorn barnacles, cemented one against another on the glass float, thickest on its upper side; Pencroff knows them from ships' bottoms (XIX bis ¶28, 42). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: Over all its upper part lay a crust of grey-white shells, dead and empty, packed one against another, thickest on the top
    - quote: I have scraped them off many a ship's bottom.
  - Goose barnacles, as Herbert tells them: they hang by a stalk, fasten on anything afloat (driftwood, a buoy, a ship), and live only near the surface where the sea brings their food; on the float only their shells and dried stalk stumps are left (XIX bis ¶43, 45). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: that hang by a stalk
    - quote: they live only near the surface, where the sea brings them their food
    - quote: a stump of the stalk, dried hard among the others
- **Appears in:** XIX bis
