# I.17: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller f. Beads corrected: 29, 30, 31, 32 (White ¶28 renders French ¶30 and ¶31; White ¶29 renders ¶32 only in Pencroff's echo "Blow up the island?"; White ¶30 renders ¶32; White ¶31 renders ¶33 and ¶34; so French ¶30–¶31 pairs with White ¶28–¶29, ¶32 with White ¶30, ¶33–¶34 with White ¶31).*

### 1. changed: the chapter heading names limestone for pyrites (I.17 ¶0; White ¶0)
- The French heading is the use of the schistose pyrites; White's heading says "schistous limestone", though the text itself says pyrites. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Emploi des pyrites schisteuses.
  - white: THE USE OF THE SCHISTOUS LIMESTONE

### 2. condensed: the depth of the lake (I.17 ¶5; White ¶4)
- The French says the water is shallow at the beach, then the bottom sinks little by little, and the centre is probably deep; White says only that the waters deepened slowly toward the centre. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: les eaux étaient peu profondes ; mais, à partir de ce point, le fond du lac s’abaissait peu à peu
  - white: the waters deepened slowly towards the centre

### 3. changed: a marked current becomes slight (I.17 ¶10; White ¶9)
- Cyrus is surprised to find a fairly pronounced current at the spot; White has "a slight current". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: un courant assez prononcé
  - white: a slight current

### 4. changed: where the fall comes down (I.17 ¶24; White ¶22)
- The French has the reporter say the water will fall onto the beach (sur la grève); White says "at the cliff". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Et formeront une chute en tombant sur la grève
  - white: “And will make a waterfall at the cliff,”

### 5. cut: Pencroff's enthusiasm and his many trades (I.17 ¶35; White ¶32)
- The French gives Pencroff's delight at gutting the granite and creating a cascade, and his saying he will be chemist, mason or bootmaker, even "professeur de danse et de maintien"; White has only that all entered into the proposal with enthusiasm. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: même professeur de danse et de maintien
  - white: All entered into the proposal with enthusiasm.

### 6. cut: Nab and Pencroff ask nothing (I.17 ¶36; White ¶32)
- The French says they left at once without asking more explanation, so absolute was their trust in the engineer; White drops both. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: sans même demander plus d’explication. La confiance qu’ils avaient en l’ingénieur était absolue.
  - white: Neb and Pencroff were at once detailed to extract the fat from the dugong

### 7. changed: the hurdle and the river (I.17 ¶37; White ¶32)
- Cyrus, Harbert and Spilett drag the hurdle (claie) up the river; White has them "carrying the hurdle" up "the shore". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: traînant la claie et remontant la rivière
  - white: carrying the hurdle, went up the shore

### 8. cut: where such pyrites are found (I.17 ¶37; White ¶32)
- The French says these pyrites occur in the most recent transition formations; White gives the specimen only. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qui se rencontrent, en effet, dans les terrains de transition les plus récents
  - white: where were to be found the schistous pyrites of which Smith had procured a specimen

### 9. changed: which constituent is in excess (I.17 ¶39; White ¶34)
- In the French the iron sulphide alone is in excess; White's "these were in excess" makes the excess all four. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: — celui-ci en excès, —
  - white: —these were in excess,—

### 10. softened: fury becomes zeal (I.17 ¶44; White ¶39)
- The French says they put into the work more than zeal, fury (acharnement); White has "the utmost zeal". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: C’était de l’acharnement.
  - white: which they undertook with the utmost zeal

### 11. changed: lime becomes chalk (I.17 ¶45; White ¶40)
- The French treats the fat with soda or lime (chaux), which gives insoluble lime soaps; White says "chalk", and calls the soap "calcareous and useless" without the word insoluble. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il suffisait de la traiter par la soude ou la chaux
  - white: to treat it with chalk or soda

### 12. cut: either one makes soap and frees the glycerine (I.17 ¶45; White ¶40)
- The French explains that either substance, attacking the fat, makes a soap and sets the glycerine free, which is what the engineer wanted; White gives the saponifying and goes straight to the contrast of soaps. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: l’une ou l’autre de ces substances, après avoir attaqué la graisse, formerait un savon en isolant la glycérine
  - white: Chalk was not wanting, but by this treatment the soap would be calcareous and useless

### 13. cut: the seaweeds named (I.17 ¶45; White ¶40)
- The French names glasswort and ficoids with the fucus family; White has "many kinds of marine plants" and the fucaceae. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: salicornes, ficoïdes
  - white: many kinds of marine plants abounded on the shore

### 14. changed: the ashes are fused, not heated through (I.17 ¶45; White ¶40)
- The French keeps the fire going until the heat is great enough to melt the ashes; White has "the heat penetrated throughout". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: de manière que la chaleur s’élevât au point d’en fondre les cendres
  - white: so that the heat penetrated throughout

### 15. cut: the vicious circle (I.17 ¶48; White ¶43)
- The French names the vicious circle (he needs nitric acid to make saltpetre and saltpetre to make nitric acid) from which he would never have emerged; White says only that he did not have the acid. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Il y avait donc là un cercle vicieux, dont il ne fût jamais sorti.
  - white: But this acid, which was precisely what he wanted in order to complete his undertaking successfully, he did not have.

### 16. cut: the saltpetre lies in the north (I.17 ¶48; White ¶43)
- The French puts Harbert's find in the north of the island at the foot of Mount Franklin; White says only "at the foot of Mount Franklin". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dans le nord de l’île, au pied du mont Franklin
  - white: Herbert had found a vein of this mineral at the foot of Mount Franklin

### 17. cut: the most skilful workmen in the world (I.17 ¶49; White ¶44)
- The French says the four, guided by Cyrus, had become the most skilful workmen in the world, and adds that necessity is the master best listened to; White has neither. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: étaient devenus les plus adroits ouvriers du monde
  - white: —

### 18. changed: "about 18 May" becomes "the very day" (I.17 ¶49; White ¶44)
- The French has the work done about the 18th, at about the moment the chemical change ended; White has it finished on the 18th, "the very day". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Tout cela fut achevé vers le 18 mai, à peu près au moment où la transformation chimique se terminait.
  - white: All was finished on the 18th of May, the very day the chemical work was completed.

### 19. condensed: the cost of industrial sulphuric acid (I.17 ¶52; White ¶47)
- The French says industrial sulphuric acid needs a costly installation, large works, platinum apparatus and lead chambers; White says it is made "by elaborate processes". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: des appareils de platine, des chambres de plomb
  - white: In commerce this acid is manufactured in large quantities and by elaborate processes.

### 20. changed: higher concentration becomes "non-concentrated" (I.17 ¶52; White ¶47)
- The Bohemian method gives the acid at a higher degree of concentration; White says its advantage is that the acid is "non-concentrated". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: à un degré supérieur de concentration
  - white: the advantage of being non-concentrated

### 21. changed: five hundred paces become five hundred feet (I.17 ¶66; White ¶58)
- The point is five hundred paces (pas) from the coast; White gives feet, a unit Verne does not use here. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: à cinq cents pas seulement de la côte
  - white: not more than 500 feet from the coast

### 22. changed: ten litres become two gallons (I.17 ¶68; White ¶60)
- The quantity of nitro-glycerine is ten litres in the French; White gives "two gallons", a smaller measure. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ne comptait pas consacrer moins de dix litres de nitro-glycérine
  - white: not less than two gallons of nitro-glycerine

### 23. cut: the weight of the iron (I.17 ¶72; White ¶64)
- The French gives the iron mass as weighing several pounds; White has "a heavy mass of iron". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: une masse de fer pesant plusieurs livres
  - white: a heavy mass of iron

### 24. changed: more than two miles (I.17 ¶75; White ¶67)
- The colonists are more than two miles from the mine; White says "two miles away". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: à plus de deux milles de la mine
  - white: though two miles away

### 25. cut: the triple hurrah (I.17 ¶77; White ¶68)
- The French has the colonists give a triple hurrah on seeing the granite frame split; White has no cheer. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Un triple hurrah s’échappa de leurs poitrines !
  - white: —
