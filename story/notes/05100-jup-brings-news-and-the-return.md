## Open and flags
- The 29th is twice in the chapter before the 27th is told. ¶4 sets the debate "during the day of 29 November", ¶31 then tells Spilett's sortie of "27 November", and ¶48 starts "It was the 29th of November. It was seven in the morning" with Jup's arrival. The debate (¶4–¶26) speaks of eight days still to wait and the scrap (¶33–¶44) is found two days before the 29th. The text gives both dates; the order is not reconciled. Card neither. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: fut traitée à fond dans la journée du 29 novembre
  - quote: à sa seconde sortie, le 27 novembre
  - quote: On était au 29 novembre.
- The debate is placed on the 29th, the day Jup arrives at seven in the morning, though Harbert sleeps through it and the colonists then say they are still waiting a week. The two cannot both be the same morning. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: si d’ici huit jours les forces lui sont revenues, eh bien, nous verrons !
- The count from 22 November (III.8 ¶11) to "huit jours" and "the first days of December": eight days from 27 or 29 November is 5 or 7 December. ¶26 says "the first days of December". *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: aux premiers jours de décembre seulement
- Nab's note is headed "Vendredi, 6 h. matin" and the chapter dates the same morning 29 November (¶48). The weekday is not given anywhere else in my four chapters, so I did not check it against 9, 10, 11 November. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: Vendredi, 6 h. matin.
- Time of Jup's run: the note is "dated six o'clock in the morning" (¶84), Jup arrives at 7 (¶48), and the text says he "had barely taken three-quarters of an hour to cover the five miles". That leaves a quarter of an hour that the text does not account for. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: mis à peine trois quarts d’heure à franchir les cinq milles
  - quote: Il était sept heures du matin.
- The plateau fire: "a thick smoke" over the mill, stables and poultry buildings (¶92) but the chapter ends before the damage is told; carry to III.10. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: une épaisse fumée qui tourbillonnait au-dessus du moulin, des étables et des bâtiments de la basse-cour
- Ben Joyce is named here as the chief of the escaped convicts and Ayrton's old Australian comrade; elsewhere the chief is called Bob Harvey (III.7 ¶53). Both lines are in the text. Card neither. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le Ben Joyce, le chef des convicts évadés
