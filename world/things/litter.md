# The litter: a stretcher of pine branches

*A litter of pine branches covered with leaves and grass on which Smith is carried, and the branch stretcher on which Harbert is carried to Granite-house.*

Keywords: litière, civière, brancards, stretcher, branches, carry, mattresses

- **What it is.**
  - The branches are stripped, with no tools but their hands, from a rather puny tree, a kind of maritime pine wasted by the wind, and made into a litter that, covered with leaves and grass, will carry the engineer (I.8 ¶49). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: sans autres outils que leurs bras
    - quote: sorte de pin maritime émacié par les vents
    - quote: on fit une litière qui, une fois recouverte de feuilles et d’herbes, permettrait de transporter l’ingénieur
  - It takes about forty minutes to make (I.8 ¶50). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: Ce fut l’affaire de quarante minutes environ
  - Its cross-branches are covered with mosses and long grass; Pencroff bears one end of the poles and Nab the other (I.8 ¶85). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: Les branches transversales avaient été recouvertes de mousses et de longues herbes
    - quote: Pencroff à une extrémité des brancards, Nab à l’autre
  - A stretcher would give the wounded Harbert gentler motion than the cart, but needs two bearers, so that two guns would be missing from the defence (III.9 ¶71). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: La civière aurait eu des mouvements plus doux pour le blessé
    - quote: elle nécessitait deux porteurs
  - At the bend of the river, a few branches arranged in the form of a stretcher receive Harbert’s mattresses (see [Harbert’s mattresses](harberts-mattresses.md)) (III.10 ¶2). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: quelques branches, disposées en forme de civière, reçurent les matelas
- **Appears in:** I.8, III.9, III.10
