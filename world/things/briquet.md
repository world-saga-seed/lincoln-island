# The briquet: the steel for striking sparks, with its tinder

*The fire-striker the colonists carry; struck on flint with burnt linen on the mountain, and used with tinder after the blast to light the torches.*

Keywords: briquet, fire-steel, tinderbox, amadou, flint, silex, sparks, light, torches, lighter

- **What it is.**
  - On the mountain the briquet was struck, and the burnt linen caught the sparks of the flint (I.10 ¶45). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: Le briquet fut battu, le linge brûlé recueillit les étincelles du silex
  - After the blast the colonists take a briquet and tinder (amadou) from the Chimneys, for the descent (I.18 ¶4). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: un briquet et de l’amadou
  - The torches for the descent are lit with the fire of the briquet (I.18 ¶18). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: allumées au feu du briquet
- **Appears in:** I.10, I.18
- **Open:**
  - The text does not say what the briquet is made of or where it came from; see [the spark pebbles](spark-pebbles.md), which hold the same gap (I.18 ¶4). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: un briquet et de l’amadou
