# I.2: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller c. Beads corrected: 17–18 (French ¶21 belongs with Bead 18, White ¶18, which opens with it; French ¶22 has no White); 24 (White ¶25 is a stray repeat of White ¶23, not a rendering of French ¶29); 46–47 (French ¶58 is the last sentence of White ¶47; French note n1 is the bracketed White ¶48).*

### 1. changed: the date of the flight from Richmond (I.2 ¶1; White ¶1)
- The French dates the arrival on the unknown coast "le 24 mars"; White puts the same clause on the 20th of March, the day of the departure. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le 24 mars, après avoir fui Richmond
  - white: on the 20th of March, after having flown from Richmond

### 2. changed: Smith's look, a scholar of the militant school (I.2 ¶4; White ¶5)
- The French calls his face that of "un savant de l’école militante"; White makes it the air of a practised officer, so a scholar becomes a soldier. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: la physionomie d’un savant de l’école militante
  - white: the air of a practiced officer

### 3. condensed: the three conditions of energy (I.2 ¶4; White ¶5)
- The French names activity of mind and body, impetuosity of desires and power of will; White drops the mind and the impetuous desires and adds "determination". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: activité d’esprit et de corps, impétuosité des désirs, puissance de la volonté
  - white: activity of body, strength of will, and determination

### 4. cut: the French military word behind "clear-headed" (I.2 ¶4; White ¶5)
- The French glosses « très-débrouillard » as a word of French military speech; White keeps the sense and drops the gloss. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: pour employer un mot de la langue militaire française
  - white: “clear-headed,”

### 5. changed: where Smith was taken (I.2 ¶5; White ¶6)
- The French has him wounded and taken on the battlefield of Richmond; White has him taken prisoner at the siege of Richmond. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: blessé et pris sur le champ de bataille de Richmond
  - white: wounded and taken prisoner at the siege of Richmond

### 6. condensed: Spilett as hero of curiosity (I.2 ¶8; White ¶9)
- The French calls him a hero of curiosity, of information, of the unpublished, the unknown, the impossible; White makes him a master of "recondite information" of those things. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: véritable héros de la curiosité, de l’information, de l’inédit, de l’inconnu, de l’impossible
  - white: a master of recondite information of the unpublished, the unknown, the impossible

### 7. changed: Spilett's age (I.2 ¶10; White ¶11)
- The French gives forty at most; White gives forty or more. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Il avait quarante ans au plus.
  - white: He was forty years old or more.

### 8. changed: Nab's character (I.2 ¶14; White ¶14)
- The French gives him as gentle and calm, serviceable and good; White has him quick and self-possessed, ready and honest. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: intelligent, doux et calme, parfois naïf, toujours souriant, serviable et bon
  - white: intelligent, quick, and self-possessed, sometimes ingenuous always smiling, ready and honest

### 9. changed: the governor of Richmond becomes the Mayor (I.2 ¶18–¶19; White ¶16–¶17)
- The French twice names the governor of Richmond; White makes him the Mayor. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Le gouverneur de Richmond
  - white: The Mayor of Richmond
  - quote: Le gouverneur autorisa la tentative.
  - white: The Mayor authorized the undertaking

### 10. softened: Forster, a rabid Southerner (I.2 ¶18; White ¶16)
- The French calls him "sudiste enragé"; White's "violent Southerner" is milder than "rabid". *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: un certain Jonathan Forster, sudiste enragé
  - white: a certain Jonathan Forster, who was a violent Southerner

### 11. changed: the wind of the departure is north-west in the French, north-east in White (I.2 ¶20; White ¶17)
- The French fixes the departure for a north-west wind of middle strength and says the north-west wind turned to hurricane; White says north-east both times. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: avec un vent de nord-ouest de moyenne force
  - white: with a moderate breeze from the northeast

### 12. cut: the 18th and 19th pass, the balloon laid flat by the gusts (I.2 ¶22; White ¶18)
- The French says the 18th and 19th of March pass with no change in the storm and that the tethered balloon is laid to the ground by the gusts; White has no such passage. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: On éprouvait même de grandes difficultés pour préserver le ballon, attaché au sol, que les rafales couchaient jusqu’à terre.
  - white: —

### 13. changed: "Quand cela ?" becomes "How?" (I.2 ¶28; White ¶24)
- Smith's first answer asks when; White has him ask how. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Quand cela ?…
  - white: “How?”

### 14. cut: Smith's judgement of the sailor's face (I.2 ¶29; White ¶25)
- The French gives Smith's study of the sailor's loyal face and his conclusion that he is an honest man; White has no such sentence. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il ne put douter qu’il n’eût devant lui un honnête homme
  - white: —

### 15. addition: a repeated line where the French has that sentence (I.2 ¶29; White ¶25)
- White ¶25 repeats Pencroff's question of White ¶23 word for word; the French has no such repeat. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Mais après avoir, d’un œil pénétrant, observé la loyale figure du marin
  - white: “Mr. Smith, do you want to escape?”

### 16. changed: the balloon "devait enlever six" becomes a question (I.2 ¶40; White ¶35)
- Pencroff says flatly that the balloon was to lift six; White makes it a question whether it can carry six. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: Or, le ballon devait enlever six…
  - white: Well, the balloon can carry six?

### 17. cut: the five will stroll there together, as curious onlookers (I.2 ¶43; White ¶38)
- Pencroff's plan that all five will loiter about the place, as if from curiosity, is not in White. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Nous flânerons tous les cinq, par là, en curieux !
  - white: “To-night, then,” said Pencroff.

### 18. changed: the day becomes a "journey" (I.2 ¶46; White ¶41)
- The French says the day was terrible; White says the journey would be terrible, a future the French does not give. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: La journée fut terrible.
  - white: The journey would be terrible.

### 19. changed: Forster's companions become one (I.2 ¶46; White ¶41)
- The French says neither Forster nor his companions; White says "his companion". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ni Jonathan Forster, ni ses compagnons
  - white: neither Jonathan Forster nor his companion

### 20. changed: "on en viendra à bout" becomes "it can't last" (I.2 ¶48; White ¶43)
- Pencroff says they will see it through all the same; White has him say the weather cannot last, which is the opposite of what the plan needs. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: on en viendra à bout tout de même
  - white: it can’t last, all the same

### 21. changed: the balloon's speed becomes the sea's fury (I.2 ¶56; White ¶46)
- The French says the wind carried the balloon along at frightful speed; White says the wind lashed the sea into fury. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: que le vent entraînait avec une vitesse effroyable
  - white: lashed by the wind into a terrific fury

### 22. addition: "(sic)" in the note on Lee (I.2 ¶57 n1; White ¶48)
- White's bracketed note adds "(sic)" after Lee's retreat to the West; the French has no such mark. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Lee se retirait dans l’ouest
  - white: Lee retreated into the West (sic)

## Outline slips

- Beats 1 and 12 of the outline give both "the flight lasted five days" and "Of five men leaving on 20 March, four were thrown ... on 24 March", with no note that the French itself disagrees: twenty to twenty-four is four days, and the sea is seen "cinq jours après". *(Drafted.)*
  - outline: the flight lasted five days
  - quote: Ce fut cinq jours après seulement, qu’une éclaircie laissa voir l’immense mer
  - quote: partis le 20 mars, quatre étaient jetés, le 24 mars
- Beat 9 has the party "each strolling to the square"; the French has Pencroff say all five will stroll "par là", and does not name the square at that point. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - outline: each strolling to the square
  - quote: Nous flânerons tous les cinq, par là, en curieux !
