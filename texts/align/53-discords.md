# III.11: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller p. Beads corrected: none.*

### 1. cut: the quantity of powder (III.11 ¶1; White ¶1)
- The French says the box held about two hundred grains of white powder; White gives no quantity. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Elle contenait environ deux cents grains d’une poudre blanche
  - white: It contained a white powder, which he tasted.

### 2. cut: Hetzel's footnote on the grains (III.11 ¶4, n1; White ¶3)
- The French dose is "about" eighteen grains with a footnote giving the weight in grams; White gives "eighteen grains" and no note. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: environ dix-huit grains[n1] de la quinine
  - white: eighteen grains of quinine

### 3. changed: ten days become two (III.11 ¶10–¶11; White ¶6)
- The French says that ten days later, on 20 December, Harbert entered convalescence; White says "In two days more", and drops the date. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Dix jours après, le 20 décembre, Harbert entrait en convalescence.
  - white: In two days more Herbert became convalescent.

### 4. cut: Pencroff's joy (III.11 ¶12; White ¶7)
- White drops that Pencroff was like a man drawn from an abyss, that his joy bordered on delirium, and that he hugged the reporter almost to suffocation. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Pencroff était comme un homme qu’on a retiré du fond d’un abîme. Il avait des crises de joie qui tenaient du délire.
  - white: Pencroff was beside himself with joy.

### 5. cut: the embrace promised to the unknown (III.11 ¶15; White ¶9–¶10)
- White drops the line that whoever he is, he must expect a rough embrace from Pencroff. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: cet homme, quel qu’il fût, devait s’attendre à quelque rude embrassade du digne Pencroff !
  - white: —

### 6. cut: the month's wait, and the wounds healed (III.11 ¶18–¶19; White ¶12)
- White drops that the whole colony's strength would be needed against the convicts, that the wait might be a month ("peut-être"), that the liver congestion had gone and that the wounds were healed for good. White's "In a month’s time" states the delay flatly. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ce ne serait pas trop de toutes les forces de la colonie pour avoir raison des convicts
  - white: In a month’s time, when the lad should have regained his strength, the important search would be undertaken
  - quote: La congestion du foie avait disparu, et les blessures pouvaient être considérées comme cicatrisées définitivement.
  - white: —

### 7. cut: why the buildings are not rebuilt (III.11 ¶20; White ¶13)
- White drops Smith's reason: while the colonists hunt the convicts, those might visit the plateau again, and they must not be given cause to resume plundering and burning. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ceux-ci pourraient bien rendre une nouvelle visite au plateau, et il ne fallait pas leur donner sujet de reprendre leur métier de pillards et d’incendiaires
  - white: Smith preferred to wait till the island was rid of the convicts before he repaired the damage to the mill, poultry-yard, and stable.

### 8. cut: Harbert's rising, his bearing, the sea baths (III.11 ¶21–¶22; White ¶14–¶15)
- White drops the hour, two hours, three hours a day, his promise of "noble et belle prestance", Spilett's severity as doctor, and that the baths were taken with Pencroff and Nab. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: d’abord une heure par jour, puis deux, puis trois
  - white: In the latter part of the month Herbert began to take some exercise.
  - quote: Il était grand et promettait de devenir un homme de noble et belle prestance.
  - white: —
  - quote: Quelques bains de mer qu’il prit en compagnie de Pencroff et de Nab lui firent le plus grand bien.
  - white: and strengthened himself with sea-baths.

### 9. cut: the party must keep together (III.11 ¶30; White ¶22)
- White drops that the convicts might be in the woods where a shot is quickly fired and received, and the resulting rule that the party stay compact and never divide. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: De là, nécessité pour la petite troupe des colons de rester compacte et de ne se diviser sous aucun prétexte.
  - white: —
  - quote: l’arsenal maintenant si complet de Granite-house
  - white: a careful selection of arms and ammunition.

### 10. changed: the lift's drum becomes its basket (III.11 ¶33; White ¶24)
- The French dismantles the drum of the lift; White removes "the basket of the elevator". White also drops that the ladders are buried deep so that they can serve on the return. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le tambour de l’ascenseur fut démonté
  - white: the basket of the elevator was removed
  - quote: de manière qu’elles pussent servir au retour
  - white: The ladders were carried to the Chimneys and buried there

### 11. cut: the first mile along the Mercy (III.11 ¶40; White ¶30)
- White drops the turn at the river mouth and the mile up the left bank of the Mercy before the bridge. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: après avoir remonté pendant un mille la rive gauche de la Mercy
  - white: The wagon went up the left bank of the Mercy, across the bridge

### 12. condensed: the forest's trees, birds and animals (III.11 ¶41–¶42; White ¶31)
- The French names the trees (deodars, douglas firs, casuarinas, banksias, gum trees, dragon trees), the birds (grouse, jacamars, pheasants, lories, cockatoos, parakeets, parrots) and the animals (agoutis, kangaroos, capybaras); White says only "Birds and beasts were plenty". The French also has creepers and brush to be cut where White has "a stump or some bushes". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Déodars, douglas, casuarinas, banksias, gommiers, dragonniers et autres essences déjà reconnues
  - white: Birds and beasts were plenty
  - quote: il fallait trancher quelques lianes et des forêts de broussailles
  - white: without any other obstacle than here and there a stump or some bushes

### 13. condensed: the signs of the convicts' passing (III.11 ¶44; White ¶33)
- White drops the broken branches, perhaps blazing the way, the ashes of a dead fire and the footprints kept in clay. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ici, des brisées faites aux arbres, peut-être dans le but de jalonner le chemin ; là, des cendres d’un foyer éteint, et des empreintes de pas que certaines portions glaiseuses du sol avaient conservées
  - white: they saw where a party of men had passed, or built a fire

### 14. cut: why no hunting and no going alone (III.11 ¶45; White ¶34)
- White keeps the ban on hunting for the sound of shots but drops that hunters would be drawn far from the cart and that walking alone was strictly forbidden. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il était sévèrement interdit de marcher isolément
  - white: —

### 15. cut: Top and Jup's signal (III.11 ¶46; White ¶35)
- White drops the way the engineer used the dog and the orang: when they returned having signalled nothing, there was nothing to fear. White says only that they were sent as skirmishers, and does not say that the way was felled. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: quand le chien et l’orang revenaient sans avoir rien signalé, c’est qu’il n’y avait rien à craindre
  - white: into which Top and Jup were sent as skirmishers

### 16. softened: convicts and wild beasts alike (III.11 ¶46; White ¶35)
- The narrator ranks convicts and wild beasts as two kinds of animal on one level by their ferocity; White leaves out the judgement. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: deux sortes d’individus du règne animal que leurs féroces instincts mettaient au même niveau
  - white: —

### 17. cut: the tributary and the fertile soil (III.11 ¶47; White ¶36)
- White drops that the stream was part of the water system to which the soil owed its astonishing fertility. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qui devait se rattacher au système hydrographique auquel ce sol devait son étonnante fertilité
  - white: of whose existence they had been unaware.

### 18. cut: no fires, and who stands watch with whom (III.11 ¶48–¶49; White ¶36)
- White drops that fires would draw the convicts rather than stop them, so the camp was kept dark, and the pairs of watchers (Pencroff with Spilett, the engineer with Nab). *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: les convicts, eux, eussent été plutôt attirés qu’arrêtés par ces flammes, et mieux valait dans ce cas s’entourer de profondes ténèbres
  - white: —
  - quote: Pencroff et Gédéon Spilett, d’une part, l’ingénieur et Nab, de l’autre
  - white: Two of the colonists kept guard together in watches of two hours

### 19. cut: the short night (III.11 ¶50; White ¶37)
- White drops that there was hardly any night, the darkness coming from the thick foliage. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il y eut à peine quelques heures de nuit. L’obscurité était due plutôt à l’épaisseur des ramures qu’à la disparition du soleil.
  - white: —

### 20. cut: the date and the slow march (III.11 ¶51–¶52; White ¶38)
- White drops the date 16 February, and that the march was slow rather than hard; he also drops that felling the great trees would have cost enormous fatigue. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le lendemain, 16 février, la marche, plutôt lente que pénible, fut reprise
  - white: The next day, they were unable to accomplish more than six miles.

### 21. cut: new species and the kauri's fame (III.11 ¶53; White ¶39)
- White drops that these species had not yet been noted on the island, that the carobs gave a sweet pulp, and that the kauris are the king-trees of New Zealand, as famous as the cedars of Lebanon. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: des essences nouvelles, dont la présence n’avait pas encore été signalée dans l’île
  - white: During the day Herbert discovered some specimens of the tree ferns
  - quote: C’étaient bien là ces arbres-rois de la Nouvelle-Zélande, aussi célèbres que les cèdres du Liban.
  - white: —

### 22. changed: emus "particular to" Australia (III.11 ¶54; White ¶40)
- The French says these birds are peculiar to Australia; White says they are common there. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: un couple de ces grands oiseaux qui sont particuliers à l’Australie
  - white: a couple of large birds, such as are common in Australia

### 23. changed: killed becomes shot, and the den (III.11 ¶57; White ¶43)
- Pencroff says the wretches have already killed Ayrton; White has them shoot him, which the French does not say. His question, whether the wretches have no lair where they can be hunted like tigers, becomes a statement that they must have a den, and "comme des tigres" is dropped; the reporter's "No" then answers nothing. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: c’est que ces misérables l’avaient déjà tué
  - white: the wretches have shot him
  - quote: Mais ces gueux-là n’ont donc pas une tanière où on puisse aller les traquer comme des tigres !
  - white: But they must have a den, to which we can track them.

### 24. changed: the convicts wander, to be masters (III.11 ¶58; White ¶44)
- The reporter says they go at random and it is their interest to wander until they are masters of the island; White says they "intend to camp about in places". *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: Il est plus probable qu’ils vont à l’aventure, et c’est leur intérêt d’errer ainsi
  - white: It is more likely that they intend to camp about in places, after this manner

### 25. softened: Pencroff's strangled voice, and his question (III.11 ¶59–¶61; White ¶45)
- White drops the voice strangled as if an iron fist held his throat, and "horrified" is a different feeling from that rage. White drops too his question "do you know what bullet I put in my gun?" and Smith's "No, Pencroff!". *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: sa voix était étranglée comme si un poignet de fer l’eût saisi à la gorge
  - white: he repeated in a horrified voice
  - quote: — Non, Pencroff !
  - white: —

### 26. condensed: the bullet that went through the chest (III.11 ¶62; White ¶46)
- White says the bullet "wounded" Herbert; the French says it passed through Harbert's chest. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: C’est la balle qui a traversé la poitrine d’Harbert
  - white: The ball in my gun is the one which wounded Herbert
