# III.16: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller B. Beads corrected: none.*

### 1. cut: Nemo's calm gaze (III.16 ¶2; White ¶2)
- The French says his gaze was calm; White omits the sentence. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Son regard était calme.
  - white: This man stood, resting one hand upon the divan, from which he had risen.

### 2. softened: the ardent look (III.16 ¶5; White ¶5)
- The French has Nemo fix a burning look on Smith; White has only that he "looked at" him. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: fixa un regard ardent sur l’ingénieur
  - white: Captain Nemo looked at the engineer as if he would have annihilated him.

### 3. changed: electric effluvia become light (III.16 ¶8; White ¶7)
- The French says the salon air was saturated with electric effluvia; White says it was flooded with light. The four named companions also become "the others". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dont l’air était saturé d’effluences électriques
  - white: The others stood respectfully in a corner of the superb saloon, which was flooded with light.

### 4. cut: why the name startled him (III.16 ¶11; White ¶10)
- White drops the clause that Nemo thought the name unknown to all, which is the reason for the start. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qu’il devait croire ignoré de tous
  - white: Why had the latter sprung up on hearing that name pronounced?

### 5. cut: "sixteen years ago"; changed: "on my ship" (III.16 ¶21; White ¶20)
- White drops the sixteen years since the Frenchman came aboard, and "à mon bord" becomes "in my way". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Ce Français que le hasard jeta à mon bord il y a seize ans ?
  - white: “The Frenchman whom chance threw in my way?”

### 6. changed: "outlawed by humanity" becomes "a scourge" (III.16 ¶27; White ¶26)
- The French has "a rebel, perhaps outlawed by humanity"; White has "a revolutionist, a scourge to humanity", a harder charge that reverses who stands outside whom, and the "peut-être" is lost. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: un révolté, mis peut-être au ban de l’humanité
  - white: Yes, a revolutionist, a scourge to humanity.

### 7. changed: "owe our lives" becomes "owe everything" (III.16 ¶30; White ¶29)
- Smith says all owe their lives to the good being; White has "owe everything". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: tous nous devons la vie à un être bon, généreux, puissant
  - white: that we owe everything to a being good, generous, and powerful

### 8. cut: footnote n1, the Publisher's note on dates (III.16 ¶34 n1; White —)
- White has no footnote: the Publisher's remark that the story was published as Twenty Thousand Leagues and that the same discordance of dates noted on Ayrton applies here. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: sur la discordance de quelques dates
  - white: —

### 9. changed: "a rajah" becomes "the rajah" (III.16 ¶38; White ¶35)
- The French has the son of a rajah of the territory; White makes him the son of "the rajah" and later "the rajah" again. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: fils d’un rajah du territoire alors indépendant du Bundelkund
  - white: the son of the rajah of the then independent territory of Bundelkund

### 10. changed: how he hated England (III.16 ¶41; White ¶38)
- The French says he hated England all the more because on more than one point he admired it; White says "more and more as he admired her", which makes the hatred grow with time and loses "on more than one point". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il haïssait l’Angleterre et d’autant plus que sur plus d’un point il l’admirait
  - white: he hated England more and more as he admired her

### 11. changed: "disdainful of action" (III.16 ¶43; White ¶39)
- The French says the cosmopolite was curious to know but disdainful of acting; White says "disdaining to use it" (knowledge). *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: curieux de savoir, mais dédaigneux d’agir
  - white: curious after knowledge, but disdaining to use it

### 12. changed: "driving on the stranger" (III.16 ¶44; White ¶40)
- The French says he hoped to drive the stranger out of the country; White's "driving on the stranger" does not say so. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: d’en chasser l’étranger
  - white: of driving on the stranger

### 13. condensed: the influence of the United Kingdom (III.16 ¶48; White ¶44)
- The French says the influence and the domination of the United Kingdom in Asia might have been finished; White gives only dominion. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: c’en était fait peut-être en Asie de l’influence et de la domination du Royaume-Uni
  - white: Asia would not to-day, perhaps, be under the dominion of the United Kingdom

### 14. changed: civilization borrows its rights from necessity (III.16 ¶50; White ¶46)
- The French says civilization "seems to borrow all its rights from necessity"; White says her laws "are like those of necessity", losing "il semble" and the rights. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il semble qu’elle emprunte tous les droits à la nécessité
  - white: her laws are like those of necessity

### 15. cut: the inexhaustible sources of electricity (III.16 ¶53; White ¶49)
- White drops that he drew the electricity from inexhaustible sources. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: et qu’il puisait à d’intarissables sources
  - white: By means which will some day be known, he utilized electricity, that incommensurable force, for all the necessities of his apparatus as a motor, for lighting and for heat.

### 16. cut: footnote n2, the Candiotes (III.16 ¶54 n2; White —)
- White has no footnote saying the peoples fighting for independence include the rising of the Candiotes, whom Nemo helped. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Il s’agit du soulèvement des Candiotes
  - white: —

### 17. cut: "always" (III.16 ¶54; White ¶50)
- The French says the Vigo wealth was one he always drew on; White says only that he gave it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dont il disposa toujours, et anonymement
  - white: which he gave, anonymously, to people fighting for their independence.

### 18. changed: the three men "attempted to escape" (III.16 ¶58; White ¶54)
- The French says they succeeded in escaping after seizing the boat; White says they "attempted" to escape. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: parvinrent à s’échapper, après s’être emparés du canot du Nautilus
  - white: seized the boat belonging to the Nautilus and attempted to escape

### 19. cut: the Nautilus empties (III.16 ¶59; White ¶55)
- White drops that the Nautilus emptied as the companions died. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Le vide se fit dans le Nautilus
  - white: One by one his companions died and found their rest in the coral cemetery at the bottom of the Pacific

### 20. changed: why he took the Nautilus to port (III.16 ¶60; White ¶56)
- The French says that once alone he managed to bring the Nautilus to one of the ports that sometimes served as stopovers; White says it was "necessary", and that the ports served him "in former days". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il parvint à ramener son Nautilus vers un des ports sous-marins qui lui servaient quelquefois de points de relâche
  - white: As he was alone, it was necessary to take his Nautilus to one of those submarine ports which served him in former days as a harbor.

### 21. cut: "of the Southerners" (III.16 ¶62; White ¶57)
- The prisoners in the balloon are "prisoners of the Southerners" in the French; White has "the prisoners". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qui emportait les prisonniers des sudistes
  - white: the fall of the balloon which carried the prisoners

### 22. changed: the diving dress becomes an "impermeable jacket" (III.16 ¶62, ¶64; White ¶57, ¶59)
- The French has the scaphandre (diving dress); White has an "impermeable jacket" both times. Also "rivage de l’île" becomes "shore of the islet". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Revêtu de son scaphandre
  - white: Clothed in his impermeable jacket

### 23. condensed: the bar and the Nautilus's draught (III.16 ¶63; White ¶58)
- White drops that the Nautilus's draught was relatively considerable, and "la barre". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il n’y en avait plus assez pour le Nautilus, dont le tirant d’eau était relativement considérable
  - white: although there was still sufficient water for a boat of light draught

### 24. condensed: the torpedo (III.16 ¶65; White ¶60)
- White drops that the torpedo was laid at the bottom of the channel and exploded by the shock. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: par le choc d’une torpille disposée au fond du canal
  - white: who blew up the brig by means of a torpedo

### 25. changed: "struck" becomes "killed" (III.16 ¶65; White ¶60)
- The French says he struck the convicts with electric bullets whose secret he had; White says he killed them and drops the secret. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qui frappa les convicts de ces balles électriques dont il avait le secret
  - white: who, finally, killed the convicts by those electric balls

### 26. cut: his heart given back to him (III.16 ¶66; White ¶61)
- White drops that he felt his heart beat, given back to him by the approach of death. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: sentant battre son cœur rendu à lui-même par les approches de la mort
  - white: feeling the approach of death

### 27. cut: the alphabetic apparatus (III.16 ¶66; White ¶61)
- White drops that the Nautilus was fitted with an alphabetic apparatus. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qui était muni d’un appareil alphabétique
  - white: by means of the wire which reached from the corral to the Nautilus

### 28. changed: the consequence of the incident (III.16 ¶70; White ¶65)
- The French says the incident's repercussion must have been terrible; White says its "recital" was terrible. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dont le retentissement devait avoir été terrible
  - white: an incident whose very recital was terrible

### 29. softened: "must absolve" and "combat" (III.16 ¶79; White ¶74)
- Smith says human reason must absolve; White has the human mind "exonerates". The French says such a man may be fought; White says "disagree". *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: que la raison humaine doit absoudre
  - white: which the human mind exonerates
  - quote: on peut le combattre, on ne cesse pas de l’estimer
  - white: We can disagree with one who misleads himself in an intention which he believes laudable

### 30. softened: the tear (III.16 ¶85; White ¶80)
- The French says a tear slid from the dying man's eyes; White says a tear glistened in his eye. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: Une larme glissa des yeux du mourant.
  - white: A tear glistened in the eye of the dying man.
