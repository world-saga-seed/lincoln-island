# III.2: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller n. Beads corrected: none.*

### 1. cut: the Chimneys' fittings might escape the pirates' eyes (III.2 ¶2; White ¶2)
- The French says the bridge and also "les aménagements des Cheminées" might escape the pirates' notice; White names only the bridge. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: et les aménagements des Cheminées, échappassent à leurs regards
  - white: and the bridge, a mile and a half up stream, might well escape their eye.

### 2. cut: the flag and the gun questioned (III.2 ¶3; White ¶3)
- The French has Smith ask why the flag and the cannon-shot, and answer bravado or perhaps a sign of taking possession; White gives only the colonists' knowledge of the heavy guns. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Pure forfanterie sans doute, à moins que ce ne fût l’indice d’une prise de possession !
  - white: —

### 3. changed: Smith's statement becomes a question (III.2 ¶6; White ¶6)
- Smith says flatly that they have no means of stopping the pirates; White ends his reply with a question mark. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: et nous n’avons aucun moyen de les en empêcher.
  - white: “and we have no means of preventing them?”

### 4. changed: the brig's distance from the coast (III.2 ¶17; White ¶17)
- Harbert gives a mile and a quarter; White makes it a mile and a half. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le brick est à un mille un quart de la côte
  - white: “Do you know that the brig is a mile and a half from the coast?”

### 5. addition: "and it is just" (III.2 ¶23–¶25; White ¶23)
- Ayrton's bare "Hélas !" becomes an avowal that the distrust is deserved. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Puis, plus humblement :
  - white: Then, he added, more humbly, “and it is just.”

### 6. cut: the improbability of a landing on the islet (III.2 ¶27; White ¶25)
- Pencroff says it is unlikely that one of the rascals has landed; White drops the qualification. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: quoique cela soit peu probable
  - white: One of these rascals may possibly have gone on shore there

### 7. cut: Ayrton will go on alone (III.2 ¶27; White ¶25)
- Pencroff's closing words, that Ayrton will go alone to the ship since he proposed it, are left out. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: et il ira seul au navire, puisqu’il a proposé de le faire.
  - white: I will wait for Ayrton on the islet.”

### 8. condensed: bobstays and chain-plates (III.2 ¶28; White ¶26)
- Two places to hang on, the bobstays or the chain-plates of the shrouds, are reduced to "the chains of the shrouds". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: accroché, soit aux sous-barbes, soit aux cadènes des haubans
  - white: by clinging to the chains of the shrouds

### 9. condensed: the lights had been lit lately (III.2 ¶34; White ¶28)
- The brig's lights were "allumées depuis peu" and showed her exact position; White has them "streaming across the water". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: quelques lumières, allumées depuis peu, indiquaient alors la situation exacte
  - white: swam noiselessly towards the ship’s lights, which now were streaming across the water.

### 10. changed: Ayrton puts on a pair of trousers (III.2 ¶37; White ¶30)
- Ayrton takes a pair of the drying sailor's trousers and puts them on, then fixes himself firmly; White has him only find an easy position, and the trousers become "clothes". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Il en passa une.
  - white: He found an easy position, and listened.

### 11. cut: Hetzel's footnote on Speedy (III.2 ¶40 n1; White ¶33)
- The French footnote glosses the ship's name as an English word meaning "active"; White carries no note. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Mot anglais qui signifie actif.
  - white: —

### 12. cut: more ferocious than the Malays themselves (III.2 ¶44; White ¶36)
- The French says the pirates were more ferocious than the Malays; White stops at the massacring of crews. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: plus féroces que les Malais eux-mêmes !
  - white: destroying ships and massacring their crews.

### 13. cut: Harvey a former master mariner, well known to Ayrton (III.2 ¶49; White ¶37)
- The French says Harvey was once a captain of the merchant service, now a sea-robber, and well known to Ayrton; White gives only the seizure and the year. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: autrefois capitaine au long cours, maintenant écumeur de mers, et que connaissait bien Ayrton !
  - white: and for a year had made the brig the terror of the Pacific.

### 14. cut: the gun was not a signal (III.2 ¶53; White ¶38)
- The French says the shot was no signal and that there was as yet no communication between the Norfolk escapees and Lincoln Island; White keeps only the freak. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Ce n’était point un signal, et aucune communication n’existait encore entre les évadés de Norfolk et l’île Lincoln.
  - white: The black flag and the cannon-shot were a mere freak of the pirates, to imitate a ship-of-war running up her colors.

### 15. changed: warships lower their colours, not run them up (III.2 ¶53; White ¶38)
- The French says warships fire as they strike their colours; White says as they run them up. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: au moment où ils amènent leurs couleurs
  - white: to imitate a ship-of-war running up her colors

### 16. condensed: impunity for a long time (III.2 ¶54; White ¶39)
- The French says the island's being unknown would assure the convicts impunity with security, perhaps for long; White has only "security". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: elle leur assurerait, pour longtemps peut-être, l’impunité avec la sécurité
  - white: the fact of its being unknown would add to their security

### 17. softened: any means against them (III.2 ¶54; White ¶39)
- The French says the pirates are unworthy of pity and that any means would be good against them; White drops the second clause. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: indignes de pitié, et contre lesquels tout moyen serait bon
  - white: every one of whom must be destroyed before the colonists would be safe

### 18. changed: the sleepers below, more numerous (III.2 ¶59; White ¶41)
- The French supposes that others, more numerous than the ten on deck, slept inside the brig; White says only that others might be asleep in the hold. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il était supposable que d’autres, plus nombreux, dormaient à l’intérieur du brick
  - white: but it might be that others were asleep in the hold

### 19. changed: barrels of powder become tons (III.2 ¶71; White ¶49)
- The French has a shot among "tonneaux de poudre" (casks); White says "tons of powder". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Un coup de revolver au milieu de ces tonneaux de poudre
  - white: One shot into those tons of powder

### 20. condensed: two convicts fell (III.2 ¶73; White ¶51)
- The French says Ayrton's two shots brought down two convicts; White says he rid himself of the pirates with two shots. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Deux coups de son revolver retentirent, et deux convicts tombèrent
  - white: He rid himself of them with two shots from his revolver

### 21. changed: a general waking becomes a dozen (III.2 ¶74; White ¶51)
- The French says the between-decks stirred as the pirates woke generally; White says a dozen were half-awake. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: un mouvement qui indiquait un réveil général des pirates
  - white: a dozen pirates were half-awake

### 22. cut: flight doubtful (III.2 ¶75; White ¶52)
- The French doubts that flight is still possible, though Ayrton resolves to try everything to rejoin his companions; White omits it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Mais la fuite était-elle encore possible ? C’était douteux
  - white: —

### 23. changed: Harvey not gravely hit (III.2 ¶76; White ¶52)
- The French says the shot at Harvey did not hit him, "at least not gravely", leaving a hit open; White says "without effect". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ne l’atteignit pas, du moins grièvement
  - white: one at Harvey, though without effect

### 24. condensed: fear that the islet was invaded (III.2 ¶82; White ¶54)
- The French sets out the silence (the firing has stopped, neither Ayrton nor Pencroff reappears) and two questions: was the islet invaded, and ought they to run to help? White condenses them to "they could not go to the assistance", keeping the fear of a descent from ¶81. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: L’îlot était-il donc envahi ? Ne fallait-il pas courir au secours d’Ayrton et de Pencroff ?
  - white: They could not go to the assistance of Ayrton or Pencroff

### 25. changed: Smith points to the sky (III.2 ¶93; White ¶65)
- Smith points to the sky with his hand; White has him only look upward. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: mais il montra le ciel de la main
  - white: Smith did not answer, but he looked upwards
