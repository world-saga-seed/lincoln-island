*Handed over by the author, 2026-11-21, for phase 5. The period dictionary: the anachronism check for the renders' English.*

- **Edition:** Noah Webster, *An American Dictionary of the English Language*, revised by Chauncey A. Goodrich and Noah Porter (the 1864 "Webster–Mahn" revision), Springfield, Mass.: G. & C. Merriam; this copy printed 1865. The University of Toronto (Robarts Library) copy.

- **File:** `americandictiona00websuoft_djvu.txt`, the Archive's OCR text, unchanged. The md5 `d4c744a908fb834cdb89096ba49013af` matches the Archive's listing; the sha256 is in `SHA256SUMS`.
- **Fetched:** 2026-11-21.

## How good the OCR is (checked against the page images)

- **Headwords are badly garbled.** The pronunciation marks fuse with the letters ("MOII-SOOII'" for Mon-soon'), so a headword lookup misses many real entries. On page 723 (Jack-stays to Jambeux), checked against its image, most of the first column's 27 headwords cannot be read from the text, and that column's definitions are partly lost where the page curves.
- **The running text is mostly sound.** About 77% of all tokens are clean words. Definitions, etymologies and quotations carry most words in ordinary spelling ("monsoons" in a definition, "computer" twice).
- **So a word counts as present if it appears anywhere in the text,** not only as a headword. A word missing everywhere is a flag for the voice critic to judge, not a finding. It may be an OCR loss.
- Spot results: present: barometer, longitude, Greenwich, telegraph, isothermal, glacier, iceberg, meteorology, electricity, computer (as "one who computes"). Absent: scientist, Coriolis, okay.

## Backup
