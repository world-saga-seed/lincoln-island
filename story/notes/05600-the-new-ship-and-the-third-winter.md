# The new ship and the third winter (notes)

## Notes for drafting
- **Where it sits.** The French III.14, `texts/fr-1875/56.md`; the first chapter after the third anniversary (III.13 ¶100); it carries the colony from April to 7 September of the third year's end, and closes on smoke over the crater. *(Drafted.)*
- **Cast.** Cyrus Smith (¶6), Pencroff (¶4), Nab (¶5), Ayrton (¶6, his residence; ¶19), Harbert (¶20), Gédéon Spilett (¶20), Top (¶20), Jup (¶16); Bob Harvey, dead chief of the convicts, named (¶22). The "genius" of the island, unseen (¶4, ¶53). *(Drafted.)*
- **Places.** The Far-West forest and road (¶15); the Chimneys, where the building yard is (¶15); Serpentine Peninsula (¶15); Grande-Vue plateau (¶18); Granite-house (¶18); the corral (¶20); the Mercy (¶20); Shark Gulf and Port-Balloon (¶5); Union Bay and Washington Bay (¶22); the islet and Wreck Point redan (¶27–¶28); the inner well (¶54); Mount Franklin (¶56); Tabor Island (¶6); Norfolk (¶22). *(Drafted.)*
- **Things.** The ship's plan and keel: 110 feet of oak, a beam of 25 feet, 250 to 300 tons (¶10, ¶26); the plank shed (¶17); the rebuilt mill, new poultry buildings, five onaggas, a plough (¶18); the telegraph wire restored (¶21); the blockhaus plan (¶24); the brig's rigging (¶48); soda and chlorine made from sea salt (¶48); Jup's quilted dressing gown (¶50). *(Drafted.)*
- **Time.** Three years from the Richmond escape (¶1). April: felling and the equinox gales (¶16); a fine month (¶18). About 15 May: keel laid (¶26). The following week: first stern frames (¶26). Last days of May: very bad weather (¶27). About 10 June: building stopped (¶29). June, July and August: the winter months (¶49). "seven months" since the last search around the mountain, and September, which brings fine days (¶53). 7 September: smoke over the crater (¶56). Pencroff's target: a ship ready "for next November" (¶11). The chapter covers about five months, April to 7 September. *(Drafted.)*
- **Clues.** The island's genius is silent for seven months (¶53); the dog and the orang have stopped haunting the mouth of the inner well (¶54); the question "Who knows what the future held?" (¶54); the first smoke over the crater (¶56). Smith's remark that the new ship must carry a document to Tabor (¶6). *(Drafted.)*
- **Length.** 56 paragraphs (¶1–¶56, plus the summary ¶0); 3,382 words in the ¶ lines. *(Drafted.)*

## Open and flags
- Dates in the chapter: ¶16 and ¶18 both place April's work, and ¶53 gives "the seven months" from the last search to September. The summary ¶0 says "Three years have passed"; ¶1 repeats it. The text of III.13 ends on 25 March (the third anniversary), so April here is three years and one month after the arrival. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: Trois ans s’étaient écoulés depuis que les prisonniers de Richmond s’étaient enfuis
- ¶53 says seven months passed since the last search around the mountain, up to September; the last search around the mountain is in an earlier chapter I was not given, so the reckoning is not checked. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: Pendant les sept mois qui s’écoulèrent depuis les dernières recherches opérées autour de la montagne
- ¶29 says the cold stopped the building "about 10 June", but ¶26 says the cold and bad weather had already ended the work of the keel after the following week (about 22 May) and ¶27 gives the last days of May as very bad; the text speaks of two stops, a first suspension and a final abandonment. Card neither. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: puis, il fallut suspendre les travaux
  - quote: il fallut définitivement abandonner la construction du bateau
- ¶49 reads "eight degrees Fahrenheit (13.33 centigrade below zero)". The French says eight degrees Fahrenheit, i.e. below zero; the conversion given is the text's. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: huit degrés Fahrenheit (13°,33 centig au-dessous de zéro)
- ¶49 says the winter was colder than "the previous winter", ¶48 says the colonists suffered in the first winter. Both are in the text; no conflict. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Elle fut donc inférieure à la température du précédent hivernage.
- ¶5 and ¶7: Pencroff names Port-Balloon and Shark Gulf; ¶7 says "the Bonadventure no longer existing", i.e. destroyed in an earlier chapter. Not checked against it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Le Bonadventure n’existant plus
- Smith's reasoning on the cold (¶33–¶39) is the narrator's report of what Smith says; he first says the southern hemisphere is colder and later that the sun is nearer the southern hemisphere in its summer. ¶39 states the earth is "at its apogee" in the southern winter. These are Smith's lines. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: elle est à son point le plus éloigné du soleil
- ¶48 says the brig's wreck gave "the complete rigging"; ¶18 speaks of rebuilt mill and poultry buildings destroyed in the convict attack. Both from earlier chapters, not checked. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: L’incident du brick détruit avait été une nouvelle source de richesses.
- No Wikisource correction touches this chapter; none of its facts rests on one. *(Drafted.)*
