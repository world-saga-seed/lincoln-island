# The brig with the black flag (notes)

## Notes for drafting

- **Where it sits.** The French III.1, the first chapter of the third part, "Le secret de l’île"; follows 04200 without a gap, on the afternoon and night of 17 October 1867. *(Drafted.)*
- **Cast.**
  - Cyrus Smith (¶3), Harbert (¶3), Gédéon Spilett (¶3), Pencroff (¶3), Nab (¶3). *(Drafted.)*
  - Ayrton, in the telegram exchange (¶20) and at Granite-house from ¶37. *(Drafted.)*
  - Lord Glenarvan, in talk (¶15). *(Drafted.)*
  - The crew of the brig, unseen; "pirates" is the colonists' inference (¶75–¶77). *(Drafted.)*
- **Places.**
  - The great hall of Granite-house, its window (¶3, ¶39); the telegraph at Granite-house (¶17); the corral (¶17); Tabor Island (¶12, ¶15). *(Drafted.)*
  - Union Bay (¶62); Claw Cape (¶57); Mandible Cape (¶92); Washington Bay and Port Balloon (¶57, ¶61, ¶81); Prospect Heights and its mill (¶79); the edge of Jacamar Wood (¶82). *(Drafted.)*
- **Things.**
  - The long telescope, the telegraph (¶3, ¶17); the brig, with a black flag at her gaff (¶66–¶74); a cannon (¶99–¶100); an anchor chain (¶103). *(Drafted.)*
  - The mill's wings (¶79); branches and creepers laid over the windows (¶82); arms and ammunition (¶82). *(Drafted.)*
  - Two plates: Ayrton with the telescope (pl1, after ¶53) and the black flag (pl2, after ¶75). *(Drafted.)*
- **Time.**
  - 17 October (¶1); the telegram about 3 p.m., since Ayrton arrives "Vers quatre heures, — une heure après qu’il avait été mandé" (¶37) (hour of the telegram is from that line). *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - About five p.m., the ship may vanish behind Claw Cape (¶57). About half past seven, sun gone twenty minutes (¶92). "Avant une demi-heure, nous serons fixés" (¶68). Night, new moon (¶97). *(Drafted.)*
  - Two and a half years since the landing (¶1). *(Drafted.)*
  - The chapter covers some six or seven hours, from the photograph's afternoon to the first anchor. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
- **Clues.**
  - The ship's black flag and cannon: pirates, or "un navire suspect" (¶75). *(Drafted.)*
  - "Ce n’est point le Duncan." Ayrton's reaction, "Déjà !", his silence, his "Mon devoir" (¶43–¶53, ¶65, ¶90). *(Drafted.)*
  - Smith asks whether the pirates have been to the island before, which "aurait expliqué certaines particularités restées inexplicables jusqu’alors", and whether there is an accomplice (¶94). *(Drafted.)*
  - Smith had earlier said the Duncan might carry the black flag "si les convicts avaient réussi dans leurs criminels projets" (¶77). *(Drafted.)*
  - Smith's wish to leave and annex the island for the Union (¶32). *(Drafted.)*
- **Length.** 104 paragraphs (¶1–¶104), about 3,650 words of paragraph text. Two plates (pl1 after ¶53, pl2 after ¶75). *(Drafted.)*

## Open and flags

- The telegraph is described in II.18 ¶48 as an electromagnet, a plate, and "une aiguille disposée sur un cadran"; here ¶19 reads "le timbre résonnait". Card neither. *(Drafted.)*
- ¶61 decides to light a fire at Port Balloon; ¶79 orders "Que tous les feux soient éteints". The text moves from one to the other when the black flag is seen (¶62–¶77). Card neither. *(Drafted.)*
- ¶92 puts the brig at "plus de huit milles" at half past seven; ¶102 says "à un mille un quart de la côte" after the shot. Card neither. *(Drafted.)*
- ¶1 says "Depuis deux ans et demi"; the chapter gives no landing date (II.18 ¶82 gives 26 March as the second anniversary). *(Drafted.)*
- ¶15 gives Tabor at 150 miles in longitude and 75 in latitude; II.19 ¶8 says the nearest land is "au moins" twelve hundred miles, which is about land other than Tabor. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
- ¶97 gives a new moon on 17 October; II.19 ¶73 gives a full moon on 18 April. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
- No Wikisource correction touches this chapter. *(Drafted.)*
