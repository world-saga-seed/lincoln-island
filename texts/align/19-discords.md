# I.19: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller g. Beads corrected: none.*

### 1. condensed: the ladder "makes access impossible" (I.19 ¶7; White ¶6)
- Cyrus says that once drawn up the ladder will make access to the dwelling impossible; White says only that they can pull it up after them. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qui, une fois retirée, rendra impossible l’accès de notre demeure
  - white: which we can pull up after us

### 2. changed: "no natives" made certain (I.19 ¶8; White ¶7)
- Pencroff says that, as for the island being inhabited by natives, it is not; White adds "certainly", so that Pencroff's claim is stronger than the French (he then admits he is not sure). *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: Quant à être habitée par des indigènes, notre île ne l’est pas !
  - white: and there are certainly no natives

### 3. cut: Cyrus spoke wisely, and Pencroff made no further objection (I.19 ¶12; White ¶11)
- The sentence that Pencroff, with no other objection, prepared to carry out the orders is left out. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: sans faire aucune autre objection, Pencroff se prépara à exécuter ses ordres
  - white: —

### 4. cut: where the facade lies on the wall (I.19 ¶13; White ¶11)
- The French places the facade on the stretch of wall between the salient at the mouth of the Mercy and a line above the heap of rocks of the Chimneys; White gives no such bounds. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: entre le saillant faisant angle sur l’embouchure de la Mercy, et une ligne perpendiculairement tracée au-dessus de l’entassement de roches qui formaient les Cheminées
  - white: —

### 5. changed: the north-east winds strike it aslant (I.19 ¶13; White ¶11)
- The French says the bad (north-east) winds strike the facade only aslant because of the salient; White says the facade is protected by the slope of the rock from the "piercing" north-east wind. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ceux du nord-est, ne la frappaient que d’écharpe
  - white: It was protected by the slope of the rock from the piercing northeast wind.

### 6. changed: how much nitro-glycerine, and its effect (I.19 ¶14; White ¶11)
- The French says Cyrus still had a certain quantity and the effect was well localised; White says he used it "in small quantities". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Il avait encore une certaine quantité de nitro-glycérine à sa disposition
  - white: Smith again had recourse to the nitro-glycerine, which, used in small quantities, had the desired effect

### 7. changed: thirty feet wide becomes thirty feet long (I.19 ¶15; White ¶12)
- The kitchen-room (and so the dining-room and bedroom) is wide (large) in the French, along the front; White gives "long". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: une première chambre-cuisine, large de trente pieds, une salle à manger, mesurant quarante pieds
  - white: a kitchen thirty feet long, a dining-room forty feet long

### 8. cut: the kind of fibre in the ladder (I.19 ¶19; White ¶14)
- The French makes the uprights of the fibres of the "curry-jonc"; White says only "fibres". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: formés des fibres du « curry-jonc » tressées au moyen d’un moulinet
  - white: its sides were twisted of fibres by means of a shuttle

### 9. changed: the winch becomes a shuttle (I.19 ¶19; White ¶14)
- The fibres are twisted by a winch (moulinet); White has a shuttle. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: tressées au moyen d’un moulinet
  - white: twisted of fibres by means of a shuttle

### 10. changed: more ropes and a rough pulley become tackle and a derrick (I.19 ¶20; White ¶15)
- The French has other ropes made and a rough pulley (mouffle) fixed at the door; White has "another kind of tackle" and a derrick. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: D’autres cordes furent également fabriquées avec des fibres végétales, et une sorte de mouffle grossière fut installée à la porte
  - white: Another kind of tackle was made of vegetable fibre, and a sort of derrick was setup at the door of Granite House

### 11. condensed: Pencroff's faith in Cyrus and what he expects of him (I.19 ¶21; White ¶16)
- The French lists what Pencroff thinks easy: clothes and shoes, lighting for winter nights, the working of the fertile parts, wild flora made tame, and says he thought Cyrus able to undertake and succeed in everything; White gives "All their wants would be supplied". The railways of the French are a "network" covering the island, and its "rivières canalisées" become "canals". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: La question des vêtements et des chaussures, — question grave assurément, — celle de l’éclairage pendant les nuits d’hiver
  - white: All their wants would be supplied in Smith’s own time.

### 12. cut: Cyrus lets the exaggerations stand (I.19 ¶22; White ¶16)
- The French says Cyrus took nothing off the exaggerations of that brave heart; White has only that he let Pencroff talk. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Il ne rabattait rien des exagérations de ce brave cœur
  - white: —

### 13. changed: a poor boat is perilous, not impossible (I.19 ¶22; White ¶16)
- The French says that to venture on a boat of necessarily poor build would be grave and perilous; White says no boat could dare put out to sea. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: se hasarder sur un bateau, de construction nécessairement médiocre, serait chose grave et périlleuse
  - white: no boat could dare put out to sea

### 14. changed: the Robinsons of old become the Swiss Family Robinson (I.19 ¶23; White ¶17)
- Pencroff says they outdo by a hundred cubits the Robinsons of former times; White names the Swiss Family Robinson. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ils dépassaient de cent coudées les Robinsons d’autrefois, pour qui tout était miracle à faire
  - white: they were far ahead of the Swiss Family Robinson, for whom miracles were always being wrought

### 15. addition: "they knew Nature" (I.19 ¶24; White ¶17)
- The French says "they knew" (in quotation marks) and that the man who knows succeeds; White supplies the object, "Nature". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ils « savaient », et l’homme qui « sait » réussit
  - white: In truth they knew Nature; and he who knows Nature will succeed

### 16. cut: Nab's look, and Nab and Pencroff using "tu" (I.19 ¶26; White ¶18)
- The French says Nab looked as if answering "Nothing is more natural", and that he and Pencroff had soon come to say "tu" to each other; White gives only that they were great friends. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: n’avaient pas tardé à se tutoyer
  - white: The sailor and he were great friends.

### 17. cut: the season that hurries the work (I.19 ¶30; White ¶21)
- The French says the works were pressed because the bad season was near, and that the reporter and Herbert had become the colony's purveyors; White has neither. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: car la mauvaise saison approchait
  - white: —

### 18. changed: the first fine days become the first five days (I.19 ¶30; White ¶21)
- The long excursion into the forests of the Far West is kept for the first fine days of next spring; White has "the first five days of the coming spring". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: On réservait cette importante excursion pour les premiers beaux jours du printemps prochain.
  - white: This important excursion was set apart for the first five days of the coming spring.

### 19. condensed: the aromatic herbs (I.19 ¶30; White ¶21)
- The French names thyme, wild thyme, basil and savory, all labiates; White gives thyme, basil, "all sorts of mint". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: telles que thym, serpolet, basilic, sarriette
  - white: such as thyme, basil, and all sorts of mint

### 20. condensed: the medicinal herbs Herbert gathers (I.19 ¶31; White ¶21)
- The French names basil, rosemary, lemon balm and betony; White says "a quantity of plants". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: pousses de basilic, de romarin, de mélisse, de bétoine
  - white: Herbert gathered a quantity of plants

### 21. changed: monarda becomes mountain mint (I.19 ¶34; White ¶24)
- The French has the monarda didyma; White has "Mountain Mint". *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: une notable quantité de monardes didymes
  - white: some Mountain Mint

### 22. cut: Gideon's "I see them" (I.19 ¶37; White ¶27)
- The reporter's reply, that he can see the burrows, is left out. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Oui, répondit le reporter, je les vois bien.
  - white: —

### 23. cut: the hour of digging (I.19 ¶41; White ¶29)
- The French says it took an hour of digging to take four rabbits; White has no time. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: après une heure de fouilles, quatre rongeurs furent pris au gîte
  - white: until four rodents were taken

### 24. changed: "American rabbits" become "American hares" (I.19 ¶41; White ¶30)
- The French says they are commonly known as "lapins d'Amérique"; White gives "American hares". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: « lapins d’Amérique »
  - white: “American hares.”

### 25. condensed: the warren as an inexhaustible resource (I.19 ¶42; White ¶30)
- The French says the resource seemed to be inexhaustible; White gives "a most valuable reserve", without the claim. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qui semblait devoir être inépuisable
  - white: the warren bade fair to be a most valuable reserve

### 26. cut: the stove-pipe likened to a sheet-iron stove's, and the east wind full on the facade (I.19 ¶43; White ¶31)
- The French likens the flue to a sheet-iron stove's and says the east winds beat directly on the facade; White says only that the pipe would smoke in an eastern gale. White's sentence also breaks off at "head-cook Neb was not particular", without its full stop, in the text as set. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: par les grands vents d’est qui battaient directement la façade
  - white: No doubt during an eastern gale the pipe would smoke

### 27. changed: the lake to be raised "to its first level" (I.19 ¶44; White ¶32)
- The French says Cyrus means to drown the opening under the lake by bringing the waters back to their first level with a dam; White says he means to dam the lake "so as to conceal" the weir. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: noyer cet orifice sous les eaux du lac en les ramenant à leur premier niveau par un barrage
  - white: damming up the waters of the lake so as to conceal this weir

### 28. addition: square blocks and thistles (I.19 ¶44; White ¶32)
- The French rolls "quarters of rock" to the opening and plants herbs, shrubs and brush ("broussailles") in the gaps; White has "great square blocks" and thistles. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Des quartiers de roches furent roulés à l’ouverture et cimentés fortement
  - white: Great square blocks were rolled to the opening, and strongly cemented together

### 29. cut: the footnote on the gallon, and the pure inexhaustible spring (I.19 ¶45 n1; White ¶32)
- The French carries a footnote that the gallon is about four and a half litres, and calls the supply a pure and inexhaustible spring; White has neither. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Le gallon vaut environ 4 litres et demi.
  - white: —

### 30. addition: glass "from the sand" (I.19 ¶46; White ¶33)
- The French has the engineer make window-glass; White says he will make it from the sand. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: que l’ingénieur eût eu le temps de fabriquer du verre à vitre
  - white: till Smith should have time to make glass from the sand

### 31. condensed: the joke about the mezzanine (I.19 ¶48; White ¶33)
- Pencroff calls it his apartment on the fifth floor above the mezzanine; White drops "above the mezzanine". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: « son appartement au cinquième au-dessus de l’entresol ! »
  - white: “his suite on the fifth floor.”
