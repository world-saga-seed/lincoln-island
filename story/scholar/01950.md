## b

After: Toward eight o’clock the ebb began to near its lowest.

The papers give the tide on this coast once by the clock: low water about ten in the morning of the 25th of March, when the party waded across to the islet (chapter III). The tide comes some fifty minutes later from one day to the next, and carried forward from that morning it brings the low water of the 2nd of June to about half-past six, not toward eight. The 1st of June agrees with the reckoning well enough, for the sailor found the tide near its lowest at first light. I find nothing in the papers to account for this day or the next, and leave them open.

## c

After: Toward the middle of the morning, at low water,

See note (b). By the same reckoning the low water of the 3rd of June falls about seven.
