# II.16: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller l. Beads corrected: none.*

### 1. changed: "refait homme" turned to a future (II.16 ¶1; White ¶1)
- The French says he had remade himself a man by tears; White says he "would be made a man". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il s’était refait homme par les larmes
  - white: he would be made a man through his tears

### 2. softened: "obstination" becomes "disinclination" (II.16 ¶3; White ¶3)
- Verne's stubborn refusal to speak is made a mere reluctance. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: une étrange obstination à ne pas parler aux colons
  - white: a strange disinclination to speak to them

### 3. cut: "one evening" (II.16 ¶3; White ¶3)
- White drops the time of the overheard words. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: car, un soir, Pencroff
  - white: Pencroff, listening at his room

### 4. condensed: farm tools to "little chores" (II.16 ¶7; White ¶7)
- The French says he took up the ploughing tools; White gives only "little chores". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: avait commencé à se servir des outils de labourage
  - white: had begun to do some little chores

### 5. changed: remorse as likely cause becomes an alternative (II.16 ¶8; White ¶7)
- The French says remorse seemed to weigh on him, "one could believe it", and Spilett remarked on it; White makes remorse one choice against Spilett's suggestion. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Était-ce donc le remords qui l’accablait ainsi ? on pouvait le croire
  - white: Could it be by remorse? or, was it, as Spilett once suggested:—

### 6. softened: "trop graves" sharpened to "too terrible" (II.16 ¶9; White ¶8)
- Spilett's "too grave" becomes "too terrible to mention" and "I believe" is moved; the force is raised. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: des choses trop graves à dire
  - white: something on his mind too terrible to mention

### 7. cut: "one must be patient and wait" (II.16 ¶10; White —)
- The narrator's line that they must wait is left out. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Il fallait être patient et attendre.
  - white: —

### 8. cut: the date, 3 November (II.16 ¶11; White ¶9)
- White gives "some days later" with no date. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Quelques jours plus tard, le 3 novembre
  - white: Some days later

### 9. softened: "je le veux" (II.16 ¶14; White ¶12)
- Cyrus's command "I will it" becomes "I wish you to look at me", though White adds "with decision". *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: regardez-moi, je le veux !
  - white: I wish you to look at me.

### 10. changed: how he came back into humanity (II.16 ¶23; White ¶20)
- The French says he re-entered humanity only by the road of remorse; White says remorse was his first human sensation. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il ne soit rentré dans l’humanité que par la voie du remords
  - white: his first human sensation was remorse

### 11. changed: "funeste" past becomes "hateful" (II.16 ¶26; White ¶23)
- The past is "fatal, baleful"; White makes it "hateful". *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: un passé funeste sans doute
  - white: a past which, doubtless, was hateful enough

### 12. addition: "Suddenly" (II.16 ¶27; White ¶24)
- The French has the resolution come after the two hours; White says "suddenly". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Cependant, après deux heures, il parut avoir pris une résolution
  - white: Suddenly he seemed to have taken a resolution

### 13. cut: "but he wept no more" (II.16 ¶27; White ¶24)
- *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: mais il ne pleurait plus
  - white: His eyes were red with the traces of tears

### 14. softened: "J’aime mieux cela" (II.16 ¶31; White ¶27)
- "I prefer that" becomes "I am glad of that". *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: J’aime mieux cela !
  - white: I am glad of that.

### 15. cut: "hastily" (II.16 ¶33; White ¶29)
- *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Anglais, » répondit-il précipitamment.
  - white: “English,” he responded, as if these few words had cost him a great effort.

### 16. changed: he goes away from the shore, not to it (II.16 ¶34; White ¶29)
- The French says he moved away from the beach and walked along it; White says he rushed to the shore. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il s’éloigna de la grève
  - white: He rushed to the shore

### 17. condensed: the route "from the cascade" (II.16 ¶34; White ¶29)
- White loses the starting point of his walk. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: depuis la cascade jusqu’à l’embouchure de la Mercy
  - white: traversed its length to the mouth of the Mercy

### 18. changed: December becomes November (II.16 ¶37; White ¶32)
- Harbert answers "December" in the French; White has "November". The French is out of step with the 3 November date; White corrects it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Décembre, répondit Harbert.
  - white: “November,” replied the lad.

### 19. condensed: the questions and answer (II.16 ¶41; White ¶36)
- *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Harbert avait rapporté aux colons les demandes et la réponse qui lui avaient été faites.
  - white: Herbert related this incident to the others.

### 20. changed: "il faudrait retourner" (II.16 ¶48; White ¶43)
- In the French it is Pencroff who says that they would have to go back; White makes it the man who would wish to go back. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il faudrait retourner, et…
  - white: he would want to go back, and—

### 21. condensed: why the bottle could not have lain on the shore (II.16 ¶60; White ¶55)
- White loses that the bottle was still floating when found, that it could not have been taken back by the sea after lying ashore, and that the south coast is all rocks. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: On ne peut pas même supposer qu’après avoir séjourné plus ou moins longtemps sur le rivage, elle ait pu être reprise par la mer, car c’est tout rochers sur la côte sud
  - white: “And have remained floating? No,” answered the sailor, “for sooner or later it would have been dashed to pieces on the rocks.”

### 22. cut: "an incomprehensible fact" (II.16 ¶63; White ¶58)
- *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il y avait là un fait incompréhensible
  - white: The sailor’s remark was just, and, moreover, this paper, recently written, gave the situation of the island

### 23. condensed: "en latitude et en longitude" (II.16 ¶63; White ¶58)
- White drops that the document gave the island's latitude and longitude. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: la situation de l’île Tabor en latitude et en longitude avec précision
  - white: gave the situation of the island with an exactness

### 24. changed: veranda becomes arbor (II.16 ¶66; White ¶61)
- *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: réunis sous la vérandah
  - white: seated in the arbor

### 25. condensed: "about eight in the evening" (II.16 ¶66; White ¶61)
- *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: vers huit heures du soir
  - white: At dusk

### 26. softened: "maudit" becomes "wicked" (II.16 ¶68; White ¶62)
- "Cursed being" becomes "wicked being". *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: un être maudit
  - white: a wicked being

### 27. changed: meadows become fields (II.16 ¶79; White ¶73)
- Verne moves the meadows outside the creeks; White says the fields. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: reporter en dehors les prairies
  - white: extend the fields

### 28. cut: protection against the animals (II.16 ¶79; White ¶73)
- *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: contre les déprédations des quadrumanes et des quadrupèdes
  - white: which required no protection

### 29. changed: "une dizaine" becomes "a dozen" (II.16 ¶80; White ¶74)
- About ten bushels of seed in the French; a dozen in White. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il suffisait de semer une dizaine de boisseaux
  - white: it was only necessary to sow a dozen bushels each year

### 30. cut: the first fall drives the fulling mill (II.16 ¶82; White ¶75)
- White omits why the second fall was spoken of. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: la première étant déjà occupée à mouvoir les pilons du moulin à foulon
  - white: Smith could have used the other waterfall which fell into the Mercy

### 31. condensed: "exposé aux brises du large" (II.16 ¶82; White ¶75)
- *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: exposé aux brises du large
  - white: open elevation

### 32. changed: who the carpenters are (II.16 ¶86; White ¶79)
- The French has Nab and Pencroff; White has Neb and Herbert. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Nab et Pencroff étaient devenus de très-habiles charpentiers
  - white: Neb and Herbert had become expert carpenters

### 33. changed: the two millstones (II.16 ¶86; White ¶79)
- The bed stone and the running stone become "the runner and the feeder". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: la meule gisante et la meule courante
  - white: the runner and the feeder

### 34. cut: the "babillard" (II.16 ¶86; White ¶79)
- White gives the oscillating trough but not its nickname or the reason. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: auquel son perpétuel tic-tac a fait donner le nom de « babillard »
  - white: the oscillating bucket, designed to regulate the passage of the grain

### 35. changed: who is anxious (II.16 ¶95; White ¶87)
- In the French Spilett and Harbert are worried by the absence; White says all. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Ils s’inquiétaient sérieusement de cette disparition prolongée.
  - white: all began to be seriously alarmed at his prolonged absence

### 36. addition: "alone" (II.16 ¶95; White ¶87)
- White says Smith alone persisted. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Toutefois, Cyrus Smith, par une sorte de pressentiment
  - white: Smith alone, by a sort of presentiment

### 37. cut: "ce pauvre repenti" (II.16 ¶96; White ¶88)
- *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ce pauvre repenti
  - white: —

### 38. changed: who hears the cries (II.16 ¶102; White ¶92)
- In the French Cyrus and Spilett are too far to hear and only Pencroff and Nab run; in White they call the others and all run. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Cyrus Smith et le reporter, trop éloignés, n’avaient pu entendre ces cris.
  - white: who summoned the others, and all rushed towards the lake

### 39. cut: he pushes the jaguar with his foot (II.16 ¶106; White ¶96)
- *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: L’inconnu le poussa du pied
  - white: —

### 40. changed: who asks for the hand (II.16 ¶113; White ¶103)
- In the French Cyrus asks; White gives the question to Herbert. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Voulez-vous me donner votre main ? »
  - white: “Will you not shake hands with me?” asked Herbert.

### 41. changed: "que prétendez-vous être pour moi" (II.16 ¶115; White ¶105)
- "What do you claim to be to me" becomes "what are you going to do with me". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: que prétendez-vous être pour moi ?
  - white: what are you going to do with me?

## Outline slips

- Beat 6 of section 6 says "A dozen bushels"; the French says about ten. *(Drafted.)*
  - outline: A dozen bushels sown yearly are enough to feed men and beasts.
  - quote: il suffisait de semer une dizaine de boisseaux
- Beat 4 of section 3 gives "December" without remark; the scene is placed on 3 November in the French, so the month is Verne's slip and a later date count may need it flagged. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - outline: Passing Harbert he asks the month and Harbert says December.
  - quote: Décembre, répondit Harbert.
