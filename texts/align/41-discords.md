# II.19: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller m. Beads corrected: none.*

### 1. softened: the civil war (II.19 ¶2; White ¶2)
- "La guerre civile" becomes "civil strife", a milder name for the Civil War. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: cette patrie déchirée par la guerre civile
  - white: that land which, when they left it, was torn by civil strife

### 2. changed: the twelve years are Ayrton's years on Tabor (II.19 ¶18; White ¶18)
- The French says Ayrton was abandoned twelve years ago; White says he "has been here" twelve years, which puts the twelve years on Lincoln Island, where he has been five months. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: voilà douze ans qu’Ayrton a été abandonné
  - white: as already Ayrton has been here twelve years!

### 3. cut: the notice cannot fail to be found (II.19 ¶22; White ¶22)
- White drops that the notice would be one Lord Glenarvan or his crew cannot fail to find, and that the cabin was Ayrton's home as well as Grant's. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: notice que Lord Glenarvan ou son équipage ne pourront manquer de trouver
  - white: a notice, giving the situation of our island

### 4. changed: the Scots become "Stockmen" (II.19 ¶30; White ¶30)
- The French says the Scots (the Duncan's people); White calls them "Stockmen". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: car si les Écossais y sont revenus
  - white: for those Stockmen would, necessarily, leave some trace of their visit

### 5. changed: who answers Pencroff (II.19 ¶33; White ¶33)
- The French gives the line to the engineer; White gives it to the reporter. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Non, Pencroff, répondit l’ingénieur
  - white: “No indeed, Pencroff,” replied the reporter

### 6. cut: the ship that would not be built, and where it would have gone (II.19 ¶34; White ¶34)
- White drops the two destinations such a ship would have served, the northern archipelagos and New Zealand. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: soit jusqu’aux archipels, dans le nord, soit jusqu’à la Nouvelle-Zélande, dans l’ouest
  - white: there was no longer any question of building a ship

### 7. cut: the bay is shaped like a shark's jaw (II.19 ¶35; White ¶35)
- White drops the likeness that gives the Shark Gulf its name. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: comme une mâchoire de requin
  - white: and of the narrow bay between them

### 8. changed: the west gale lasts days, not weeks (II.19 ¶37; White ¶37)
- The French says the gale lasted five or six days; White says five or six weeks. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: un fort coup de vent d’ouest qui dura cinq à six jours
  - white: which lasted for five or six weeks

### 9. condensed: the barometer reading (II.19 ¶37; White ¶37)
- The French gives the figure, 29.9 inches (759 mm 45); White gives "a high figure". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: vingt-neuf pouces et neuf dixièmes (759mm,45)
  - white: at a high figure

### 10. cut: the island's perimeter (II.19 ¶40; White ¶40)
- White drops that the island measures ninety miles round, of which the south coast has some twenty. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Sur les quatre-vingt-dix milles que mesurait le périmètre de l’île
  - white: The distance between these two points, following the coast, was twenty miles.

### 11. cut: six hours of flood against them (II.19 ¶41; White ¶40)
- White gives the two hours of ebb but not the six hours of flood that were hard to stem, which is why the day was lost. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: eut, au contraire, six heures de flot qu’il fut très-difficile d’étaler
  - white: they had had on starting but two hours of the ebb

### 12. cut: the date, 17 April, and the west coast (II.19 ¶44; White ¶43)
- White has "the next morning" and "the shore"; the French gives the date and says they ran close along the west coast. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Le lendemain, 17 avril, Pencroff appareilla dès le point du jour
  - white: at daylight the next morning the sailor raised anchor, and, with a free wind, skirted the shore

### 13. changed: height of the basalt blocks (II.19 ¶48; White ¶47)
- The French has blocks from twenty to three hundred feet high; White has twenty to thirty. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: depuis vingt pieds jusqu’à trois cents pieds de hauteur
  - white: from twenty to thirty feet high

### 14. cut: how high the cirrus stand (II.19 ¶57; White ¶55)
- White drops that the cirrus are never lower than five thousand feet. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dont la hauteur n’est jamais inférieure à cinq mille pieds au-dessus du niveau de la mer
  - white: scattered high above, in the zenith

### 15. cut: what Pencroff's "œuvres vives" are (II.19 ¶67; White —)
- White has nothing for the narrator's gloss that the "œuvres vives" are the immersed part of the hull and that Pencroff holds it dearer than his own skin. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Ce que Pencroff appelait œuvres vives, c’était la partie immergée de la carène de son embarcation
  - white: —

### 16. cut: when they will be abreast of the cape (II.19 ¶72; White ¶69)
- White drops Pencroff's reckoning that they will be abreast of the cape between noon and one. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: nous serons par le travers du cap entre midi et une heure
  - white: That will take two hours and a half.

### 17. changed: the tide turns at seven, and they enter at sunrise (II.19 ¶77; White ¶74)
- The French has Pencroff keep the sea until the flood, at seven in the evening, and if no light is left enter the gulf at sunrise; White says the tide turns about one o'clock, which is the hour the reckoning in ¶72 gave for the ebb, and says only that they will beat on and off until daylight. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: jusqu’au flot, c’est-à-dire jusqu’à sept heures du soir
  - white: until the tide turns, which will be about 1 o’clock
