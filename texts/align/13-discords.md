### 1. condensed: tailor's assistant on state ships (I.13 ¶8; White ¶5)
- Pencroff was an assistant tailor on the ships of the state in the French; White has an assistant tailor on board ship. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: aide-tailleur sur les bâtiments de l’état
  - white: an assistant tailor on board ship

### 2. cut: Cyrus's eye falls on Top (I.13 ¶22; White ¶19)
- White drops that Cyrus's gaze turned to Top, who was going to and fro on the shore; his face brightens without a cause. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ses regards se portèrent vers Top, qui allait et venait sur le rivage
  - white: Suddenly his face brightened:

### 3. changed: "five miles at most" becomes "about five miles" (I.13 ¶29; White ¶24)
- The French sets an upper limit of five miles to the clearing; White says about five. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: après une marche de cinq milles au plus
  - white: after a walk of about five miles

### 4. cut: the crejimba bears no edible fruit (I.13 ¶30; White ¶25)
- White drops that the palm bears no edible fruit. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qui ne porte pas de fruits comestibles
  - white: It was the “crejimba,” of the palm family.

### 5. addition: "Indian hemp" (I.13 ¶30; White ¶25)
- White adds a gloss, Indian hemp, for the hibiscus. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: un « hibiscus heterophyllus », qui fournit des fibres d’une ténacité remarquable
  - white: “hibiscus heterophyltus” (Indian hemp)

### 6. changed: fixing the island's orientation becomes its position (I.13 ¶33; White ¶28)
- Cyrus sets about fixing the orientation of the island in the French; White says its position, which the later latitude and longitude work will mean something else by. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: s’occupa de fixer l’orientation de l’île
  - white: Smith occupied himself in determining the position of the island.

### 7. cut: the footnote on the sunrise and sunset (I.13 ¶34 n1; White —)
- White drops the footnote giving the hours of sunrise and sunset for that date and latitude. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le soleil se levait à 5 h. 33 m. du matin, et se couchait à 6 h. 17 m. du soir
  - white: —

### 8. changed: the jacamar becomes a kingfisher (I.13 ¶36; White ¶31)
- The wood is named for the bird they chased; White names the Jacamar Wood but calls the bird a kingfisher. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le nom de bois du Jacamar, en souvenir du volatile que Pencroff et Harbert avaient poursuivi
  - white: Jacamar Wood, after the kingfisher which Pencroff and Herbert had pursued there

### 9. changed: they could not have come away unhurt (I.13 ¶39; White ¶34)
- The French says Spilett and Harbert would perhaps not have got off without a serious wound; White says they could hardly have killed the beast without being wounded. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: car ils ne s’en seraient peut-être pas tirés sans quelque grave blessure
  - white: as they could hardly have killed it without being themselves wounded

### 10. condensed: cups moulded on pebbles (I.13 ¶47; White ¶39)
- White drops that the cups were moulded on pebbles of suitable shapes. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: des tasses qui avaient été moulées sur des galets de formes convenables
  - white: they moulded pots and mugs

### 11. changed: coarse charming pipes become large pipes he would have found perfect (I.13 ¶48; White ¶40)
- Pencroff's pipes are rather coarse, which he finds charming; White makes them large and nearly perfect but for tobacco. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: se fabriqua quelques pipes assez grossières, qu’il trouva charmantes
  - white: made some large pipes, which he would have found perfect

### 12. changed: the genus of the armoise (I.13 ¶51; White ¶43)
- The genus counts among its species wormwood, lemon-balm, tarragon and gépi; White has wormwood and mint. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: l’absinthe, la citronnelle, l’estragon, le gépi
  - white: wormwood, mint, etc.

### 13. changed: Pencroff's question becomes a guess (I.13 ¶54; White ¶46)
- Pencroff asks whether the plant is tobacco; in White he announces it is. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Bonté du ciel ! Est-ce du tabac ?
  - white: Ah, I know! It’s tobacco!

### 14. changed: the Southern Cross is placed at the zenith (I.13 ¶58; White ¶50)
- The French has the circumpolar constellations shining in the southern zenith, the Cross among them; White has the Cross itself riding in the zenith above them. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Au zénith austral, les constellations circompolaires resplendissaient
  - white: in the zenith, shining resplendent above the circumpolar constellations, rode the Southern Cross.

### 15. cut: the Cross greeted from Mount Franklin, and the lunar dawn (I.13 ¶58; White ¶50)
- White drops that this is the Cross the engineer had saluted a few days before on the summit of Mount Franklin, and the silvering of the horizon he calls the lunar dawn. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: cette Croix du Sud que l’ingénieur, quelques jours auparavant, saluait à la cime du mont Franklin
  - white: —

### 16. condensed: the three places to measure the distance from (I.13 ¶65; White ¶55)
- White reduces the American and Australian continents and the principal archipelagoes of the Pacific to "its locality". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: soit du continent américain, soit du continent australien, soit des principaux archipels du Pacifique
  - white: we must find out its locality.

### 17. condensed: the acacia thorn (I.13 ¶69; White ¶58)
- White drops that the pivot of the wooden compass was a strong acacia thorn from the dead wood of the woodpile. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: une forte épine d’acacia, que fournit le bois mort du bûcher
  - white: two little flat sticks which he fastened together with a thorn

### 18. cut: the Mercy was too deep to cross (I.13 ¶70; White ¶58)
- White drops that the best station, the south-facing shore, would need crossing the Mercy, then deep. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il eût fallu traverser la Mercy, alors profonde, et c’était une difficulté
  - white: But as the sea horizon was hidden from this point by Claw Cape, the engineer determined to make his observation from Prospect Plateau

### 19. changed: the plateau edge facing north-west and south-east (I.13 ¶72; White ¶59)
- The edge they stand on runs north-west to south-east along the Mercy, and they go up its left bank; White says the edge faced the southeast. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ils vinrent se placer sur la lisière qui s’orientait nord-ouest et sud-est
  - white: went to the edge of the plateau which faced the southeast
