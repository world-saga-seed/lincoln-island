# The brig with the black flag (notes)

## Notes for drafting

- **Where it sits.** The French III.1, the first chapter of the third part, "Le secret de l’île"; follows 04200 without a gap, on the afternoon and night of 17 October 1867. *(Drafted.)*
- **Cast.**
  - Cyrus Smith (¶3), Harbert (¶3), Gédéon Spilett (¶3), Pencroff (¶3), Nab (¶3). *(Drafted.)*
  - Ayrton, in the telegram exchange (¶20) and at Granite-house from ¶37. *(Drafted.)*
  - Lord Glenarvan, in talk (¶15). *(Drafted.)*
  - The crew of the brig, unseen; "pirates" is the colonists' inference (¶75–¶77). *(Drafted.)*
- **Places.**
  - The great hall of Granite-house, its window (¶3, ¶39); the telegraph at Granite-house (¶17); the corral (¶17); Tabor Island (¶12, ¶15). *(Drafted.)*
  - Union Bay (¶62); Claw Cape (¶57); Mandible Cape (¶92); Washington Bay and Port Balloon (¶57, ¶61, ¶81); Prospect Heights and its mill (¶79); the edge of Jacamar Wood (¶82). *(Drafted.)*
- **Things.**
  - The long telescope, the telegraph (¶3, ¶17); the brig, with a black flag at her gaff (¶66–¶74); a cannon (¶99–¶100); an anchor chain (¶103). *(Drafted.)*
  - The mill's wings (¶79); branches and creepers laid over the windows (¶82); arms and ammunition (¶82). *(Drafted.)*
  - Two plates: Ayrton with the telescope (pl1, after ¶53) and the black flag (pl2, after ¶75). *(Drafted.)*
- **Time.**
  - 17 October (¶1); the telegram about 3 p.m., since Ayrton arrives "About four o'clock, an hour after he had been sent for" (¶37) (hour of the telegram is from that line). *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - About five p.m., the ship may vanish behind Claw Cape (¶57). About half past seven, sun gone twenty minutes (¶92). "In half an hour we shall know." (¶68). Night, new moon (¶97). *(Drafted.)*
  - Two and a half years since the landing (¶1). *(Drafted.)*
  - The chapter covers some six or seven hours, from the photograph's afternoon to the first anchor. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
- **Clues.**
  - The ship's black flag and cannon: pirates, or "a suspicious ship" (¶75). *(Drafted.)*
  - "It is not the Duncan." Ayrton's reaction, "Already!", his silence, his "My duty" (¶43–¶53, ¶65, ¶90). *(Drafted.)*
  - Smith asks whether the pirates have been to the island before, which "would have explained certain peculiarities that had remained inexplicable until then", and whether there is an accomplice (¶94). *(Drafted.)*
  - Smith had earlier said the Duncan might carry the black flag "if the convicts had succeeded in their criminal plans" (¶77). *(Drafted.)*
  - Smith's wish to leave and annex the island for the Union (¶32). *(Drafted.)*
- **Length.** 104 paragraphs (¶1–¶104), about 3,650 words of paragraph text. Two plates (pl1 after ¶53, pl2 after ¶75). *(Drafted.)*

## Open and flags

- The telegraph is described in II.18 ¶48 as an electromagnet, a plate, and "a needle set on a dial"; here ¶19 reads "the bell rang". Card neither. *(Drafted.)*
- ¶61 decides to light a fire at Port Balloon; ¶79 orders "Let all the fires be put out." The text moves from one to the other when the black flag is seen (¶62–¶77). Card neither. *(Drafted.)*
- ¶92 puts the brig at "not more than eight miles" ("Il n'en était pas à plus de huit milles") at half past seven; ¶102 says "a mile and a quarter from the coast" after the shot. Card neither. *(Drafted.)*
- ¶1 says "For two and a half years"; the chapter gives no landing date (II.18 ¶82 gives 26 March as the second anniversary). *(Drafted.)*
- ¶15 gives Tabor at 150 miles in longitude and 75 in latitude; II.19 ¶8 says the nearest land is "at least" twelve hundred miles, which is about land other than Tabor. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
- ¶97 gives a new moon on 17 October; II.19 ¶73 gives a full moon on 18 April. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
- No Wikisource correction touches this chapter. *(Drafted.)*
