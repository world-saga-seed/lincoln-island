# A2: I.1 ¶14

¶14

No one, doubtless, has forgotten the terrible north-east gale which broke loose in the midst of the equinox of that year, and during which the barometer fell to seven hundred and ten millimetres. It was a hurricane, without intermission, which lasted from the 18th to the 26th of March. The ravages which it produced were immense in America, in Europe, and in Asia, over a zone eighteen hundred miles broad, which was drawn obliquely to the equator, from the thirty-fifth parallel north to the fortieth parallel south! Towns overthrown, forests uprooted, shores laid waste by mountains of water which rushed upon them like the bore of a river, vessels cast on the coast, which the returns of the Bureau Veritas numbered by hundreds, whole territories levelled by whirlwinds which crushed everything in their passage, several thousand persons crushed on land or swallowed up at sea: such were the testimonies of its fury which were left behind it by this formidable hurricane. It surpassed in disasters those which so frightfully ravaged Havana and Guadaloupe, the one on the 25th of October, 1810, the other on the 26th of July, 1825.
