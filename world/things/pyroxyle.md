# The pyroxyle: guncotton made from elder pith to replace the powder

*A guncotton Smith made in early 1866 by steeping cellulose from elder pith in nitric acid and sulphuric acid, to spare the powder and lead of the chest; a stronger and a more dangerous charge than ordinary powder.*

Keywords: pyroxyle, fulmi-coton, guncotton, coton-poudre, cellulose, sureau, elder, poudre, powder, charge, hunters

- **What it is.**
  - Smith could readily make a substance like guncotton, having nitric acid at his disposal (I.17 ¶70). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: il pouvait facilement obtenir une substance analogue au coton-poudre, puisqu’il avait de l’acide azotique à sa disposition
  - Smith says it would be easy to make guncotton or even ordinary powder, having nitric acid, saltpetre, sulphur and charcoal, but it is arms that the colonists lack (I.18 ¶9). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: il serait aisé de fabriquer de la poudre-coton, ou même de la poudre ordinaire
  - In 1866 Smith tells the hunters to spare their ammunition and takes steps to replace the chest's powder and lead, which he wants to keep for the future (II.8 ¶7). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: il prit des mesures pour remplacer la poudre et le plomb qui avaient été trouvés dans la caisse
    - quote: qu’il voulait réserver pour l’avenir
  - He might have made powder, having saltpetre, sulphur and charcoal, but the preparation needs extreme care and, without special tools, is hard to make in good quality (II.8 ¶8). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: il avait à sa disposition du salpêtre, du soufre et du charbon
    - quote: cette préparation demande des soins extrêmes
  - He prefers to make pyroxyle, that is guncotton, in which cotton is not indispensable, since it enters only as cellulose (II.8 ¶9). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: fabriquer du pyroxyle, c’est-à-dire du fulmi-coton
    - quote: il n’y entre que comme cellulose
  - Cellulose is the elementary tissue of plants, found nearly pure in cotton, in the textile fibres of hemp and flax, in paper, old linen and elder pith (II.8 ¶9). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: le tissu élémentaire des végétaux
    - quote: la moelle de sureau
  - The cellulose comes from elder pith, to be gathered; elders grow plentifully on the island near the mouth of the Red Creek (II.8 ¶9, ¶10). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: les sureaux abondaient dans l’île, vers l’embouchure du Creek-Rouge
    - quote: il suffisait de la récolter
  - The other substance needed is fuming nitric acid; Smith, having sulphuric acid, had already easily made nitric acid by attacking the natural saltpetre (see [the nitric acid](nitric-acid.md)) (II.8 ¶10). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: de l’acide sulfurique à sa disposition, avait déjà pu facilement produire de l’acide azotique, en attaquant le salpêtre que lui fournissait la nature
  - Its drawbacks are a great inequality of effect, an excessive inflammability (it takes fire at a hundred and seventy degrees instead of two hundred and forty) and too instantaneous a deflagration that can damage firearms (II.8 ¶11). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: une grande inégalité d’effet
    - quote: il s’enflamme à cent soixante-dix degrés au lieu de deux cent quarante
    - quote: une déflagration trop instantanée qui peut dégrader les armes à feu
  - Its advantages are that it is not altered by damp, does not foul the gun barrel, and has a propulsive force four times that of ordinary powder (II.8 ¶11). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: il ne s’altérait pas par l’humidité
    - quote: qu’il n’encrassait pas le canon des fusils
    - quote: sa force propulsive était quadruple de celle de la poudre ordinaire
  - After the powder of Tabor is brought home, its use is to go on, since it gives excellent results and there is no reason to replace it with ordinary powder (see [the Tabor powder](the-tabor-powder.md)) (II.15 ¶46). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: l’emploi du pyroxyle dut être continué, car, cette substance donnant d’excellents résultats, il n’y avait aucune raison pour y substituer la poudre ordinaire
  - Smith says it will never run short, and so uses it instead of ordinary powder, whose stock he wishes to leave intact, to try the cannon (III.5 ¶27). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: dont je tiens à laisser l’approvisionnement intact
    - quote: le pyroxile, qui ne nous manquera jamais
  - Its explosive power is four times that of ordinary powder (III.5 ¶34). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: sa puissance explosive, qui, on l’a dit, est quadruple de celle de la poudre ordinaire
  - Smith thinks the steel cannon will bear the expansion of its gases without risk (III.5 ¶31). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: nos canons supporteront sans risque l’expansion des gaz du pyroxile
- **Made from.**
  - To make it one steeps cellulose for a quarter of an hour in fuming nitric acid, washes it in plenty of water and dries it (II.8 ¶12). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: plonger pendant un quart d’heure de la cellulose dans de l’acide azotique fumant, puis de laver à grande eau et de faire sécher
  - Smith had only ordinary nitric acid, not fuming, so he mixed it with concentrated sulphuric acid in the proportion of three volumes to five, and got the same result (II.8 ¶13). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: de l’acide azotique ordinaire, mélangé dans la proportion de trois volumes à cinq volumes d’acide sulfurique concentré
    - quote: devait obtenir le même résultat, et il l’obtint
  - The hunters soon have a perfectly prepared substance that, used with discretion, gives excellent results (II.8 ¶13). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: employée avec discrétion, donna d’excellents résultats
- **Appears in:** I.17, I.18, II.8, II.15, III.5
- **Open:**
  - The sulphuric acid is said to be mixed in "three volumes to five volumes" with the nitric acid, without saying which is the larger (II.8 ¶13). *(Drafted.)*
    - quote: dans la proportion de trois volumes à cinq volumes d’acide sulfurique concentré
