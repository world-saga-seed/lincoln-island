# III.9: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller o. Beads corrected: 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 18, 19 (White ¶10 renders French ¶11 only; French ¶12 and ¶13 have no White; White ¶11 renders ¶14, ¶12 renders ¶15, ¶13 ¶16, ¶14 ¶17, ¶15 ¶18, ¶16 ¶19; White ¶18 renders ¶21–¶22 and ¶19 renders ¶22's "And I too"; White ¶20 renders ¶23).*

### 1. cut: the convicts will try to retake the corral (III.9 ¶12; White —)
- Pencroff's agreement, that the convicts will do everything to regain the well-stocked corral and that Smith alone could not hold it, is dropped. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Ils feront tout pour reprendre le corral, qu’ils savent être bien approvisionné !
  - white: —

### 2. cut: at Granite-house three could search the forests (III.9 ¶13; White —)
- Smith's reply, that at Granite-house one could stay with Harbert and three search the forests, but at the corral all must leave together, is dropped. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Là, je ne craindrais pas de laisser Harbert avec l’un de nous, et les trois autres iraient fouiller les forêts de l’île.
  - white: —

### 3. cut: the return put off to December (III.9 ¶26; White —)
- The narrator's note that eight days puts the return to Granite-house only in the first days of December is dropped. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Cela remettait le retour à Granite-house aux premiers jours de décembre seulement.
  - white: —

### 4. changed: full foliage becomes "fall leaf" (III.9 ¶27; White ¶23)
- The French says the forests were in full leaf; White's "fall leaf" reads as autumn. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: Les forêts de l’île étaient en pleine frondaison
  - white: The woods were in fall leaf

### 5. cut: farm work after the return (III.9 ¶27; White ¶23)
- The French says the return to the plateau would be followed by farm work interrupted only by the expedition; White drops it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: La rentrée au plateau de Grande-Vue serait donc suivie de grands travaux agricoles
  - white: It can easily be understood how this siege in the corral upset the plans of the colonists.

### 6. cut: the convicts busy elsewhere (III.9 ¶30; White ¶25)
- The French concludes that for the moment at least there was nothing to fear and the convicts were busy in another part of the island; White says only that there was nothing to fear. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: les convicts étaient occupés dans une autre partie de l’île
  - white: so long as the dog did not bark, there was nothing to fear.

### 7. cut: Ben Joyce and the hope of a traitor (III.9 ¶41; White —)
- The French supposes one convict may have found in Ayrton an old Australian comrade, Ben Joyce, the escaped convicts' chief, who might hope to turn him; White drops the paragraph. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Peut-être même l’un d’eux avait-il retrouvé dans Ayrton un ancien compagnon d’Australie, le Ben Joyce, le chef des convicts évadés ?
  - white: —

### 8. cut: Ayrton as a prisoner might escape (III.9 ¶42; White —)
- The French says the incident was taken as a hope, and that Ayrton, if a prisoner, would try to escape and be a strong ally; White drops it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ce serait un puissant auxiliaire pour les colons !
  - white: —

### 9. cut: Ayrton does not know of the attack on Harbert (III.9 ¶43; White —)
- Spilett says that if Ayrton escapes he will go straight to Granite-house, not knowing of the attempt on Harbert; White drops it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il ne connaît pas la tentative d’assassinat dont Harbert a été victime
  - white: —

### 10. cut: the plateau, plantations and poultry-yard at risk (III.9 ¶44; White —)
- Pencroff's fear that the convicts, unable to touch Granite-house, may sack the plateau, plantations and poultry-yard is dropped. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: saccager le plateau, nos plantations, notre basse-cour
  - white: —

### 11. cut: Pencroff the farmer (III.9 ¶45; White ¶34)
- The narrator's remark that Pencroff had become a true farmer, attached to his harvests, is dropped. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Pencroff était devenu un vrai fermier, attaché de cœur à ses récoltes.
  - white: —

### 12. cut: Spilett refuses to give the order to go (III.9 ¶46; White —)
- Harbert presses Spilett several times, who fears the wounds, ill-healed, may reopen on the road; White drops it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: que les plaies d’Harbert, mal cicatrisées, ne se rouvrissent en route
  - white: —

### 13. cut: pain and remorse foretold (III.9 ¶47; White —)
- The narrator's warning that yielding to Harbert may cost them pain and remorse, and the mention of an incident, are dropped. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Dieu sait ce que cette détermination pouvait leur causer de douleurs et de remords !
  - white: —

### 14. cut: the wagon brought by Ayrton (III.9 ¶71; White ¶51)
- The French says the wagon was the one Ayrton had brought to the corral; White speaks only of the wagon. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: le chariot qui avait été amené par Ayrton au corral
  - white: The question of Herbert’s transportation was quickly decided.

### 15. condensed: his will holds his ebbing strength (III.9 ¶79; White ¶57)
- The French says Harbert holds back by sheer will his failing strength; White says only a tremendous effort. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: par une suprême volonté, il retenait ses forces prêtes à s’éteindre
  - white: They could see that the poor fellow was making a tremendous effort.

### 16. softened: to refuse might kill him (III.9 ¶80; White ¶57)
- The French says to refuse would be to drive Harbert to despair, perhaps to kill him; White leaves out the killing. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Mais c’eût été désespérer Harbert, le tuer peut-être.
  - white: But to stay would have thrown Herbert into despair.

### 17. condensed: another road would have been better (III.9 ¶83; White ¶60)
- The French says a road other than the direct one would have been better but the wagon could not go under the trees; White gives only that the wagon required the direct road. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: mieux aurait valu prendre une route autre que celle qui allait directement du corral à Granite-house
  - white: It was necessary, on account of the wagon, to keep to the direct road from the corral to Granite House, although it was known to the convicts.

### 18. cut: the hour they left the corral (III.9 ¶86; White ¶61)
- The French fixes departure at half past seven; White says only an hour after leaving. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Il avait quitté le corral à sept heures et demie.
  - white: an hour after leaving the corral

### 19. cut: the woods as deserted as at the landing (III.9 ¶87; White —)
- The road and the Jacamar wood between the Mercy and the lake are empty, the thickets as deserted as on the day of landing; White drops it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Les taillis semblaient être aussi déserts qu’au jour où les colons atterrirent sur l’île.
  - white: —

### 20. cut: Smith's reasoning about the bridge (III.9 ¶88; White ¶62)
- Smith expects the Glycerine Creek bridge to be in place, either because the convicts came in by it or lowered it after crossing another stream to keep a retreat; White drops this. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: soit que, après avoir passé un des cours d’eau qui fermaient l’enceinte, ils eussent pris la précaution de l’abaisser, afin de se ménager une retraite
  - white: They drew near the plateau another mile, and they saw the causeway over Glycerine Creek.
