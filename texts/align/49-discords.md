# III.7: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller o. Beads corrected: 37, 38, 39, 40 (White ¶37 renders French ¶53–¶54; White ¶38 renders ¶55; White ¶39 renders ¶56; White ¶40 renders ¶57 and ¶59, with ¶58 dropped).*

### 1. cut: the props behind the inner door (III.7 ¶9; White ¶8)
- The French has Spilett and Pencroff throw down the props that held the door from inside; White has them simply make their way into the house. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ils renversaient les étais qui maintenaient la porte intérieurement
  - white: made their way into the empty house

### 2. cut: Pencroff's wish to break his head (III.7 ¶11; White ¶9)
- Pencroff's despair, that he wanted to dash his head against the wall, is dropped. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il voulait se briser la tête contre la muraille
  - white: He sobbed and cried bitterly

### 3. condensed: wounds by blade and by firearm (III.7 ¶12; White ¶10)
- Spilett has treated wounds from a blade and from a firearm; White gives only gunshot wounds. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: soit par une arme blanche, soit par une arme à feu
  - white: the surgery of gunshot wounds

### 4. changed: shock from the ball striking bone becomes concussion of the brain (III.7 ¶13; White ¶11)
- The French puts the stupor down to blood loss or to the shock of a ball striking a bone; White says concussion of the brain. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: si la balle avait heurté un os avec assez de force pour déterminer une secousse violente
  - white: caused either by the hemorrhage or by concussion of the brain

### 5. softened: a roar becomes a groan (III.7 ¶22; White ¶16)
- Pencroff roars at the word "dead"; White has a groan. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qui poussa un rugissement !
  - white: with a groan

### 6. cut: "a mortal wound perhaps" (III.7 ¶27; White ¶20)
- Spilett's thought that the wound may be mortal, and his questions on which topics to use, are dropped. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: blessure mortelle peut-être !
  - white: —

### 7. softened: Pencroff's curse and fist (III.7 ¶34–¶35; White —)
- Pencroff's curse and his fist raised to heaven, and Smith's rebuke, are dropped. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Malédiction ! s’écria le marin, dont le poing se tourna vers le ciel.
  - white: —

### 8. cut: the wounds need no enlarging (III.7 ¶41; White ¶29)
- The French says the wounds did not seem to need enlarging; White drops it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il ne sembla pas qu’elles eussent besoin d’être débridées
  - white: It was decided therefore to dress the two wounds, but not to press them together.

### 9. changed: the ball may have crossed the lung (III.7 ¶49; White ¶34)
- Spilett says the ball may have passed through the lung; White says touched. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: peut-être même la balle a-t-elle traversé le poumon
  - white: perhaps the ball has touched the lung

### 10. cut: Ayrton carried off, surprised or killed (III.7 ¶53; White ¶37)
- The three questions, whether Ayrton was carried off by his old accomplices, surprised in the corral, or killed after a struggle, are dropped; White gives only the last. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Le malheureux avait-il été entraîné par ses anciens complices ?
  - white: it seemed probable that he had resisted his former companions, and fallen by their hands

### 11. cut: the convict seen fleeing by the south buttress (III.7 ¶53; White ¶37)
- Spilett saw a convict flee by the south buttress of Mount Franklin with Top after him, one of those whose boat broke at the mouth of the Mercy; White has none of it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: avait parfaitement aperçu l’un des convicts qui s’enfuyait par le contrefort sud du mont Franklin
  - white: —

### 12. cut: the dead man belonged to Bob Harvey's band (III.7 ¶53; White ¶37)
- The convict Smith killed, found outside the enclosure, belonged to Bob Harvey's band; White drops this. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: appartenait bien à la bande de Bob Harvey
  - white: —

### 13. changed: no sign of a struggle becomes no sign of the pirates (III.7 ¶54; White ¶37)
- The French says there was no trace of a struggle at house or palisade; White says no trace of the pirates. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: On ne voyait, non plus, aucune trace de lutte
  - white: They could see no traces of the pirates either in the dwelling or the palisade.

### 14. cut: Ayrton was not there, dead or alive (III.7 ¶56; White ¶39)
- Spilett's closing point, that Ayrton, dead or alive, was no longer in the corral when the convicts fled, is dropped. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Il est bien évident aussi qu’à ce moment Ayrton, mort ou vivant, n’était plus ici.
  - white: “Yes,” replied the reporter, “and then, no doubt, they took possession here, where they found everything in great plenty, and took to flight only when they saw us coming.”

### 15. cut: Pencroff's foreboding (III.7 ¶57; White ¶40)
- Smith's remark that Pencroff was right to want the convicts hunted like wild beasts, and that it would have spared them misfortune, is dropped. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Les pressentiments de Pencroff ne le trompaient pas, quand il voulait qu’on leur donnât la chasse comme à des bêtes fauves.
  - white: “We must beat the woods,” said the engineer, and rid the island of these wretches.

### 16. softened: "the right to be without pity" (III.7 ¶58; White —)
- Spilett's answer, that now they have the right to be pitiless, is dropped. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: maintenant nous avons le droit d’être sans pitié !
  - white: —

### 17. cut: Nab will be struck down (III.7 ¶68; White —)
- Spilett's reply, that Nab will be even less on his guard than they and will be struck, is dropped. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il sera encore moins sur ses gardes que nous ne le serions nous-mêmes
  - white: —

### 18. cut: "Is there no way to warn him?" (III.7 ¶69; White —)
- Smith's question whether there is no means of warning Nab is dropped. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: N’y a-t-il donc pas moyen de le prévenir ?
  - white: —

### 19. cut: Top can make the road in under half an hour (III.7 ¶79; White ¶58)
- The French says Top could cover the road in less than half an hour and might pass unseen in grass or woodland edge; White keeps only that the road was familiar. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: En moins d’une demi-heure, il pouvait l’avoir franchie
  - white: He understood what was wanted, and the road was familiar to him.
