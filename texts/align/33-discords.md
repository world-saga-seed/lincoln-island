# II.11: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller j. Beads corrected: 22 to 35. From Bead 22 the machine pairing runs one or more White paragraphs out of step, and I labelled from the true pairing: French ¶24 with White ¶21; ¶25–¶26 with White ¶22; ¶27–¶29 with White ¶23; ¶30 with White ¶24; ¶31 with ¶25; ¶32 with ¶26; ¶33 with ¶27; ¶34 with ¶28; ¶35 with ¶29; ¶36–¶38 with White ¶30–¶32; ¶39–¶41 with White ¶33; ¶42–¶65 with nothing in White; ¶66 with White ¶34. Bead 36 onward is paired correctly.*

### 1. cut: June as "the December of the northern zones" (II.11 ¶1; White ¶1)
- The French glosses June as the December of the northern zones; White says only that winter came with June. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ce mois de juin, qui est le décembre des zones boréales
  - white: Winter came with June

### 2. condensed: the machines Smith lacks (II.11 ¶3; White ¶2)
- The French lists carders, combers, smoothers, drawers, twisters, the mule-jenny and the self-acting spinner and the loom; White says only "mill machinery". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ni cardeuses, ni peigneuses, ni lisseuses, ni étireuses, ni retordeuses
  - white: not having any mill machinery

### 3. condensed: seventy degrees and twenty-four hours (II.11 ¶4; White ¶3)
- The French has the wool soaked twenty-four hours in vats at seventy degrees, then washed in soda baths; White says it was soaked in warm water and washed with soda. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: portées à la température de soixante-dix degrés, et dans lesquelles la laine plongea pendant vingt-quatre heures
  - white: soaking it in warm water

### 4. cut: felt known from the earliest times (II.11 ¶5; White —)
- The French remark that felt is of the oldest kind of woollen stuff and was made this way first is not in White. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: doit avoir été connu dès les époques les plus reculées
  - white: —

### 5. cut: the fulling mill's history (II.11 ¶7; White ¶5)
- The French says the machine is as it was for centuries, until pestles gave way to compressing cylinders; White stops at "such was the machine in question". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: telle elle avait été pendant des siècles, jusqu’au moment où l’on eut l’idée de remplacer les pilons par des cylindres compresseurs
  - white: such was the machine in question

### 6. condensed: the fabrics Lincoln felt is not (II.11 ¶8; White ¶6)
- The French lists merino, muslin, cashmere, reps, satin, Orleans, alpaca, cloth and flannel; White says "one of the stuffs of commerce". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: ni du mérinos, ni de la mousseline, ni du cachemire d’écosse
  - white: It was not, indeed, one of the stuffs of commerce

### 7. changed: "perhaps" Pencroff does not understand his obstinacy becomes flat (II.11 ¶12; White ¶9)
- The French says Pencroff perhaps does not understand his own obstinacy; White states it as fact. *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: dont peut-être celui-ci ne se rendait pas bien compte
  - white: an obstinacy for which Pencroff himself could not account

### 8. changed: "console ourselves" becomes "forgive ourselves" (II.11 ¶23; White ¶20)
- Smith asks whether they would ever be consoled for the loss of Pencroff and Harbert; White asks whether they would ever forgive themselves, which is a different feeling. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: croyez-vous que nous nous en consolerions jamais
  - white: do you think that we would ever forgive ourselves

### 9. changed: Pencroff's promise not to cause grief becomes "we won't discuss such mishaps" (II.11 ¶24; White ¶21)
- Pencroff answers "we shall not cause you that grief"; White has him decline to discuss mishaps. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: nous ne vous causerons pas ce chagrin-là
  - white: we won’t discuss such mishaps

### 10. changed: the editor's name (II.11 ¶34; White ¶28)
- "John Benett" becomes "John Bennett". *(My reading, to confirm.)*
  - quote: l’honorable John Benett
  - white: the honorable John Bennett

### 11. cut: the bag on the neck, not the leg, and why (II.11 ¶35; White ¶29)
- The French ties the bag to the albatross's neck rather than its leg because these birds rest on the surface of the sea; White gives the neck and no reason. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: et non à sa patte, car ces oiseaux ont l’habitude de se reposer à la surface de la mer
  - white: This little bag was fastened around the neck of the albatross

### 12. cut: the debate on coal running out (II.11 ¶42–¶58; White —)
- White has nothing between the evening of talk of America and Top's barking at the well: the French has Spilett asking whether industry must end for lack of coal, Smith naming the diamond as pure carbon, the hundred thousand miners and hundred million quintals a year, American and Australian fields lasting "at least two hundred and fifty or three hundred years", and Spilett's warning of no machines, railways or steamers. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Au moins deux cent cinquante ou trois cents ans.
  - white: —

### 13. cut: water as the fuel of the future (II.11 ¶59–¶63; White —)
- Smith's prophecy that water, decomposed by electricity into hydrogen and oxygen, will replace coal is not in White. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: L’eau est le charbon de l’avenir.
  - white: —

### 14. cut: Nab's one remark (II.11 ¶64–¶65; White —)
- Pencroff says he would like to see it, and Nab, who speaks only here, says he has risen too early; White has neither. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Tu t’es levé trop tôt, Pencroff
  - white: —

### 15. changed: the incident darkens the engineer's brow, or does not (II.11 ¶73; White ¶40)
- The French says nothing more was said of the incident, which nevertheless darkened Smith's brow; White says it was not the incident that darkened his countenance, which reverses the sense. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Il ne fut plus question de l’incident, qui, cependant, assombrit le front de l’ingénieur.
  - white: It was not, however, the question of this incident that darkened the countenance of the engineer.

### 16. cut: July's greatest cold (II.11 ¶74; White ¶41)
- The French gives the maximum cold of the winter so far as eight degrees Fahrenheit (13.33 degrees centigrade below zero); White gives no figure. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: son maximum ne dépassa pas huit degrés fahrenheit (13,33° centigrades au-dessous de zéro)
  - white: though the temperature was not as low as during the preceding winter

### 17. cut: the spectacle of the storm-sea (II.11 ¶75; White —)
- The French paragraph of the colonists watching the waves from the windows, the shore flooded and spray rising over a hundred feet, is not in White. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dont les embruns s’élevaient à une hauteur de plus de cent pieds
  - white: —

### 18. cut: the Fahrenheit scale of the August cold (II.11 ¶77; White ¶43)
- The French says eight degrees Fahrenheit below zero (22 degrees centigrade below freezing); White says "8° below zero" with no scale. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: huit degrés Fahrenheit au-dessous de zéro (22° centig. au-dessous de glace)
  - white: the thermometer of the colonists indicated 8° below zero

### 19. condensed: the marsh birds (II.11 ¶78; White ¶44)
- The French names wild ducks, snipe, pintail, teal and grebes; White says "aquatic birds". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Canards sauvages, bécassines, pilets, sarcelles, grèbes
  - white: the great number of aquatic birds

### 20. cut: the marsh lies in the south-east (II.11 ¶78; White ¶44)
- The French places the marsh in the south-east of the island; White gives no direction. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dans le sud-est de l’île
  - white: an excursion, which had been planned for some time was made to Tadorn’s Fen

### 21. cut: the road by Port Balloon, and the bridge raised (II.11 ¶80; White ¶45)
- The French has the hunters take the Port Balloon road and Smith, once they have crossed the Mercy bridge, raise it behind them; White says only that he left them and returned. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: l’ingénieur le releva et revint
  - white: the engineer left them, and returned

### 22. cut: the well communicates with the sea, once carrying the lake's water (II.11 ¶81; White ¶45)
- White says only that Smith meant to explore the well; the French adds that it communicates with the sea because it once served as the passage for the lake's waters. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: puisqu’autrefois il servait de passage aux eaux du lac
  - white: This was to explore minutely the well opening into the corridor.

### 23. cut: the well's width (II.11 ¶83; White ¶47)
- The hole is about six feet across in the French; White gives no width. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dont le diamètre mesurait six pieds environ
  - white: —

### 24. cut: the wall is compact granite (II.11 ¶88; White ¶48)
- The French names the wall as compact granite; White says only "a compact mass". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: C’était un granit compacte
  - white: It was a compact mass

### 25. changed: "no living being" becomes "no human being" (II.11 ¶88; White ¶48)
- The French says no living being could force a way through the wall; White says no human being. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: nul être vivant ne pouvait se frayer un chemin
  - white: no human being could make his way
