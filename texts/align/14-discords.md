# I.14: White against the French (discords)

*Labeller f. Beads corrected: 12, 13, 14, 15 (White ¶11 renders French ¶15, White ¶12 renders ¶16, White ¶13 renders ¶17, White ¶14 renders ¶18–¶24); 16, 17 (White ¶15 renders French ¶25 only, White ¶16 renders ¶26, White ¶17 renders ¶27).*

### 1. cut: the link to yesterday's observations (I.14 ¶3; White ¶2)
- The French says the measuring completes the elements of the previous day's observations; White gives only the task. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Il s’agissait donc de compléter les éléments des observations de la veille
  - white: The first thing to be done was to measure the height of Prospect Plateau above the sea.

### 2. changed: "an instrument like yesterday's" becomes "compasses" (I.14 ¶4; White ¶3)
- Harbert asks about an instrument analogous to yesterday's; White names it a pair of compasses (and again in White ¶15, "taking the compasses"), where the French says only the instrument made the day before, with its two small boards. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Ne vous faut-il pas un instrument analogue à celui qui vous a servi hier
  - white: “Do you not need another pair of compasses?”

### 3. changed: where Cyrus walks (I.14 ¶6; White ¶5)
- In the French Cyrus moves away from the foot of the wall, down to the edge of the beach; in White he goes along the beach to the base of the wall. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: qui s’écarta du pied de la muraille de granit, en descendant jusqu’au bord de la grève
  - white: who proceeded along the beach to the base of the granite wall

### 4. changed: stake becomes stone (I.14 ¶9; White ¶6)
- The French marks the sighting point with a stake (piquet), the word the triangle talk later uses; White marks it with a stone, and then speaks of "the stake". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Puis il marqua soigneusement ce point avec un piquet.
  - white: he carefully marked this place with a stone

### 5. condensed: the two triangles (I.14 ¶15; White ¶11)
- The French sets out the three sides of each triangle, including the line of sight as hypotenuse of both; White reduces the first triangle's sides to "the distances from its base and top to the stake" and loses the visual ray as the common hypotenuse. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: la distance qui sépare le piquet du bas de la perche, et mon rayon visuel pour hypoténuse
  - white: the distances from its base and top to the stake

### 6. cut: the point of the method (I.14 ¶17; White ¶13)
- The French says the proportion spares the trouble of measuring the wall directly; White keeps only the calculation. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: nous évitera la peine de la mesurer directement
  - white: we have only to calculate the proportion in order to find the height of the wall

### 7. cut: the pole stands ten feet above the sand (I.14 ¶18; White ¶14)
- The French gives the figure that the proportion needs (the twelve-foot pole, two feet buried, is exactly ten feet above the sand); White gives no height for the pole, so its 15 and 500 cannot be worked. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: dont la longueur au-dessus du sable était exactement de dix pieds
  - white: The measurements were made with the pole

### 8. condensed: the proportion and the slate (I.14 ¶22–¶24; White ¶14)
- The French writes out the proportion and its working and says the stone is a slate-like schist scratched with a sharp shell; White gives a "flat stone" and "a bit of shell" and the result only. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: 15 : 500 : : 10 : x
  - white: using a flat stone and a bit of shell to figure with, determined the height of the wall to be 333.33 feet

### 9. cut: the note on the foot (I.14 ¶24; White ¶14)
- The French carries a note that the foot is the English foot of 30 centimetres; White has no note. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Il s’agit du pied anglais, qui vaut 30 centimètres.
  - white: —

### 10. condensed: how the latitude is reached (I.14 ¶25; White ¶15)
- The French adds the twenty-seven degrees between alpha and the Antarctic pole and reduces the plateau's height to sea level; White has "the differences already explained" and never names the star, the twenty-seven degrees, or the correction. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: en y ajoutant les vingt-sept degrés qui séparent alpha du pôle antarctique, et en réduisant au niveau de la mer la hauteur du plateau
  - white: allowing for the differences already explained

### 11. changed: the ground to be explored (I.14 ¶27; White ¶17)
- The French has the walk lie between the north of the lake and Shark Gulf; White places it to the north of the Gulf and the lake. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: entre le nord du lac et le golfe du Requin
  - white: situated to the north of Shark Gulf and the lake

### 12. changed: northern side becomes western side (I.14 ¶27; White ¶17)
- The French pushes the survey to the northern slope of South Mandible Cape; White says the western side. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: jusqu’au revers septentrional du cap Mandibule-Sud
  - white: as far as the western side of South Mandible Cape

### 13. changed: the day's plan (I.14 ¶27; White ¶17)
- The French gives the whole Sunday to the walk, with the party back in the evening; White speaks of spending "the morning" in it. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Il fut décidé que ce dimanche serait employé à une promenade
  - white: They therefore resolved to spend the morning in a walk

### 14. changed: the penguins (I.14 ¶28; White ¶18)
- The French calls the birds divers of the penguin kind (manchots); White gives "birds of the sphemiscus family" and then "divers", so the English reader has no penguin. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: C’étaient des plongeurs, de l’espèce des manchots
  - white: a number of birds of the sphemiscus family strutted gravely about. There were divers

### 15. changed: islet becomes island (I.14 ¶29; White ¶18)
- Cyrus announces a visit to the islet (îlot); White says "the island", which in this book is Lincoln Island itself. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: on ferait une visite à l’îlot
  - white: they would very soon, make a visit to the island

### 16. condensed: the shells (I.14 ¶30; White ¶18)
- The French names three kinds of shell; White says only that they would have delighted a malacologist. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: des phasianelles, des térébratules, des trigonies
  - white: The shore followed by the colonists was strewn with mollusks, which would have delighted a malacologist.

### 17. changed: net becomes basket (I.14 ¶36; White ¶24)
- Nab's carrier is a net of hibiscus fibre in the French; White calls it a basket. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: une sorte de filet en fibres d’hibiscus
  - white: in a basket which Neb had made from the hibiscus fibres

### 18. cut: the cape closes Union Bay (I.14 ¶38; White ¶26)
- The French says South Mandible Cape is the point closing Union Bay; White names the cape only. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: cette pointe qui fermait la baie de l’Union
  - white: as far as South Mandible Cape

### 19. changed: arrows become shooting (I.14 ¶38; White ¶26)
- Pencroff tries the ducks with arrows; White says he "tried to shoot", which reads as a gun, and sits oddly with the complaint about having no guns that follows. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Il essaya bien de les abattre à coups de flèche
  - white: He tried to shoot some

### 20. condensed: the utensils thrown overboard (I.14 ¶43; White ¶31)
- The French lists weapons, utensils and pocket knives; White has "the arms and everything else, even our penknives". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: et nos ustensiles, et jusqu’à nos couteaux de poche
  - white: the arms and everything else, even our penknives

### 21. changed: Forster and his companions (I.14 ¶47; White ¶34)
- The French names Jonathan Forster and his several companions, and Pencroff's picture of the empty place and the flown machine; White has "Mr. Forster and his friend" and drops the full name, the plural and the bewilderment (ahurissement). *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: Jonathan Forster et de ses compagnons
  - white: Mr. Forster and his friend

### 22. condensed: west and east (I.14 ¶54; White ¶41)
- The French puts the island across from New Zealand to the west and Chile to the east; White says only "in the latitude of New Zealand and Chili". *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: par le travers de la Nouvelle-Zélande à l’ouest, et de la côte du Chili à l’est
  - white: places the island in the latitude of New Zealand and Chili

### 23. cut: the longitude's accuracy (I.14 ¶54; White ¶41)
- The French hopes the longitude will give the position with sufficient approximation; White drops the qualification. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: avec une approximation suffisante, je l’espère
  - white: that I hope to get pretty soon from the longitude

### 24. changed: half past eleven becomes eleven (I.14 ¶65; White ¶51)
- The time at the halt; the French is half past eleven. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: car il était onze heures et demie
  - white: It was 11 o’clock

### 25. cut: the rod is six feet high (I.14 ¶66; White ¶52)
- The French gives the rod's height; White does not. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: la baguette, haute de six pieds
  - white: the rod which he stuck in the sand

### 26. cut: what the shadow is watched for (I.14 ¶68; White ¶53)
- The French says the point of watching is to see the instant when the shadow, after shortening, begins to lengthen; White says only to watch the end of the shadow. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: après avoir successivement diminué, elle recommencerait à s’allonger
  - white: all they had to do was to watch carefully the end of the shadow

### 27. cut: the shadow is the hand of a dial (I.14 ¶68; White —)
- The French compares the rod's shadow to the hand of a dial, longer hand and easier reading; White has nothing. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: L’ombre de la baguette n’était pas autre chose que l’aiguille d’un cadran.
  - white: —

### 28. changed: "What time?" becomes "Now!" (I.14 ¶72; White ¶57)
- Cyrus asks the time; in White he calls "Now!" and the reporter's answer follows. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: « Quelle heure ? dit-il.
  - white: “Now!”

### 29. cut: which way the five hours run (I.14 ¶74; White ¶59)
- The French says it was noon at Lincoln Island when it was already five in the evening at Washington, which is why the island lies west; White says only that there is five hours' difference. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: il était midi à l’île Lincoln, quand il était déjà cinq heures du soir à Washington
  - white: There was, as we have seen, five hours difference between the meridian of Washington and that of the island.

### 30. condensed: four minutes to a degree (I.14 ¶74; White ¶59)
- The French gives the sun's rate as one degree in four minutes, fifteen degrees an hour; White gives only the hourly rate. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: un degré par quatre minutes, soit quinze degrés par heure
  - white: the sun passes around the earth at the rate of 15° an hour

### 31. cut: the last addition and the Greenwich convention (I.14 ¶75; White ¶59)
- The French adds seventy-seven to seventy-five west of Greenwich to reach the hundred and fifty-second degree, and notes that Americans and English reckon from Greenwich; White gives the sum "in the neighborhood of longitude 152° west", with no addition and no remark on the convention. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: que les Américains prennent pour point de départ des longitudes, concurremment avec les Anglais
  - white: it follows that the island was in the neighborhood of longitude 152° west

### 32. condensed: sixty miles to the degree (I.14 ¶77; White ¶61)
- The French gives the scale (sixty miles a degree) from which five degrees become three hundred miles; White gives the result only. *(Drafted.)*
  - quote: à soixante milles par degré
  - white: 5° or 300 miles in each direction
